\subsection{Research}\label{app:research}

\paragraph{Equilibrium with research.}
Market makers and the rival do not observe the research choice; they use a conjectured intensity
\(\hat\lambda\). An equilibrium with research is a tuple \((\hat\lambda,\sigma_H,\sigma_L,\mu,P,\bar\mu,\zeta)\),
together with the bids. Conditions (E1)--(E6) of Definition~\ref{def:equilibrium} hold at the intensity
\(\hat\lambda\). Condition (E5) holds also at \(\hat\lambda=0\): a deviation in research makes a success possible,
and the order after it must be optimal. A seventh condition completes the definition.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(E\arabic*),start=7,leftmargin=3em]
\item \emph{Research optimality.} Hold \(P\) and \(\zeta\) at their values in the tuple. Then \(\hat\lambda\) is
the research choice of a best response among all joint deviations in research and orders.
\end{enumerate}
A joint deviation changes the true order probabilities, but not the prices or the entry rule. With the research
value \(\Psi\) below, the expected trading payoff after a success at the candidate's prices,
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint} shows that the best research choice is unique and equals \(\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}\). Condition
(E7) therefore reads \(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}\), and research never has a tie.

\paragraph{Setting.}
The classes satisfy the class ranking: \(G_H\le G_L\), both class values are at least the reserve \(p>0\), and both class
means are finite. The incumbent law \(F\) is continuous, \(\DT=t_H-t_L>0\), \(\gamma=g_H-g_L>0\), and
\(\tau=(c_H-g_L)/\gamma>1/2\). The prior is \(1/2\), the preparation cost is zero with probability
\(\rho\in(0,1)\) and \(c_H>0\) otherwise, \(k,a>0\), \(0<\beta<1/2\), and \(0<\lb<1\).
Research costs \((a/2)\lambda^2\), succeeds with probability \(\lambda\), and is unobserved by market makers
and the rival. At a success the informed trader learns the class. The independence assumptions of the model hold.
Write
\[
 b=(1-\lambda)\beta,\qquad b_0=(1-\lambda)(1-2\beta).
\]
\[
 m_\lambda=\frac{\beta(1-\lambda)}{2\beta+(1-2\beta)\lambda},\quad M_\lambda=1-m_\lambda,
 \qquad
 m_\lambda'=-\frac{\beta}{[2\beta+(1-2\beta)\lambda]^2}.
\]
For \(1/2<\tau<1\), let
\[
 \lambda_E(\tau)=\frac{\beta(2\tau-1)}{\beta+(1-2\beta)(1-\tau)},
 \qquad J=\DT(1-\tau).
\]
For \(\rho\DT\ge2k\), let \(\lambda_Q(\DT)\) be the unique root in \([0,1)\) of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-floor}
 a\lambda_Q=\rho\DT m_{\lambda_Q}-k.
\end{equation}
It equals zero exactly when \(\rho\DT=2k\).
A path \((F_r)_{r\in I}\) holds every other primitive fixed and strengthens the incumbent in first-order
stochastic dominance as \(r\) rises. The spread and, on \(\tau>1/2\), the preparation threshold are
nondecreasing along such a path.

For a candidate with conjectured research \(\hat\lambda\), write
\(\hat b=(1-\hat\lambda)\beta\), \(\hat b_0=(1-\hat\lambda)(1-2\beta)\), and
\(f_j(q)=(1-\hat\lambda)\eta(q)+\hat\lambda\sigma_j(q)\).
A candidate satisfies beliefs, competitive pricing, preparation and truthful bidding.
Lemma~\ref{lem:trading} gives its trading payoffs after a success:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-payoffs}
 U_H(q)=q\,e(q)\DT[1-\mu(q)]-k|q|,\qquad
 U_L(q)=-q\,e(q)\DT\mu(q)-k|q|.
\end{equation}
The inverse posterior map satisfies
\(d\lambda_E/d\tau=\beta/[\beta+(1-2\beta)(1-\tau)]^2>0\).


\begin{lemma}[Joint deviations]\label{lem:a5-joint}
For a candidate satisfying beliefs, competitive pricing, preparation and truthful bidding, let
\(\Psi=\tfrac12\max_qU_H(q)+\tfrac12\max_qU_L(q)\) be its best trading value after a research success.
At its fixed prices and preparation rule, the best payoff of a joint deviation to any
\(\tilde\lambda\in[0,\lb]\) is \(\tilde\lambda\Psi-(a/2)\tilde\lambda^2\).
The unique best research choice is \(\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}\). Thus research optimality is exactly
\(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}\), under all four tie rules.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A joint deviation changes the true order law to \((1-\tilde\lambda)\eta+\tilde\lambda\sigma'_j\). It does not change the prices or the
preparation rule, because market makers and the rival use the candidate's \(\hat\lambda\) and \(\sigma_j\).
The informed trader trades only after a success, so its expected payoff is
\[
\Pi(\tilde\lambda,\sigma')=\tilde\lambda\Bigl[\tfrac12\sum_q\sigma'_H(q)U_H(q)+\tfrac12\sum_q\sigma'_L(q)U_L(q)\Bigr]-\frac a2\tilde\lambda^2 ,
\]
with \(U_j\) from \eqref{eq:a5-payoffs} at the candidate's prices and entry. The bracket is at most \(\Psi\), with
equality exactly when each \(\sigma'_j\) puts weight only on maximizers of \(U_j\). So
\(\sup_{\sigma'}\Pi(\tilde\lambda,\sigma')=\tilde\lambda\Psi-(a/2)\tilde\lambda^2\). This function of \(\tilde\lambda\) is strictly concave with derivative \(\Psi-a\tilde\lambda\). If
\(\Psi\ge a\lb\), the derivative is positive on \([0,\lb)\), and the maximizer is \(\lb\). If \(\Psi<a\lb\), the
maximizer is \(\Psi/a\in[0,\lb)\). Strict concavity makes the maximizer unique, and it also excludes a nondegenerate
mixture over research intensities.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounds under every profile]\label{lem:a5-bounds}
Let \(0\le\hat\lambda\le\lb<1\). Under all four tie rules:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item For every order profile, the nonzero-order posteriors lie in
\([m_{\hat\lambda},M_{\hat\lambda}]\), with \(M_{\hat\lambda}\le M_{\lb}\).
If also \(\beta\le1/3\), the same bound holds for the zero-order posterior under every profile.
\item For every candidate satisfying beliefs, competitive pricing, preparation and truthful bidding,
\(P(q)=P(q')\) implies \(\mu(q)=\mu(q')\), and \(\bar\mu(P(q))=\mu(q)\).
\item Wrong-signed orders satisfy \(U_H(-1)\le-k\) and \(U_L(1)\le-k\); every equilibrium has
\(\sigma_H(-1)=\sigma_L(1)=0\).
\item With no wrong-signed orders, \(\mu(-1)\le1/2\le\mu(1)\).
If also \(\sigma_H(1)=1\), then \(\mu(1)=M_{\hat\lambda}\) and \(\mu(0)\le1/2\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) At each nonzero order, its class likelihood lies between \(\hat b\) and \(\hat b+\hat\lambda\).
Bayes' rule at the prior \(1/2\) therefore puts its posterior between
\(\hat b/(2\hat b+\hat\lambda)=m_{\hat\lambda}\) and \(M_{\hat\lambda}\).
At zero the same argument uses \(\hat b_0\); its bounds lie inside this interval when
\(\hat b_0\ge\hat b\), or \(\beta\le1/3\). The top bound rises with research, so
\(M_{\hat\lambda}\le M_{\lb}\).

(b) Orders with the same price have the same preparation probability and hence the same entry.
Competitive pricing gives
\(P(q)=t_0+e(q)[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu(q)]\). Since \(e(q)\ge\rho>0\) and \(\DT>0\), equal prices
then imply equal posteriors. Averaging the posteriors at a common price gives that same posterior as the
rival's belief.

(c) The two wrong-signed payoffs in \eqref{eq:a5-payoffs} are at most \(-k\), while abstention earns zero.
They therefore receive zero probability under either tie rule for the informed trader.

(d) With no wrong-signed orders,
\(f_H(1)=\hat b+\hat\lambda\sigma_H(1)\ge\hat b=f_L(1)\) and
\(f_H(-1)=\hat b\le\hat b+\hat\lambda\sigma_L(-1)=f_L(-1)\).
With a full high-class purchase,
\(f_H(0)=\hat b_0\le\hat b_0+\hat\lambda\sigma_L(0)=f_L(0)\) and
\(\mu(1)=(\hat b+\hat\lambda)/(2\hat b+\hat\lambda)=M_{\hat\lambda}\).
Bayes' rule gives the stated comparisons with the prior.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Positive research needs a strict full purchase]\label{lem:a5-buy}
Under either tie rule for the informed trader and either tie rule for the rival, every equilibrium with \(\hat\lambda>0\) has
\(U_H(1)>0\) and \(\sigma_H(1)=1\). Its posteriors satisfy
\(\mu(1)=M_{\hat\lambda}\), \(\mu(0)\le1/2\) and \(\mu(-1)\le1/2\); entry after a sale or no order is
\(\rho\). This holds for every \(0<\beta<1/2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint}, \(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}>0\), so \(\Psi>0\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-bounds}(c) and
\(U_j(0)=0\), either \(U_H(1)>0\) or \(U_L(-1)>0\). Suppose \(U_L(-1)>0\). Then the sale is the unique maximizer of
class \(L\), and (E5) gives \(\sigma_L(-1)=1\). With \(\sigma_H(-1)=0\), \(f_H(-1)=\hat b\) and
\(f_L(-1)=\hat b+\hat\lambda\), so \(\mu(-1)=m_{\hat\lambda}\le1/2<\tau\). Hence \(e(-1)=\rho\) and
\(U_L(-1)=\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k>0\). The purchase then earns
\(U_H(1)=e(1)\DT[1-\mu(1)]-k\ge\rho\DT(1-M_{\hat\lambda})-k=\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k>0\), by
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-bounds}(a). So \(U_H(1)>0\) in both cases. The purchase is the unique maximizer of class \(H\), and
\(\sigma_H(1)=1\) under both tie rules for the informed trader. Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-bounds}(d) gives the three posteriors. Since
\(\tau>1/2\), Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(d) gives \(\zeta(P(0))=\zeta(P(-1))=0\) under both tie rules for the rival.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The low class's sale]\label{lem:a5-sale}
Let \(0<\lambda\le\lb\), let class \(H\) buy with probability one after a success, and suppose an indifferent informed trader
may mix over its best orders. By Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-low} at presence \(\lambda\) and prior \(1/2\),
the unique low-class sale rate consistent with its best orders is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-sale}
 s_-=\lambda\sigma_L(-1)=\min\{\lambda,\ b(\rho\DT/k-2)^+\}.
\end{equation}
The remaining low-class probability is on abstention, and
\[
 \mu(-1)=\frac{b}{2b+s_-},\qquad
 \mu(0)=\frac{b_0}{2b_0+\lambda-s_-},\qquad \mu(1)=M_\lambda.
\]
Both nonpurchase orders have entry \(\rho\), under either tie rule for the rival. Class \(L\) abstains when
\(\rho\DT\le2k\); it mixes when \(\rho\DT m_\lambda<k<\rho\DT/2\), with
\(\mu(-1)=k/(\rho\DT)\); and it sells with probability one when \(\rho\DT m_\lambda\ge k\).
At \(\rho\DT=2k\) its sale rate is zero; at \(\rho\DT m_\lambda=k\) its sale probability is one.
Its best net payoff is
\[
 \max_q U_L(q)=(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k)^+.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-low}, at presence \(\lambda\) and prior \(1/2\), gives \eqref{eq:a5-sale}.
In order \((-1,0,1)\), the class likelihoods are
\[
 f_H=(b,b_0,b+\lambda),\qquad f_L=(b+s_-,b_0+\lambda-s_-,b).
\]
Bayes' rule gives the displayed posteriors. Both nonpurchase posteriors are at most \(1/2<\tau\),
so entry there is \(\rho\). The sale payoff
\[
 U_L(-1)=\frac{\rho\DT b}{2b+s_-}-k
\]
falls strictly in \(s_-\). At zero sale rate it is nonpositive exactly when \(\rho\DT\le2k\);
at full selling it is nonnegative exactly when \(\rho\DT m_\lambda\ge k\).
Between these cases its unique zero gives the mixing rate and the posterior \(k/(\rho\DT)\).
At the first equality any positive rate makes selling lose; at the second equality any smaller rate makes
selling strictly profitable. This proves both boundary choices. During abstention or mixing the best low-class
payoff is zero; at full selling it is \(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k\), which gives its positive-part expression.
\end{proof}

\begin{named}{Proposition~\ref{prop:research} (Competition creates research, full statement)}\fixedlabel{prop:research-app}{4}
Maintain the setting above. An indifferent informed trader may mix over its best orders and an indifferent high-cost
rival prepares. When an indifferent high-cost rival may prepare with any probability, select full
preparation after a purchase that reaches the threshold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item Recruiting research, an equilibrium with positive research and full preparation after a purchase, exists
exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:research-exist}
 \DT>2k,\qquad \frac12<\tau\le M_{\min\{\lambda_R(\DT),\lb\}},
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda_R(\DT)\) is the unique root in \((0,1)\) of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:research-im}
 2a\lambda=\DT m_\lambda-k+(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k)^+.
\end{equation}
Its research is \(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lambda_R(\DT),\lb\}\). Class \(H\) buys strictly and fully, class \(L\)
uses Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-sale}, and entry is \(e(1)=1\), \(e(0)=e(-1)=\rho\). The posteriors are those of that
lemma, prices are \(P(x)=t_0+e(x)[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu(x)]\), price beliefs are
\(\bar\mu(P(x))=\mu(x)\), and bids are truthful. Equal nonpurchase posteriors have equal entry and equal prices.

\item Research is continuous and strictly increasing in \(\DT\) below its cap, and constant at the cap.
Total entry and class entry are
\begin{align*}
 \Ent&=\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\hat\lambda],\\
 e_H&=\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1-\beta)\hat\lambda],\qquad
 e_L=\rho+(1-\rho)\beta(1-\hat\lambda).
\end{align*}
Thus total entry and class-\(H\) entry rise, class-\(L\) entry falls, and the class-\(H\) share of prepared
rivals rises with the spread, strictly below the cap and weakly throughout the existence set.
The prepared-value distribution
\((e_HG_H+e_LG_L)/(e_H+e_L)\) improves in first-order stochastic dominance; its mean rises strictly when research
rises strictly. For almost every fixed incumbent value \(x\), the high-class acquisition probability is
\[
 \Pr(H\text{ acquires}\mid R=x)=
 \begin{cases}e_H/2,&x<p,\\ e_H[1-G_H(x)]/2,&x\ge p.\end{cases}
\]
It rises strictly with the spread below the cap wherever class \(H\) can win. At a class-value atom the auction's
tie allocation gives the probability of winning; the same comparison holds using that fixed allocation.
At a value outside the incumbent's support, this expression gives the counterfactual allocation at that value.

While the low class abstains or mixes, and after a sure sale becomes strictly profitable, respectively,
\[
 \frac{d\lambda_R}{d\DT}=
 \frac{m_{\lambda_R}}{2a-\DT m'_{\lambda_R}},\qquad
 \frac{d\lambda_R}{d\DT}=
 \frac{(1+\rho)m_{\lambda_R}}{2a-(1+\rho)\DT m'_{\lambda_R}}.
\]
If \((1-\rho)k/(2a\rho)<\lb\), these slopes meet with a strict upward jump at
\(\lambda_R=(1-\rho)k/(2a\rho)\). On an equilibrium path that passes through this intensity, a sure sale's
profit adds a second return to research. Research and entry are continuous through the first sale, the onset of
strictly profitable full selling, and the cap. The cap spread, at which research reaches its cap, is
\[
 \begin{cases}
 (2a\lb+k)/m_{\lb},&\lb\le(1-\rho)k/(2a\rho),\\
 (2a\lb+2k)/[(1+\rho)m_{\lb}],&\lb>(1-\rho)k/(2a\rho).
 \end{cases}
\]

\item High-class ownership is \(\Own_H(F)=e_H(F)W_H(F)/2\). For two recruiting economies on a path,
\[
 \Own_H(F_{r'})\ge\Own_H(F_r)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{W_H(F_{r'})}{W_H(F_r)}\ge\frac{e_H(F_r)}{e_H(F_{r'})},
\]
with the corresponding strict equivalence. These win probabilities are positive. If class \(H\) outbids every
incumbent along the recruiting part of the path, ownership rises weakly with strength, and strictly for a strict
increase in the spread from a research intensity below the cap. Decisive advantage (Lemma~\ref{lem:decisive}),
\(g_H(F)>c_H\Longrightarrow W_H(F)=1\) throughout the family, supplies this outbidding condition at every
recruiting economy.

\item Let \(I=[r_-,r_+]\) be compact and \(\DT,\tau\) continuous along the path. The capped recruiting set is
\[
 \{r\in I:\DT(r)m_{\lb}-k+(\rho\DT(r)m_{\lb}-k)^+\ge2a\lb,\ \tau(r)\le M_{\lb}\}.
\]
It is empty, a singleton, or a nondegenerate closed interval. If nonempty, it ends at
\(\max\{r\in I:\tau(r)\le M_{\lb}\}\).

Suppose the path first reaches the cap spread at \(r_{\lb}>r_-\), with a strictly smaller spread at \(r_-\).
On \([r_-,r_{\lb}]\), suppose \(\tau<1\), and define
\[
 \varphi_a^*=J-k+(\rho J-k)^+-2a\lambda_E(\tau).
\]
If \(\varphi_a^*\) is strictly increasing on \([r_-,r_{\lb}]\) and
\(\varphi_a^*(r_-)<0<\varphi_a^*(r_{\lb})\), the full recruiting set is
\[
 [r_R,\ \max\{r\in I:\tau(r)\le M_{\lb}\}],
\]
where \(r_R\) is the unique zero of \(\varphi_a^*\) before \(r_{\lb}\).
Research is below its cap up to \(r_{\lb}\) and at the cap thereafter. A threshold plateau at \(M_{\lb}\) belongs
to the recruiting set through its last point.
On a path with continuously differentiable \(\DT,\tau\) on \([r_-,r_{\lb}]\), the cheap-research condition
{\renewcommand{\theHequation}{A.CR}%
\begin{equation}\tag{CR}\label{eq:a5-cheap}
 [1+\rho\1\{\rho J>k\}]J'
 -\frac{2a\beta\tau'}{[\beta+(1-2\beta)(1-\tau)]^2}>0
 \quad\text{almost everywhere on }[r_-,r_{\lb}]
\end{equation}}%
makes \(\varphi_a^*\) strictly increasing there.
\end{enumerate}
\end{named}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part (a).} Positive research forces the strict full purchase in Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-buy}.
With full preparation after that purchase, its net payoff is \(\DT m_\lambda-k\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-sale} gives the other class's best payoff. Thus
\[
 \Psi=\tfrac12\bigl[\DT m_\lambda-k+(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k)^+\bigr].
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint} gives \eqref{eq:research-im} below the cap and its weak inequality at the cap.
The residual
\[
 \DT m_\lambda-k+(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k)^+-2a\lambda
\]
is continuous and strictly decreasing in \(\lambda\). If \(\DT>2k\), it is positive at zero and negative at
one, so it has exactly one root in \((0,1)\). If \(\DT\le2k\), every positive research intensity makes
\(\DT m_\lambda-k<0\), contrary to the strict purchase. This proves the root condition and the capped
research choice. At the capped choice the residual is nonnegative. A nonpositive purchase payoff would make
the sale's positive part zero, contradicting \(2a\hat\lambda>0\); the purchase is therefore strict.

A purchase recruits exactly when \(M_{\hat\lambda}\ge\tau\), including equality under the stated preparation
choice. This gives \eqref{eq:research-exist}. Conversely, under that condition use the sale lemma's
likelihoods and posteriors, the stated entry, and the competitive prices. For distinct posteriors
\(\mu(x)>\mu(y)\), entry is nondecreasing in the posterior and
\[
 P(x)-P(y)=e(x)\DT[\mu(x)-\mu(y)]
 +[e(x)-e(y)][t_L-t_0+\DT\mu(y)]>0,
\]
since \(t_L\ge t_0\) and \(e(x)\ge\rho>0\). Equal nonpurchase posteriors have equal entry \(\rho\)
and equal prices, so each price belief equals the posterior of its orders. The threshold rule gives
preparation, the strict purchase and the sale lemma give optimal orders, and truthful bids give the auction
allocation. The research equality or cap inequality gives optimality of every joint deviation by
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint}. These conditions also determine the positive-research tuple uniquely.

\emph{Part (b).} For a fixed positive intensity, the residual rises strictly with \(\DT\).
Its strict decrease in intensity makes its root continuous and strictly increasing in the spread.
Clipping the root at \(\lb\) preserves continuity and gives constancy at the cap.
A purchase has probability
\(\tfrac12(b+\hat\lambda)+\tfrac12b=\beta+(1/2-\beta)\hat\lambda\).
Only a purchase raises entry above \(\rho\), which gives \(\Ent\). Conditional purchase probabilities
\(b+\hat\lambda\) and \(b\) give \(e_H\) and \(e_L\). Their derivatives in research have signs
positive and negative, respectively, and
\[
 \frac{d}{d\hat\lambda}\frac{e_H}{e_H+e_L}
 =\frac{(1-\rho)[\rho+(1-\rho)\beta]}{(e_H+e_L)^2}>0.
\]
Preparation is independent of values conditional on class. An increase in this share therefore shifts the
prepared-value distribution toward \(G_H\), which dominates \(G_L\). Its mean rises strictly because
\(\E_H\theta-\E_L\theta=\DT+\gamma>0\).
The same independence gives the displayed acquisition probability at a fixed incumbent value; its win factor
is fixed by the class law and the auction's allocation at a tie, so its change has the sign of the change in
\(e_H\) wherever that factor is positive.

Implicit differentiation of the two smooth pieces of \eqref{eq:research-im} gives the two slopes in the
statement. At the sale-profit boundary, \(\rho\DT m_{\lambda_R}=k\); combining this with
\(2a\lambda_R=\DT m_{\lambda_R}-k\) gives \(\lambda_R=(1-\rho)k/(2a\rho)\).
At the common point, subtracting the first slope from the second gives
\[
 \frac{2a\rho m_{\lambda_R}}
 {(2a-\DT m'_{\lambda_R})[2a-(1+\rho)\DT m'_{\lambda_R}]}>0.
\]
The sale rate and its positive-part payoff are continuous at both sale boundaries. The research root and
entry formulas are continuous there and at the cap. Substituting \(\lambda=\lb\) in the research equation
on each side of the sale-profit boundary gives the stated cap spread.

\emph{Part (c).} Conditional independence gives
\(\Own_H(F)=\Pr(H)\Pr(\text{preparation}\mid H)W_H(F)=e_H(F)W_H(F)/2\).
At a recruiting economy, \(c_H\le g_L+M_{\hat\lambda}\gamma<g_H\); hence \(g_H>0\) and \(W_H(F)>0\).
Dividing the two positive ownership expressions proves the weak and strict equivalences.
If class \(H\) outbids every incumbent, \(W_H=1\), and part (b) gives the stated monotonicity.
The inequality \(g_H>c_H\) also makes the implication in decisive advantage apply.

\emph{Part (d), research at the cap.}
At a positive research intensity, class \(H\) buys strictly and fully. A sale and no order have posteriors at most
\(1/2\), and hence entry \(\rho\). With full preparation after a purchase, the best trading payoffs are
\(\DT m_\lambda-k\) in class \(H\) and \((\rho\DT m_\lambda-k)^+\) in class \(L\). Holding prices and the
preparation rule fixed, a joint deviation to \(\tilde\lambda\) yields
\[
\frac{\tilde\lambda}{2}
 \bigl[\DT m_\lambda-k+(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k)^+\bigr]
 -\frac a2\tilde\lambda^2.
\]
Its maximum over \([0,\lb]\) is at the cap exactly when
\[
\DT m_{\lb}-k+(\rho\DT m_{\lb}-k)^+\ge2a\lb.
\]
The left side is continuous and strictly increasing in \(\DT\). Equality has exactly one solution. If its sale
term is zero, that solution is \((2a\lb+k)/m_{\lb}\), and the sale term is zero exactly when
\(\rho(2a\lb+k)\le k\), or \(\lb\le(1-\rho)k/(2a\rho)\). Otherwise equality gives the second displayed value
of the cap spread. Both pieces agree at their common boundary. Since \(m_{\lb}<1/2\), the residual at
\(\DT=2k\) is strictly negative, and the cap spread exceeds \(2k\).

At or above the cap spread, the high-class purchase earns strictly more than zero: a nonpositive purchase payoff
would make the sale's positive part zero and contradict the positive right side \(2a\lb\). The low class uses
the unique sale rate
\[
 s_-=\min\{\lb,\ \bar b(\rho\DT/k-2)^+\},\qquad \bar b=(1-\lb)\beta.
\]
The purchase posterior is \(M_{\lb}\). It recruits exactly when \(\tau\le M_{\lb}\), with full preparation at
equality under the stated rule. Bayes' rule, competitive prices and truthful bids give the remaining equilibrium
conditions. In particular, if a sale and no order have the same posterior, they both have entry \(\rho\) and the
same price, so the belief at that price is their common posterior. This proves the pointwise description.

Along the path, \(\DT\) is nondecreasing by Lemma~\ref{lem:twonumbers}.
Each class's gross profit and their positive gap are nonincreasing;
since \(c_H-g_L>0\), \(\tau=(c_H-g_L)/(g_H-g_L)\) is nondecreasing too. Consequently
the set of strengths at or above the cap spread contains every strength above any of its members, and
\(\{\tau\le M_{\lb}\}\) contains every strength below any of its members. Continuity and compactness make
both sets closed. Their intersection is empty, a singleton, or the interval with the endpoints in the statement.
A stretch on which \(\tau=M_{\lb}\) belongs to this interval whenever research has reached the cap. If the right
endpoint lies in the interior of \(I\), continuity gives \(\tau=M_{\lb}\) there; if the path ends first, its
last strength is the endpoint.

\emph{Part (d), the recruiting interval.}
For a fixed \(\DT>2k\), the function
\[
 \DT m_\lambda-k+(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k)^+-2a\lambda
\]
is continuous and strictly decreasing in \(\lambda\), positive at zero and negative at one. Its unique zero is
\(\lambda_R(\DT)\). At \(\lambda=\lambda_E(\tau)\), the function equals \(\varphi_a^*\), because
\(m_{\lambda_E}=1-\tau\). Therefore, when \(1/2<\tau<1\), the purchase posterior reaches the threshold at the
capped root exactly when
\[
 \varphi_a^*\ge0\quad\hbox{and}\quad\lambda_E(\tau)\le\lb.
\]
A nonnegative margin implies \(J-k+(\rho J-k)^+\ge2a\lambda_E>0\). If \(J\le k\), its left side is nonpositive,
so \(J>k\), which gives \(\DT>2k\) since \(1-\tau<1/2\). Thus these two inequalities also imply that the root
exists. Conversely, the root comparison proves their necessity.

Continuity, strict increase and the endpoint signs of \(\varphi_a^*\) yield exactly one zero \(r_R\), with a
negative margin below it and a positive margin above it.

At \(r_{\lb}\), continuity makes the spread equal to the cap spread, so \(\lambda_R=\lb\). The positive margin there gives
\(\lambda_E(\tau(r_{\lb}))<\lb\), or \(\tau(r_{\lb})<M_{\lb}\). Since \(\tau\) is nondecreasing, the cap
posterior reaches the threshold throughout \([r_-,r_{\lb}]\). On that interval, the exact root comparison
therefore makes recruitment equivalent to \(\varphi_a^*\ge0\), or \(r\ge r_R\). Below \(r_{\lb}\), the spread
is strictly below the cap spread, so research is below its cap. From \(r_{\lb}\) on, research optimality at the
cap persists because \(\DT\) is nondecreasing. The cap result gives exactly the upper interval in the statement,
including its last point and every threshold plateau within it.

When \(\DT,\tau\) are continuously differentiable, \(\varphi_a^*\) is absolutely continuous on
\([r_-,r_{\lb}]\). Away from \(\rho J=k\) its derivative is the expression in \eqref{eq:a5-cheap}.
On the level set \(\rho J=k\), \(J'=0\) almost everywhere, so that expression is its derivative almost
everywhere there as well. Integrating the strictly positive derivative over any nondegenerate subinterval
proves that \(\varphi_a^*\) rises strictly.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The research margin.}
Where \(\tau'>0\), condition~\eqref{eq:a5-cheap} says that
\[
 a<\frac{[1+\rho\1\{\rho J>k\}]J'}{2\lambda_E'(\tau)\tau'}.
\]
Where \(\tau'=0\), it says \(J'>0\). Before a sure sale pays, the derivative of the margin is
\(J'-2a\lambda_E'(\tau)\tau'\); once it pays, the derivative is
\((1+\rho)J'-2a\lambda_E'(\tau)\tau'\). The margin is continuous at \(\rho J=k\); at a crossing into
\(\rho J>k\), its derivative jumps up by \(\rho J'\). The condition applies where the recruiting interval
starts with a strictly profitable sure sale.

\paragraph{Ownership at the same incumbent.}\fixedlabel{par:a5-ownership-ratio}{4}
Where recruiting research coexists with zero research, \(\rho\DT\le2k\), their same-incumbent ownership ratio is
\(\Own_H^{\rm R}/\Own_H^{\rm Z}=e_H/\rho>1\), which rises strictly with the spread below the research cap and
stays constant at the cap.

Entry \(\rho\) after every order in Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-outcomes}(a) gives \(\Own_H^{\rm Z}=\rho W_H(F)/2\).
Dividing the recruiting ownership formula by it cancels the same positive win probability; the formula for
\(e_H\) in Proposition~\ref{prop:research}(b) gives the strict increase below the cap and constancy at the cap.

\begin{lemma}[The three research outcomes]\label{lem:a5-outcomes}
Maintain the setting above. An indifferent informed trader may mix over its best orders and an indifferent high-cost
rival prepares. The following outcomes exhaust the equilibria.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item Zero research exists exactly when \(\rho\DT\le2k\). All posteriors are \(1/2\), entry is \(\rho\), and
every order has price \(t_0+\rho[t_L-t_0+\DT/2]\). If the inequality is strict, both classes abstain after a
hypothetical success. At equality, each class uses any mixture of its correct order and abstention; all these
strategies give the same zero-research outcome.
\item Recruiting research is exactly the outcome of Proposition~\ref{prop:research}(a).
\item Floor-entry research exists exactly when
\[
 \rho\DT>2k,\qquad \tau>M_{\min\{\lambda_Q(\DT),\lb\}},
\]
where \(\lambda_Q\) is the root of \eqref{eq:a5-floor}.
Research is \(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}\), both classes trade strictly and fully after a success, the
posteriors are \((m_{\hat\lambda},1/2,M_{\hat\lambda})\), and entry is \(\rho\) after every order. Prices are
\(t_0+\rho[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu(x)]\), price beliefs are the corresponding posteriors, and bids are truthful.
\end{enumerate}
Each positive-research outcome has a unique tuple. The two positive-research outcomes coexist exactly when
\[
 \rho\DT>2k,\qquad
 M_{\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}}<\tau\le M_{\min\{\lambda_R,\lb\}}.
\]
The uncapped roots satisfy \(\lambda_Q<\lambda_R\); their capped values are strictly ordered when
\(\lambda_Q<\lb\) and coincide when both roots reach the cap. Thus the coexistence band has positive width in
\(\tau\) exactly when \(\lambda_Q<\lb\). At least one of the three outcomes exists at every auction in the
stated domain, including \(\tau\ge1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At conjectured research zero, \(f_j(x)=\eta(x)>0\) for both classes, regardless of their orders after a
hypothetical success. Bayes' rule gives the posterior \(1/2\) at every order. A belief at a price averages these identical posteriors,
so it too is \(1/2\), even if several orders share the price. Since \(\tau>1/2\), the high-cost rival
strictly prefers abstention after each price, under either tie rule for the rival. Entry is therefore \(\rho\).
Competitive pricing then gives the common price in the statement. This establishes beliefs, preparation and
prices without any restriction on the hypothetical-success orders.

At these fixed prices and entry, the correct-order payoffs are
\[
 U_H(1)=U_L(-1)=\rho\DT/2-k,
\]
while \(U_H(0)=U_L(0)=0\), and the wrong-signed payoffs are
\(U_H(-1)=U_L(1)=-\rho\DT/2-k<0\). The best trading value after a success is therefore
\(\Psi=(\rho\DT/2-k)^+\). A joint deviation to \(\tilde\lambda\in[0,\lb]\), with optimal orders at those
prices and entry, gives
\[
 \tilde\lambda\Psi-\frac a2\tilde\lambda^2.
\]
If \(\rho\DT\le2k\), then \(\Psi=0\), and the unique optimal research choice is zero. If \(\rho\DT>2k\),
then \(\Psi>0\), and the unique optimum \(\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}\) is positive, so zero research fails research
optimality. This proves necessity and sufficiency for research, including all joint order deviations.

When \(\rho\DT<2k\), both correct orders are strictly worse than abstention, which gives the unique pair of
hypothetical-success distributions. At equality, the high class is indifferent exactly between buying and
abstaining, and the low class exactly between selling and abstaining. Any mixtures over these best orders obey
order optimality when an indifferent informed trader may mix. They leave \(f_j=\eta\), beliefs, prices and entry
unchanged because their occurrence probability is zero. Truthful bids complete each equilibrium. Preparation
is independent of values conditional on class, so high-class ownership is \(\rho W_H(F)/2\). The abstention
rule selects the point masses on zero from the same best-order sets.


When class \(H\) buys and class \(L\) sells after a success and no order recruits, entry is \(\rho\) after every
order, and the purchase and the sale both earn \(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k\) at the conjectured research \(\lambda\). For
\(\rho\DT\ge2k\), the root \(\lambda_Q(\DT)\) of \eqref{eq:a5-floor} exists and is unique, because
the gap \(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k-a\lambda\) falls strictly in \(\lambda\), from
\(\rho\DT/2-k\ge0\) at \(\lambda=0\) to \(-k-a\) at \(\lambda=1\). The root is zero at
\(\rho\DT=2k\) and positive when \(\rho\DT>2k\). The gap rises
strictly in \(\DT\), and its derivative in \(\lambda\) is negative, so \(\lambda_Q\) rises strictly and continuously
in \(\DT\).

Under an indifferent informed trader who may mix and an indifferent high-cost rival who prepares, take an
equilibrium with positive research. Class \(H\) buys strictly and fully, so \(\mu(1)=M_\lambda\) and
\(e(0)=e(-1)=\rho\). If \(M_\lambda\ge\tau\), the purchase recruits fully and the sale rule and research
condition give recruiting research, with \(\lambda=\min\{\lambda_R(\DT),\lb\}\).
If \(M_\lambda<\tau\), the strict purchase payoff is \(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k>0\). Any low-class sale payoff is
at least this, because \(\mu(-1)\ge m_\lambda\). Thus class \(L\) sells strictly and fully, both best trading
payoffs equal \(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k\), and research is
\(\min\{\lambda_Q(\DT),\lb\}\), with \(\lambda_Q\) from \eqref{eq:a5-floor}.
This root is positive exactly when \(\rho\DT>2k\). At equality, every positive \(\lambda\) has
\(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k<0\), so floor-entry research has no positive-intensity endpoint there.

For \(\rho\DT>2k\), the recruiting residual at \(\lambda_Q\) equals
\[
 \DT m_{\lambda_Q}-k+(\rho\DT m_{\lambda_Q}-k)^+-2a\lambda_Q
 =(1-\rho)\DT m_{\lambda_Q}>0.
\]
Its strict decrease yields \(\lambda_Q<\lambda_R\), hence
\(\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}\le\min\{\lambda_R,\lb\}\), with strict inequality whenever \(\lambda_Q<\lb\).
The two positive-research outcomes coexist exactly when
\[
 \rho\DT>2k,\qquad
 M_{\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}}<\tau\le M_{\min\{\lambda_R,\lb\}}.
\]
If both roots reach the cap, this interval is empty. Otherwise it has positive width in the threshold
coordinate. Together with the zero-research case, this exhausts the outcomes. Each positive-research outcome
fixes its research, orders, posteriors, competitive prices, preparation probabilities and truthful bids;
at the zero-research boundary, the best-order mixtures above give all the hypothetical-success strategies.

For the floor-entry construction, its research choice makes
\(\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k\ge a\hat\lambda>0\), with equality below the cap.
Both correct orders are therefore strictly best. Full orders give the three stated posteriors, and constant
entry \(\rho\) makes the three prices distinct. The strict threshold inequality gives preparation;
competitive prices, their posterior beliefs and truthful bids give the remaining conditions. The research
choice follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint}.
If \(\rho\DT\le2k\), zero research exists. Otherwise the ordering of the capped roots implies that either
\(\tau\le M_{\min\{\lambda_R,\lb\}}\) and recruitment exists, or
\(\tau>M_{\min\{\lambda_R,\lb\}}\ge M_{\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}}\) and floor-entry research exists.
This also covers \(\tau\ge1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Strong incumbents]\label{lem:a5-strong}
Maintain the setting above. If \(M_{\lb}\ne\tau\) and
{\renewcommand{\theHequation}{A.U}%
\begin{equation}\tag{U}\label{eq:a5-U}
 \rho\DT m_{\lb}-k>a\lb,
\end{equation}}%
then under all four tie rules the equilibrium is unique. Research is \(\lb\), class \(H\) buys and class \(L\)
sells strictly after a success, and the posteriors are \((m_{\lb},1/2,M_{\lb})\). Entry after a sale and no order
is \(\rho\), while \(\zeta(P(1))=\1\{M_{\lb}>\tau\}\). Competitive prices, their posterior beliefs and
truthful bids complete the tuple. With \(\bar b=(1-\lb)\beta\), total entry is
\(\rho+(1-\rho)(\bar b+\lb/2)\) if \(M_{\lb}>\tau\) and \(\rho\) otherwise.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Uniqueness.} Take any candidate. By Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-bounds}(a), every nonzero-order posterior lies in
\([m_{\lb},M_{\lb}]\) whatever the orders, and \(e\ge\rho\) after every price. Hence
\(U_H(1)\ge\rho\DT(1-M_{\lb})-k\) and \(U_L(-1)\ge\rho\DT m_{\lb}-k\). Both lower bounds equal
\(\rho\DT m_{\lb}-k>a\lb\ge0\) by \eqref{eq:a5-U}. So the purchase and the sale are the strict best orders after a
success, under both tie rules for the informed trader. Then \(\Psi=\tfrac12[U_H(1)+U_L(-1)]>a\lb\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint} gives
\(\hat\lambda=\lb\). Write \(\bar b_0=(1-\lb)(1-2\beta)\). Full orders give
\(f_H=(\bar b,\bar b_0,\bar b+\lb)\) and \(f_L=(\bar b+\lb,\bar b_0,\bar b)\), so the
posteriors are \((m_{\lb},1/2,M_{\lb})\). Since \(\tau>1/2\), \(\zeta(P(0))=\zeta(P(-1))=0\). Since
\(M_{\lb}\ne\tau\), \(\zeta(P(1))\) is \(1\) or \(0\) with no tie, under both tie rules for the rival. Every component of
the tuple is fixed.

\emph{Existence.} (E1): the likelihoods above. (E2): the stated entry is nondecreasing in the posterior, so the
three prices differ, and each price reveals its order. (E3): \(B(M_{\lb})\) lies strictly on one side of \(c_H\),
and \(B(1/2),B(m_{\lb})<c_H\). (E4): \eqref{eq:a2-terminal}. (E5): the strict bounds above, read at the tuple.
(E6): truthful bids. (E7): \(\Psi>a\lb\). The purchase has probability \(\tfrac12(\bar b+\lb)+\tfrac12\bar b=\bar b+\lb/2\),
and it recruits exactly when \(M_{\lb}>\tau\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Access to prices with research.}\fixedlabel{par:a5-access}{A.5}
Maintain the general setting, condition~\eqref{eq:a5-U}, and \(\tau<M_{\lb}\). Under all four tie rules, both the
economy with price access and the economy in which the rival does not see the price have full orders and
research at the cap, uniquely. With access only a purchase recruits; with the price hidden entry is \(\rho\)
after every order. Hence access raises expected proceeds by
\[
 (1-\rho)(\bar b+\lb/2)[t_L-t_0+\DT M_{\lb}]>0
\]
and surplus, net of research, trading and preparation costs, by
\[
 (1-\rho)(\bar b+\lb/2)[\gamma(M_{\lb}-\tau)+pF(p)]>0.
\]

\begin{proof}
With the price hidden, the rival retains its prior \(1/2<\tau\), so entry is \(\rho\) after every order.
The nonzero-order bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-strong} apply with this constant entry, and condition~\eqref{eq:a5-U}
forces full orders and research at the cap, uniquely. With access the same lemma gives full orders, cap
research and recruitment only after a purchase. Thus the order law, research cost \((a/2)\lb^2\) and trading
cost \(k\lb\) coincide. A purchase has probability \(\bar b+\lb/2\), with conditional high-class probability
\(M_{\lb}\). Multiplying its additional preparation probability by its conditional proceeds gain gives
the first display.
For any value \(\theta\ge p\) of the rival,
\[
 \max\{\theta,R\}-R\1\{R\ge p\}
 =(\theta-\max\{p,R\})_++p\1\{R<p\}.
\]
An additional prepared rival therefore adds \(B(M_{\lb})+pF(p)\) to expected allocation value at a
purchase. Subtracting \(c_H\) gives \(\gamma(M_{\lb}-\tau)+pF(p)\), and multiplying by the additional
preparation probability gives the surplus formula. Both gains are positive since
\(M_{\lb}>\tau\), \(\gamma>0\), \(\DT>0\), and \(t_L\ge t_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Tie rules with research]\label{rem:researchties}
Maintain the general setting. When an indifferent informed trader may mix and an indifferent high-cost rival may
prepare with any probability, the outcomes above are supplemented by the following threshold choices.
For \(1/2<\tau<1\) and \(0<\lambda_E(\tau)<\lb\), the threshold pair has research \(\lambda_E\), full high-class
purchasing and the low-class sale rule of Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-sale}. Its purchase entry is
\[
 e^*=\frac{2a\lambda_E+k-(\rho J-k)^+}{J},
\]
and it exists exactly when \(\rho\le e^*\le1\). Entry at a sale and no order is \(\rho\), and the high-cost
preparation probability at a purchase is \((e^*-\rho)/(1-\rho)\). At \(e^*=1\) it coincides with recruiting
research; at \(e^*=\rho\) it is floor-entry research with a purchase posterior equal to \(\tau\).

At \(\lambda_E(\tau)=\lb\), the complete threshold family has purchase entry
\[
 e(1)\in\left[\max\left\{\rho,\frac{2a\lb+k-(\rho J-k)^+}{J}\right\},1\right],
\]
provided the lower endpoint is at most one. Each choice has its own competitive purchase price, research at the
cap, and total entry \(\rho+(e(1)-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lb]\).

When an indifferent informed trader abstains, a positive-research candidate at intensity \(\lambda\), including a
threshold pair, survives exactly when
\[
 \rho\DT\le2k\quad\hbox{or}\quad\rho\DT m_\lambda>k.
\]
At zero research the abstention rule selects abstention after every hypothetical success. Research itself is a
unique best response at fixed prices and preparation, because its objective is strictly concave.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
Positive research has the strict full purchase of Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-buy}. Partial high-cost preparation can
therefore occur only at its posterior \(M_\lambda=\tau\), so \(\lambda=\lambda_E(\tau)\).
The sale lemma still applies, because the other posteriors are below the threshold. At an interior research
choice, Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint} gives
\[
 2a\lambda_E=e(1)J-k+(\rho J-k)^+.
\]
Solving for \(e(1)\) gives \(e^*\). Feasibility of the preparation probability is exactly
\(\rho\le e^*\le1\). If the sale term is zero, the purchase earns \(2a\lambda_E>0\); if it is positive,
the purchase earns at least \(\rho J-k>0\). The sale rule, competitive prices, price beliefs and truthful
bids then complete the tuple as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:research}(a). At the two endpoints,
the research equality becomes the recruiting equation or the floor equation, respectively.

At \(\lambda_E=\lb\), research optimality requires the left side of the same display to be at most its
right side. Solving this inequality and intersecting with \([\rho,1]\) gives the entire stated interval.
Each feasible purchase entry has its own competitive purchase price; only a purchase changes entry above
\(\rho\), yielding the total-entry formula.

An indifferent informed trader who abstains permits the zero sale rate when \(\rho\DT\le2k\) and a full sale
when \(\rho\DT m_\lambda>k\). If \(\rho\DT/2>k\ge\rho\DT m_\lambda\), zero selling would make the sale
strictly profitable, while the consistent positive rate has zero sale profit and violates the abstention
rule. These cases prove the survival condition. At zero research the same rule selects abstention from
the best-order sets in Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-outcomes}(a), and strict concavity gives the unique research response.
\end{proof}

\begin{named}{Corollary 3 (Research equilibria in the example, full statement)}\fixedlabel{cor:research}{3}
Take the example on \([r_0,r_2]\), when an indifferent informed trader may mix over its best orders and an indifferent
high-cost rival prepares.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item The equilibrium outcomes are exactly as follows:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item on \([r_0,r_R)\), zero research alone;
\item on \([r_R,r_N]\), zero research and recruiting research;
\item on \((r_N,r_Q)\), recruiting research at the cap and floor-entry research;
\item on \([r_Q,r_C]\), recruiting research at the cap alone;
\item on \((r_C,r_2]\), floor-entry research at the cap alone.
\end{itemize}
Recruiting research is below its cap on \([r_R,r_{\lb})\) and at the cap on \([r_{\lb},r_C]\). Its research
rises from \(\lambda_E(r_R)\approx\reg{res_lambda_rR}\) to \(\lb\), and its entry rises from
\(\reg{res_entry_rR}\) to \(\reg{res_entry_cap}\), then stays constant through \(r_C\).
Class \(L\) abstains through \(r_N\), mixes on \((r_N,r_S)\), and sells with probability one on \([r_S,r_C]\).
Floor-entry research has full orders and entry \(\rho\) throughout its existence set. At \(r_N\), zero research
has all the hypothetical-success order mixtures in Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-outcomes}(a).
\item At \(r_0\), the unique equilibrium has zero research and entry \(\rho\).
At each \(r\in\{\reg{str_r155},\reg{str_r160},\reg{str_r165}\}\), exactly two equilibria coexist: zero research
and recruiting research with \(\lambda_E(r)<\lambda_R(r)<\lb\). In the recruiting equilibrium class \(H\) buys
and class \(L\) abstains after a success. At \(r_1\) and \(r_2\), the unique equilibrium has full orders and
research at the cap, with entry \(\reg{res_entry_r1}\) at \(r_1\) and \(\rho\) at \(r_2\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{named}

\begin{proof}
Take the example with \(a=\reg{res_a}\), \(\lb\approx\reg{res_cap}\) and \(r\in[r_0,r_2]\). On this interval,
\[
 \DT(r)=\frac{(r-\ell)^2}{2r},\qquad
 g_L(r)=\frac{\ell^2-p^2}{2r},\qquad
 g_H(r)=h-\frac r2-\frac{p^2}{2r}.
\]
Here \(\DT'>0\), \(g_L'<0\), and
\(\tau'=(-g_L'+\tau\DT')/(g_H-g_L)>0\).
Substituting \(\lb=u\beta/(1+u\beta)\) in \(m_\lambda\) gives
\(m_{\lb}=1/(2+u)=1/(1+\eu)\) and \(M_{\lb}=\eu/(1+\eu)\).
The margins
\(M_{\lb}-\tau(r_1)\approx\reg{margin_res_tau_r1_B}\) and
\(\tau(r_2)-M_{\lb}\approx\reg{margin_res_tau_r2_B}\) give a unique root
\(r_C\in(r_1,r_2)\) of \(\tau(r_C)=M_{\lb}=\eu/(1+\eu)\).
The ratio \((1-\rho)k/(2a\rho)\) exceeds one, and therefore exceeds the cap, so
the cap spread is \((2a\lb+k)(1+\eu)\). Thus the left endpoint is
\(r_{\lb}=\fr((2a\lb+k)(1+\eu))\), where
\(\fr(y)=\ell+y+\sqrt{y^2+2\ell y}\).
The positive margin \(r_N-r_{\lb}\approx\reg{margin_res_rN_B}\), together with \(r_N<r_1<r_C\), places it before the
right endpoint. Recruiting research at the cap is therefore an equilibrium exactly on
\([r_{\lb},r_C]\), with \(r_{\lb}\approx\reg{res_rcap}\) and \(r_C\approx\reg{res_rC}\).

\emph{Recruiting research below the cap.}

On \([r_0,r_N]\), a sale at the prior earns at most its trading cost, and \(\tau>1/2\), so \(\rho J<k\) and
\(\varphi_a^*=\varphi_a=J-k-2a\lambda_E\). In the example \(r_{\lb}<r_N<\reg{res_bound_strength}\).
For \(r\in[r_0,\reg{res_bound_strength}]\), differentiating the auction formulas gives
\[
 \DT'(r)\ge\reg{res_bound_dDelta},\quad
 1-\tau(r)\ge\reg{res_bound_tau74},\quad
 \DT(r)\le\reg{res_bound_Delta74},\quad
 \tau'(r)\le\reg{res_bound_taup}.
\]
Here \(\DT'\), \(\DT\) and \(\tau\) rise, while \(-g_L'\) and \(\gamma=g_H-g_L\) fall. The formula
\(\tau'=(-g_L'+\tau\DT')/\gamma\) gives the upper bound
\[
 \tau'(r)\le
 \frac{-g_L'(r_0)+\tau(\reg{res_bound_strength})\DT'(\reg{res_bound_strength})}
 {\gamma(\reg{res_bound_strength})}
 <\reg{res_bound_taup}.
\]
Also \(\lambda_E'(\tau)\) rises with \(\tau\). Substitution in
\(\varphi_a'=\DT'(1-\tau)-[\DT+2a\lambda_E'(\tau)]\tau'\) therefore gives
\[
 J'(r)\ge \reg{res_bound_dDelta}\cdot\reg{res_bound_tau74}
 -\reg{res_bound_Delta74}\cdot\reg{res_bound_taup}>0,\qquad
 \varphi_a'(r)\ge\reg{res_phip_lb}>0
 \quad\text{at }a=\reg{res_a}.
\]
The same bound is positive for \(a<a_{\rm mono}\approx\reg{res_amono}\).
The endpoint values are
\[
 \varphi_a^*(r_0)=\reg{res_phi_r0_frac}<0,
 \qquad \varphi_a^*(r_{\lb})\approx\reg{res_phi_rcap}>0.
\]
Hence recruiting research begins at the unique zero \(r_R\approx\reg{res_rR}\), reaches its cap at
\(r_{\lb}\approx\reg{res_rcap}\), and continues to \(r_C\approx\reg{res_rC}\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(b), \(J\) rises on the whole path. Since \(\lambda_E\) also rises, for
\(r\in[\reg{res_bound_strength},r_2]\),
\begin{gather*}
 \varphi_a(r)\ge J(\reg{res_bound_strength})-k-2a\lambda_E(r_2)>0,\\
 J(\reg{res_bound_strength})=\reg{res_J74_frac}.
\end{gather*}
Thus \(r_R\) is the unique zero of \(\varphi_a\) on \([r_0,r_2]\).

\emph{Part (a).} Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-outcomes}(a) gives zero research exactly through \(r_N\).
The preceding margin and cap arguments give recruiting research on \([r_R,r_C]\), below the cap until
\(r_{\lb}\). Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-floor} gives the two floor-entry intervals and uniqueness above \(r_Q\).
Together with the exhaustion in Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-outcomes}, these give the five intervals in the statement.
The research root equals \(\lambda_E(r_R)\) at the start, and the entry formula of
Proposition~\ref{prop:research}(b) gives its starting and cap values. At the cap the sale lemma gives
abstention through \(r_N\), mixing up to \(r_S\), and full selling from \(r_S\) onward.

\emph{Part (b), the weak incumbent.} At \(r_0\), the gross return of any nonzero order is at most
\(\DT(r_0)=\reg{res_Delta_r0}<k\), with margin \(\reg{margin_res_weak_B}\).
Every such order loses, so \(\Psi=0\) and research is zero. At the prior,
\(c_H-B_{r_0}(1/2)\approx\reg{margin_res_half_B}>0\), giving entry \(\rho\).

\emph{Part (b), the intermediate strengths.} Clearing the research equation gives \eqref{eq:a5-F}.
Its positive root has the closed form
\[
 \lambda_R(r)=
 \frac{2\beta[\DT(r)-2k]}
 {\Lambda_r'(0)+\sqrt{[\Lambda_r'(0)]^2+
 8a\beta(1-2\beta)[\DT(r)-2k]}}.
\]
At \(r\in\{\reg{str_r155},\reg{str_r160},\reg{str_r165}\}\), exact rational arithmetic and bounds on
the square root give \(1/2<\tau(r)<1\), with
\(k-\rho\DT(r)/2\) equal to \(\reg{res_ULgap_r155}\), \(\reg{res_ULgap_r160}\) and
\(\reg{res_ULgap_r165}\), respectively, to the displayed precision.
The differences \(\lambda_R(r)-\lambda_E(r)\) are approximately
\(\reg{res_lamgap_r155}\), \(\reg{res_lamgap_r160}\) and \(\reg{res_lamgap_r165}\);
the differences \(\lb-\lambda_R(r)\) are approximately
\(\reg{res_capgap_r155}\), \(\reg{res_capgap_r160}\) and \(\reg{res_capgap_r165}\).
All are strictly positive. Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-outcomes} therefore gives exactly zero research and recruiting
research below the cap; the sale lemma gives strict low-class abstention. The entry formula gives the
values in Table~\ref{tab:research}.

\emph{Part (b), the strong and the very strong incumbent.} The cap posterior lies strictly between
\(\tau(r_1)\) and \(\tau(r_2)\), with margins \(\reg{margin_res_tau_r1_B}\) and
\(\reg{margin_res_tau_r2_B}\). Condition~\eqref{eq:a5-U} holds at both strengths, with margins
\(\reg{margin_res_bound_r1_B}\) and \(\reg{margin_res_bound_r2_B}\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-strong} gives uniqueness, full orders and research at the cap, with entry
\(\reg{res_entry_r1}\) at \(r_1\) and \(\rho\) at \(r_2\).
Class \(H\) outbids the incumbent throughout the path because \(r\le r_2<h\), so its ownership is
\(\tfrac12[\rho+(1-\rho)(\beta+(1-\beta)\hat\lambda)]\).
At \(r_1\), the price-access formulas give the proceeds gain \(\reg{res_dR_r1}\) and the surplus gain
\(\reg{res_dW_r1}\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Floor-entry research.}
For \(\rho\DT(r)>2k\), with \(\lambda_Q\) the root of \eqref{eq:a5-floor}, \emph{floor-entry research} is the tuple
with \(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lambda_Q(\DT(r)),\lb\}\),
\(\sigma_H=\delta_1\), \(\sigma_L=\delta_{-1}\), \((\mu(-1),\mu(0),\mu(1))=(m_{\hat\lambda},1/2,M_{\hat\lambda})\),
\(\zeta\equiv0\), prices \(P(q)=t_0+\rho\,w(\mu(q))\), \(\bar\mu(P(q))=\mu(q)\) and truthful bids. Its entry is
\(\rho\).

\emph{Recruiting research at the cap} is the tuple with \(\hat\lambda=\lb\), \(\sigma_H=\delta_1\),
\(\sigma_L(-1)=\hat s_-(r)/\lb\) and \(\sigma_L(0)=1-\hat s_-(r)/\lb\), where \(\hat s_-(r)\) is the sale rate
of Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-sale} at the presence \(\lb\):
\[
\hat s_-(r)=\min\bigl\{\lb,\ \bar b\,[\rho\DT(r)/k-2]^+\bigr\},\qquad \bar b=(1-\lb)\beta,\qquad
\bar b_0=(1-\lb)(1-2\beta).
\]
Its posteriors are \(\mu(-1)=\bar b/\bigl(2\bar b+\hat s_-(r)\bigr)\),
\(\mu(0)=\bar b_0/\bigl(2\bar b_0+\lb-\hat s_-(r)\bigr)\) and \(\mu(1)=M_{\lb}\). It has \(\zeta(P(1))=1\) and
\(\zeta(P(0))=\zeta(P(-1))=0\), prices \(P(q)=t_0+e(q)\,w(\mu(q))\), \(\bar\mu(P(q))=\mu(q)\) and truthful bids.
Class \(L\) abstains when \(\rho\DT\le2k\) and sells with probability one when \(\rho\DT m_{\lb}\ge k\); in between it
mixes, and its sale just pays the trading cost.

The \emph{floor margin} is
\[
\varphi_\rho(r)=\rho\DT(r)\,[1-\tau(r)]-k-a\lambda_E(r).
\]
Since \(m_{\lambda_E(r)}=1-\tau(r)\), it is the gap \(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k-a\lambda\) of \eqref{eq:a5-floor} at
\(\lambda=\lambda_E(r)\): the return to a full order at the entry floor, at the research that lets a purchase just
reach the threshold, net of the trading cost and of the marginal cost of that research.

\begin{lemma}[Floor-entry research along the path]\label{lem:a5-floor}
Take the example on \([r_0,r_2]\), when an indifferent informed trader may mix over its best orders and an indifferent
high-cost rival prepares.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item The floor margin \(\varphi_\rho\) rises strictly on the path. Its unique zero is \(r_Q\), with
\(r_N<r_Q<r_U<r_C\). On \((r_N,r_C]\), the threshold exceeds the top posterior at research
\(\min\{\lambda_Q(\DT(r)),\lb\}\) exactly on \((r_N,r_Q)\), and equals it exactly at \(r_Q\) and \(r_C\).
\item Floor-entry research is an equilibrium exactly on \((r_N,r_Q)\) and \((r_C,r_2]\), with entry \(\rho\).
The floor root reaches the cap at \(r_U\). On \([r_N,r_Q]\), it rises strictly from zero to
\(\lambda_E(r_Q)\approx\reg{res_lambda_floor_rQ}<\lb\); on \((r_C,r_2]\) research is at the cap.
\item Recruiting research at the cap is an equilibrium exactly on \([r_{\lb},r_C]\), with entry
\(\reg{res_entry_cap}\).
\item On \((r_N,r_Q)\), exactly these two equilibria coexist. On \([r_Q,r_C]\), recruiting research at the cap
is unique; on \((r_C,r_2]\), floor-entry research at the cap is unique.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Throughout, \(r\in[r_0,r_2]\), \(\lambda_Q\) stands for \(\lambda_Q(\DT(r))\), and \emph{the gap} is
\(\rho\DT(r)\,m_\lambda-k-a\lambda\), the gap of \eqref{eq:a5-floor} at \(\DT=\DT(r)\), as a function of \(\lambda\).
Recall \(r_N=\fr(2k/\rho)\), \(r_{\lb}=\fr\bigl((2a\lb+k)(1+\eu)\bigr)\),
\(r_U=\fr\bigl((k+a\lb)(1+\eu)/\rho\bigr)\), and the entry ceiling \(r_C\), at which \(\tau(r_C)=M_{\lb}\).

\emph{Step 0: facts along the path.} The spread \(\DT\) and the threshold \(\tau\) rise strictly. Since
\(B_r(1/2)=h/2-r/4+(\ell^2-2p^2)/(4r)<c_H\), \(\tau(r)>1/2\), and \(\tau(r)\le\tau(r_2)=\reg{tau_r2_frac}<1\); so \(\lambda_E(r)\in(0,1)\)
is defined and rises strictly. Since \(M_\lambda\) rises strictly in \(\lambda\) and \(M_{\lambda_E(r)}=\tau(r)\),
\(\lambda_E(r)\le\lb\) exactly when \(r\le r_C\), with equality only at \(r_C\). Condition \eqref{eq:a5-U} holds at
\(r_1\), with margin \(\reg{margin_res_bound_r1_B}\), and \(M_{\lb}-\tau(r_1)\approx\reg{margin_res_tau_r1_B}\) and
\(\tau(r_2)-M_{\lb}\approx\reg{margin_res_tau_r2_B}\), so \(r_U<r_1<r_C<r_2\). Also \(r_N-r_{\lb}\approx\reg{margin_res_rN_B}>0\).
Since \(m_{\lb}=1/(1+\eu)\), the gap at \(\lb\), \(\rho\DT(r)\,m_{\lb}-k-a\lb\), is nonnegative exactly when
\(r\ge r_U\), with equality only at \(r_U\). For \(r\ge r_N\), the gap falls strictly in \(\lambda\) and vanishes at
\(\lambda_Q\), so \(\lambda_Q\ge\lb\) exactly when \(r\ge r_U\).

\emph{Step 1: the comparison.} Let \(r\in(r_N,r_C]\). Then \(\lambda_E(r)\le\lb\), and \(m_{\lambda_E(r)}=1-\tau(r)\)
makes the gap at \(\lambda_E(r)\) equal to \(\varphi_\rho(r)\). Since \(M_\lambda\) rises strictly, \(\tau(r)\)
exceeds \(M_\lambda\) at \(\lambda=\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}\) exactly when \(\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}<\lambda_E(r)\). Because
\(\lambda_E(r)\le\lb\), this is \(\lambda_Q<\lambda_E(r)\), and because the gap falls strictly in \(\lambda\) and
vanishes at \(\lambda_Q\), it is \(\varphi_\rho(r)<0\). In the same way, \(\tau(r)\) equals \(M_\lambda\) at
\(\lambda=\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}\) exactly when \(\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}=\lambda_E(r)\), which holds exactly when
\(\lambda_Q=\lambda_E(r)\), that is \(\varphi_\rho(r)=0\), or when \(\lb=\lambda_E(r)\le\lambda_Q\), that is \(r=r_C\),
by Step 0 and \(r_C>r_U\).

\emph{Step 2: the floor margin rises.} By the formula for \(d\lambda_E/d\tau\),
\[
\varphi_\rho'(r)=\rho\bigl[\DT'(r)\,(1-\tau(r))-\DT(r)\,\tau'(r)\bigr]
-\frac{a\beta\,\tau'(r)}{\{\beta+(1-2\beta)[1-\tau(r)]\}^2},
\]
with \(\DT'(r)=\tfrac12(1-\ell^2/r^2)\) and \(\tau'(r)=2[c_Hr^2-(\ell^2-p^2)r+h(\ell^2-p^2)-c_H\ell^2]/(2hr-r^2-\ell^2)^2\). On \([r_0,r_2]\) the factors
\(\DT'\), \(\DT\), \(1-\tau\) and \(\tau'\) are positive. On a piece \([r',r'']\subset[r_0,r_2]\), \(\DT'\), \(\DT\)
and \(\tau\) rise, \(c_Hr^2-(\ell^2-p^2)r+h(\ell^2-p^2)-c_H\ell^2\) rises, and \(\tau'\le T(r',r'')\), since \(2hr-r^2-\ell^2\) is positive and rises; the
denominator \(\{\beta+(1-2\beta)[1-\tau]\}^2\) of the last term falls as \(\tau\) rises, so on the piece it is at
least its value at \(r''\). Hence, for \(r\in[r',r'']\),
\begin{gather*}
\varphi_\rho'(r)\ge\rho\bigl[\DT'(r')\,(1-\tau(r''))-\DT(r'')\,T(r',r'')\bigr]
-\frac{a\beta\,T(r',r'')}{\{\beta+(1-2\beta)[1-\tau(r'')]\}^2},\\
T(r',r'')=\frac{2[c_H(r'')^2-(\ell^2-p^2)r''+h(\ell^2-p^2)-c_H\ell^2]}{(2hr'-(r')^2-\ell^2)^2}.
\end{gather*}
Partition \([r_0,r_2]\) into pieces of width \(\reg{res_floor_step}\). An exact rational computation shows that the
right side of the first line is at least \(\reg{res_floor_dphi_lb}\) on each piece, so \(\varphi_\rho\) rises
strictly on \([r_0,r_2]\).

\emph{Step 3: the zero.} At \(r_N\), \(\rho\DT(r_N)=2k\), so \(\varphi_\rho(r_N)=k[1-2\tau(r_N)]-a\lambda_E(r_N)<0\).
Since \(m_{\lb}=1-M_{\lb}\),
\[
\varphi_\rho(r)=\bigl[\rho\DT(r)\,m_{\lb}-k-a\lb\bigr]+\rho\DT(r)\bigl[M_{\lb}-\tau(r)\bigr]+a\bigl[\lb-\lambda_E(r)\bigr].
\]
On \([r_U,r_C]\), the first bracket is nonnegative, with equality only at \(r_U\), and the other two terms are
nonnegative and vanish only at \(r_C\). Since \(r_U<r_C\), \(\varphi_\rho>0\) on \([r_U,r_C]\). By Step 2,
\(\varphi_\rho\) has exactly one zero \(r_Q\) on \([r_0,r_2]\), with \(r_N<r_Q<r_U\); it is negative on \([r_0,r_Q)\)
and positive on \((r_Q,r_2]\). The floor margin is a rational function of \(r\) with rational coefficients, so its
sign at a rational strength follows from exact rational arithmetic; these signs give \(r_Q\approx\reg{res_rQ}\) and
\(\lambda_E(r_Q)\approx\reg{res_lambda_floor_rQ}\). With Step 1, this proves part (a).

\emph{Step 4: floor-entry research.} Let \(r\in(r_N,r_2]\), so \(\rho\DT(r)>2k\), and take floor-entry research,
with \(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}>0\). (E1): \(f_H=(\hat b,\hat b_0,\hat b+\hat\lambda)\) and
\(f_L=(\hat b+\hat\lambda,\hat b_0,\hat b)\) on \((-1,0,1)\) give the stated posteriors, and
\(m_{\hat\lambda}<1/2<M_{\hat\lambda}\). (E2): the stated entry is \(\rho\) after every order, so by the paragraph
on constructed tuples in Appendix~\ref{app:beliefs} the three prices differ, and each price reveals its order.
Suppose \(\tau(r)>M_{\hat\lambda}\). (E3): every posterior is at most \(M_{\hat\lambda}<\tau(r)\), so \(B_r<c_H\) at
every price, and \(\zeta\equiv0\) with no tie. (E4): \eqref{eq:a2-terminal}. (E5): by \eqref{eq:a5-payoffs},
\(U_H(1)=\rho\DT(1-M_{\hat\lambda})-k\) and \(U_L(-1)=\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k\). Both equal
\(\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k\), which is at least \(a\hat\lambda>0\), because \(\hat\lambda\le\lambda_Q\) and the gap is
nonnegative on \([0,\lambda_Q]\). With \(U_j(0)=0\) and Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-bounds}(c), the purchase and the sale are
the strict best orders. (E6): truthful bids. (E7): \(\Psi=\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k\). If \(\lambda_Q<\lb\), then
\(\hat\lambda=\lambda_Q\) and \(\Psi/a=\lambda_Q<\lb\); if \(\lambda_Q\ge\lb\), then \(\hat\lambda=\lb\) and
\(\Psi-a\lb=\rho\DT m_{\lb}-k-a\lb\ge0\). In both cases \(\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}=\hat\lambda\). Conversely, if
\(\tau(r)\le M_{\hat\lambda}\), then \(B_r(\mu(1))\ge c_H\), and (E3) with an indifferent rival that prepares
requires \(\zeta(P(1))=1\), so the tuple is not an equilibrium.

Floor-entry research is therefore an equilibrium exactly when \(\tau(r)>M_{\hat\lambda}\) at
\(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}\). By part (a), on \((r_N,r_C]\) this holds exactly on \((r_N,r_Q)\). On
\((r_C,r_2]\), \(r>r_U\) gives \(\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}=\lb\), and \(\tau(r)>M_{\lb}\), so it holds there, with research
at the cap. On \([r_N,r_Q]\), \(r<r_U\), so \(\lambda_Q<\lb\); it rises strictly and continuously with \(r\), from \(0\)
at \(r_N\), where the gap at \(\lambda=0\) is \(\rho\DT(r_N)/2-k=0\), to \(\lambda_E(r_Q)\) at \(r_Q\), where the gap
at \(\lambda_E(r_Q)\) is \(\varphi_\rho(r_Q)=0\). This proves part (b).

\emph{Step 5: recruiting research at the cap.} Let \(r\in[r_{\lb},r_C]\), and write \(\hat s_-=\hat s_-(r)\). (E1):
\(f_H=(\bar b,\bar b_0,\bar b+\lb)\) and \(f_L=(\bar b+\hat s_-,\bar b_0+\lb-\hat s_-,\bar b)\) give the stated
posteriors, with \(\mu(-1),\mu(0)\le1/2<\tau(r)\le M_{\lb}=\mu(1)\). (E2): the stated entry, \(\rho\) after a sale and
after no order and \(1\) after a purchase, is nondecreasing in the posterior; by the paragraph on constructed tuples,
orders with equal posteriors have equal entry \(\rho\) and share a price, orders with distinct posteriors have
distinct prices, and the belief at each price is the posterior of its orders. (E3): \(B_r(M_{\lb})\ge c_H\), with
equality only at \(r_C\), where the indifferent rival prepares, and \(B_r<c_H\) at the posteriors at most
\(1/2\). (E4): \eqref{eq:a2-terminal}. (E5): the purchase earns \(\DT m_{\lb}-k\ge\DT(r_{\lb})\,m_{\lb}-k=2a\lb>0\),
so by Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-bounds}(c) it is the strict best order of class \(H\). Class \(L\) earns \(U_L(1)\le-k\),
\(U_L(0)=0\) and \(U_L(-1)=\rho\DT\mu(-1)-k\). If \(\hat s_-=0\), then \(\rho\DT\le2k\) and
\(U_L(-1)=\rho\DT/2-k\le0\). If \(0<\hat s_-<\lb\), then \(2\bar b+\hat s_-=\bar b\rho\DT/k\), and \(U_L(-1)=0\). If
\(\hat s_-=\lb\), then \(\rho\DT m_{\lb}\ge k\), since \(m_{\lb}=\bar b/(2\bar b+\lb)\), and
\(U_L(-1)=\rho\DT m_{\lb}-k\ge0\). In each case \(\sigma_L\) puts weight only on best orders, and an indifferent
informed trader may mix. (E6): truthful bids. (E7): \(\Psi\ge\tfrac12(\DT m_{\lb}-k)\ge a\lb\), so
\(\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}=\lb\). A purchase has probability \(\tfrac12(\bar b+\lb)+\tfrac12\bar b=\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lb\), and
only the purchase raises entry above \(\rho\), so entry is \(\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lb]\approx\reg{res_entry_cap}\).

For \(r>r_C\), \(\mu(1)=M_{\lb}<\tau(r)\), so (E3) requires \(\zeta(P(1))=0\), and the tuple is not an equilibrium. For
\(r<r_{\lb}\), \(r<r_N\) gives \(\hat s_-=0\) and \(U_L(-1)<0\), so \(\Psi=\tfrac12(\DT m_{\lb}-k)^+<a\lb\), because
\(\DT(r)<\DT(r_{\lb})\); (E7) fails. This proves part (c).

\emph{Step 6: every equilibrium above \(r_N\).} Let \(r\in(r_N,r_C]\), and take any equilibrium. If
\(\hat\lambda=0\), every posterior is \(1/2\), entry is \(\rho\) after every order, and \(U_H(1)=\rho\DT/2-k>0\); then
\(\Psi>0\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint} gives \(\hat\lambda>0\), a contradiction. So \(\hat\lambda>0\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-buy} gives \(\sigma_H=\delta_1\), \(\mu(1)=M_{\hat\lambda}\) and \(e(0)=e(-1)=\rho\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(d) gives \(e(1)=1\) if \(M_{\hat\lambda}\ge\tau(r)\) and \(e(1)=\rho\) otherwise.

If \(M_{\hat\lambda}<\tau(r)\), then \(U_H(1)=\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k\), which is positive by
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-buy}. Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-bounds}(a) gives \(\mu(-1)\ge m_{\hat\lambda}\), so
\(U_L(-1)\ge\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k>0\), and the sale is the unique best order of class \(L\). Then
\(\sigma_L=\delta_{-1}\), \(\mu(-1)=m_{\hat\lambda}\) and \(\Psi=\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-joint} gives \(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lb,\Psi/a\}\): if \(\hat\lambda<\lb\), then
\(a\hat\lambda=\rho\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k\), so the gap vanishes at \(\hat\lambda\) and \(\hat\lambda=\lambda_Q<\lb\);
if \(\hat\lambda=\lb\), then the gap at \(\lb\) is nonnegative, and \(\lambda_Q\ge\lb\). So
\(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}\). The orders give the posteriors, Lemma~\ref{lem:prices} gives the preparation,
the prices and the beliefs, and the bids are truthful, so the tuple is floor-entry research.

If \(M_{\hat\lambda}\ge\tau(r)\), then \(e(1)=1\) and \(U_H(1)=\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k\). Class \(L\) can abstain, so
\(\Psi\ge\tfrac12(\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k)\). The map \(\lambda\mapsto\DT(r)\,m_\lambda-k-2a\lambda\) falls strictly and is
nonnegative at \(\lb\), because \(r\ge r_{\lb}\). If \(\hat\lambda<\lb\), then
\(a\hat\lambda=\Psi\ge\tfrac12(\DT m_{\hat\lambda}-k)>a\hat\lambda\), a contradiction; so \(\hat\lambda=\lb\). Let
\(s_-=\lb\,\sigma_L(-1)\) be the low-class sale rate. Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-bounds}(c) gives \(\sigma_L(1)=0\), so
\(\mu(-1)=\bar b/(2\bar b+s_-)\), and \(U_L(-1)=\rho\DT\bar b/(2\bar b+s_-)-k\) falls strictly in \(s_-\). If
\(\rho\DT m_{\lb}\ge k\), then \(U_L(-1)>0\) at every \(s_-<\lb\), and (E5) gives \(s_-=\lb\). Otherwise \(U_L(-1)\) is
positive at \(s_-=0\), because \(\rho\DT>2k\), and negative at \(s_-=\lb\), so (E5) admits only the \(s_-\in(0,\lb)\)
at which \(U_L(-1)=0\). So \(s_-=\hat s_-(r)\), and as in the first case the tuple is recruiting research at the cap.

Every equilibrium on \((r_N,r_C]\) is therefore floor-entry research or recruiting research at the cap. By parts (b)
and (c), the equilibria are exactly these two on \((r_N,r_Q)\), and recruiting research at the cap alone on
\([r_Q,r_C]\). For \(r\in(r_C,r_2]\), \(r>r_U\) gives \eqref{eq:a5-U}, and \(M_{\lb}<\tau(r)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-strong} then gives a unique equilibrium, with research at the cap, full orders and
\(\zeta(P(1))=0\), which is floor-entry research at the cap. This proves part (d).
\end{proof}

The research \(\lambda_Q(\DT(r))\) of floor-entry research and the recruitment intensity \(\lambda_E(r)\) both rise
with strength. On \((r_N,r_C]\), the floor margin has the sign of \(\lambda_Q(\DT(r))-\lambda_E(r)\); it is negative at
\(r_N\), where \(\lambda_Q\) starts from zero, and it rises, so \(\lambda_Q\) crosses the recruitment intensity once, at
\(r_Q\). From \(r_Q\) to \(r_C\), a purchase at the research \(\min\{\lambda_Q,\lb\}\) recruits, and only recruiting
research at the cap remains. Above \(r_C\), even research at the cap leaves the purchase posterior below the
threshold, and entry returns to the floor.

\begin{proposition}[The research cap and cost]\label{prop:researchregion}
Keep the example's primitives other than \(a\) and \(\lb\), with \(\beta=\reg{beta_B}\).
Under an indifferent informed trader who may mix and an indifferent high-cost rival who prepares, the six-strength
equilibrium pattern in Corollary~\ref{cor:research}(b), with
\(\lambda_E(r)<\lambda_R(r)<\lb\) at each intermediate strength, holds with strict margins exactly when
\[
 \lambda_E(r_1)<\lb<\lambda_E(r_2),\qquad a_{\rm lo}(\lb)<a<a_{\rm up},
\]
where
\[
 a_{\rm lo}(\lb)=\frac{\DT(\reg{str_r165})m_{\lb}-k}{2\lb},\qquad
 a_{\rm up}=\frac{\DT(\reg{str_r155})[1-\tau(\reg{str_r155})]-k}{2\lambda_E(\reg{str_r155})}.
\]
The cap strip is approximately \((\reg{res_cap_lo},\reg{res_cap_hi})\). At the example's cap,
\(\lb\approx\reg{res_cap}\), the cost window
is approximately \((\reg{res_a_lo},\reg{res_a_up})\); at \(a=\reg{res_a}\) the cap range is approximately
\((\reg{res_cap_lo_a},\reg{res_cap_hi})\). Entry at \(r_1\) is
\(\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lb]\).
With \(\beta\) varying, \(1+u=\eu\), \(\lb=\lb(\beta)=u\beta/(1+u\beta)\) and \(a=\reg{res_a}\), the
six-strength pattern, with entry \(\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lb]\) at \(r_1\), holds for
\(\beta\in(\beta_{\rm lo},\reg{res_beta_upper}]\), where \(\beta_{\rm lo}\approx\reg{res_beta_lo}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:researchregion}]
Fix \(\beta=\reg{beta_B}\) and the other primitives of the example. The required equilibrium patterns are those of Corollary~\ref{cor:research}(b)
with \(\lambda_E(r)<\lambda_R(r)<\lb\) at each intermediate strength.

\emph{Sufficiency.} Let the two conditions hold. The weak-incumbent claim holds for every \((\lb,a)\), since
\(\DT(r_0)<k\). At the intermediate strengths, \(1/2<\tau<1\) and \(\rho\DT<2k\) do not involve \((\lb,a)\) and hold
at the three strengths, so Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-outcomes} leaves zero research and recruiting research.
The condition \(a<a_{\rm up}\) is \(\varphi_a(\reg{str_r155})>0\). Since
\(a_{\rm up}<a_{\rm mono}\), \(\varphi_a\) rises on \([r_0,\reg{res_bound_strength}]\), so \(\varphi_a>0\) at \(\reg{str_r160}\) and
\(\reg{str_r165}\) too. The condition \(a>a_{\rm lo}(\lb)\) is \(\Lambda_{\reg{str_r165}}(\lb)>0\), that is
\(\lambda_R(\reg{str_r165})<\lb\). Since \(\lambda_R\) rises in \(r\), \(\lambda_R<\lb\) at the two lower strengths
too. Then \(\lambda_E<\lambda_R<\lb\), and Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-outcomes} gives the sets. At the strong and the very strong incumbent, \(M_\lambda\) rises
in \(\lambda\) and \(M_{\lambda_E(r)}=\tau(r)\), so the strip gives \(\tau(r_1)<M_{\lb}<\tau(r_2)\). Condition
\eqref{eq:a5-U} at \(r_1\) is \(a<a_{U}(r_1;\lb):=[\rho\DT(r_1)m_{\lb}-k]/\lb\), and at \(r_2\) it is
\(a<a_{U}(r_2;\lb):=[\rho\DT(r_2)m_{\lb}-k]/\lb\). Both bounds fall in \(\lb\), so on the strip they exceed their
values at \(\lb=\lambda_E(r_2)\), which are \(\reg{res_region_aU3_6}\) and \(\reg{res_region_a36_6}\). Both exceed
\(a_{\rm up}=\reg{res_a_up}\), so Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-strong} applies at both strengths.
Its entry formula gives \(\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lb]\) at \(r_1\).

\emph{Necessity.} Each failure of a condition breaks one claim.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \(\lb\le\lambda_E(r_1)\): every posterior at \(r_1\) is at most \(M_{\lb}\le\tau(r_1)\), so entry above \(\rho\)
needs a tie for the rival, and strict recruitment at \(r_1\) fails.
\item \(\lb\ge\lambda_E(r_2)\): research at the cap gives \(\mu(1)=M_{\lb}\ge\tau(r_2)\), so an indifferent high-cost rival who prepares raises entry above
\(\rho\) at \(r_2\), and exclusion at \(r_2\) fails.
\item \(a\ge a_{\rm up}\): \(\varphi_a(\reg{str_r155})\le0\), so recruiting research below the cap is absent at \(\reg{str_r155}\), or has
\(\mu(1)=\tau\) with a tie for the rival.
\item \(a\le a_{\rm lo}(\lb)\): \(\Lambda_{\reg{str_r165}}(\lb)\le0\), so research at \(\reg{str_r165}\) sits at the cap.
\end{itemize}
The cost window is nonempty on the whole strip: \(a_{\rm lo}\) falls in \(\lb\), because its numerator falls and stays
positive there, and \(a_{\rm lo}(\lambda_E(r_1))=\reg{res_region_alo_0}<a_{\rm up}\). The cap strip is
\((\reg{res_cap_lo},\reg{res_cap_hi})\); the margins of \(\lb\approx\reg{res_cap}\) are \(\reg{margin_res_caplo_B}\) and
\(\reg{margin_res_caphi_B}\), and the margins of \(a=\reg{res_a}\) in its window are \(\reg{margin_res_alo_B}\) and
\(\reg{margin_res_aup_B}\). The upper cap margin corresponds to the posterior margin \(\tau(r_2)-M_{\lb}\approx
\reg{margin_res_tau_r2_B}\); the two numbers measure one boundary in two coordinates.

\emph{General \(\beta\).} Set \(\lb=\lb(\beta)=u\beta/(1+u\beta)\) with \(1+u=\eu\), and keep \(a=\reg{res_a}\).
Then \(m_{\lb}=1/(2+u)=1/(1+\eu)\) for every \(\beta\), so \(M_{\lb}=\eu/(1+\eu)\) and \(m_{\lb}\) do not depend on \(\beta\),
and the endpoint conditions \(\tau(r_1)<M_{\lb}<\tau(r_2)\) hold for every \(\beta\). Condition \eqref{eq:a5-U} at
\(r_1\) holds for every \(\beta\le\reg{res_beta_upper}\), because \(\lb(\beta)\) rises in \(\beta\) and
\(\rho\DT(r_1)/(1+\eu)-k-a\lb(\reg{res_beta_upper})>0\); it also holds at \(r_2\), since \(\DT(r_2)>\DT(r_1)\).
At the intermediate strengths,
\(a_{\rm lo}=[\DT(\reg{str_r165})/(1+\eu)-k]/[2\lb(\beta)]\) falls in \(\beta\), so \(a_{\rm lo}<a\) exactly when
\(\beta>\beta_{\rm lo}\approx\reg{res_beta_lo}\). Here \(\beta_{\rm lo}\) is the unique solution of
\[
 2a\frac{u\beta}{1+u\beta}=\frac{\DT(\reg{str_r165})}{1+\eu}-k;
\]
the left side rises strictly from zero, so the equality determines the boundary.
The upper bound \(a_{\rm up}(\beta)\) falls in \(\beta\), because
\(\partial\lambda_E/\partial\beta=(2\tau-1)(1-\tau)/\{\beta+(1-2\beta)(1-\tau)\}^2>0\), and it exceeds \(a\) for every
\(\beta<\reg{res_beta_hi}\). The monotonicity bound \(a_{\rm mono}(\beta)\) falls in \(\beta\) on \((0,\reg{res_beta_upper}]\) and equals
\(\reg{res_amono_third}>a\) at \(\beta=\reg{res_beta_upper}\). The steps above then apply at each \(\beta\in(\reg{res_beta_lo},\reg{res_beta_upper}]\).

\end{proof}

\begin{named}{Remark~\ref{rem:researchties}, the example}\fixedlabel{rem:researchties-example}{A.1}
At the three intermediate strengths, allowing an indifferent high-cost rival to prepare with any probability
adds the threshold pair, whose entry is \(\reg{res_th_entry_r155}\), \(\reg{res_th_entry_r160}\) and
\(\reg{res_th_entry_r165}\), respectively. At \(r_C\), its cap family permits every \(e(1)\in[\rho,1]\), and
total entry fills \([\rho,\reg{res_entry_cap}]\).

When an indifferent informed trader abstains and an indifferent high-cost rival prepares, recruiting research is
absent on \((r_N,r_S]\), and floor-entry research is the unique equilibrium there. When an indifferent high-cost
rival may prepare with any probability, the threshold pair at \(r=\reg{str_r200}\) has
\[
 \begin{aligned}\lambda=\lambda_E(r)&\approx\reg{res_I0CM_lambda_r200},\\
 e(1)&\approx\reg{res_I0CM_e1_r200},\\
 \Ent&\approx\reg{res_I0CM_entry_r200}.\end{aligned}
\]
Both trades are strictly profitable in this pair, so it is also an equilibrium when an indifferent informed trader
abstains. At all strengths above \(r_U\) other than \(r_C\), the equilibrium is unique under all four tie rules.
\end{named}

\begin{proof}
At each intermediate strength the sale payoff is strictly negative. Thus
\(e^*=(2a\lambda_E+k)/J\), with the respective values
\(\reg{res_th_estar_r155}\), \(\reg{res_th_estar_r160}\) and \(\reg{res_th_estar_r165}\).
Substitution in \(\rho+(e^*-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lambda_E]\) gives the three entry values.
At \(r_C>r_U\), condition~\eqref{eq:a5-U} makes cap research optimal even at purchase entry \(\rho\);
the cap family in Remark~\ref{rem:researchties} therefore has the whole interval \([\rho,1]\).

On the recruiting segment at the cap, \(\rho\DT>2k\) and \(\rho\DT m_{\lb}\le k\) hold together exactly
on \((r_N,r_S]\). The survival condition in Remark~\ref{rem:researchties} excludes recruiting research
there when an indifferent informed trader abstains. This interval lies inside \((r_N,r_Q)\), where floor-entry
research has strict orders and a strict preparation decision, so that outcome remains unique with full
preparation at a tie for the rival. At \(r=\reg{str_r200}\), substituting the rational auction quantities into
\(\lambda_E\), \(e^*\), and the total-entry formula gives the displayed values.
There \(\rho J-k>0\) and \(e^*>\rho\), so both trades are strictly profitable and the threshold pair survives the rule that an indifferent informed trader abstains. Finally, every \(r>r_U\) satisfies condition~\eqref{eq:a5-U}; except at \(r_C\),
its cap posterior differs from the threshold, and Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-strong} gives uniqueness.
\end{proof}
