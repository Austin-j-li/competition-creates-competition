\subsection{Coexistence}\label{app:coexistence}

This subsection states in full and proves Proposition~\ref{prop:coexistence}, and it states and proves
Corollary~\ref{prop:priors}. Throughout, the auction is the
benchmark: point masses \(h\) and \(\ell\), \(R\sim U[0,r]\), and the benchmark values of
\((h,\ell,p,\rho,c_H,k)\). The path is \(r\in[r_0,r_2]\). The slot is any pair \((\lambda,\beta)\) with
\(0<\lambda<1\), \(0<\beta<1/2\) and \(\tau(r_1)<M(1/2)<\tau(r_2)\), the band of Proposition~\ref{prop:region}. On
the path, \(g_H>g_L\) and \(\DT>0\) because \(\ell<r<h\), so Lemma~\ref{lem:prices} applies at every strength.

\subsubsection*{The path and its thresholds}

Two functions organize the path. The first is the gross return of a purchase that just reaches the posterior
\(\tau(r)\) and recruits,
\[
J(r)=\DT(r)\,[1-\tau(r)]=\frac{(r-\ell)^2\,[2r(h-c_H)-r^2-p^2]}{2r\,(2rh-r^2-\ell^2)} .
\]
The second is \(\fr(d)=\ell+d+\sqrt{d^2+2\ell d}\), the root above \(\ell\) of \(\DT(r)=d\). Since
\(\DT'(r)=\frac12-\ell^2/(2r^2)>0\) for \(r>\ell\), \(\DT(r)\le d\) holds exactly when \(r\le\fr(d)\).

\begin{lemma}[The path]\label{lem:a4-path}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \(\tau\) is strictly increasing on \([r_0,r_2]\), with \(\tau(r_0)=\reg{tau_r0_frac}>1/2\) and
\(\tau(r_2)=\reg{tau_r2_frac}<3/4\).
\item \(J\) is strictly increasing on \([r_0,r_2]\), with \(J(r_0)=\reg{J_r0}<k\) and
\(\rho J(r_2)=\reg{rhoJ_r2}>k\).
\item \(\DT(r)\,[1-\tau(r)]^2\) is strictly increasing on \([r_0,2]\).
\item \(\DT(r)\,\tau(r)\) is strictly increasing on \([r_0,r_2]\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Write \(\tau=N_\tau/D_\tau\) with \(N_\tau=2rc_H-\ell^2+p^2\) and \(D_\tau=2rh-r^2-\ell^2\). The derivative
of \(\tau\) has the sign of
\[
N_\tau'D_\tau-N_\tau D_\tau'=2c_Hr^2-2(\ell^2-p^2)\,r+2h(\ell^2-p^2)-2c_H\ell^2 ,
\]
which at the benchmark is \(12r^2-\tfrac32r+3\). Its discriminant \(\tfrac94-144\) is negative, so it is positive
for every \(r\). Also \(D_\tau=20r-r^2-1>0\) on the path. The two values follow from the closed form
\(\tau(r)=(48r-3)/[4(20r-r^2-1)]\).

(b) The derivative \(J'\) has the sign of
\[
Q_J(r)=4r^6-160r^5+743r^4-12r^3-614r^2+40r-1 .
\]
The script \nolinkurl{P1_core/p1_certificates.py} (run log
\nolinkurl{P1_core/p1_certificates.log}) builds \(Q_J\) from the
primitives in exact rational arithmetic. A Sturm sequence counts no real root of \(Q_J\) in \((r_0,r_2]\), and
\(Q_J(r_0)=\reg{QJ_r0}>0\). The two values follow from the closed form of \(J\).

(c) The derivative of \(\DT(1-\tau)^2\) has the sign of the degree-10 polynomial
\[
16r^{10}-1280r^9+29256r^8-229344r^7+562917r^6-32808r^5-374607r^4+48456r^3-2685r^2+80r-1 .
\]
The same script counts no real root in \((r_0,2]\) with a Sturm sequence in exact rational arithmetic, and it
evaluates the polynomial at \(r_0\) with a positive value. The independent script
\nolinkurl{XP1/xp1_signs.py} (run log
\nolinkurl{XP1/xp1_signs.log}) derives both polynomials again and repeats
both Sturm counts.

(d) \(\DT\) and \(\tau\) are positive and strictly increasing on the path, by (a) and \(\DT'>0\).

Parts (a) and (d) are proved by hand. Parts (b) and (c) rest on the exact Sturm counts.
\end{proof}

At the prior \(1/2\), with \(M=(1+u)/(2+u)\) and \(m=1-M\), define six strengths.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \(r_A\): the root of \(J(r)=k\) in \((r_0,r_2)\). A purchase that just reaches \(\tau\) and recruits earns
\(k\).
\item \(r_F=\fr\bigl(k/(1-M)\bigr)\). A full purchase that recruits earns \(\DT(1-M)\); this equals \(k\) at
\(r_F\).
\item \(r_N=\fr(2k/\rho)\). Against no informed trade, a unit order earns \(\rho\DT/2\); this equals \(k\) at
\(r_N\).
\item \(r_J\): the root of \(\rho J(r)=k\) in \((r_0,r_2)\). A purchase that just reaches \(\tau\) without
recruitment earns \(\rho J\); this equals \(k\) at \(r_J\).
\item \(r_S=\fr\bigl(k/(\rho m)\bigr)\). A full sale earns \(\rho\DT m\); this equals \(k\) at \(r_S\).
\item \(r_C\): the root of \(\tau(r)=M\), that is of \(B_r(M)=c_H\). In closed form,
\[
r_C=\frac{Mh-c_H+\sqrt{(Mh-c_H)^2+M[(1-M)\ell^2-p^2]}}{M}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(a)--(b) make \(r_A\), \(r_J\) and \(r_C\) unique, and Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(d) makes the root
\(r_*\) of \(\rho\DT(r)\tau(r)=k\) unique. The strengths \(r_A\), \(r_N\) and
\(r_J\) do not depend on the slot.

\begin{lemma}[The order of the thresholds]\label{lem:a4-order}
For every slot in the band, \(r_0<r_A<r_F<r_N<r_J<r_S<r_1<r_C<r_2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each inequality reduces to a fact of Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path} or to the band.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \(r_0<r_A<r_1\): \(J(r_0)<k<\rho J(r_1)<J(r_1)\), with \(\rho J(r_1)=\reg{rhoJ_r1}\), and \(J\) is
increasing.
\item \(r_A<r_F\): \(\DT(r_A)<k/(1-M)\) is \((1-M)\DT(r_A)<k=(1-\tau(r_A))\DT(r_A)\), that is \(M>\tau(r_A)\).
This holds because \(M>\tau(r_1)>\tau(r_A)\), since \(r_A<r_1\).
\item \(r_F<r_N\): \(k/(1-M)<2k/\rho\) is \(M<1-\rho/2\). This holds because \(M<\tau(r_2)<3/4<1-\rho/2\).
\item \(r_N<r_J\): \(\rho J(r_N)=\rho\DT(r_N)[1-\tau(r_N)]=2k[1-\tau(r_N)]<k\), because \(\tau(r_N)>1/2\), with
\(\tau(r_N)-1/2=\reg{margin_tau_rN_half_B}\).
\item \(r_J<r_S\): \(\DT(r_J)<k/(\rho m)\) is \(\rho(1-M)\DT(r_J)<k=\rho(1-\tau(r_J))\DT(r_J)\), that is
\(M>\tau(r_J)\). The value \(\rho J(r_1)>k\) gives \(r_J<r_1\), so \(M>\tau(r_1)>\tau(r_J)\).
\item \(r_S<r_1\): \(\rho(1-M)\DT(r_1)>k\) is \((1-M)/6>1/50\), that is \(M<22/25\). This holds because
\(M<3/4\).
\item \(r_1<r_C<r_2\): this is \(\tau(r_1)<M<\tau(r_2)\), the band, since \(\tau\) is increasing.\qedhere
\end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{The two investor problems}

Fix a strength \(r\) on the path and a prior \(\pi<\tau(r)\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:trading}(a), no equilibrium puts
weight on a wrong-signed order. Write \(s=s_+=\lambda\sigma_H(1)\) and \(t=s_-=\lambda\sigma_L(-1)\) for the
probabilities of an informed purchase and an informed sale. On the orders \((-1,0,1)\),
\[
f_H=(b,\;b_0+\lambda-s,\;b+s),\qquad f_L=(b+t,\;b_0+\lambda-t,\;b).
\]
Bayes' rule gives
\[
\mu_+(s):=\mu(1)=\frac{\pi(b+s)}{b+\pi s},\qquad 1-\mu_+(s)=\frac{(1-\pi)b}{b+\pi s},\qquad
\mu_-(t):=\mu(-1)=\frac{\pi b}{b+(1-\pi)t},
\]
and \(\mu_0(s,t):=\mu(0)\) has the odds \(\frac{\pi}{1-\pi}\cdot\frac{b_0+\lambda-s}{b_0+\lambda-t}\). On
\([0,\lambda]\), \(\mu_+\) is continuous and rises strictly from \(\pi\) to \(M(\pi)\), and \(\mu_-\) is continuous
and falls strictly from \(\pi\) to \(m(\pi)\).

On the path \(\tau(r)<3/4\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(a). For
\(\pi<\tau<1\), define
\[
s^*=\frac{b(\tau-\pi)}{\pi(1-\tau)}>0 .
\]
The formula for \(\mu_+(s)\) rises strictly in \(s\ge0\), and \(\mu_+(s)=\tau\) is the linear equation
\(\pi(b+s)=\tau(b+\pi s)\), so \(s^*\) is its unique root. Since \(\mu_+(\lambda)=M(\pi)\), \(s^*<\lambda\) exactly when
\(\tau<M(\pi)\), and \(s^*=\lambda\) when \(\tau=M(\pi)\). If \(\tau\ge M(\pi)\), no rate in \((0,\lambda)\) reaches the
threshold, and the forms (R) and (T) below are empty. Entry after each order follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(d).

\begin{lemma}[The high class]\label{lem:a4-high}
Fix \(r\) and \(\pi\) with \(\pi<\tau(r)\). In a candidate with no wrong-signed order, the pair \((s,e(1))\) satisfies (E1) to (E3) at
the order \(1\), and (E5) for class \(H\), exactly when it is one of these five pairs.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.5em]
\item[(N)] \(s=0\) and \(e(1)=\rho\), with \(\rho\DT(1-\pi)\le k\).
\item[(Q)] \(0<s<\min\{s^*,\lambda\}\), \(e(1)=\rho\) and \(\rho\DT[1-\mu_+(s)]=k\). Only under rule IM.
\item[(R)] \(s^*<s<\lambda\), \(e(1)=1\) and \(\DT[1-\mu_+(s)]=k\). Only under rule IM.
\item[(T)] \(s=s^*\in(0,\lambda)\) and \(e(1)=e^*:=k/J(r)\). Under rule C1 this requires \(e^*=1\), that is
\(J(r)=k\). Under rule CM it requires \(\rho\le e^*\le1\). Only under rule IM.
\item[(F)] \(s=\lambda\). Here \(e(1)=1\) if \(M(\pi)>\tau\) and \(e(1)=\rho\) if \(M(\pi)<\tau\). If
\(M(\pi)=\tau\), then \(e(1)=1\) under rule C1, and \(e(1)\in[\rho,1]\) under rule CM. The return must satisfy
\(e(1)\DT[1-M(\pi)]\ge k\) under rule IM, and \(>k\) under rule I0.
\end{enumerate}
Each equation in (Q) and (R) has at most one solution.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:trading}(b), (E5) for class \(H\) requires \(K_H\le k\) at \(s=0\). At \(s=\lambda\) it requires
\(K_H\ge k\) under rule IM, and \(K_H>k\) under rule I0, because rule I0 selects abstention at a tie. At
\(0<s<\lambda\) it requires \(K_H=k\). An interior \(s\) is a mixture at indifference, so it requires rule IM.
Here \(K_H=e(1)\DT[1-\mu_+(s)]\), and \(e(1)\) follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(d) at the posterior
\(\mu_+(s)\).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item At \(s=0\), \(\mu_+(0)=\pi<\tau\), so \(e(1)=\rho\). This is (N).
\item At \(0<s<\lambda\) with \(\mu_+(s)<\tau\): \(e(1)=\rho\), and \(K_H=k\) is the equation of (Q).
\item At \(0<s<\lambda\) with \(\mu_+(s)>\tau\): \(e(1)=1\), and \(K_H=k\) is the equation of (R).
\item At \(0<s<\lambda\) with \(\mu_+(s)=\tau\): then \(s=s^*\), and \(K_H=e(1)\DT(1-\tau)=e(1)J(r)=k\) gives
\(e(1)=e^*\). Rule C1 sets \(e(1)=1\). Rule CM allows any \(e(1)\in[\rho,1]\). This is (T).
\item At \(s=\lambda\), \(\mu_+(\lambda)=M(\pi)\). Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(d) gives \(e(1)\), and the condition on
\(K_H\) is that of (F).
\end{itemize}
The function \(1-\mu_+\) falls strictly, so each equation has at most one root.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The low class]\label{lem:a4-low}
Fix \(r\) and \(\pi\) with \(\pi<\tau(r)\). Then \(\mu_-(t)\le\pi<\tau\) for every \(t\), so \(e(-1)=\rho\) in
every candidate. In a candidate with no wrong-signed order, the rate \(t\) satisfies (E1) to (E3) at the order
\(-1\), and (E5) for class \(L\), exactly when it is one of these:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.5em]
\item[(N)] \(t=0\), with \(\rho\DT\pi\le k\);
\item[(Q)] \(0<t<\lambda\) with \(\rho\DT\mu_-(t)=k\); only under rule IM;
\item[(F)] \(t=\lambda\), with \(\rho\DT m(\pi)\ge k\) under rule IM, and \(>k\) under rule I0.
\end{enumerate}
Under rule IM exactly one rate qualifies at each \(r\):
\[
s_-^*(r)=\begin{cases}0, & \rho\DT\pi\le k,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{b\,(\pi\rho\DT/k-1)}{1-\pi}, & \rho\DT m(\pi)<k<\rho\DT\pi,\\[6pt]
\lambda, & \rho\DT m(\pi)\ge k.\end{cases}
\]
Under rule I0 no rate qualifies when \(\rho\DT m(\pi)\le k<\rho\DT\pi\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\mu_-\) is decreasing, \(\mu_-(t)\le\mu_-(0)=\pi<\tau\), so Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(d) gives \(e(-1)=\rho\).
Then \(K_L=\rho\DT\mu_-(t)\) is continuous and strictly decreasing in \(t\), from \(\rho\DT\pi\) at \(t=0\) to
\(\rho\DT m(\pi)\) at \(t=\lambda\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:trading}(b), (E5) for class \(L\) requires \(K_L\le k\) at
\(t=0\); \(K_L\ge k\) at \(t=\lambda\) under rule IM, and \(>k\) under rule I0; and \(K_L=k\) at
\(0<t<\lambda\), under rule IM only. If \(\rho\DT\pi\le k\), then \(K_L<k\) for every \(t>0\), so only \(t=0\)
qualifies. If \(\rho\DT m(\pi)\ge k\), then \(K_L>k\) for every \(t<\lambda\), so only \(t=\lambda\) qualifies.
Otherwise \(\rho\DT m(\pi)<k<\rho\DT\pi\), and the intermediate value theorem gives exactly one interior root. The
equation \(\pi b/[b+(1-\pi)t]=k/(\rho\DT)\) gives the formula. Under rule I0 with
\(\rho\DT m(\pi)\le k<\rho\DT\pi\), the rate \(t=0\) fails because \(K_L>k\) there; the rate \(t=\lambda\)
fails because \(K_L\le k\) there; and an interior rate requires rule IM.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Product structure]\label{lem:a4-product}
Fix \(r\) and \(\pi\) with \(\pi<\tau(r)\), and rates with \(s\ge t\). Then \(\mu_0(s,t)\le\pi<\tau\). The tuple with
the rates \((s,t)\) is an equilibrium if and only if these four conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.5em]
\item \((s,e(1))\) is a pair of Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-high};
\item \(t\) is a rate of Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-low};
\item \(e(0)=e(-1)=\rho\);
\item the prices are \(P(x)=t_0+e(x)\,w(\mu(x))\), and \(\bar\mu(P(x))=\mu(x)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(s\ge t\), then \(b_0+\lambda-s\le b_0+\lambda-t\), so the odds of \(\mu_0\) are at most \(\pi/(1-\pi)\). Hence
\(\mu_0\le\pi<\tau\).

\emph{Necessity.} In any equilibrium, Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(d) gives \(e(0)=\rho\) at the posterior \(\mu_0<\tau\),
and Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(b)--(c) give condition (iv). Lemma~\ref{lem:trading}(d) shows that (E5) for class \(H\)
involves only \((s,\mu(1),e(1))\), and (E5) for class \(L\) only \((t,\mu(-1),e(-1))\). So (E5) gives (i) and
(ii).

\emph{Sufficiency.} Take rates and entry that satisfy (i) to (iv), and check each condition by direct computation.
(E1): the posteriors are computed from \(f_H\) and \(f_L\). (E2): the stated entry is \(\rho\) at the posteriors
\(\mu_-(t)\) and \(\mu_0\), both at most \(\pi<\tau\), and it is \(e(1)\ge\rho\) at \(\mu_+(s)\ge\pi\), with
\(e(1)=1\) when \(\mu_+(s)>\tau\). So entry lies in \([\rho,1]\) and is nondecreasing in the posterior. By the
paragraph on constructed tuples in Appendix~\ref{app:beliefs}, orders with distinct posteriors have distinct
prices, and orders with equal posteriors have equal entry and equal prices. Hence the orders with one price share
one posterior, and (E2) gives \(\bar\mu(P(x))=\mu(x)\). (E3): at each price the belief is a posterior of an order,
and the stated entry is the one that Lemma~\ref{lem:prices}(d) allows at that posterior under the tie rule in use;
condition (i) states it at the order \(1\), and the posteriors \(\mu_-(t)\) and \(\mu_0\) lie below \(\tau\). (E4):
\eqref{eq:a2-terminal} with the stated entry. (E5): by Lemma~\ref{lem:trading}(d), the payoffs of class \(H\)
involve only \((s,\mu(1),e(1))\), and those of class \(L\) only \((t,\mu(-1),e(-1))\); conditions (i) and (ii) state
optimality for each. (E6): Appendix~\ref{app:payoffs}.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{The equilibrium set at the prior \(1/2\)}

At \(\pi=1/2\), \(1-\mu_+(s)=b/(2b+s)\), \(\mu_-(t)=b/(2b+t)\) and \(s^*=b(2\tau-1)/(1-\tau)\). Five forms of the
high class's pair appear. In each, the low class uses the rate of Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-low}, which at \(\pi=1/2\) is
\[
s_-^*(r)=0\ \text{on}\ [r_0,r_N],\qquad s_-^*(r)=b\,[\rho\DT(r)/k-2]\ \text{on}\ (r_N,r_S),\qquad
s_-^*(r)=\lambda\ \text{on}\ [r_S,r_2].
\]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{N} (no informed trade): \(s=0\), \(e(1)=\rho\).
\item \textbf{R} (a mixed purchase that recruits): \(s=s_R(r):=b[\DT(r)/k-2]\), \(e(1)=1\).
\item \textbf{F} (a full purchase): \(s=\lambda\); \(e(1)=1\) for \(r\le r_C\), and \(e(1)=\rho\) for \(r>r_C\).
\item \textbf{Q} (mixed orders at the entry floor): \(s=s_Q(r):=b[\rho\DT(r)/k-2]=s_-^*(r)\), \(e(1)=\rho\).
\item \textbf{T} (the threshold pair): \(s=s^*(r)\), \(e(1)=e^*(r)=k/J(r)\).
\end{itemize}
Each form defines the equilibrium tuple completely:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item orders: \(\sigma_H(1)=s/\lambda\), \(\sigma_H(0)=1-s/\lambda\), \(\sigma_L(-1)=s_-^*(r)/\lambda\),
\(\sigma_L(0)=1-s_-^*(r)/\lambda\);
\item posteriors: \(\mu_-(s_-^*)\), \(\mu_0(s,s_-^*)\) and \(\mu_+(s)\) after the orders \(-1\), \(0\) and \(1\);
\item challenger beliefs: \(\bar\mu(P(x))=\mu(x)\) at each price;
\item high-cost preparation: \(\zeta(P(1))=(e(1)-\rho)/(1-\rho)\), and \(\zeta(P(x))=0\) at the prices of the
orders \(0\) and \(-1\);
\item prices: \(P(x)=t_0+e(x)\,w(\mu(x))\);
\item bids: truthful.
\end{itemize}
If two orders share a price, they share the posterior and the entry (Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-product}), so \(\zeta\) is
well defined as a function of the price.

\begin{named}{Proposition~\ref{prop:coexistence} (full statement)}
Take the benchmark auction, the prior \(1/2\) and any slot in the band of Proposition~\ref{prop:region}(a). Then
\(r_0<r_A<r_F<r_N<r_J<r_S<r_1<r_C<r_2\), and the equilibrium set at each strength is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Strength & Equilibria\\
\midrule
\(r_0\le r<r_A\) & N\\
\(r=r_A\) & N; T with entry one after a purchase (the left end of R)\\
\(r_A<r<r_F\) & N; R\\
\(r_F\le r\le r_N\) & N; F\\
\(r_N<r<r_J\) & F; Q\\
\(r_J\le r\le r_2\) & F\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Entry is \(\rho\) in N and Q, \(\Ent_R(r)=\rho+(1-\rho)\,b\DT(r)/(2k)\) in R, and
\(\Ent_F=\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda/2)\) in F up to \(r_C\), with \(\Ent_F=\rho\) above \(r_C\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item No informed trade and an informative equilibrium coexist exactly on \([r_A,r_N]\).
\item On the informative branch that coexists with no trade, entry is
\(\rho+(1-\rho)\min\{b\DT(r)/(2k),\,b+\lambda/2\}\). It is continuous, rises strictly on \([r_A,r_F]\), and is
constant on \([r_F,r_N]\). It keeps this value along F up to \(r_C\) and equals \(\rho\) above \(r_C\).
\item The equilibrium set is nonempty at every strength.
\end{enumerate}
\end{named}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:coexistence}]
Fix any slot in the band and the prior \(1/2\). The proof has eight steps.

\emph{Step 1: the frame.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(a), \(\tau(r)\ge\tau(r_0)>1/2=\pi\) on the path, so
Lemmas~\ref{lem:a4-high} to~\ref{lem:a4-product} apply at every \(r\). Every equilibrium has a high-class pair
from Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-high} and a low-class rate from Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-low}. Step 4 shows \(s\ge t\) for every
such combination. Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-product} then gives the equilibrium set as their product. Recall
\(1-M=1/(2+u)\) and \(ub=\lambda\).

\emph{Step 2: the high-class pairs.} Use Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}, the definitions of the thresholds, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-order}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item (N) holds if and only if \(\rho\DT/2\le k\), that is \(r\le r_N\).
\item (R): \(\DT b/(2b+s)=k\) gives \(s=s_R(r)\). Then \(\mu_+(s_R)>\tau\) if and only if \(k/\DT<1-\tau\), that is
\(J(r)>k\), that is \(r>r_A\). Also \(s_R<\lambda\) if and only if \(\DT/k<2+u\), that is \(\DT(1-M)<k\), that is
\(r<r_F\). So (R) holds if and only if \(r_A<r<r_F\), under rule IM.
\item (Q): \(\rho\DT b/(2b+s)=k\) gives \(s=s_Q(r)\). Then \(s_Q>0\) if and only if \(r>r_N\); \(\mu_+(s_Q)<\tau\) if
and only if \(\rho J(r)<k\), that is \(r<r_J\); and \(s_Q<\lambda\) if and only if \(\rho\DT(1-M)<k\), that is
\(r<r_S\). Since \(r_J<r_S\), (Q) holds if and only if \(r_N<r<r_J\), under rule IM.
\item (T): \(s^*\in(0,\lambda)\) if and only if \(1/2<\tau(r)<M\), that is \(r<r_C\). Also \(\rho\le e^*\le1\) if and
only if \(\rho J(r)\le k\le J(r)\), that is \(r_A\le r\le r_J\), and \(e^*=1\) if and only if \(r=r_A\). So (T)
holds at \(r_A\) under rule C1, and on \([r_A,r_J]\) under rule CM, under rule IM in both cases.
\item (F): for \(r<r_C\), \(M>\tau(r)\), so \(e(1)=1\), and the condition is \(\DT(1-M)\ge k\), that is \(r\ge r_F\)
(\(r>r_F\) under rule I0). For \(r>r_C\), \(M<\tau(r)\), so \(e(1)=\rho\), and \(\rho\DT(1-M)\ge k\) holds because
\(r>r_C>r_S\). At \(r=r_C\), rule C1 gives \(e(1)=1\); rule CM allows any \(e(1)\in[\rho,1]\), and every such value
satisfies the condition because \(\rho\DT(r_C)(1-M)>k\). So (F) holds if and only if \(r\ge r_F\) (rule IM) or
\(r>r_F\) (rule I0).
\end{itemize}

\emph{Step 3: the low-class rate.} At \(\pi=1/2\), Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-low} gives \(t=0\) if and only if
\(r\le r_N\), \(t=\lambda\) if and only if \(r\ge r_S\), and \(s_-^*(r)=b(\rho\DT/k-2)\) on \((r_N,r_S)\), under
rule IM. Under rule I0 no rate qualifies if and only if \(\rho\DT m\le k<\rho\DT/2\), that is on \((r_N,r_S]\).

\emph{Step 4: \(s\ge t\).} For \(r\le r_N\), \(t=0\le s\). For \(r>r_N\), the pairs are F with \(s=\lambda\ge t\);
Q with \(s=s_Q(r)=s_-^*(r)\); and T with \(r_N<r\le r_J\). For T, \(s^*\ge s_-^*(r)\) if and only if
\((2\tau-1)/(1-\tau)\ge\rho\DT/k-2\), that is \(1/(1-\tau)\ge\rho\DT/k\), that is \(\rho J(r)\le k\), which holds
for \(r\le r_J\). By Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-product}, entry after the zero order and after a sale is \(\rho\) in every
equilibrium.

\emph{Step 5: the set.} Combine Steps 2 to 4 with the order of Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-order}. Under rules IM and C1 the
set is: N on \([r_0,r_A)\); N and T with \(e^*=1\) at \(r_A\); N and R on \((r_A,r_F)\); N and F on \([r_F,r_N]\);
F and Q on \((r_N,r_J)\); F on \([r_J,r_2]\). Under rule CM, T also exists on \((r_A,r_J]\), and at \(r_C\) entry
after a purchase in F can be any \(e(1)\in[\rho,1]\). Under rule I0, (Q), (R) and (T) drop out, (F) needs
\(r>r_F\), and Step 3 removes every equilibrium on \((r_N,r_S]\). The set under rule I0 is N on \([r_0,r_F]\); N
and F on \((r_F,r_N]\); empty on \((r_N,r_S]\); and F on \((r_S,r_2]\).

\emph{Step 6: verification of each tuple.} Fix a form and a strength at which Steps 2 and 3 admit it. Step 4 gives
\(s\ge t\), so the sufficiency part of Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-product} applies: it checks (E1) to (E6) against the
tuple by direct computation. In particular, if two orders have equal posteriors, one of them is \(0\) or \(-1\), so
the common posterior is at most \(1/2<\tau\), and both orders have entry \(\rho\) and the same price.

\emph{Step 7: entry.} Entry is \(\Ent=\sum_x\Pr(X=x)\,e(x)\). Since \(e(0)=e(-1)=\rho\) and
\(\Pr(X=1)=\tfrac12(b+s)+\tfrac12b=b+s/2\), \(\Ent=\rho+[e(1)-\rho](b+s/2)\).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item R: \(b+s_R/2=b\DT/(2k)\) and \(e(1)=1\), so \(\Ent_R(r)=\rho+(1-\rho)\,b\DT(r)/(2k)\). It rises strictly in
\(r\) because \(\DT\) does. At \(r_F\), \(\DT(r_F)=k/(1-M)=k(2+u)\), so \(b\DT(r_F)/(2k)=b+ub/2=b+\lambda/2\): the
branch joins F continuously.
\item F: \(b+\lambda/2\), with \(e(1)=1\) below \(r_C\) and \(e(1)=\rho\) above \(r_C\). So
\(\Ent_F=\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda/2)\) on \([r_F,r_C)\) and \(\Ent_F=\rho\) on \((r_C,r_2]\). At \(r_C\), rule C1
gives \(e(1)=1\) and the upper value; rule CM gives every value
\(\Ent_F(r_C)=\rho+(e(1)-\rho)(b+\lambda/2)\in[\rho,\,\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda/2)]\).
\item N and Q: \(e(1)=\rho\), so \(\Ent=\rho\).
\item T: \(e^*-\rho=(k-\rho J)/J\) and \(b+s^*/2=b/[2(1-\tau)]\), so
\[
\Ent_T(r)=\rho+\frac b2\cdot\frac{k-\rho J(r)}{J(r)\,[1-\tau(r)]}
=\rho+\frac b2\cdot\frac{k-\rho J(r)}{\DT(r)\,[1-\tau(r)]^2}.
\]
\end{itemize}
On the informative branch that coexists with N, entry is \(\rho+(1-\rho)\min\{b\DT(r)/(2k),\,b+\lambda/2\}\). It is
continuous, rises strictly on \([r_A,r_F]\), and is constant on \([r_F,r_N]\). The strict rise needs rule IM,
because R is a mixture at indifference. At \(r_A\), \(J=k\) and \(\DT(r_A)/k=1/(1-\tau(r_A))\), so
\(\Ent_T(r_A)=\rho+\tfrac12(1-\rho)\,b/(1-\tau(r_A))=\Ent_R(r_A)\). At \(r_J\), \(\rho J=k\), so
\(\Ent_T(r_J)=\rho\). On \([r_A,r_J]\), \(k-\rho J\) is positive except at \(r_J\) and strictly decreasing, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(b). The term \(\DT(1-\tau)^2\) is positive and strictly increasing, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(c) and \(r_J<2\); the last inequality holds because \(\rho J(2)-k=\reg{margin_rhoJ_2_B_frac}>0\) and \(J\)
rises. So \(\Ent_T\) falls strictly from \(\Ent_R(r_A)\) to \(\rho\). For
\(r_A<r\le r_N\): \(r<r_J\) gives \(e^*>\rho\), so \(\Ent_T>\rho\); and \(J>k\) gives \(e^*<1\) with \(s^*<s\) in R
or F, so \(\Ent_T<\Ent_R\) on \((r_A,r_F)\) and \(\Ent_T<\Ent_F\) on \([r_F,r_N]\).

\emph{Step 8: existence.} Under rule IM, N exists on \([r_0,r_N]\) and F on \([r_F,r_2]\), and \(r_F<r_N\). The
set is nonempty at every strength under rule IM, with either challenger rule.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Coexistence at other priors}

% STRUCTURE: Corollary 2 and its definitions <- Section 4.3
Coexistence also holds at other priors. For a prior \(\pi\), write \(r_N(\pi)=\fr\bigl(k/(\rho\max\{\pi,1-\pi\})\bigr)\).
When some strength on the path has \(\tau(r)\le M(\pi)\), let \(r_C(\pi)\) be the largest one. When
\(\tau(r_0)\le\pi\le\tau(r_2)\), let \(r_\pi\) solve \(\tau(r_\pi)=\pi\). Three priors divide the cases: \(\pi_A^{\mathrm{lo}}\) solves
\(M(\pi)=\tau(r_A)\); \(\pi_A^{\mathrm{hi}}=\tau(r_A)\); and \(\pi_{\max}=\tau(r_*)\), where \(r_*\) solves
\(\rho\DT(r)\tau(r)=k\).

\begin{corollary}[Coexistence at every prior]\label{prop:priors}
Take the benchmark auction, any slot, and any prior \(\pi\in(0,1)\). The set \(C(\pi)\) of strengths on the path
at which no informed trade and an informative equilibrium coexist is
\[
C(\pi)=\bigl\{r\in[r_0,r_2]:\ r\ge r_A,\ \ \pi<\tau(r)\le M(\pi),\ \ \rho\DT(r)\max\{\pi,1-\pi\}\le k\bigr\}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item If \(\pi<\pi_A^{\mathrm{lo}}\), \(C(\pi)\) is empty. At \(\pi=\pi_A^{\mathrm{lo}}\), \(C(\pi)=\{r_A\}\).
\item If \(\pi_A^{\mathrm{lo}}<\pi<\pi_A^{\mathrm{hi}}\), then \(C(\pi)=[r_A,\min\{r_N(\pi),r_C(\pi)\}]\), a
nondegenerate interval. Coexistence starts at \(r_A\) for every prior in this window and every slot.
\item If \(\pi_A^{\mathrm{hi}}\le\pi<\pi_{\max}\), then \(C(\pi)=(r_\pi,\min\{r_N(\pi),r_C(\pi)\}]\), a nonempty
interval with \(r_\pi\ge r_A\). Coexistence starts where the prior reaches the threshold.
\item If \(\pi\ge\pi_{\max}\), \(C(\pi)\) is empty.
\end{enumerate}
At each \(r\in C(\pi)\), the informative equilibrium is R or F (the threshold pair at \(r_A\) in case (b)). Its
entry is \(\rho+(1-\rho)\min\{(1-\pi)\,b\DT(r)/k,\ b+\pi\lambda\}\), which rises strictly while the purchase is
mixed and is then constant.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{prop:priors}]
Fix any slot with \(0<\lambda<1\) and \(0<\beta<1/2\), any prior \(\pi\in(0,1)\), and the rules IM and C1. For a
prior \(\pi\), write \(r_N(\pi)=\fr\bigl(k/(\rho\max\{\pi,1-\pi\})\bigr)\). When some strength on the path has
\(\tau(r)\le M(\pi)\), let \(r_C(\pi)\) be the largest one; otherwise the condition \(\tau(r)\le M(\pi)\) holds
nowhere on the path. When \(\tau(r_0)\le\pi\le\tau(r_2)\), let \(r_\pi\) solve \(\tau(r_\pi)=\pi\); this root is
unique by Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(a). For \(\pi>\tau(r_2)\), no strength on the path satisfies \(\pi<\tau(r)\).
Write \(\tau_A=\tau(r_A)\), and let \(r_*\) solve \(\rho\DT(r_*)\tau(r_*)=k\) on the path; it is unique by
Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(d). Define
\[
\pi_A^{\rm lo}=\frac{\tau_A}{\tau_A+(1-\tau_A)(1+u)},\qquad \pi_A^{\rm hi}=\tau_A,\qquad \pi_{\max}=\tau(r_*).
\]
So \(M(\pi_A^{\rm lo})=\tau_A\). The numbers \(\pi_A^{\rm hi}\) and \(\pi_{\max}\) do not depend on the slot. The
claim is that the set of strengths on the path at which no informed trade and an informative equilibrium coexist is
\[
C(\pi)=\bigl\{r\in[r_0,r_2]:\ r\ge r_A,\ \ \pi<\tau(r)\le M(\pi),\ \ \rho\DT(r)\max\{\pi,1-\pi\}\le k\bigr\},
\]
with the four cases of the corollary. The proof has three steps.

\emph{Step 1: strengths with \(\pi<\tau(r)\).} Lemmas~\ref{lem:a4-high} to~\ref{lem:a4-product} apply. No informed
trade is an equilibrium if and only if (N) holds in both lemmas, that is \(\rho\DT\max\{\pi,1-\pi\}\le k\); then
\(\mu_0=\pi<\tau\) and \(e(0)=\rho\). Suppose this holds. Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-low} then gives \(t=0\) in every
equilibrium, so an informative equilibrium has \(s>0\). Pair (Q) is impossible, because
\(\rho\DT[1-\mu_+(s)]<\rho\DT(1-\pi)\le k\) for \(s>0\). Pair (F) with \(M(\pi)<\tau\) is impossible for the same
reason. The other pairs, (R), (T) and (F) with \(M(\pi)\ge\tau\), need \(\mu_+(s)\ge\tau\) for some
\(s\le\lambda\), so \(\tau\le M(\pi)\). They also need \(k\le e(1)\DT[1-\mu_+(s)]\le\DT(1-\tau)=J(r)\), so
\(r\ge r_A\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(b).

Conversely, let \(\tau(r)\le M(\pi)\) and \(r\ge r_A\), so \(J(r)\ge k\). If \(\DT(1-M(\pi))\ge k\), pair (F) holds
with \(e(1)=1\). Otherwise \(\tau<M(\pi)\), and \(s\mapsto\DT[1-\mu_+(s)]\) is continuous and strictly decreasing on
\([s^*,\lambda]\), from \(J(r)\ge k\) to \(\DT(1-M(\pi))<k\). It equals \(k\) at exactly one
\(s\in[s^*,\lambda)\). At \(s=s^*\) this is (T) with \(J(r)=k\) and \(e(1)=1\); at \(s>s^*\) it is (R). With
\(t=0\le s\), Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-product} completes the tuple. So a strength with \(\pi<\tau(r)\) lies in \(C(\pi)\)
if and only if it satisfies the three conditions in the display.

\emph{Step 2: strengths with \(\pi\ge\tau(r)\).} Under rule C1, no informed trade gives the posterior
\(\pi\ge\tau\) after every order, so \(e\equiv1\). It is an equilibrium if and only if
\(\DT\max\{\pi,1-\pi\}\le k\). Suppose this holds, and take any candidate with \(s>0\). Then
\(\mu_+(s)>\pi\ge\tau\), so \(e(1)=1\) and \(K_H=\DT[1-\mu_+(s)]<\DT(1-\pi)\le k\): class \(H\) strictly prefers
abstention, a contradiction. Take any candidate with \(t>0\). Then \(\mu_-(t)<\pi\) and \(e(-1)\le1\), so
\(K_L\le\DT\mu_-(t)<\DT\pi\le k\): again a contradiction. So no strength with \(\pi\ge\tau(r)\) lies in
\(C(\pi)\).

\emph{Step 3: the four cases.} By Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(a), \(\pi<\tau(r)\) holds for every \(r\) if
\(\pi<\tau(r_0)\), for \(r>r_\pi\) if \(\tau(r_0)\le\pi\le\tau(r_2)\), and for no \(r\) if \(\pi>\tau(r_2)\). The
condition \(\tau(r)\le M(\pi)\) holds for \(r\le r_C(\pi)\) when \(r_C(\pi)\) exists, and the last condition holds
for \(r\le r_N(\pi)\). Also
\(\rho\DT(r_A)\max\{\pi,1-\pi\}<\rho\DT(r_A)=\rho k/(1-\tau_A)<k\), because \(\tau_A<3/4=1-\rho\). So
\(r_A<r_N(\pi)\) for every prior; at the benchmark \(3/4-\tau_A=\reg{margin_tauA_3q_B}\).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em]
\item[(a)] \(M\) is increasing in \(\pi\), so \(\pi<\pi_A^{\rm lo}\) gives \(M(\pi)<\tau_A\le\tau(r)\) for every
\(r\ge r_A\). No strength qualifies. At \(\pi=\pi_A^{\rm lo}\) only \(r_A\) qualifies, and there \(M(\pi)=\tau_A\):
the informative equilibrium is the full purchase F, with \(K_H=\DT(r_A)(1-\tau_A)=k\).
\item[(b)] Let \(\pi_A^{\rm lo}<\pi<\pi_A^{\rm hi}\). For \(r\ge r_A\), \(\tau(r)\ge\tau_A>\pi\). Also
\(\tau_A<M(\pi)\) gives \(r_A<r_C(\pi)\), and \(r_A<r_N(\pi)\). So \(C(\pi)=[r_A,\min\{r_N(\pi),r_C(\pi)\}]\), a
nondegenerate interval. At \(r_A\), \(\DT(r_A)(1-M(\pi))<\DT(r_A)(1-\tau_A)=k\), so Step 1 gives the threshold pair
with \(J=k\). There \(\mu_+=\tau\), so the challenger is indifferent, and \(K_H=k\), so the investor is
indifferent. The start \(r_A\) is the same for every prior in this window and every slot.
\item[(c)] Let \(\pi_A^{\rm hi}\le\pi<\pi_{\max}\). Now \(r_\pi\ge r_A\), and \(\pi<\tau(r)\) holds exactly for
\(r>r_\pi\). The interval \(C(\pi)=(r_\pi,\min\{r_N(\pi),r_C(\pi)\}]\) is nonempty: \(r_\pi<r_C(\pi)\) because
\(\tau(r_\pi)=\pi<M(\pi)\); and \(r_\pi<r_N(\pi)\) if and only if \(\rho\DT(r_\pi)\pi<k\) (as \(\pi>1/2\)), that is
\(\rho\DT(r_\pi)\tau(r_\pi)<k\), that is \(r_\pi<r_*\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-path}(d), that is \(\pi<\pi_{\max}\).
\item[(d)] Let \(\pi\ge\pi_{\max}\). Then \(\pi>1/2\), and any \(r\) with \(\pi<\tau(r)\) has \(r>r_\pi\ge r_*\), so
\(\rho\DT(r)\pi>\rho\DT(r_*)\tau(r_*)=k\). No informed trade is not an equilibrium there. Step 2 covers the other
strengths, so \(C(\pi)\) is empty.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Entry.} Entry after the orders \(0\) and \(-1\) is \(\rho\), and \(\Pr(X=1)=\pi(b+s)+(1-\pi)b=b+\pi s\). In R the
equation \(\DT(1-\pi)b/(b+\pi s)=k\) gives \(b+\pi s=(1-\pi)b\DT/k\); in F, \(\Pr(X=1)=b+\pi\lambda\), and
\(\DT(1-M(\pi))\ge k\) is \((1-\pi)b\DT/k\ge b+\pi\lambda\). Entry on the informative branch is therefore
\(\rho+(1-\rho)\min\{(1-\pi)b\DT(r)/k,\ b+\pi\lambda\}\). It rises strictly while the purchase is mixed and then
stays constant. At calibration B, \(\pi_A^{\rm lo}\approx\reg{piA_lo_B}\), \(\pi_A^{\rm hi}\approx\reg{piA_hi}\) and
\(\pi_{\max}\approx\reg{pimax}\), from \nolinkurl{P1_core/p1_numbers.py}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Other tie rules.}

The results use the default rules IM and C1. Two alternatives show which conclusions depend
on these choices:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item under rule I0, an investor indifferent between abstention and a nonzero order abstains;
\item under rule CM, an indifferent high-cost challenger may prepare with any probability.
\end{itemize}
This paragraph states each place on the benchmark path where I0 or CM changes a conclusion;
Remark~\ref{rem:researchties} in Appendix~\ref{app:research} does the same with research.

% STRUCTURE: Remark 1 <- Section 4.3 (prior 1/2); label rem:ties retired
At the prior \(1/2\): under the challenger rule CM, the threshold pair T is also an equilibrium on \((r_A,r_J]\). Its entry falls strictly
from \(\Ent_R(r_A)\) at \(r_A\) to \(\rho\) at \(r_J\); at the benchmark it is \(\reg{entry_th_r155_B}\),
\(\reg{entry_th_r160_B}\) and \(\reg{entry_th_r165_B}\) at \(\reg{str_r155}\), \(\reg{str_r160}\) and
\(\reg{str_r165}\). At \(r_C\), entry after a purchase can then take any value in \([\rho,1]\). 

Under the investor rule I0, the equilibrium set is N on \([r_0,r_F]\); N and F on \((r_F,r_N]\); empty on \((r_N,r_S]\); and F on
\((r_S,r_2]\). On \((r_N,r_S]\) the low class has no admissible sale rate under I0. Abstention fails, because a
sale earns more than \(k\). A full sale fails, because it earns at most \(k\). Under the investor rule IM, the
equilibrium set is nonempty along the whole path. The strict rise of entry along R also needs IM.

At a general prior, under rule CM, a challenger tie at the prior adds exactly one strength. At \(r=r_\pi\), the prior equals
\(\tau(r_\pi)\), and rule CM lets no informed trade keep entry \(\rho\). That tuple is an equilibrium exactly when
\(\rho\DT(r_\pi)\max\{\pi,1-\pi\}\le k\). An informative purchase at \(r_\pi\) has \(\mu_+(s)>\pi=\tau\), so it
recruits. It needs \(\DT(r_\pi)[1-\mu_+(s)]=k\) for some \(s\in(0,\lambda)\), or \(\DT(r_\pi)[1-M(\pi)]\ge k\) at
\(s=\lambda\); one of the two holds exactly when \(\DT(r_\pi)(1-\pi)>k\), that is \(J(r_\pi)>k\), that is
\(r_\pi>r_A\). In this tuple the low class abstains, so the sale keeps the posterior \(\pi=\tau\), and rule CM lets
entry stay \(\rho\) after the sale as well; the zero order has a posterior below \(\pi\). The two conditions hold exactly when \(r_A<r_\pi\le r_*\), that is
\(\tau_A<\pi\le\pi_{\max}\). Hence
\[
C_{\rm CM}(\pi)=C(\pi)\cup\{r_\pi\}\quad\text{for }\tau_A<\pi\le\pi_{\max},\qquad C_{\rm CM}(\pi)=C(\pi)\ \text{otherwise}.
\]
At \(\pi=\pi_{\max}\), the point \(r_\pi=r_*=\reg{rstar}\) is the whole set: there \(M(\pi_{\max})=\reg{M_pimax_B}\), the
zero-order posterior is \(\reg{mu0_pimax_B}\), the full purchase earns \(\DT(r_*)[1-M(\pi_{\max})]-k=\reg{margin_cmend_B}\),
and entry is \(\reg{entry_cmend_B}\). The pair T adds equilibria inside \(C(\pi)\), since it
needs \(J(r)\ge k\). Under rule I0, R and T drop out, and F needs \(\DT(1-M(\pi))>k\). Coexistence then holds on
the strengths of \(C(\pi)\) above \(\fr\bigl(k/(1-M(\pi))\bigr)\); under rules I0 and CM, the point \(r_\pi\) above
joins it when \(\DT(r_\pi)[1-M(\pi)]>k\). Table~\ref{tab:a4-ties} records the dependence at the prior \(1/2\).
