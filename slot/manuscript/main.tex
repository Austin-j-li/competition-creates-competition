\begin{abstract}
A stronger incumbent bidder can induce rival entry through a more informative stock
price. I study a takeover auction in which a potential rival bidder sees the target's stock price before it
pays to prepare a bid. When
the high class dominates the low class, stronger incumbents never raise acquisition profit at any belief or narrow
the target-payoff spread. On the main result's open set, entry rises, high-class ownership rises when a prepared
high-class rival surely beats the strong incumbent, and access to prices raises expected proceeds and net
acquisition surplus against the strong incumbent. Under the paper's default tie rules,
quiet and informative markets coexist exactly on a region of the spread and preparation threshold, informative
entry rising in the spread. At an even prior, where an indifferent informed trader may mix and the
threshold lies above the prior, research and entry rise with the spread until research caps in the equilibrium in
which a purchase after successful research recruits.
\end{abstract}
