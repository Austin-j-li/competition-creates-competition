\section{Competition creates research}\label{sec:research}

So far the investor is informed with a fixed probability \(\lambda\). This section lets it choose that probability.
Before it learns the class, the investor chooses a research intensity \(z\in[0,\lb]\) at the cost \((a/2)z^2\),
where \(a>0\) and the cap satisfies \(0<\lb<1\). Research succeeds with probability \(z\), independently of the
class, the costs and the incumbent's value. A success reveals the class, and the investor then fills the slot with
an order. After a failure, a liquidity trader fills the slot.

Research is therefore the presence probability of Section~\ref{sec:slot}, chosen at a cost. It is the specialist's
own work on the target's customers, technology and demand, and it must end before the order opportunity. The cap
\(\lb\) is the probability that this work can reveal the class at all before the challenger decides. Some facts about
the match become visible only through the buyer's own diligence, and no research by an outsider reaches them. The
marginal cost rises, because the specialist uses cheap sources first, such as its sector coverage, and costly ones
later, such as expert calls. The quadratic cost is the simplest form whose marginal cost rises.

A success takes the place of a liquidity order because the opportunity has room for one order. An investor that
knows the class takes it. After a failure, the investor knows no more than the market, so an order would lose at
least its cost \(k\). The opportunity then passes to a liquidity trader. More research therefore also removes
liquidity orders, and it reduces the camouflage of the investor's own orders.

Market makers and the challenger do not observe the research choice; they use a conjectured intensity
\(\hat\lambda\). The prior is \(1/2\) throughout this section. An equilibrium with research is a tuple \((\hat\lambda,\sigma_H,\sigma_L,\mu,P,\bar\mu,\zeta)\), together with the
bids. Conditions (E1)--(E6) of Definition~\ref{def:equilibrium} hold at the intensity \(\hat\lambda\). Condition
(E5) holds also at \(\hat\lambda=0\): a deviation in research makes a success possible, and the order after it must
be optimal. A seventh condition completes the definition.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(E\arabic*),start=7,leftmargin=3em]
\item \emph{Research optimality.} Hold \(P\) and \(\zeta\) at their values in the tuple. Then \(\hat\lambda\) is
the research choice of a best response among all joint deviations in research and orders.
\end{enumerate}
A joint deviation changes the true order probabilities, but not the prices or the entry rule. Let
\(V=\tfrac12\max_qU_H(q)+\tfrac12\max_qU_L(q)\) be the expected trading payoff after a success, at the candidate's
prices. Appendix~\ref{app:research} shows that the best research choice is unique and equals
\(\min\{\lb,V/a\}\). Condition (E7) therefore reads \(\hat\lambda=\min\{\lb,V/a\}\), and research never has a tie.

Two functions of the intensity describe the branch on which research responds to competition. At intensity
\(\lambda\), write \(u_\lambda=\lambda/[(1-\lambda)\beta]\), \(M_\lambda=(1+u_\lambda)/(2+u_\lambda)\) and
\(m_\lambda=1-M_\lambda\); these are the posterior bounds of Section~\ref{sec:inference} at the prior \(1/2\). When
\(1/2<\tau(r)<1\), the \emph{recruitment intensity} \(\lambda_E(r)\) solves \(M_\lambda=\tau(r)\):
\[
\lambda_E(r)=\frac{\beta\,[2\tau(r)-1]}{\beta+(1-2\beta)[1-\tau(r)]}.
\]
A full purchase recruits exactly when research is at least \(\lambda_E(r)\).

 Suppose that class \(H\) buys after a
success, class \(L\) abstains, and only a purchase recruits. A purchase then earns \(\DT(r)m_\lambda-k\), and
research optimality at an interior intensity reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:research}
2a\lambda=\DT(r)\,m_\lambda-k .
\end{equation}
The left side rises with \(\lambda\), and the right side falls, because more research removes camouflage. When
\(\DT(r)>2k\), the two sides meet at one intensity \(\lambda_R(r)\in(0,1)\).

The benchmark research parameters are \(a=\reg{res_a}\) and the cap \(\lb=\reg{res_cap}\), the presence
probability of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}. Research at the cap therefore reproduces the benchmark slot.

\begin{proposition}[Research at the benchmark strengths; computer-assisted]\label{prop:research}
Take the benchmark \(B\) with the research cost \(a=\reg{res_a}\) and the cap \(\lb=\reg{res_cap}\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item At \(r_0\), the unique equilibrium has no research and no trade. Entry is \(\rho\).
\item At each \(r\in\{\reg{str_r155},\reg{str_r160},\reg{str_r165}\}\), there are exactly two equilibria. In the
first, the investor does no research, and entry is \(\rho\). In the second, the research branch, research is
\(\lambda_R(r)\), with \(\lambda_E(r)<\lambda_R(r)<\lb\). After a success, class \(H\) buys and class \(L\) abstains,
only a purchase recruits, and entry is \(\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lambda_R(r)]\).
\item At \(r_1\) and \(r_2\), the unique equilibrium has research at the cap and trade in both directions. It is the
equilibrium of Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc} with \(\lambda=\lb\). Entry is \(\reg{res_entry_r1}\) at \(r_1\) and
\(\rho\) at \(r_2\).
\end{enumerate}
The investor's tie rule plays no role at these six strengths.
\end{proposition}

The equilibrium lists rest on analytical arguments; the strict signs at the benchmark use exact rational and
interval arithmetic. At \(r_0\) every order after a success loses, so the research value is zero. At \(r_1\) and
\(r_2\), a bound on every candidate profile makes a full order profitable net of the marginal research cost at
the cap. At the intermediate strengths, no research confirms itself. Against an uninformative price,
the best nonzero order after a success earns \(\rho\DT/2-k<0\), because these strengths lie below \(r_N\). On the
research branch, a purchase reveals enough to recruit, and its return pays for the research that makes it
informative.

Table~\ref{tab:research} shows the main result of this section. Across the three strengths, research rises from
\(\reg{res_lambda_r155}\) through \(\reg{res_lambda_r160}\) to \(\reg{res_lambda_r165}\), and entry rises from
\(\reg{res_entry_r155}\) through \(\reg{res_entry_r160}\) to \(\reg{res_entry_r165}\). A stronger incumbent widens the
spread, so a purchase earns more, and the investor buys more information. The posterior after a purchase then rises
above the threshold by more, and a purchase becomes more likely. The next result shows that this comovement is
general along the branch.

\begin{corollary}[Research and entry rise together; analytical]\label{prop:researchbranch}
Take the benchmark auction at the prior \(1/2\), with \(0<\beta\le1/3\) and \(a>0\). On the strengths with
\(\DT(r)>2k\), research \(\lambda_R(r)\) is continuously differentiable and rises strictly in \(r\). Wherever the
research branch is an equilibrium, entry
\[
\Ent_R(r)=\rho+(1-\rho)\bigl[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lambda_R(r)\bigr]
\]
and high-class ownership \(\tfrac12[\rho+(1-\rho)(\beta+(1-\beta)\lambda_R(r))]\) rise strictly with \(r\).
\end{corollary}

The slope of entry is \((1-\rho)(1/2-\beta)\lambda_R'(r)\), which is positive for every \(\beta\le1/3\). A purchase
has probability \(\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lambda\): research adds informed purchases in class \(H\) and removes liquidity
orders in both classes, and the first effect dominates.

At the benchmark, the research branch is an equilibrium exactly on \([r_R,\bar r_R)\), with \(r_R\approx\reg{res_rR}\)
and \(\bar r_R\approx\reg{res_rcap}\) (computer-assisted). The start \(r_R\) is the strength at which the purchase
posterior just reaches the threshold and the purchase also pays the marginal research cost:
\(\DT(1-\tau)=k+2a\lambda_E(r)\). The end \(\bar r_R\) is the strength at which research reaches the cap \(\lb\). Along
the branch, research rises from \(\lambda_E(r_R)=\reg{res_lambda_rR}\) to the cap, and entry rises from
\(\reg{res_entry_rR}\) to \(\reg{res_entry_cap}\). Zero research is an equilibrium along the whole branch, because
\(\bar r_R<r_N=\reg{res_rN}\). On \([\bar r_R,r_N)\), research at the cap coexists with zero research, and entry equals
\(\reg{res_entry_cap}\).

Against strong incumbents, research sits at the cap. Write
\(r_U=\fr\bigl((k+a\lb)(1+\eu)/\rho\bigr)\approx\reg{res_rUR}\). At every strength above \(r_U\), a bound on every
candidate profile forces full orders and research at the cap (Appendix~\ref{app:research}). On \((r_N,r_U)\), the
equilibrium set with research is open.

Figure~\ref{fig:research} shows the branch. It starts later than the informative branch of
Section~\ref{sec:coexistence}, which starts at \(r_A=\reg{res_rA}\). A purchase must now pay for research as well
as for trade. The two branches also adjust in different ways. With a fixed presence probability, the purchase
rate adjusts through a mixture of the high class. With research, the research intensity adjusts, and the high class
buys with probability one after a success.

The quadratic cost gives a unique research response, so no equilibrium needs indifference over research. Its
marginal cost rises and the research value falls, so they meet once, at \(\lambda_R(r)\). This root moves smoothly
with \(r\), so entry inherits a strict slope.

The research parameters have an admissible region, which Proposition~\ref{prop:researchregion} in
Appendix~\ref{app:research} gives exactly at \(\beta=\reg{beta_B}\). The equilibrium patterns of Proposition~\ref{prop:research}
hold with strict margins exactly when two conditions hold. The cap lies in the strip
\((\lambda_E(r_1),\lambda_E(r_2))\approx(\reg{res_cap_lo},\reg{res_cap_hi})\), and the cost lies in a window that
depends on the cap. At the benchmark cap, this window is \((\reg{res_a_lo},\reg{res_a_up})\). With \(1+u=\eu\), the cap
\(\lb=u\beta/(1+u\beta)\) and \(a=\reg{res_a}\), these patterns hold for every \(\beta\in(\reg{res_beta_lo},1/3]\).
Entry at \(r_1\) is \(\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lb]\).

The upper end of the cap strip is the tightest hypothesis: the benchmark cap lies \(\reg{margin_res_caphi_B}\) below
it in research intensity. This margin corresponds to the posterior margin \(\reg{margin_return_post_B}\) of
Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}; the two numbers measure one boundary in different coordinates.

\begin{remark}[Tie rules with research]\label{rem:researchties}
Research never has a tie, and Proposition~\ref{prop:research} needs no investor tie rule. Under the challenger rule CM,
each intermediate strength has one more equilibrium. In it, research is \(\lambda_E(r)\), and the purchase posterior
equals the threshold. The indifferent challenger prepares with the probability that makes this research optimal. Its
entry falls across the three strengths (Table~\ref{tab:research}). Above \(r_U\), the whole tuple is unique under C1,
and under CM at every strength except the ceiling \(r_C=\reg{res_rC}\). At \(r_C\) under CM, entry can take any value
in \([\rho,\reg{res_entry_cap}]\).
\end{remark}
