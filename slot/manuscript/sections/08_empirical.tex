\section{Empirical implications}\label{sec:empirical}

The model gives predictions for publicly visible sale processes in which one bidder is prepared and a further buyer
has not yet committed to diligence. I state them for the benchmark auction at the prior \(1/2\), with a
slot in the band of Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}. Appendix~\ref{app:empirical} states each result in full and proves
it. Full two-sided trade is the unique outcome at every strength \(r\) between the sale threshold
\(r_S\) and \(h\), where \(r_S\) is the exact strength at which a full sale earns the trading cost,
\(\rho\DT(r)m(1/2)=k\). This interval contains \(r_1\), the entry ceiling \(r_C\) and \(r_2\). Under full two-sided
trade, the informed share of nonzero orders is \(u/(2+u)\).

\paragraph{Entry and incumbent strength.} When the price reaches the further buyer before its decision, a stronger
prepared bidder can raise the probability that the buyer prepares; in the benchmark, that probability rises by
\(\reg{entry_jump_B}\) from \(r_0\) to \(r_1\). When the price does not reach the buyer, a
stronger prepared bidder can only lower it. Against a strong enough prepared bidder, preparation returns to its
floor while the price stays informative. Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc} gives the first and third comparisons, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:control} the second. The control holds the information that reaches the buyer fixed: the
law of the class and of a signal that the challenger observes, the class value laws and the cost law are the
same at both strengths. Given the class, the signal tells the challenger nothing more about either
value: its own value depends on the signal only through the class, the incumbent's value is independent of the
class and the signal, and the two values are independent; the cost is independent of the class, both values
and the signal.

\paragraph{The spread and its entry component.} The purchase impact exceeds the sale impact exactly when a purchase
recruits the buyer, and this test needs no knowledge of the auction objects. The spread is the difference between the
conditional execution prices of a unit purchase and a unit sale. It has a class component, \(\rho\DT\) times the
informed share, and an entry component, which is positive exactly when a purchase recruits
(Proposition~\ref{prop:spread}). The sale impact contains only half of the class component, so the whole entry
component sits in the purchase impact.

In the benchmark at \(r_1\), the spread is \(\reg{ba_r1_B}\), and
its entry component is \(\reg{ba_share_r1_B}\) of it. With a hidden price, the spread would be
\(\reg{ba_class_r1_B}\). At \(r_2\) the spread is \(\reg{ba_r2_B}\), the class component alone, and at the entry
ceiling \(r_C\) it falls by \(\reg{ba_jump_rC_B}\).

\paragraph{The informed share.} Prices separate slots that order counts cannot. Under full two-sided trade, the
order law depends on \((\lambda,\beta)\) only through the volume of nonzero orders
(Proposition~\ref{prop:informedshare}), and the investor's incentives limit the shares that the
counts allow: at \(r_1\), the shares that the counts and the incentives allow form the interval from
\(\approx\reg{theta_min_counts_B}\) to \(\reg{theta_max_full_eq_r1_B}\).

A slot with share \(\reg{alt_theta_B}\) has the
benchmark volume and the benchmark entry, but its spread at \(r_1\) is \(\reg{alt_ba_r1_B}\), against
\(\reg{ba_r1_B}\).
Given the entry floor and the target-payoff spread, the sale impact and the volume identify the informed share.

\paragraph{Winners.} Orders predict the winner even where they no longer predict entry. In the benchmark, a prepared
challenger wins more often after a purchase than after a sale, by \(\reg{wgap_r1_B}\) at \(r_1\) and by
\(\reg{wgap_r2_B}\) at \(r_2\). The covariance of the order with entry is
\(\reg{cov_entry_r1_B}\) at \(r_1\) and zero at \(r_2\), while its covariance with a challenger win stays
positive. Among prepared challengers, the share of the high class at each price is the price-implied posterior,
which rises with the price (Proposition~\ref{prop:winner}). A prepared high-class challenger always outbids the incumbent, and a low-class one
loses whenever the incumbent's value exceeds \(\ell\).

\paragraph{Announcements.} If a public event after the price reveals only whether the challenger prepared, the mean
price change in a sample of preparation reports alone is positive: \(\reg{evprep_mean_r1_B}\) at \(r_1\) in
the benchmark (Proposition~\ref{prop:announcement}). The mean change over both reports, with their model
probabilities, is zero. Below the entry ceiling \(r_C\), an order opportunity with no order is bad news about entry
and no news about the class. At \(r_1\), the price after no order minus the expected price before the opportunity is
\(\reg{pdrop_neutral_r1_B}\).

\paragraph{Two clusters at intermediate strengths.} At one public strength in the range \((r_F,r_N)\) of
Section~\ref{sec:coexistence}, processes can form two clusters.
In one cluster there is no informed trade, the mean order is zero and the three prices coincide. In the other,
the high class buys and the low class abstains; the mean order is then \(\lambda/2\) (\(\reg{imb_buy_B}\) in
the benchmark), every challenger prepares after a purchase, and a sale executes above the price after no order,
by \(\reg{U_gap_r160_B}\) at \(\reg{str_r160}\).

\paragraph{Measurement.} The counterpart of preparation is the start of substantive diligence or a proposal that
requires costly preparation; public offers are a narrower outcome. Incumbent strength is measured from information
that is public before that decision, and the price must precede it.

The order is the first prespecified public order opportunity after the sale becomes public, and an opportunity with
no trade is recorded as a zero. A scheduled call auction is one example, such as the opening auction on the first
trading day after the approach is disclosed; its time is fixed in advance and does not depend on any trader's
information, and the auction takes place whether or not an order arrives. The sign of its net order imbalance is the
order, and an imbalance below a size fixed in advance records the zero order. A sample of first executed trades omits
the zero order, so it cannot identify the volume, the effect of an inactive opportunity, or the informed share.

The announcement formulas need events that reveal only preparation. Target returns before a bid also
partly reflect anticipation of that bid \citep{Schwert1996}, so each test dates the price before the buyer's decision. The trading counterpart is outside trade with a view
on the further buyer's fit, such as flow from sector specialists.
