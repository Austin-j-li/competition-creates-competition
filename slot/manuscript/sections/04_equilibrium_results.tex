\section{Equilibrium results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Competition creates competition}\label{sec:ccc}

The main result compares three economies that differ only in incumbent strength. In the first, the price carries
no information and only a zero-cost challenger prepares. In the second, the price is informative and a purchase
recruits a high-cost challenger. In the third, the price stays informative and entry returns to its floor. Write
\(F_0\), \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) for the three incumbent distributions.
% STRUCTURE: the shorthand B_i, Delta_i, M, m, kappa and the point masses delta_q -> written out in Proposition 2 and below

\begin{proposition}[Competition creates competition; analytical]\label{prop:ccc}
Let the classes satisfy the class ranking~\eqref{eq:classes}. Let \(F_1\) be a continuous first-order stochastic
strengthening of the continuous incumbent distribution \(F_0\), and let \(F_2\) strengthen \(F_1\) in the same sense. Fix
\(0<\pi<1\), \(0<\rho<1\), \(k>0\) and a slot \((\lambda,\beta)\). Suppose
\begin{align*}
&\text{(H1)}\qquad B_{F_0}(\pi)<c_H<B_{F_1}(M(\pi)),\\
&\text{(H2)}\qquad \DT(F_0)<k<\rho\,\kappa(\pi)\,\DT(F_1),\\
&\text{(H3)}\qquad B_{F_2}(M(\pi))<c_H.
\end{align*}
Then each economy has a unique equilibrium outcome.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Weak incumbent \(F_0\).} The investor does not trade: both classes abstain with probability one.
Every posterior equals \(\pi\), entry equals \(\rho\) after every order, and every order has the price
\(t_0+\rho\,[t_L-t_0+\DT(F_0)\pi]\). The price carries no information.
\item \emph{Strong incumbent \(F_1\).} The investor trades in both directions: class \(H\) buys and class \(L\)
sells, each with probability one. The posteriors after the orders \((-1,0,1)\) are \((m(\pi),\pi,M(\pi))\). Only a
purchase recruits
the high-cost challenger, so entry after the three orders is \((\rho,\rho,1)\), and \(P(-1)<P(0)<P(1)\). Entry is
\[
\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\pi\lambda)>\rho.
\]
Entry given class \(H\) is \(\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)\). High-class ownership rises from \(\pi\rho W_H(F_0)\)
to \(\pi[\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)]W_H(F_1)\) if and only if
\[
[\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)]\,W_H(F_1)>\rho\,W_H(F_0).
\]
\item \emph{Very strong incumbent \(F_2\).} The investor again trades in both directions, with posteriors
\((m(\pi),\pi,M(\pi))\). Entry equals \(\rho\) after every order. The three prices differ, so the price stays informative
while entry returns to \(\rho\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Every investor comparison in the proof is strict, and every equilibrium posterior lies strictly on one side of the
threshold. No tie rule is therefore used, and the same unique outcome holds under all four pairs of tie rules.
Appendix~\ref{app:prop2} states the three equilibrium tuples and checks each condition of
Definition~\ref{def:equilibrium} against them.

The hypotheses fix trading before any order profile is known. By the trading bound of Section~\ref{sec:informed},
every correct order earns at most \(\DT(F_0)-k<0\) in the weak economy and at least \(\rho\kappa(\pi)\DT(F_1)-k>0\) in
the strong one. The two
sides of (H1) set entry. Gross profit at the prior falls short of the high cost at \(F_0\), and gross profit at the
top posterior exceeds it at \(F_1\). Hypothesis (H3) stops recruitment at \(F_2\) (the ceiling of Section~\ref{sec:inference}).
Appendix~\ref{app:prop2} shows that the proof uses each hypothesis once and that (H1) cannot be weakened to
\(B_{F_1}(\pi)<c_H\).

The trading window in (H2) is nonempty exactly when \(\DT(F_0)/\DT(F_1)<\rho\kappa(\pi)\). This condition involves the
two incumbent distributions, the classes, \(\rho\), the slot and \(\pi\), and no unit of money. A prior away from
one half narrows the window, since \(\kappa(\pi)\) falls. For uniform incumbents, (H1)--(H3) are strict
inequalities between continuous functions of \((r_0,r_1,r_2,c_H,k,\rho,\pi,\lambda,\beta)\). They define an open
set. Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} gives its slice through the benchmark.

The additional entry favors the high class: class \(H\) places a purchase with probability \(b+\lambda\), class
\(L\) with probability \(b\). Ownership needs its own condition when the high class can lose to the incumbent,
because a stronger incumbent then lowers \(W_H\). The condition in part~(ii) holds whenever
\((1+\rho)W_H(F_1)\ge2\rho W_H(F_0)>0\) and \(b+\lambda>1/2\). In the point-mass benchmark \(W_H=1\), so ownership
rises.

Part~(iii) completes a rise and a later fall of entry with incumbent strength. Against the very strong incumbent,
even the top posterior fails to justify high-cost preparation, while trading stays informative.
Section~\ref{sec:coexistence} describes the strengths between the three economies.

\subsection{The benchmark and the fixed-information control}\label{sec:benchmark}

The benchmark, which I call \(B\), has point-mass classes \(h=\reg{prim_h}\) and \(\ell=\reg{prim_ell}\) and the
prior \(\pi=\reg{prim_pi}\). The reserve is \(p=\reg{prim_p}\), the zero-cost probability is \(\rho=\reg{prim_rho}\),
the high cost is \(c_H=\reg{prim_cH}\), and the trading cost is \(k=\reg{prim_k}\). The incumbent is uniform on
\([0,r]\), at the strengths \(r_0=\reg{prim_r0}\), \(r_1=\reg{prim_r1}\) and \(r_2=\reg{prim_r2}\). Money is
measured in units of the low value.

The slot has two primitives. The largest likelihood ratio of an order is \(1+u=\eu\), where \(\eu\) is Euler's
number; I print it upright to separate it from entry \(e(x)\). The liquidity probability is \(\beta=\reg{beta_B}\), an exact decimal.
The presence probability follows: \(\lambda=u\beta/(1+u\beta)=\reg{lambda_B}\), and \(b=\reg{b_B}\). Every
computation keeps \(\lambda\) unrounded, and every statement that depends on \(\eu\) uses interval arithmetic. The
posterior bounds at the prior are \(M(1/2)=\eu/(1+\eu)=\reg{M_half_B}\) and \(m(1/2)=\reg{m_half_B}\).
Appendix~\ref{app:parameters} lists these declarations.

The admissible region is a result. Proposition~\ref{prop:region} in Appendix~\ref{app:prop2} gives it exactly at the
benchmark auction. At the prior \(1/2\), (H1)--(H3) hold if and only if \(\reg{u_lo_frac}<u<\reg{u_hi_frac}\).

\(\tau(r_2)=\reg{M_hi_frac}\). With \(1+u=\eu\), the hypotheses hold exactly for priors between

the benchmark point and its distance to each boundary.

The band is exact. Below it a purchase at \(r_1\) does not recruit, and above it a conclusion of

still hold under rule C1, but under rule CM entry at \(r_1\) can stay at \(\rho\).

Both ends of the band are preparation thresholds. The lower end \(\tau(r_1)\) is the information that a purchase
needs to recruit against the strong incumbent. The upper end \(\tau(r_2)\) is the information at which a purchase
would still recruit against the very strong incumbent. The band is narrow because the threshold rises slowly with
strength. A high-class challenger outbids every incumbent on the path, and its expected payment
\(r/2+p^2/(2r)\) rises by less than one half per unit of strength. This change is small next to the gap \(g_H-g_L\)
in Table~\ref{tab:payoffs}.

The upper margin is the smallest margin of Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc} in cost units, and it measures one distance in three units. The benchmark top posterior lies
\(\reg{margin_return_post_B}\) below \(\tau(r_2)\). In cost units, \(c_H-B_{r_2}(M(1/2))=\reg{margin_return_B}\), and
\(r_2\) lies \(\reg{gap_r2_rC_B}\) above the ceiling \(r_C=\reg{rC_B}\), the strength at which \(\tau(r)=M(1/2)\).

Given the auction primitives, the margin depends only on the top posterior and on the place of \(r_2\). Gross
profit at every belief falls strictly with strength above \(\ell\), so (H3) holds at every strength in \((r_C,h)\).
Parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc} do not use (H3). Table~\ref{tab:margins} in
Appendix~\ref{app:parameters} lists every hypothesis with its positive margin at the benchmark.

Two controls show where the reversal comes from. The first holds the information in the price fixed while the

can recruit, because the threshold \(\tau(r)\) exceeds the prior at every strength. Entry is therefore
\(\reg{p4_frozen_r0_entry}\) at every strength up to the ceiling \(r_C=\reg{rC_B}\), and \(\rho\) above it. From

\subsection{Coexistence}\label{sec:coexistence}

Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc} compares \(r_0\) with \(r_1\). Between them, informative and uninformative equilibria
coexist. This section describes the complete equilibrium set along the benchmark path \(r\in[r_0,r_2]\) at the prior
\(1/2\), for every slot in the band of Proposition~\ref{prop:region}(a); Appendix~\ref{app:coexistence} lists it in
full.

% STRUCTURE: the functions J and fr, the table of six strengths, the shorthand M, m, s_+, s_- and the table of the five forms -> Appendix A.4 (already stated there)
Five strengths organize the path. At \(r_A\), a purchase that just reaches the threshold and recruits earns exactly
the trading cost; at \(r_F\), a full purchase that recruits earns it; at \(r_N\), a unit order against an
uninformative price earns it, so no trade stops being an equilibrium above \(r_N\); at \(r_J\), a purchase that just
reaches the threshold without recruitment earns it; and at \(r_S\), a full sale earns it. The strengths \(r_A\),
\(r_N\) and \(r_J\) do not depend on the slot. Figure~\ref{fig:coexistence} labels the equilibrium forms N, R, F, Q
and T, and Appendix~\ref{app:coexistence} defines each of them.

% STRUCTURE: the threshold order, the table of the equilibrium set and the entry of each form -> Appendix A.4, full statement of Proposition 3
\begin{proposition}[Coexistence on the benchmark path; computer-assisted]\label{prop:coexistence}
Take the benchmark auction, the prior \(1/2\) and any slot in the band of Proposition~\ref{prop:region}(a). An
equilibrium exists at every strength on the path from \(r_0\) to \(r_2\), and Appendix~\ref{app:coexistence} lists
the whole equilibrium set.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item No informed trade and an informative equilibrium coexist exactly on \([r_A,r_N]\).
\item On the informative branch that coexists with no trade, entry is
\(\rho+(1-\rho)\min\{b\DT(r)/(2k),\,b+\lambda/2\}\). It is continuous, rises strictly on \([r_A,r_F]\), and is
constant on \([r_F,r_N]\). It keeps this value along full purchases up to \(r_C\) and equals \(\rho\) above
\(r_C\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Two monotonicity steps of the proof rest on exact Sturm counts; every other step is analytical
(Appendix~\ref{app:coexistence}). At the benchmark slot,
\(r_A=\reg{rA}\), \(r_F=\reg{rF_B}\), \(r_N=\reg{rN}\), \(r_J=\reg{rJ}\), \(r_S=\reg{rS_B}\) and
\(r_C=\reg{rC_B}\). On R, entry is \(\reg{entry_rA_B}\) at \(r_A\), \(\reg{entry_mb_r140_B}\) at
\(\reg{str_r140}\), and \(\reg{entry_mb_r145_B}\) at \(\reg{str_r145}\). From \(r_F\) it stays at
\(\reg{entry_r1_B}\) until \(r_C\). The low class abstains up to \(r_N\), mixes on \((r_N,r_S)\), and sells with
probability one from \(r_S\).

Figure~\ref{fig:coexistence} shows entry along the path. Against an uninformative price with entry \(\rho\), a
unit order earns \(\rho\DT/2\), which stays below \(k\) up to \(r_N\), so no trade survives until \(r_N\). An
informative equilibrium changes the entry response and with it the return to trade, so it can hold at the same
strengths.

Entry rises strictly along R because the purchase rate adjusts. At \(r_A\) a purchase that just reaches the
threshold earns exactly \(k\). Above \(r_A\), class \(H\) mixes, and the purchase posterior stays above the
threshold. The purchase then earns \(\DT[1-\mu(1)]=k\), so a larger spread needs a more informative purchase. The
purchase rate \(b\,[\DT(r)/k-2]\) therefore rises with \(r\), and entry rises with it. At \(r_F\) the purchase
rate reaches \(\lambda\), and from there entry is flat. Entry therefore rises weakly along the informative branch,
and strictly where the investor mixes.

Above \(r_N\), no trade fails. On \((r_N,r_J)\) the investor can also mix in both directions without recruitment
(Q), and from \(r_J\) on, full purchases are the only high-class choice.

Coexistence also holds away from the even prior. At the benchmark slot, the informative branch starts at the same
strength \(r_A\) for every prior between \(\approx\reg{piA_lo_B}\) and \(\approx\reg{piA_hi}\), and later, where the
prior reaches the threshold, for priors up to \(\approx\reg{pimax}\); below the first prior even the top posterior
cannot reach the threshold once a purchase can pay for itself, and from the last prior on the uninformative
equilibrium fails before the prior falls below the threshold. Corollary~\ref{prop:priors} in
Appendix~\ref{app:coexistence} gives the coexistence set at every prior.
