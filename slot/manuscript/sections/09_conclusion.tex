\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The bidder pool in a takeover depends on the information that buyers have before they commit to preparation. A
stronger incumbent moves the class value gap from the challenger's profit to target proceeds, and because a
liquidity order can match the investor's direction in the order slot, an investor who knows the challenger's
class keeps a residual advantage after every order. A larger target-payoff spread can make that advantage worth
the cost of trade, so the price recruits a challenger that a weaker incumbent could not attract.

The comparison of the weak, strong and very strong incumbent holds with unique outcomes on an open set of
parameters. Between the two economies, informative
and uninformative equilibria coexist, and along the informative branch entry rises with incumbent strength
while the investor mixes between a purchase and abstention, and it stays constant once the high class buys
for sure. When
information is costly, competition creates research: research and entry rise together over a range of strengths,
on a branch that coexists with an equilibrium without research.

Sale terms affect entry through the buyer's expected payment and through the information in the stock price. In
the recruitment region, access to prices raises target proceeds and acquisition surplus net of preparation
costs, and the weight that the payment rule places on the runner-up's value decides how competition moves the
target-payoff spread. A reserve that excludes the low class can make the price informative against both
incumbents; if informed trade is then full and only a purchase recruits at both strengths, entry is flat in incumbent strength.

The next theoretical step is the seller's choice of terms with the whole pricing and trading continuation
retained. The empirical tests ask whether further preparation in visible sale processes responds to incumbent
strength when the price precedes the decision, and whether purchase impacts carry the entry component that
Section~\ref{sec:empirical} predicts.
