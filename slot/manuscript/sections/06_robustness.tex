\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}

This section varies one part of the model at a time. It changes the cost law, the value classes and the prior, and
the challenger's information. Appendix~\ref{app:robustness} states each result in full, with its status, and
proves it.

\subsection{Preparation costs}\label{sec:costs}

The two-point cost law of Section~\ref{sec:values} is not needed. Let the cost mix a low component \(H^0\) on
\([0,L]\), with weight \(\rho\), and a high component \(H^1\) on \([c_-,c_+]\). Either component may be atomless or
have atoms. Proposition~\ref{prop:atomless} keeps parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc} under (H2),
\(L<B_{F_1}(m(\pi))\) and \(B_{F_0}(\pi)<c_-<c_+<B_{F_1}(M(\pi))\). The low component then prepares at every feasible belief, so entry stays
at least \(\rho\), and the trading bounds of Section~\ref{sec:informed} apply unchanged. Every entry comparison is
strict, so the outcome is the same under every pair of tie rules.

In the benchmark, take \(L=\reg{atom_L}\) and a high component uniform on \([c_H-\varepsilon,c_H+\varepsilon]\). With
\(\varepsilon=\reg{atom_eps}\), entry at \(r_1\) is \(\reg{p4_atomless_r1_entry}\), the value of the two-point law. The
return to the floor needs a narrower band. At \(r_2\), gross profit at the top posterior lies \(\reg{margin_return_B}\)
below \(c_H\), inside this band, and entry at \(r_2\) is \(\reg{p4_atomless_r2_entry}\). Every half-width below
\(\reg{p4_atomless_max_width_floor_r2}\) keeps all three parts of Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc}. An atomless high
component turns the drop at the ceiling into a ramp: full-order entry
\(\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\pi\lambda)\,H^1\bigl(B_r(M(\pi))\bigr)\) is continuous and nonincreasing in strength.

\subsection{Value classes and priors}\label{sec:classes}

The reversal holds for every pair of ranked classes and every prior. Take classes that satisfy
the class ranking~\eqref{eq:classes} with different means, and any reserve, prior, zero-cost probability and slot. For uniform
incumbents, a nonempty open set of strengths and costs then satisfies (H1)--(H3) and the ownership condition of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ccc} (Proposition~\ref{prop:classes}).

The construction puts the weak and the strong incumbent
just above the lowest value \(v_*\) at which the classes start to differ above the reserve. The spread vanishes near
\(v_*\), so informed trade against the weaker incumbent cannot cover its cost. The profit gap stays near the full class
gap there, so a high cost that only a favorable price justifies fits inside it. In the point-mass case
\(v_*=\ell\), so the reversal holds for every \(h>\ell\).

Table~\ref{tab:classes} in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness} reports six economies with their own costs and strengths.
A prior away from one half needs its own costs. With the benchmark costs and strengths, a prior
of \(\reg{p2_input_ex_a_pi}\) leaves entry at \(\reg{p2_fixed_prior_three_tenths_r1_entry}\) at all three strengths. A
prior of \(\reg{p2_input_ex_b_pi}\) recruits every challenger at \(r_0\) already.

Dispersion within a class also matters. With wide uniform classes, entry rises from \(\reg{p2_ex_c_r0_entry}\) to \(\reg{p2_ex_c_r1_entry}\), and
high-class ownership rises although the high class can lose to the strong incumbent. Point masses at the same means
leave entry at \(\reg{p2_ex_d_r1_entry}\). A prepared challenger keeps \((\theta-\max\{p,R\})_+\), a convex function of
its value, so a mean-preserving spread within a class weakly raises gross profit at every belief.

With more than two classes, the result holds under a separation condition (Proposition~\ref{prop:manyclasses}). The
price reveals only the posterior mean of class proceeds. Separation asks for a gap between the proceeds of each
high-group class and every feasible posterior mean, and the same gap for the low group. In the strong equilibrium,
class proportions within each group then stay at their prior proportions, so the price reveals the whole posterior
vector.

Without separation, a strict three-class equilibrium has two orders with different posterior vectors and one
price. This collision is a knife-edge in the class laws. A four-class economy near the benchmark
satisfies separation with distance \(\reg{p2_groups_gamma}\).

\subsection{Complementary private signals}\label{sec:signals}

The challenger can gain from the price even when its own information is more accurate than the investor's. A buyer
may know its integration plans, while investors who follow the target know its customers or its product market.
Proposition~\ref{prop:signals} represents the two sources with conditionally independent binary signals. The
investor's signal has accuracy \(a_T\), and the challenger's signal has accuracy \(d\).

The zero-cost challenger still
prepares after every price and signal, so the price still reveals the public posterior. The challenger combines that
posterior with its own signal. Under strict conditions on the costs, the weak incumbent has zero orders and entry
\(\rho\). The strong incumbent has full orders and entry above \(\rho\). The conditions allow \(d>a_T\).

The example uses its own parameter vector \(S(B)\). It keeps the slot, the values, the reserve and the prior of \(B\),
and it sets \(\rho=\reg{sig_rho}\), \(c_H=\reg{sig_cH}\), \(k=\reg{sig_k}\), \(r_0=\reg{sig_r0}\) and
\(r_1=\reg{sig_r1}\). With investor accuracy \(a_T=\reg{sig_aT}\) and challenger accuracy \(d=\reg{sig_d}\), entry
rises from \(\reg{p4_s_signal_r0_entry}\) to \(\reg{p4_s_signal_r1_entry}\). A favorable signal
alone does not justify high-cost preparation against the weak incumbent. A favorable signal and a favorable price
together do so against the strong incumbent. Both sources are needed: without the challenger's signal, entry is
\(\reg{p4_s_no_y_r0_entry}\) at both strengths.

At the benchmark \(B\), the conditions cannot hold with \(d\ge a_T\): the strong trading bound needs an accurate
investor signal, and then the weak bound fails. They hold at \(B\) with \((a_T,d)=(\reg{sig_comp_aT},\reg{sig_comp_d})\).
Entry then rises from \(\reg{p4_comp_b_r0_entry}\) to \(\reg{p4_comp_b_r1_entry}\), and without the challenger's signal
it is \(\reg{p4_comp_b_no_y_r1_entry}\) at both strengths.

With the accuracies of \(S(B)\), the benchmark \(B\) has a unique equilibrium at each strength under rule IM. At \(r_0\), the investor mixes between trade
and abstention after either signal, and it trades with probability \(\reg{p4_b_signal_r0_buy_rate}\). At \(r_1\), it
buys with probability one after a favorable signal, and after an unfavorable signal it sells with probability
\(\reg{p4_b_signal_r1_sell_rate}\). Entry falls from \(\reg{p4_b_signal_r0_entry}\) to \(\reg{p4_b_signal_r1_entry}\).
Under rule I0, neither strength has an equilibrium.
