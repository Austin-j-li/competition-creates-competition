\noindent
This note sets two trading stages for the model of \emph{Competition Creates Competition} side by side. In the
order slot, the trading stage of the paper, an informed trader and a liquidity trader are alternative arrivals in
one public order opportunity. In the Laplace set-up, the informed trader chooses a continuous order and Laplace noise hides
it; this is the trading stage of the manuscript of 22 October 2026 with the same title. The auction, the preparation
cost and the decision of the potential rival bidder
are the same in both. The Laplace manuscript calls the rival the challenger and the informed trader the investor.
Section~\ref{sec:setups} states the two set-ups,
Section~\ref{sec:results} sets out what each delivers, result by result, and Section~\ref{sec:differ} names the
features of each trading stage that the differences come from.

Propositions 1 to 4, Corollaries 1 to 3, Lemmas A.1 to A.5, Propositions A.1 to A.13, Corollary A.2 and the tables
are those of the paper. A number with the prefix L refers to the Laplace manuscript: Proposition~L-2 is its
Proposition~2, and Proposition~L-A.5 its Proposition~A.5. Strengths that belong to the Laplace set-up carry the
superscript \(\mathrm{Lap}\), and the Laplace noise scale is \(\xi\).

\section{The two set-ups}\label{sec:setups}

\paragraph{Shared primitives.} One bidder, the incumbent bidder, or the incumbent, is already prepared. A second
bidder, the potential rival bidder, or the rival, has a class \(j\in\{H,L\}\), with prior \(\pi=\Pr(j=H)\), and a
value \(\theta\sim G_j\). The classes satisfy the class ranking: \(G_H\) dominates \(G_L\), and both supports lie above
the reserve \(p\). The incumbent's value is \(R\sim F\). The rival's preparation cost is zero with probability
\(\rho\) and \(c_H\) otherwise, and prepared bidders bid their values in a second-price auction with reserve \(p\).
The auction gives the proceeds \(t_0\) without entry and \(t_j\) after entry in class \(j\), the gross profits
\(g_j\), the target-payoff spread \(\DT=t_H-t_L\), gross profit \(B(\mu)=g_L+\mu(g_H-g_L)\) at the belief \(\mu\), and
the preparation threshold \(\tau=(c_H-g_L)/(g_H-g_L)\). Entry \(\Ent\) is the probability that the rival
prepares, and high-class ownership \(\Own_H\) the probability that a class-\(H\) rival acquires the target.

In the example the classes are point masses at \(h=\reg{prim_h}\) and \(\ell=\reg{prim_ell}\), the incumbent is
uniform on \([0,r]\), and \((p,\rho,c_H,k)=(\reg{prim_p},\reg{prim_rho},\reg{prim_cH},\reg{prim_k})\), with prior
\(\reg{prim_pi}\) and the strengths \(r_0=\reg{prim_r0}\), \(r_1=\reg{prim_r1}\) and \(r_2=\reg{prim_r2}\). Both
set-ups use this example.

\paragraph{The order slot.} One public order opportunity comes before the rival's decision. With probability
\(\lambda\in(0,1)\) the informed trader fills it: it sees the class and chooses \(q\in\{-1,0,1\}\), and a nonzero
order costs \(k\). With probability \(1-\lambda\) a liquidity trader fills it and sells, abstains or buys one unit
with probabilities \(\beta\), \(1-2\beta\) and \(\beta\), where \(0<\beta\le1/3\), for reasons apart from the sale. No one observes who filled
the slot. Competitive market makers see the order \(X\in\{-1,0,1\}\) and set \(P=\E[V_T\mid X]\), where \(V_T\) is the terminal
payoff of a target share. The device is the alternative-arrival structure of \citet{DowGorton1997}, in which an
informed order and a liquidity order of the same size are alternative arrivals in one opportunity; the unit menu,
the presence probability and the unit cost follow \citet{EdmansGoldsteinJiang2015}. The liquidity mass in one
direction is \(b=(1-\lambda)\beta\), and \(1+u\), with \(u=\lambda/b\), is the largest likelihood ratio that one order
can carry. In the example \(1+u=\eu\) and \(\beta=\reg{beta_B}\), so \(\lambda=\reg{lambda_B}\) and \(b=\reg{b_B}\).
Section~5 of the paper adds a research stage in which the informed trader chooses \(\lambda\) at a cost.

\paragraph{The Laplace set-up.} The informed trader observes the class and submits an order \(q\in[-1,1]\) at the cost
\(k|q|\). Noise demand has the Laplace density \(\eu^{-|z|/\xi}/(2\xi)\) with scale \(\xi>1\). Market makers see the
flow, the sum of the order and the noise, and set its price at the expected terminal payoff. The likelihood ratio of
a flow lies in \([\eu^{-2/\xi},\eu^{2/\xi}]\). In the example \(\xi=\reg{lap_b}\), so \(\eu^{2/\xi}=\eu\), the slot's
likelihood-ratio bound.

With \(1+u=\eu^{2/\xi}\) the two set-ups have the same posterior bounds,
\(M(\pi)=\pi(1+u)/[\pi(1+u)+1-\pi]\) and \(m(\pi)=\pi/[\pi+(1-\pi)(1+u)]\), and in the example
\(M(1/2)=\eu/(1+\eu)=\reg{M_half_B}\) and \(m(1/2)=\reg{m_half_B}\) in both. Every order or flow has positive
probability or density in both classes, so every price is on path and neither set-up needs an off-path belief. The
zero-cost rival prepares after every price, so entry is at least \(\rho\), and the gross return of a correct unit
order lies between \(\rho\kappa(\pi)\DT\) and \(\DT\), with \(\kappa(\pi)=\min\{m(\pi),1-M(\pi)\}\), in both
(Lemma~A.3; equation L-11). The informed trader's problem differs: in the slot each class compares one gross return with
\(k\), while in the Laplace set-up a score bound, \(|f'|\le f/\xi\) for the noise density \(f\), shows that a
correctly signed order of size \(s\) has marginal profit at least \((1-s/\xi)\) times its gross return minus \(k\).
The slot's trading bound therefore carries no factor \(1-1/\xi\).

\paragraph{Beliefs and prices.} The posterior bounds come from the liquidity trader in the slot and from the score
bound of the noise in the Laplace set-up. In the slot the bound after a nonzero order holds for every \(\beta<1/2\),
and the bound after the zero order uses \(\beta\le1/3\). Both set-ups read the posterior from the price: in the slot,
two orders with one price have the same entry and therefore the same posterior (Lemma~A.2); in the Laplace set-up, the
rival inverts the identity \(P=t_0+e(P)[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu]\) through the entry floor (Proposition~L-A.2). When
\(\DT>0\) and \(g_H>g_L\), the auction enters the slot's orders, posteriors, entry and research only through \(\DT\) and \(\tau\) (Lemma~A.4), and a
stronger incumbent never narrows the spread and, while \(g_L\le c_H\), never lowers the threshold (Lemma~A.5).

\paragraph{Proposition 1.} The result concerns the auction alone, so it is the same in both set-ups: a stronger
incumbent moves the class value gap from the rival's profit to target proceeds.

\paragraph{Proposition 2.} The three economies have the same outcomes in both set-ups: a quiet market against the
weak incumbent, full orders that recruit after a purchase against the strong incumbent, and full orders with entry at
its floor against the very strong incumbent. In the example, entry is \(\reg{entry_r0}\), \(\reg{entry_r1_B}\) and
\(\reg{entry_r2}\) at \(r_0\), \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) in both. High-class ownership is \(\reg{own_r0}\),
\(\reg{own_r1_B}\) and \(\reg{own_r2_B}\) in the slot, and \(\reg{own_r0}\), \(\reg{lap_own_r1}\) and
\(\reg{own_r2_B}\) in the Laplace set-up. Uniqueness in the Laplace set-up covers mixed orders on a continuum of
sizes and every unilateral deviation in \([-1,1]\); in the slot it covers mixtures over three orders and holds under
all four tie rules. In the slot, Proposition~2 also gives the class entry rates \(e_H=\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)\) and
\(e_L=\rho+(1-\rho)b\), the exact ownership and takeover-share conditions, and outbidding from a decisive advantage of
class \(H\); in the example class \(H\)'s takeover share rises from \(\reg{share_H_r0}\) to \(\reg{share_H_r1_B}\).

With the same posterior bounds, every margin that involves only the auction and the top posterior is the same number
in the two set-ups: the cost window \((\reg{lap_cH_lo},\reg{lap_cH_hi})\), the prior window
\((\reg{prior_lo_B},\reg{prior_hi_B})\) for all three parts and the entry ceiling \(r_C\approx\reg{rC_B}\), at which
\(\tau(r_C)=M(1/2)\). The prior window for parts (i) and (ii) reaches \(\reg{prior_ii_hi}\) in the slot and
\(\reg{lap_prior_ii_hi}\) in the Laplace set-up. In the slot the region in the example is an exact band for \(u\)
(Corollary~1).

\paragraph{Fixed information.} Both set-ups hold the information in the price fixed and let the rival decide
again: a stronger incumbent then lowers gross profit at every belief, so entry cannot rise (Proposition~A.2;
Proposition~L-A.3). In the slot, Proposition~A.2 covers every experiment whose signal is independent of both values
given the class, and every cost law independent of the class, the values and the signal. With full orders held fixed,
the slot's frozen posteriors are three atoms \(m<1/2<M\), and \(\tau(r)\) exceeds the prior at every strength, so only
the top atom recruits and entry is a step function of strength: \(\reg{p4_frozen_r0_entry}\) up to \(r_C\) and
\(\rho\) above it. In the Laplace set-up the flow at which the posterior reaches \(\tau\) moves continuously with
strength, so the probability of crossing it has a density, and entry falls strictly, from
\(\reg{lap_frozen_r0_entry}\) to \(\reg{lap_frozen_r1_entry}\).

\paragraph{Proposition 3.} In the slot, Proposition~3 gives coexistence of a quiet and an informative market at every
incumbent law, in terms of the two numbers \(\DT\) and \(\tau\). In the example at the prior \(1/2\), Corollary~2 lists
every equilibrium on \([r_0,r_2]\) for every slot in the band of Corollary~1. Coexistence holds exactly on
\([r_A,r_N]\approx[\reg{rA},\reg{rN}]\). In the informative market class \(H\) buys at the rate \(b[\DT(r)/k-2]\) up to
\(r_F\approx\reg{rF_B}\), so entry rises strictly from \(\reg{entry_rA_B}\) at \(r_A\) through \(\reg{entry_mb_r145_B}\) at
\(\reg{str_r145}\) to \(\reg{entry_r1_B}\) at \(r_F\), and keeps that value up to \(r_C\). The low class abstains up to
\(r_N\), mixes on \((r_N,r_S)\) and sells for sure from \(r_S\approx\reg{rS_B}\). When an indifferent high-cost rival
may prepare with any probability, the threshold pair is also an equilibrium on \((r_A,r_J]\), with
\(r_J\approx\reg{rJ}\), and its entry falls strictly from \(\reg{entry_rA_B}\) to \(\rho\) (Table~A.3). At other priors
(Corollary~A.2), coexistence starts at \(r_A\) for priors between \(\approx\reg{piA_lo_B}\) and \(\approx\reg{piA_hi}\), starts where
the prior reaches the threshold for priors up to \(\approx\reg{pimax}\), and is empty below \(\approx\reg{piA_lo_B}\) and from
\(\approx\reg{pimax}\) on.

In the Laplace set-up a quiet market is an equilibrium exactly for \(r\le r_N\Lap\approx\reg{lap_rN}\), the slot's \(r_N\):
against a price that carries no information, an order of size \(s\) earns \(s(\rho\DT/2-k)\) at the prior \(1/2\) in
both set-ups. The quiet market is the unique equilibrium below \(\approx\reg{lap_r_unique_zero}\), where \(\DT=k\), and full
orders are unique above \(r_U\Lap\approx\reg{lap_rU}\) (Proposition~L-A.4). Between these strengths, Proposition~L-3 gives,
at \(\reg{str_r155}\), \(\reg{str_r160}\) and \(\reg{str_r165}\), an equilibrium in which class \(H\) buys one unit and
class \(L\) sells \(\reg{lap_P3_v_r155}\), \(\reg{lap_P3_v_r160}\) and \(\reg{lap_P3_v_r165}\) of a unit, each beside
the quiet market. Entry in these equilibria rises strictly, from \(\reg{lap_P3_entry_r155}\) through
\(\reg{lap_P3_entry_r160}\) to \(\reg{lap_P3_entry_r165}\). In the slot, at the same three strengths, entry in the
informative market is \(\reg{entry_r1_B}\) beside the quiet market.

\paragraph{Proposition 4.} Only the slot has a research stage. Before it learns the class, the informed trader chooses an
intensity \(\lambda\in[0,\bar\lambda]\) at the cost \((a/2)\lambda^2\), and the intensity is the presence probability.
At the prior \(1/2\), when an indifferent informed trader may mix and an indifferent high-cost rival prepares,
recruiting research solves \(2a\lambda=\DT m_\lambda-k+(\rho\DT m_\lambda-k)^+\), capped at
\(\bar\lambda\), and research and entry rise with the spread up to the cap (Proposition~4). In the example, with
\(a=\reg{res_a}\) and \(\bar\lambda\approx\reg{res_cap}\) (Corollary~3), zero research is an equilibrium exactly on
\([r_0,r_N]\). Recruiting research is an equilibrium exactly on \([r_R,r_C]\), with \(r_R\approx\reg{res_rR}\): below the cap
up to \(r_{\bar\lambda}\approx\reg{res_rcap}\) and at the cap from there, with entry rising from \(\reg{res_entry_rR}\) to
\(\reg{res_entry_cap}\). Floor-entry research is an equilibrium exactly on \((r_N,r_Q)\), with \(r_Q\approx\reg{res_rQ}\),
and on \((r_C,r_2]\), and there is no other equilibrium. At \(\reg{str_r155}\), \(\reg{str_r160}\) and
\(\reg{str_r165}\), entry with recruiting research is \(\reg{res_entry_r155}\), \(\reg{res_entry_r160}\) and
\(\reg{res_entry_r165}\). Proposition~A.1 gives the cap strip \((\reg{res_cap_lo},\reg{res_cap_hi})\) and, at the
example's cap, the cost window \((\reg{res_a_lo},\reg{res_a_up})\) on which this pattern holds at the example's
strengths.

\paragraph{Robustness.} The cost-law results keep parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition~2 in both set-ups. In the slot the
low and the high cost components can have atoms (Proposition~A.3); in the example every half-width of a uniform high
component below \(c_H-B_{F_2}(M)\approx\reg{p4_atomless_max_width_floor_r2}\) keeps all three parts, and at half-width \(\reg{atom_eps}\)
entry is \(\reg{p4_atomless_r1_entry}\) at \(r_1\) and \(\reg{p4_atomless_r2_entry}\) at \(r_2\). With atomless costs
of half-width \(\reg{atom_eps}\), Laplace entry at \(r_1\) is \(\reg{lap_mixture_entry_r1}\) (Proposition~L-A.6). For
value classes, both set-ups give a nonempty open set of strengths and costs for every ranked pair. The slot's
Proposition~A.4 holds at every reserve, prior, zero-cost probability and slot, and Table~A.4 gives six economies with
their own costs and strengths; in Table~L-4 of the Laplace manuscript, cases A to C meet (A1), and case D has margin
\(\reg{lap_caseD_A1_upper}\) in (A1). With many classes, separation splits the classes into a high and a low group in
both set-ups (Proposition~A.5; Online Appendix~L-A.13). In the slot, without separation, a strict three-class
equilibrium has two orders with different posterior vectors and one price. With complementary private signals, the
signal example raises entry from \(\reg{p4_s_signal_r0_entry}\) to \(\reg{p4_s_signal_r1_entry}\) in the slot
(Proposition~A.6) and from \(\reg{lap_signal_entry_r0}\) to \(\reg{lap_signal_entry_r1}\) in the Laplace set-up
(Proposition~L-A.7). In the slot's example, the accuracies \((\reg{sig_comp_aT},\reg{sig_comp_d})\) raise entry from
\(\reg{p4_comp_b_r0_entry}\) to \(\reg{p4_comp_b_r1_entry}\).

\paragraph{Welfare.} Price access raises expected proceeds and acquisition surplus net of preparation costs at \(r_1\)
in both set-ups (Proposition~A.7; Proposition~L-A.9), and in the slot the gains are the same with research at the
cap. If an order unit is a given number of shares per share of float, initial shareholders
gain in total whenever that number is below \(\reg{p2_access_break_even}\) in the slot; in the Laplace example the
corresponding scale is \(\reg{lap_break_even_scale}\). The payment rule moves the spread with the sign of
\(1-2\omega\), where \(\omega\) is the seller's bargaining weight, in both (Proposition~A.8; Proposition~L-A.8); in the
slot the move is weak in general and strict when \(\E[(R-\ell)_+]\) changes strictly and \(\omega\ne1/2\). The reserve
\(\reg{rsv_points_excl}\) excludes the low class. In the Laplace example, preparation at this reserve falls from
\(\reg{lap_reserve_excl_r0_entry}\) at \(r_0\) to \(\reg{lap_reserve_excl_r1_entry}\) at \(r_1\), with unique
continuations at both (Table~L-5). In the slot, full orders are unique at both strengths and only a purchase recruits,
so entry is \(\reg{p2_reserve_points_exclude_r0_entry}\) at both (Proposition~A.9(a)). Strict deterrence holds at the
reserve \(\reg{rsv_deter}\), which also excludes the weak incumbent: entry falls from
\(\reg{p2_reserve_points_deter_r0_entry}\) to \(\reg{p2_reserve_points_deter_r1_entry}\) (Proposition~A.9(b)).

\paragraph{Empirical predictions.} Both set-ups give three predictions. When the price precedes the decision, a
stronger incumbent can raise the probability of further preparation; when it does not, it can only lower it; and
against a strong enough incumbent, preparation returns to its floor while the price stays informative. The slot
derives observables from one order opportunity. Under full two-sided trade at a prior below the threshold, the
purchase impact weighted by the frequency of a purchase exceeds the sale impact weighted by the frequency of a sale
exactly when a purchase recruits (Proposition~A.10). The price gap \(P(1)-P(-1)\) is \(\reg{ba_r1_B}\) at \(r_1\),
and \(\reg{ba_share_r1_B}\) of it is the entry component. Order counts identify the informed trader's presence at a known
prior away from \(1/2\), and the sale impact identifies it at any known prior; at the prior \(1/2\) the shares
consistent with counts and incentives at \(r_1\) lie between \(\approx\reg{theta_min_counts_B}\) and
\(\reg{theta_max_full_eq_r1_B}\) (Proposition~A.11). Under full two-sided trade a prepared rival wins
more often after a purchase than after a sale exactly when, with positive probability, a high-class value beats the
incumbent more often than a low-class value; this holds also above \(r_C\), where orders do not predict entry
(Proposition~A.12), and the mean price change over preparation reports is positive exactly when
preparation is still uncertain after some order (Proposition~A.13). In the Laplace set-up the price is that of the net
demand over the decision interval, and its three predictions are stated for that price.

\section{What separates the two set-ups}\label{sec:differ}

\paragraph{The order menu.} A continuous menu gives the low class a sale of any size. Its marginal profit falls
strictly with the size of the sale, so a root of its first-order condition is a global optimum (Online
Appendix~L-B.2), and the partial sales of Proposition~L-3 follow. On the unit menu a sale is all or nothing, and the
continuous margin is a mixture probability: the low class mixes on \((r_N,r_S)\) when an indifferent informed trader may mix
(Corollary~2; Table~A.3). The same holds for the rise of entry at intermediate strengths. In the Laplace set-up the
size of the sale carries it; in the slot, the high class's purchase rate carries it on \([r_A,r_F]\) at a fixed
presence probability, and the research intensity carries it on \([r_R,r_{\bar\lambda}]\). The slot's rise at a fixed
presence probability lies below the three Laplace strengths, and its rise with research passes through them at lower
entry.

\paragraph{The number of prices.} The slot has three prices, so entry moves in steps along the path, and a strict fall
at fixed orders needs the threshold to cross one of the three posteriors. Hence entry with full orders held fixed is
flat up to \(r_C\), and at the reserve that excludes the low class, strict deterrence needs the stronger incumbent to
push gross profit at the top posterior below \(c_H\) (Proposition~A.9(b)). The Laplace flow has a continuum of prices,
the crossing probability has a density, and both falls are strict. In return, the three prices make the equilibrium
set along the path a finite list: each class has three orders, and each gross return falls strictly with the class's
own rate, so Corollaries~2 and~3 list every equilibrium at every strength from \(r_0\) to \(r_2\). In the example,
full two-sided trade is the unique outcome for \(r_S<r<h\) (Section~8).

\paragraph{What the market observes.} Both set-ups price one trade before the rival's decision. The slot is one
public order opportunity at a known date, and its counterpart is the first prespecified public opportunity after the
sale becomes public, with an opportunity without an order recorded as a zero; the Laplace flow is the aggregate net
demand of the decision interval. In both set-ups the entry ceiling \(r_C\) is a tie for the high-cost rival after
the top posterior, so the rival's tie rule decides entry at \(r_C\) itself.
