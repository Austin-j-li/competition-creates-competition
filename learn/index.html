A company is for sale. Buyer A is ready to bid. Buyer B must decide whether to pay to prepare. We will follow that decision through the auction, the stock market, and the proofs. One example runs through the course, with new symbols explained as they become useful.

Start here

Read a short section, work through its example, and try its question. Explain your answer before revealing the solution. The course aims for about five hours of guided study; rebuilding every proof may take longer, especially in Lessons 4 and 5.

  1. Why would a stronger rival bring another buyer in?

  2. Calculate what the buyer keeps and the seller receives

    Work out individual auction outcomes, average over possible rival values, and prove Proposition 1.

  3. What can Buyer B learn from Target's share price?

    Update a probability, calculate the stock price, and explain why the informed investor still has an advantage.

  4. Prove that the stronger rival can bring B in

    Follow all six proof steps, calculate the extra preparation, and distinguish the theorem's different thresholds.

  5. What survives when we change the model?

    Study multiple equilibria, rigorous numerical certificates, other information and costs, welfare, and the seller's choice of terms. Finish with the course quiz.

Tell the tutor the lesson and section, and which transition lost you. For example: “Lesson 3, Section 7: why can we divide by the entry probability?” We can stay on that step and work through another calculation before moving on.
