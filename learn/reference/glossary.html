Glossary

Every term and symbol in "Competition Creates Competition", with a plain-words meaning and the exact definition.

Reference documentSource: paper sections 2 to 6, Appendix A

Use this page as the shared vocabulary for all lessons. Equation numbers such as (4) or (A.6) refer to the manuscript; OA numbers refer to the Online Appendix.

Institutions and players

TermPlain wordsPrecise definitionWhere
TargetThe listed company that is up for sale.Firm with standalone value normalized to zero, sold through a cash second-price auction with reserve \(p\). Its shares trade before the sale.Sec. 2.1
IncumbentA buyer who is already ready to bid.Bidder with acquisition value \(R\sim U[0,r]\), already prepared, no further participation cost, knows \(R\) before bidding.Sec. 2.1
ChallengerA possible second buyer who has to pay to find out what the target is worth to it.Bidder with value \(\theta\in\{\ell,h\}\), equal priors, who learns \(\theta\) only after paying preparation cost \(C\).Sec. 2.1
Preparation costThe bill for due diligence, lawyers, and bankers before you can make a real offer.\(C\in\{c_L,c_H\}\), equal to \(c_L\) with probability \(\rho\); paying \(C\) reveals \(\theta\) and permits bidding.Sec. 2.1
EntryDeciding to pay the preparation cost and become a bidder.Indicator \(\mathbf 1\{C\le B_r(\mu)\}\); its probability averaged over costs is \(e_r(\mu)\) in (9).(9)
Second-price auctionHighest bidder wins but pays the second-highest bid.Highest admissible bidder acquires the target and pays \(\max\{\text{reserve},\text{highest competing bid}\}\).Sec. 2.1
ReserveThe lowest price the seller will accept.\(p\), publicly committed before trading; no sale without a bid of at least \(p\).(1)
Admissible bidA bid that clears the minimum price.A bid at least equal to the reserve \(p\); a bid equal to \(p\) is admissible.Sec. 2.1
Truthful biddingBidding exactly what the target is worth to you.Each bidder bids its value; weakly dominant in a second-price auction, so it does not depend on any conjecture about rivals.Sec. 2.1, A.1
Tie ruleSomeone exactly on the fence goes ahead and enters.A challenger indifferent about preparation enters; matters at the posterior plateau \(\mu=M\) and at \(r_C\).Sec. 2.1, 4.3
Market makerThe dealer who quotes the stock price after seeing total buy and sell orders.Competitive agents observing aggregate flow \(X\) and setting \(P(X)=\mathbb E[V_T\mid X]\), anticipating entry and the auction.(3)
InvestorThe one trader who knows the challenger's value.Agent observing \(\theta\), submitting order \(q\in[-1,1]\), paying \(k|q|\), with no control rights and no ability to acquire the target.(2)
Noise traderPeople buying or selling for reasons unrelated to the deal.Random demand \(Z\) with Laplace density (2), independent of everything else; it hides the investor's order.(2)
Order flowThe total of everyone's orders.\(X=q+Z\).(2)

Beliefs and information

TermPlain wordsPrecise definitionWhere
PriorWhat you believe before seeing any evidence.\(\Pr(\theta=h)=1/2\).Sec. 2.1
PosteriorWhat you believe after seeing the evidence.\(\mu=\Pr(\theta=h\mid\text{information})\); \(\mu_X\) conditions on flow, the price posterior conditions on \(P\).(7)
Candidate scheduleA proposed rule for prices and entry that we test for consistency.A pricing function and preparation rule held fixed while one player's deviation is evaluated.Sec. 2.3
Unilateral deviationOne player changes plan while everyone else sticks to theirs.A change in the investor's order distribution with the candidate pricing and preparation schedules held fixed.Sec. 2.3
EquilibriumA set of plans where nobody wants to change theirs.Conditional order distributions \(\sigma_H,\sigma_L\), measurable price, Bayesian beliefs given price, optimal preparation, truthful bidding, all mutually consistent.Sec. 2.3
Residual advantageThe edge the insider keeps after the market has priced in everything it can.\(A_H(x)=\mathbb E[V_T\mid H,x]-P(x)\), \(A_L(x)=P(x)-\mathbb E[V_T\mid L,x]\).(11)
FOSD"Stronger" in the sense that higher values are more likely at every level.First-order stochastic dominance: \(F_1(u)\le F_0(u)\) for all \(u\); raises \(\mathbb E[g(R)]\) for increasing \(g\).Prop. 1
ExperimentA signal viewed as a machine that produces different outputs in different states.The pair of conditional distributions of the signal given \(\theta=h\) and \(\theta=\ell\).OA A.4
GarblingAdding noise to a signal, or throwing part of it away.Applying a state-independent Markov kernel to an experiment.OA A.4
Blackwell dominanceOne signal is at least as useful as another for every decision.Experiment 1 dominates 2 if 2 is a garbling of 1; strict if no reverse garbling exists.Prop. 2(ii)
PlateauA flat stretch where the belief cannot go any higher.Under full Laplace orders \(\mu_X=M\) on \(x\ge1\) and \(\mu_X=m\) on \(x\le-1\), events of positive probability.(A.8)
Ownership probabilityChance the good challenger ends up owning the target.\(\mathsf O_H=\Pr(\theta=h\text{ and the challenger enters})=e_H/2\) since \(h\gt r\).(12)

Proof vocabulary

TermPlain wordsPrecise definitionWhere
Interval arithmeticDoing arithmetic with ranges so that the true answer is guaranteed to lie inside.Operations on closed intervals with outward rounding; every result contains the exact real result.OA B
EnclosureA range that certainly contains the true number.An interval \([\underline x,\overline x]\) proved to contain the exact value.(13)
CertificateA machine-checked list of inequalities that together prove a theorem.Interval evaluations of \(\Psi\) at bracket endpoints and of \(U_H'\) on a mesh, with the regularity arguments of OA B.A.3, OA B.6
Computer-assisted proofA proof where a computer checks finitely many rigorous inequalities.Existence proof by interval sign change plus analytic uniqueness and optimality arguments.Prop. 3
AnalyticalProved with pen and paper.Status of a result established by the arguments of Appendix A and OA A.OA C.0
Numerical diagnosticFound by a computer search, not proved.Status of continuation branches and searches whose acceptance passed tolerances but carry no certificate.Sec. 4.3, OA C
OpenNot known either way.Status of unresolved nodes or questions, for example the full intermediate correspondence.Sec. 4.3
ContinuationWhat happens after a given reserve is announced.The complete object \(\sigma=(\sigma_H,\sigma_L;P(\cdot);\mu_P;\text{preparation rule})\in\mathcal E(p,r)\); two continuations with the same orders but different price mappings are different elements.(A.43)
Price poolSeveral different order flows that all show the same price.A set of flows mapped to one price, on which the buyer conditions on the whole set; possible only where entry can vanish, so not on the benchmark support.Prop. A.10, (A.45)
Exact reserve eventA reserve at which a participation inequality is exactly tied.\(p_L=h-c_L/m\) (cheap floor, incumbent excluded) and \(p_H\) solving \(Mg_H=c_H\); evaluated from the identities, not from rounded numbers.(A.50), (A.51)

Symbols: primitives

SymbolPlain wordsPrecise definitionWhere
\(R\)How much the incumbent values the target.Incumbent acquisition value, \(R\sim U[0,r]\), per share.Sec. 2.1
\(r\)Incumbent strength: the top of its possible values.Upper support of \(R\); larger \(r\) is an FOSD increase.Sec. 2.1
\(\theta\)What the target is worth to the challenger.Challenger value in \(\{\ell,h\}\), each with probability 1/2.Sec. 2.1
\(\ell,h\)Low and high challenger values.\(0\lt p\lt \ell\lt r\lt h\) in the benchmark.(1)
\(p\)The reserve price.Minimum acceptable bid, committed before trading.(1)
\(C, c_L, c_H\)The preparation bill and its two possible sizes.\(C=c_L\) w.p. \(\rho\), \(c_H\) w.p. \(1-\rho\); \(0\le c_L\lt c_H\).Sec. 2.1
\(\rho\)Chance preparation is cheap.\(\Pr(C=c_L)\in(0,1)\); also the entry floor.Sec. 2.1
\(q\)How much the informed investor buys (positive) or sells (negative).Order in \([-1,1]\); \(q_H,q_L\) are the state-contingent orders, \(\sigma_H,\sigma_L\) their mixed distributions.(2)
\(k\)Trading cost per unit.Linear cost \(k|q|\), \(k\gt 0\).(2)
\(Z\)Random noise demand.Laplace with density \(f(z)=e^{-|z|/b}/(2b)\).(2)
\(b\)How spread out the noise is.Laplace scale, \(b\gt 1\); the log-density slope is bounded by \(1/b\).(2)
\(X\)Total order flow.\(X=q+Z\).(2)
\(P\)The stock price.\(P(X)=\mathbb E[V_T\mid X]\).(3)
\(V_T\)What a share finally pays out.Terminal payoff of a target share: proceeds from the sale, or zero if no sale.(2)
\(\varepsilon_C\)How far preparation costs can spread around each atom.Half-width of atomless low- and high-cost supports; declared value 0.1.A.8, Prop. A.6
\(\varepsilon_V\)How far acquisition values can spread around \(\ell\) and \(h\).Half-width of conditional uniform value bands in the reserve exercise; declared value 0.05.Sec. 6.3, A.8

Symbols: beliefs and payoffs

SymbolPlain wordsPrecise definitionWhere
\(\mu\)Belief that the challenger is the high type.\(\Pr(\theta=h\mid\cdot)\); \(\mu_X(x)=a_H(x)/(a_H(x)+a_L(x))\).(7)
\(m, M\)The lowest and highest belief any trade can ever produce.\(m=1/(1+e^{2/b})\), \(M=1-m\).(7)
\(t_0\)Expected sale proceeds with no challenger.\(p(1-p/r)\).(4)
\(t_H, t_L\)Expected proceeds if the challenger enters and is high or low.\(t_H=r/2+p^2/(2r)\), \(t_L=\ell-(\ell^2-p^2)/(2r)\).(4)
\(g_H, g_L\)Challenger's expected profit from winning, by type, before paying \(C\).\(g_H=h-r/2-p^2/(2r)\), \(g_L=(\ell^2-p^2)/(2r)\).(4)
\(\Delta_T\)How much more the target fetches with a high challenger than a low one.\(t_H-t_L=(r-\ell)^2/(2r)\); in general \(\mathbb E_F[(R-\ell)_+]\).(4), (6)
\(B_r(\mu)\)Expected gross profit from preparing, given belief \(\mu\).\(g_L+\mu(g_H-g_L)\).(5)
\(e_r(\mu)\)Probability the challenger enters at belief \(\mu\).\(\rho+(1-\rho)\mathbf 1\{B_r(\mu)\ge c_H\}\).(9)
\(A_H, A_L\)Insider's per-unit edge when buying on good news or selling on bad news.\(A_H=e_r\Delta_T(1-\mu_X)\), \(A_L=e_r\Delta_T\mu_X\); both in \([\rho m\Delta_T,\Delta_T]\).(11)
\(F_\theta(s)\)Average edge of a correctly signed order of size \(s\).\(F_H(s)=\int f(x-s)A_H(x)dx\), \(F_L(s)=\int f(x+s)A_L(x)dx\).(A.4)
\(U_\theta(s)\)Net trading profit of an order of size \(s\).\(sF_\theta(s)-ks\).(A.4)
\(w_H, w_L\)How much entry by each type lifts proceeds.\(w_H=t_H-t_0\), \(w_L=t_L-t_0\).(A.28)
\(\mathcal R_T\)Expected money the seller collects.\(t_0+\tfrac12\sum_\theta e_\theta(t_\theta-t_0)\).(A.44)

Symbols: equilibrium outcomes and thresholds

SymbolPlain wordsPrecise definitionWhere
\(\tau\)The belief at which expensive preparation just pays.\((c_H-g_L)/(g_H-g_L)\).(12)
\(x^*\)The order flow that produces belief \(\tau\).\((b/2)\log(\tau/(1-\tau))\) under full orders; \((b\,\mathrm{logit}\,\tau+1-v)/2\) under \((1,-v)\).(12), (A.15)
\(\alpha_H,\alpha_L\)Chance the flow crosses \(x^*\) when the challenger is high or low.\(\alpha_H=1-\tfrac12e^{(x^*-1)/b}\), \(\alpha_L=\tfrac12e^{-(x^*+1)/b}\).(12)
\(\mathsf E\)Overall probability the challenger enters.\(\rho+\tfrac{1-\rho}{2}(\alpha_H+\alpha_L)\).(12)
\(\mathsf O_H\)Probability a high-value challenger ends up owning the target.\(\tfrac12[\rho+(1-\rho)\alpha_H]\).(12)
\(\mathfrak r(d)\)The strength at which \(\Delta_T\) equals a given number \(d\).\(\ell+d+\sqrt{d^2+2\ell d}\), the root of \((r-\ell)^2/(2r)=d\).(A.10)
\(r_N\)Strongest incumbent at which "nobody trades" is still an equilibrium.\(\mathfrak r(2k/\rho)\); no trade is an equilibrium iff \(r\le r_N\).(A.10)
\(r_U\)Strength above which full orders are guaranteed unique.\(\mathfrak r\!\left(k/[(1-1/b)\rho m]\right)\).(A.10)
\(r_C\)Strength above which even the best news cannot justify expensive preparation.Unique solution of \(B_r(M)=c_H\), formula (A.11).(A.11)
\(v_j\)Size of the certified partial short sale at strength \(r_j\).\(q_L=-v_j\) with \(q_H=1\) in Proposition 3.(13)
\(\Psi(r,v)\)The low type's marginal profit at its own candidate order.\(U_L'(v;1,-v)\), derivative in the deviating magnitude with the schedule fixed.A.3
\(L_U\)How fast the high type's marginal profit can change.Lipschitz bound \(2\Delta_T/b+\Delta_T/b^2\) on \(U_H'\).(A.18)

Symbols: extensions

SymbolPlain wordsPrecise definitionWhere
\(T, a\)The investor's private signal and its accuracy.\(T\in\{+,-\}\), \(\Pr(T=+\mid H)=\Pr(T=-\mid L)=a\in(1/2,1)\).(A.26)
\(Y, d\)The buyer's private signal and its accuracy.\(Y\in\{+,-\}\), \(\Pr(Y=+\mid H)=\Pr(Y=-\mid L)=d\in(1/2,1)\); conditionally independent of \(T\).(A.26)
\(\lambda_X\)Market's belief that the investor saw good news.\(\Pr(T=+\mid X)\); then \(\mu_X=(1-a)+(2a-1)\lambda_X\).(A.27)
\(\phi_+,\phi_-\)The buyer's belief after combining the price with its own good or bad signal.\(\phi_+(\mu)=d\mu/[d\mu+(1-d)(1-\mu)]\), \(\phi_-(\mu)=(1-d)\mu/[(1-d)\mu+d(1-\mu)]\).(A.27)
\(e_H, e_L\)Entry rates when the challenger is actually high or low.\(e_H=\rho+(1-\rho)[dI_++(1-d)I_-]\), \(e_L=\rho+(1-\rho)[(1-d)I_++dI_-]\).(A.28)
\(D\)How strongly the price responds to public belief.\(e_Hw_H-e_Lw_L\), with \(\rho\Delta_T\le D\).(A.33)
\(\eta\)The seller's share in bargaining.Nash bargaining weight, \(0\le\eta\lt 1\).Sec. 6.2
\(T_\eta(R,\theta)\)What the seller gets under bargaining.\((1-\eta)\min\{R,\theta\}+\eta\max\{R,\theta\}\).(14)
\(G_{\theta,\eta}\)Challenger's profit under bargaining.\((1-\eta)\mathbb E[(\theta-R)_+]\).(14)
\(\Delta_\eta\)Target-payoff spread under bargaining.\(\eta(h-\ell)+(1-2\eta)\mathbb E[(R-\ell)_+]\).(14)
