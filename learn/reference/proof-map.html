Prop 1 A stronger incumbent widens the gap in target proceeds between high and low challengers and lowers the challenger's profit at every belief. Same bounds; positive-probability favorable event under either noise law. analytical Results for wide cost supports that violate (A.24).
Prop A.7 With conditionally independent private signals for investor and buyer, the weak economy has zero orders and entry \(\rho\); the strong economy has full signal-contingent orders and entry above \(\rho\). Prop A.8 Under Nash bargaining with seller weight \(\eta\), a stronger incumbent lowers challenger profit for every \(\eta\) and widens the target spread iff \(\eta\lt 1/2\). Zero reserve, verifiable values, \(0\lt \ell\lt R_{\max}\lt h\), \(0\le\eta\lt 1\). Payment rule (14) only. Nash bargaining solution \(P=z+\eta(V-z)\); FOSD on \((R-\ell)_+\) and \((\theta-R)_+\). analytical An entry reversal under bargaining; trading and entry are not solved for this institution.
Prop A.9 At fixed \(r_1\), letting the challenger see the price raises expected target proceeds and acquisition surplus net of preparation costs. Conditions of Prop 2; \(r=r_1\). Full orders in both games (A.6); pointwise gain (A.39). Matched coupling of the two economies; accounting identity (A.41). analytical A ranking of incumbent strengths or of sale mechanisms; it holds the sale rule fixed.
Prop A.10 At reserve \(p=7\) and \(r=r_0\), every cutoff \(c\in[-\log2,0]\) of the price rule (A.45) is a continuation equilibrium with full orders; prices, beliefs, and entry differ across the family, orders do not. Benchmark primitives; \(p\gt r\gt\ell\), so \(t_0=t_L=g_L=0\), \(t_H=7\), \(g_H=3\). Pooled posterior (A.46); residuals (A.47); existence test (A.7). Pooled belief below \(1/3\) keeps the cheap type out at price zero; belief at least \(1/3\) elsewhere; rational lower bound (A.48) on \(U_\theta'\). analytical Uniqueness of the family within \(\mathcal E(7,r_0)\); pooling on the benchmark support, where \(c_L\lt B_r(m)\) rules it out.

Reading the map

Three ideas carry the whole paper. First, the payment rule alone makes the target's payoff spread rise and the challenger's profit fall with competition (Proposition 1). Second, noise bounds beliefs, and the price reveals the bounded belief (A.1, A.2). Third, a global derivative bound on convolution profits pins down trading without guessing an equilibrium (the fourth step of Proposition 2). Every extension reuses these three ideas with a different noise law, cost law, or signal structure. Proposition 3 is the one place where pen and paper stop and a certified computation takes over, and its status label says so.

If a row here is unclear, ask the teaching agent to walk through that proof step by step.
