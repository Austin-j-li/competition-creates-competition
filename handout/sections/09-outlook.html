Conclusion and next steps

The bidder pool in a takeover depends on the information available before buyers commit. A stronger incumbent can raise the sensitivity of target proceeds to challenger quality, make informed trading profitable, and bring in a challenger that a weaker incumbent would not have faced, so the division of acquisition surplus feeds back into the formation of competition. Sale terms reach participation through the buyer's expected payment and through the information the market supplies, and the two channels can pull in opposite directions.

Theory

The next step is the seller's choice of terms with the full equilibrium continuation correspondence retained; multiplicity is what stops me from identifying the seller's objective with an envelope of found revenues. A related question is whether committing to terms before trading does better than revising them after observing prices. Alternative auction formats would show how much of the mechanism is specific to the second-price payment rule, which the bargaining institution already suggests is a lot. Endogenous investor research would add its own incentives, including manipulation through the real decision that responds to the price (Goldstein and Guembel 2008).

Empirics

The empirical counterpart of the model's decision is the start of substantive diligence or a proposal that requires costly preparation, not the count of public offers. Incumbent strength must be measured with information available before that decision; the price information must precede entry, since later returns can reflect anticipated bidder arrival; the sale opportunity must be publicly understood and remain contestable while the challenger decides. Online Appendix D specifies an institutional pilot that reconstructs these events from disclosure records. It is a research design; no sample, estimated effect, or instrument is reported.
