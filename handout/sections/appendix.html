Appendix propositions

Appendix A.1 of the paper carries nine supporting results. Each is stated in one line below with the place it does its work; the paper has the full statements and the Online Appendix has the proofs.

Appendix propositions A.1 to A.9
ResultStatementWhere it is used
A.1 (posterior bounds)For arbitrary mixed state-contingent orders on \([-1,1]\), the order-flow posterior and the price posterior lie in \([m,M]\).Feasible beliefs, eq. (7) and (8); the ceiling \(r_C\)
A.2 (price sufficiency and residual profits)If \(c_L\lt B_r(m)\), the price reveals \(\mu_X\) almost surely, entry and pricing take the form (9), and the residual advantages satisfy \(\rho m\Delta_T\le A_H,A_L\le\Delta_T\).Inversion (10), residuals (11), both trading bounds in Proposition 2
A.3 (fixed information experiment)If the posterior generated by a fixed signal does not change with \(r\), entry is weakly decreasing in \(r\), strictly when the profit decline crosses preparation costs on a positive-probability set.Frozen-orders control in Table 2; deterrence restored
A.4 (no trade, full orders, expensive-entry feasibility)No trade is an equilibrium exactly when \(\rho\Delta_T(r)/2\le k\), that is \(r\le r_N\); it is unique below \(\mathfrak r(k)\); full orders are unique above \(r_U\); expensive entry is impossible when \(B_r(M)\lt c_H\), with the unique crossing \(r_C\).Thresholds and shading in Figure 2
A.5 (logistic noise)Under (A1) to (A3) with logistic noise, the unique trading outcomes and the entry and ownership comparisons of Proposition 2 remain valid, with adjusted threshold and crossing probabilities.Robustness, Figure 3, Table 3
A.6 (atomless preparation costs)With narrow atomless low- and high-cost distributions satisfying the support conditions and (A3), the unique trading outcomes and parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition 2 hold under either noise law.Robustness, Table 3
A.7 (complementary private signals)With conditionally independent binary signals for investor and buyer and the stated cost and trading-cost bounds, the weak economy has unique zero orders and entry \(\rho\), and the strong economy has unique full signal-contingent orders and entry above \(\rho\).Complementary private information, Table 3
A.8 (bargaining)In the verifiable-value institution, a first-order stochastic strengthening weakly reduces challenger profits and moves the spread up for \(\eta\lt1/2\), not at all at \(\eta=1/2\), and down for \(\eta\gt1/2\).The payment rule, eq. (14), Figure 4
A.9 (access to prices)At \(r=r_1\) under the conditions of Proposition 2, letting the challenger observe the price strictly increases expected target proceeds and acquisition surplus net of preparation costs, also under A.5 and A.6.Access to prices, Table 2 Panel C
