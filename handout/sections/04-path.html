Coexistence on an interval

Proposition 3 · Coexistence on the benchmark path

An equilibrium exists at every strength on the path. An equilibrium without informed trade coexists with an informative equilibrium exactly on \([r_A,r_N]\). On the branch that recruits, entry for \(r\in[r_A,r_C]\) is

\[ \rho+(1-\rho)\min\Bigl\{\frac{b\,\Delta_T(r)}{2k},\ b+\frac{\lambda}{2}\Bigr\}, \]

Entry rises strictly on \([r_A,r_F]\), then stays constant through the ceiling \(r_C\). Above the ceiling, full orders continue and entry equals \(\rho\).

With no informed trade, the price leaves the challenger at its prior and entry stays at \(\rho\). A unilateral unit order earns \(\rho\Delta_T/2\) before costs, which reaches \(k\) at \(r_N\approx[[rN]]\). No informed trade therefore remains an equilibrium through that strength. An informative price changes the entry response to a purchase and raises its return. This second equilibrium starts at \(r_A\approx[[rA]]\), where a purchase just reaches the preparation threshold and earns \(k\).

Between \(r_A\) and \(r_F\), the high-class investor mixes between a purchase and abstention. A purchase recruits and earns exactly its cost: \(\Delta_T[1-\mu(1)]=k\). As the spread widens, the purchase must reveal more to keep the investor indifferent, so the informed purchase rate \(b[\Delta_T(r)/k-2]\) rises. Entry rises from [[entry_rA_B]] at \(r_A\) to [[entry_mb_r145_B]] at [[str_r145]]. From \(r_F\approx[[rF_B]]\), the high-class investor always buys when present, and entry stays at [[entry_r1_B]] through the ceiling.

The marked strengths, the equilibrium set and other priors

The informed purchase rate is the probability of an informed purchase conditional on class \(H\). Divide it by \(\lambda\) to obtain the purchase probability conditional on investor presence. On the branch that recruits, entry is [[entry_mb_r140_B]] at [[str_r140]]. The low-class investor abstains through \(r_N\), then mixes until \(r_S\approx[[rS_B]]\), where full sales start. Competition creates research

The investor can also pay to learn the class. It chooses research intensity \(\lambda\in[0,\bar\lambda]\) at cost \((a/2)\lambda^2\). Success reveals the class and gives the investor the slot; after failure, a liquidity trader fills it. Market makers and the challenger do not observe the research choice. More research adds informed orders and removes liquidity orders, so it reduces camouflage.

Consider the branch where class \(H\) buys after success, class \(L\) abstains, and only a purchase recruits. At an interior research choice, \(2a\lambda=\Delta_T(r)\,m_\lambda-k\), where \(m_\lambda\) is the lower posterior bound at intensity \(\lambda\). A wider spread raises the return to a purchase and induces more research. Proposition 4 shows that \(\lambda_R(r)\) rises strictly with \(r\) wherever \(\Delta_T(r)\gt2k\). Where this branch is an equilibrium, entry and high-class ownership also rise, for every \(0\lt\beta\le1/3\).

Set \(a=[[res_a]]\) and cap research at the benchmark presence probability. The weak incumbent has a unique equilibrium without research. At each of [[str_r155]], [[str_r160]] and [[str_r165]], exactly two equilibria exist. The first has no research and entry [[entry_r0]]. On the research branch, intensity rises from [[res_lambda_r155]] through [[res_lambda_r160]] to [[res_lambda_r165]], and entry rises from [[res_entry_r155]] through [[res_entry_r160]] to [[res_entry_r165]].

With presence fixed at the cap, entry on the full high-class purchase branch stays at [[entry_r1_B]] at these strengths. The increase across these three points therefore comes from research. At \(r_1\) and \(r_2\), the unique equilibrium has research at the cap and reproduces Proposition 2.

Research in detail

For a fixed price and entry rule, optimal intensity equals expected net trade profit after success divided by \(a\), capped at \(\bar\lambda\). The quadratic cost makes this choice unique. On this branch, a purchase has probability \(\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lambda\). Entry therefore has slope \((1-\rho)(1/2-\beta)\lambda_R'(r)\gt0\) for every admissible \(\beta\). Research adds informed purchases in \(H\) and removes liquidity purchases in both classes; the first effect is larger.

At \(r_R\), the purchase posterior just reaches the preparation threshold and the purchase covers the marginal research cost. The branch reaches the cap at \(\bar r_R\). Zero research remains an equilibrium along the entire branch because \(\bar r_R\lt r_N\). Research starts later than the informative branch in Proposition 3 because a purchase must cover research costs as well as trade costs.
