Between the three economies

4 · Coexistence and research · 3 minutes

Between the weak and the strong incumbent of Proposition 2, two equilibria can hold against the same incumbent. In an uninformative market the informed trader does not trade, the price carries no information, and entry stays at its floor. In the informative market that coexists with it, a purchase recruits a high-cost rival. Propositions 3 and 4 use the default tie rules: an indifferent informed trader may mix over its best orders, and an indifferent high-cost rival prepares.

Uninformative and informative markets

Proposition 3 · Uninformative and informative markets

Under the default tie rules, suppose the classes satisfy the class ranking, and take an incumbent distribution with a positive spread \(\Delta_T\), \(g_H\gt g_L\) and preparation threshold \(\tau\). An uninformative market and an informative market coexist if and only if

\[ \begin{gathered} \pi\lt\tau\le M(\pi),\\ \frac{k}{1-\tau}\le\Delta_T\le\frac{k}{\rho\max\{\pi,1-\pi\}} . \end{gathered} \]

The informative market is then unique: class \(L\) abstains, only a purchase recruits, and entry is

\[ \rho+(1-\rho)\min\Bigl\{\frac{(1-\pi)\,b\,\Delta_T}{k},\ b+\pi\lambda\Bigr\}, \]

which rises strictly with the spread while class \(H\) mixes between a purchase and abstention, and is constant once class \(H\) buys for sure. Along a path, the strengths at which an uninformative market coexists with a sure purchase form an interval, and every other strength with coexistence lies below it. When the product of the spread and \(g_H-c_H\) does not fall with strength, all strengths with coexistence form one interval, at every prior.

The threshold must lie above the prior and within reach of a purchase. The spread must be wide enough that a purchase that just reaches the threshold covers the trading cost, and narrow enough that no order against an uninformative price earns more than it. In an uninformative market entry stays at its floor after every order, so an informed order earns at most \(\rho\max\{\pi,1-\pi\}\Delta_T\); the upper bound on the spread keeps that below \(k\). An informative market changes the entry response to a purchase, and with it the return to trade.

While class \(H\) mixes, a recruiting purchase earns exactly the trading cost, so a wider spread needs a more informative purchase. The purchase rate rises, and entry with it, until class \(H\) buys for sure. A stronger incumbent never narrows the spread and never lowers the threshold while \(g_L\le c_H\), so the two numbers pull in opposite directions, and the economy can enter the region, leave it and enter it again. When class \(H\)'s gross profit exceeds the high cost against the weakest incumbent, the whole set stays in one piece if the spread grows, in proportion, at least as fast as that excess shrinks.

The example meets this condition, so at every prior its strengths with coexistence form one interval (Corollary 2). At the prior one half, with the slot in the band of Corollary 1, an equilibrium exists at every strength from \(r_0\) to \(r_2\), and coexistence runs from \(r_A\approx[[rA]]\), where a purchase that just reaches the threshold earns the trading cost, to \(r_N\approx[[rN]]\), where a unit order against an uninformative price earns it. Entry in the informative market is

\[ \rho+(1-\rho)\min\Bigl\{\frac{b\,\Delta_T(r)}{2k},\ b+\frac{\lambda}{2}\Bigr\}. \]

Between \(r_A\) and \(r_F\), class \(H\) buys at the rate \(b[\Delta_T(r)/k-2]\), which rises with the spread, and entry rises from [[entry_rA_B]] at \(r_A\) to [[entry_mb_r145_B]] at [[str_r145]]. From \(r_F\approx[[rF_B]]\), class \(H\) buys for sure when present, and entry stays at [[entry_r1_B]] up to the entry ceiling \(r_C\). Above the ceiling, entry with a full purchase equals \(\rho\).

In the example's slot it starts at the same strength \(r_A\) for every prior between \(\approx[[piA_lo_B]]\) and \(\approx[[piA_hi]]\), and it starts later, where the prior reaches the threshold, for priors up to \(\approx[[pimax]]\). The other tie rules change the intermediate path. When an indifferent informed trader abstains, the low class has no qualifying sale rate on \((r_N,r_S]\), so no equilibrium exists there, and coexistence shrinks to \((r_F,r_N]\). When an indifferent high-cost rival may prepare with any probability, the threshold pair is an equilibrium on \([r_A,r_J]\), and entry after a purchase at \(r_C\) can take any value between \(\rho\) and one. Proposition 2 rests on strict comparisons and gives the same three outcomes under all four tie rules.

Competition creates research

The informed trader can also pay to learn the class. Before it learns the class, it chooses a research intensity \(\lambda\in[0,\bar\lambda]\) at cost \((a/2)\lambda^2\), and research succeeds with probability \(\lambda\). Success reveals the class and gives the informed trader the slot; after failure, a liquidity trader fills it. Market makers and the rival do not observe the research choice, so they price and decide at a conjectured intensity. More research adds informed orders and removes liquidity orders, so it reduces the camouflage of the informed trader's own orders. The prior is one half.

Consider the equilibrium in which class \(H\) buys after a success and a purchase recruits a high-cost rival. Class \(L\) abstains while a sale at the entry floor cannot pay its cost, \(\rho\Delta_T\le2k\); beyond that point it sells just often enough that its sale breaks even, and once a sure sale pays, it sells for sure. A sale never recruits, and a sale that breaks even adds nothing to the value of research, so at an interior research choice

\[ 2a\lambda=\Delta_T\,m_\lambda-k+\bigl(\rho\,\Delta_T\,m_\lambda-k\bigr)^+, \]

where \(m_\lambda\) is the bottom posterior at intensity \(\lambda\). The left side rises with \(\lambda\) and the right side falls, so when \(\Delta_T\gt2k\) the two sides meet at one intensity \(\lambda_R(\Delta_T)\), and research is the smaller of \(\lambda_R\) and the cap.

Proposition 4 holds for every incumbent distribution with a positive spread, \(g_H\gt g_L\) and \(\tau\gt\tfrac12\). This recruiting equilibrium exists exactly when \(\Delta_T\gt2k\) and the posterior after a purchase, at the research the informed trader chooses, reaches the threshold. Research rises strictly with the spread until it reaches the cap and then stays there. Entry, \(\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lambda]\), and entry in class \(H\) rise with research, while entry in class \(L\) falls, so the rivals who prepare are more often of class \(H\). When class \(H\) outbids the incumbent, high-class ownership is half of entry in class \(H\) and rises with research. Take a path whose spread starts below and reaches the value at which research hits its cap, with the threshold below one up to that strength. When the recruitment margin, the value of a success less the marginal cost of research at the research that just lets a purchase recruit, rises strictly along it, from negative against the weakest incumbent to positive where research reaches its cap, the equilibrium holds exactly on one interval of strengths. On it research rises to the cap and stays there up to the last strength at which the threshold does not exceed the top posterior at the cap.

In the example (Corollary 3), set \(a=[[res_a]]\) and the cap \(\bar\lambda\approx[[res_cap]]\), the example's presence probability. Zero research, with entry \(\rho\), is an equilibrium exactly on \([r_0,r_N]\). Recruiting research is an equilibrium exactly from \(r_R\approx[[res_rR]]\) to the entry ceiling \(r_C\approx[[res_rC]]\): research is below the cap up to \(r_{\bar\lambda}\approx[[res_rcap]]\) and at the cap from there, and entry rises from [[res_entry_rR]] at \(r_R\) to [[res_entry_cap]] at \(r_{\bar\lambda}\) and keeps that value up to \(r_C\). Floor-entry research, in which both classes trade after a success, no order recruits and entry is \(\rho\), is an equilibrium exactly on \((r_N,r_Q)\) below the cap, with \(r_Q\approx[[res_rQ]]\), and on \((r_C,r_2]\) at the cap. There is no other equilibrium. The unique equilibrium thus has zero research at \(r_0\), recruiting research at the cap at \(r_1\) and floor-entry research at the cap at \(r_2\); at \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) it is the equilibrium of Proposition 2.

At each of [[str_r155]], [[str_r160]] and [[str_r165]], zero research and recruiting research below the cap coexist. Along the recruiting equilibrium, research rises from [[res_lambda_r155]] through [[res_lambda_r160]] to [[res_lambda_r165]], and entry rises from [[res_entry_r155]] through [[res_entry_r160]] to [[res_entry_r165]]. A stronger incumbent widens the spread, so a purchase earns more, the informed trader chooses more research, and the posterior after a purchase rises further above the threshold.

Research in detail

At fixed prices and entry, the best research choice is unique: it equals expected net trading profit after a success divided by \(a\), capped at \(\bar\lambda\). In the recruiting equilibrium a purchase has probability \(\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lambda\), so entry has slope \((1-\rho)(1/2-\beta)\) in research for every \(\beta\). Research adds informed purchases in \(H\) and removes liquidity purchases in both classes; the first effect is larger.

At \(r_R\), a full purchase that just reaches the threshold also pays for its research, \(\Delta_T(1-\tau)=k+2a\lambda_E\), where the recruitment intensity \(\lambda_E\) is the research at which a full purchase just reaches the threshold; there research is [[res_lambda_rR]]. The low class starts to sell at \(r_N\) and sells for sure from \(r_S\); research is at its cap before either strength, so in the example the sale's profit leaves research unchanged. Recruiting research coexists with zero research on \([r_R,r_N]\) and with floor-entry research on \((r_N,r_Q)\), where floor-entry research rises towards [[res_lambda_floor_rQ]]. It is the only equilibrium on \([r_Q,r_C]\), and floor-entry research at the cap is the equilibrium above \(r_C\).

Recruiting research starts later than the informative market of Corollary 2, which starts at \(r_A\), because a purchase must pay for research as well as for trade. With a fixed presence probability the purchase rate adjusts through a mixture of the high class; with research the intensity adjusts, and the high class buys for sure after a success.

At \(\beta=[[beta_B]]\), the pattern of Corollary 3 at \(r_0\), the three intermediate strengths, \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) holds with strict margins exactly when the cap lies between the recruitment intensities at \(r_1\) and \(r_2\), about [[res_cap_lo]] and [[res_cap_hi]], and the cost lies in a window that depends on the cap. At the example's cap the window is \(([[res_a_lo]],[[res_a_up]])\). With \(1+u=\mathrm e\), the cap \(\bar\lambda=u\beta/(1+u\beta)\) and \(a=[[res_a]]\), the pattern holds for every \(\beta\) above about [[res_beta_lo]] and up to [[res_beta_upper]].
