The reversal survives changes to the model

The reversal also holds with dispersed preparation costs, including distributions with atoms. Retain (H2), and place every low cost below \(B_{F_1}(m(\pi))\). Place every high cost strictly between \(B_{F_0}(\pi)\) and \(B_{F_1}(M(\pi))\). Parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition 2 then hold. An atomless high-cost component makes the later decline continuous while full orders and the low-cost entry floor remain.

For any prior and two ranked classes with different means, uniform incumbents admit an open set of strengths and costs with the reversal. This set satisfies (H1) to (H3) and the ownership condition. With more than two classes, prices reveal the posterior mean of class proceeds. Under a separation condition, every high-group class buys, every low-group class sells, and the reversal holds.

A price can add useful information even when the challenger has a more accurate private signal than the investor. The signal extension uses two binary signals that are independent conditional on the class. In an example with its own costs and strengths, investor accuracy is [[sig_aT]] and challenger accuracy is [[sig_d]]. Entry rises from [[p4_s_signal_r0_entry]] to [[p4_s_signal_r1_entry]]. Without the challenger's signal, entry stays at [[p4_s_no_y_r0_entry]]: this example requires both sources of information.

Price access raises proceeds and surplus

Hold the incumbent at \(F_1\) and compare price access with a hidden price, under the same sale mechanism. The trade bound forces full orders in both economies, even when entry stays at \(\rho\). Price access adds high-cost preparation after a purchase, where expected acquisition profit exceeds the preparation cost. At benchmark strength \(r_1\), expected proceeds rise from [[p2_base_hidden_proceeds]] to [[p2_base_r1_proceeds]], and acquisition surplus net of preparation costs rises by [[p2_access_surplus_gain]]. This surplus excludes transfers; trade costs coincide across the two economies.

A shareholder who holds through the sale receives the higher expected proceeds. Price access has a different effect when high-cost preparation already pays at the prior: an unfavorable price can deter preparation. The positive result applies to the recruitment region of Proposition 2.

Sale terms

The payment rule determines how incumbent strength changes the spread. Consider verifiable values and a zero reserve, with the next buyer's value as an enforceable fallback. The seller and winner divide surplus above this fallback, with seller weight \(\omega\). For point-mass classes with \(0\lt\ell\lt\bar r\lt h\) and incumbent support \([0,\bar r]\), the spread changes weakly with the sign of \(1-2\omega\). The change is strict when \(\mathbb E[(R-\ell)_+]\) rises strictly and \(\omega\ne1/2\). The benchmark change from \(r_0\) to \(r_1\) is \([[pay_gap_coef]]\,(1-2\omega)\). The second-price rule has \(\omega=0\): the winner pays the next buyer's value. This result concerns auction payoffs.

A reserve that excludes the low class can widen the spread because low-class preparation then adds nothing to target proceeds. Informed trade can consequently pay at both incumbent strengths. If full orders recruit only after a purchase at both strengths, entry is constant. In the benchmark, reserve [[rsv_points_excl]] gives entry [[p2_reserve_points_exclude_r0_entry]] at both strengths. Reserve [[rsv_deter]] also excludes the weak incumbent, and entry falls as the incumbent strengthens.

The extensions in numbers

In the benchmark extension, low costs range from zero to [[atom_L]], and high costs are uniform on \([c_H-\varepsilon,c_H+\varepsilon]\). Every half-width below [[p4_atomless_max_width_floor_r2]] preserves all three parts of Proposition 2. At \(\varepsilon=[[atom_eps]]\), entry is [[p4_atomless_r1_entry]] at \(r_1\) and [[p4_atomless_r2_entry]] at \(r_2\).

For general classes, the construction places the first two incumbent strengths just above the lowest value at which the class distributions differ. There the target-payoff spread approaches zero, while the challenger profit gap approaches the full difference in class means. With wide uniform classes, entry rises from [[p2_ex_c_r0_entry]] to [[p2_ex_c_r1_entry]], and high-class ownership rises even though the high class can lose. Point masses at the same means leave entry at [[p2_ex_d_r1_entry]]. A four-class economy near the benchmark satisfies the separation condition with distance [[p2_groups_gamma]].

The signal example keeps the benchmark slot, values, reserve and prior. It sets \(\rho=[[sig_rho]]\), \(c_H=[[sig_cH]]\), \(k=[[sig_k]]\), \(r_0=[[sig_r0]]\) and \(r_1=[[sig_r1]]\). Against the weak incumbent, a favorable private signal leaves high-cost preparation unprofitable. Against the strong incumbent, a favorable private signal and a favorable price jointly justify preparation.

At the original benchmark costs and strengths, the conditions hold with \((a_T,d)=([[sig_comp_aT]],[[sig_comp_d]])\). Entry rises from [[p4_comp_b_r0_entry]] to [[p4_comp_b_r1_entry]]. At these parameters, a challenger signal at least as accurate as the investor's cannot satisfy the sufficient conditions. The strong trade bound requires investor accuracy that makes the weak bound fail.

The investor's expected gross profit at \(r_1\), [[p2_access_gross_transfer]], is a transfer from liquidity traders. Let one order unit equal \(z\) shares per share of float, with the signal experiment fixed. Relative to an economy without an informed investor, initial shareholders gain at every \(z\lt[[p2_access_break_even]]\), even if they bear the entire transfer.

Outside the recruitment region, set \(c_H=[[access_out_cH]]\) at the strong benchmark. Price access then changes proceeds by [[p2_outside_proceeds_gain]] and net surplus by [[p2_outside_surplus_gain]]. The reserve [[rsv_deter]] lowers entry from [[p2_reserve_points_deter_r0_entry]] to [[p2_reserve_points_deter_r1_entry]].
