Robustness, welfare and sale terms

5 · Extensions · 2 minutes

The reversal survives changes to the model

The rise in entry survives other laws of preparation costs, with or without atoms. Let the cost mix a low component with weight \(\rho\) and a high component. Retain the trading-cost window (H2), place every low cost strictly below \(B_{F_1}(m(\pi))\), and place every high cost strictly between \(B_{F_0}(\pi)\) and \(B_{F_1}(M(\pi))\). Parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition 2 then hold. The low component prepares at every feasible belief, so entry stays at least \(\rho\) and the trading bound applies unchanged. With an atomless high component, the drop at the entry ceiling becomes a ramp: full-order entry is continuous and nonincreasing in strength.

For uniform incumbents, every prior and every pair of classes that satisfy the class ranking and in which the high class has the higher mean admit an open set of strengths and costs with the reversal. This set satisfies (H1) to (H3) and the ownership condition, whatever the reserve, the zero-cost probability and the slot. The construction puts the weak and the strong incumbent just above the lowest value at which the classes start to differ above the reserve, where the spread vanishes while the profit gap stays near the full class gap. With more than two classes, prices reveal the posterior mean of class proceeds. Under a separation condition, every high-group class buys, every low-group class sells, and the reversal holds.

A price can add information even when the rival has a more accurate private signal than the informed trader. The rival knows its integration plans, and the informed trader knows the target's customers or product market. Two binary signals, independent given the class, represent the two sources; at the prior one half, under strict conditions on the costs, the weak incumbent has a quiet market and entry \(\rho\), and the strong incumbent has full orders and entry above \(\rho\). In the signal example, with its own costs and strengths, the informed trader's accuracy is \(\alpha_I=[[sig_aT]]\) and the rival's accuracy is \(\alpha_C=[[sig_d]]\). Entry rises from [[p4_s_signal_r0_entry]] to [[p4_s_signal_r1_entry]]. Without the rival's signal, entry stays at [[p4_s_no_y_r0_entry]] at both strengths, so the rise needs both sources of information.

Price access raises proceeds and surplus

Hold the incumbent at \(F_1\) and compare price access with a hidden price, under the same sale mechanism. Under the hypotheses of Proposition 2, the trading bound forces unique full orders in both economies, even when the rival cannot use the price and entry stays at \(\rho\), so trading costs coincide. Price access adds high-cost preparation after a purchase, where expected acquisition profit exceeds the preparation cost, and it raises expected target proceeds and acquisition surplus net of preparation costs. In the example at \(r_1\), expected proceeds rise from [[p2_base_hidden_proceeds]] to [[p2_base_r1_proceeds]], a gain of [[p2_access_proceeds_gain]], and net surplus rises by [[p2_access_surplus_gain]]. With research at the cap, the gains are the same. Acquisition surplus excludes transfers between bidders, shareholders and traders.

A shareholder who holds through the sale receives the higher expected proceeds. Inside the recruitment region, where high-cost preparation does not pay at the prior, price access adds preparation. When gross profit at the prior exceeds the high cost and gross profit after a sale falls below it, an informative price deters high-cost preparation after a sale.

Sale terms

The payment rule determines how incumbent strength changes the spread. Consider verifiable values and a zero reserve, with a sale to the losing bidder at its value as an enforceable fallback. The seller and the winner divide the surplus above this fallback by Nash bargaining, with seller weight \(\omega\in[0,1)\). For point-mass classes with \(0\lt\ell\lt\bar r\lt h\) and incumbent support \([0,\bar r]\), a stronger incumbent never raises the rival's profit and moves the spread only in the direction of the sign of \(1-2\omega\). The move is strict when \(\mathbb E[(R-\ell)_+]\) changes strictly and \(\omega\ne1/2\); at \(\omega=1/2\) the spread is \((h-\ell)/2\) against every incumbent. In the example, the spread under this rule changes by \([[pay_gap_coef]]\,(1-2\omega)\) from \(r_0\) to \(r_1\). The second-price auction is the case \(\omega=0\): the winner pays the losing bidder's value.

A reserve that excludes the low class can widen the spread, because low-class preparation then adds nothing to target proceeds. Informed trade can then pay against both incumbents. If informed trade is full and only a purchase recruits at both strengths, entry is flat in incumbent strength, and strict deterrence needs the stronger incumbent to push gross profit at the top posterior below the high cost. In the example, the reserve [[rsv_points_excl]] gives entry [[p2_reserve_points_exclude_r0_entry]] at both strengths. The reserve [[rsv_deter]] also excludes the weak incumbent, and entry falls as the incumbent strengthens; strict deterrence holds for reserves between about [[p2_reserve_ph]] and [[p2_reserve_weak_top]].

The extensions in numbers

In the example, low costs range from zero to [[atom_L]], and high costs are uniform on \([c_H-\varepsilon,c_H+\varepsilon]\). Every half-width below [[p4_atomless_max_width_floor_r2]] preserves all three parts of Proposition 2. At \(\varepsilon=[[atom_eps]]\), entry is [[p4_atomless_r1_entry]] at \(r_1\), the value under the two-point law, and [[p4_atomless_r2_entry]] at \(r_2\), where gross profit at the top posterior lies [[margin_return_B]] below \(c_H\), inside the band of high costs.

For general classes, the construction places the first two incumbent strengths just above the lowest value at which the class distributions differ. There the target-payoff spread approaches zero, while the rival's profit gap approaches the full difference in class means. With wide uniform classes, entry rises from [[p2_ex_c_r0_entry]] to [[p2_ex_c_r1_entry]], and high-class ownership rises although the high class can lose to the strong incumbent. Point masses at the same means leave entry at [[p2_ex_d_r1_entry]]. A prepared rival's profit is convex in its value, so an increase in risk within a class never lowers gross profit at any belief. A four-class economy near the example satisfies the separation condition with distance [[p2_groups_gamma]].

The signal example keeps the slot, values, reserve and prior of the example. It sets \(\rho=[[sig_rho]]\), \(c_H=[[sig_cH]]\), \(k=[[sig_k]]\), \(r_0=[[sig_r0]]\) and \(r_1=[[sig_r1]]\). Against the weak incumbent, a favorable private signal leaves high-cost preparation unprofitable. Against the strong incumbent, a favorable private signal and a favorable price together justify preparation.

In the example itself, the conditions hold with \((\alpha_I,\alpha_C)=([[sig_comp_aT]],[[sig_comp_d]])\). Entry rises from [[p4_comp_b_r0_entry]] to [[p4_comp_b_r1_entry]], and without the rival's signal it is [[p4_comp_b_no_y_r1_entry]] at both strengths. At the example's costs and strengths, the strong trading bound needs an accurate signal for the informed trader, and the weak bound then fails for a rival's signal at least as accurate; the signal example meets the conditions with a more accurate signal for the rival at other costs and strengths.

The informed trader's expected gross profit at \(r_1\), [[p2_access_gross_transfer]], is a transfer from liquidity traders. If one order unit is a given number of shares per share of float, initial shareholders gain in total relative to an economy without the informed trader whenever that number is below [[p2_access_break_even]], even if they bear the whole transfer. A deterministic external dividend of [[p2_access_proceeds_gain]] in the hidden-price economy equalizes the mean prices and leaves the orders and entry unchanged, so the price's information content distinguishes the two economies.

Outside the recruitment region, set \(c_H=[[access_out_cH]]\) against the strong incumbent of the example. Price access then deters preparation after a sale and changes proceeds by [[p2_outside_proceeds_gain]] and net surplus by [[p2_outside_surplus_gain]]. The reserve [[rsv_deter]] lowers entry from [[p2_reserve_points_deter_r0_entry]] to [[p2_reserve_points_deter_r1_entry]], and proceeds exceed their levels at the reserve [[prim_p]]. With a reserve between \(\ell\) and \(r_0\), the top posterior recruits at both strengths and entry is flat. Narrow uniform classes give the flat pattern at the reserve [[rsv_bands_excl]].
