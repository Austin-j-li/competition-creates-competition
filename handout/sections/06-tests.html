What the data should show

6 · Empirical implications · 2 minutes

The predictions concern publicly visible sale processes in which one bidder is prepared and a rival has not yet committed to diligence. Each applies to one target in one public state and states its conditions. Under full two-sided trade, class \(H\) buys and class \(L\) sells, each with probability one, and the informed share of nonzero orders is \(u/(2+u)\) at every prior. In the example at the prior one half, full two-sided trade is the unique outcome at every strength between \(r_S\), where a full sale earns the trading cost, and \(h\), a range that contains \(r_1\), the entry ceiling and \(r_2\).

Entry and incumbent strength

When the price reaches the rival before it decides, a stronger incumbent can raise the probability that the rival prepares: by [[entry_jump_B]] from \(r_0\) to \(r_1\) in the example. When the price does not reach the rival, a stronger incumbent can only lower it. Against a strong enough incumbent, entry can return to its floor while the price stays informative. The first and third comparisons are Proposition 2; the second is the fixed-information control.

The purchase impact carries the entry response

Under full two-sided trade at a prior below the preparation threshold, the purchase impact \(P(1)-P(0)\), weighted by the frequency of a purchase, exceeds the sale impact \(P(0)-P(-1)\), weighted by the frequency of a sale, exactly when a purchase recruits the rival. The weighted difference equals the expected price before the order opportunity minus the price after no order, so the test uses only prices and order frequencies. At the prior one half the two frequencies are equal, and the test compares the two impacts directly. The price gap \(P(1)-P(-1)\) has a class component, \(\rho\Delta_T[M(\pi)-m(\pi)]\), and an entry component, which is positive exactly when a purchase recruits; the whole entry component sits in the purchase impact. In the example at \(r_1\), the price gap is [[ba_r1_B]] and its entry component is [[ba_share_r1_B]] of it; with a hidden price it would be [[ba_class_r1_B]]. At \(r_2\) the price gap is [[ba_r2_B]], the class component alone, and at the entry ceiling it falls by [[ba_jump_rC_B]].

Prices separate what order counts cannot

Under full two-sided trade the mean order is \(\lambda(2\pi-1)\), so order counts identify the informed trader's presence when the prior is known and differs from one half. The sale impact identifies presence at any known prior, one half included: when entry after a sale and after no order sits at its floor and the payoff scale \(\rho\Delta_T\) is known, the sale impact weighted by the frequency of a sale, divided by \(\rho\Delta_T\), equals \(\pi(1-\pi)\lambda\). At the prior one half, the counts depend on the slot only through the volume of nonzero orders, so prices separate slots that counts cannot.

Orders predict the winner

Under full two-sided trade, a prepared rival wins more often after a purchase than after a sale exactly when there is a positive-probability region in which a high-class value beats the incumbent with a higher probability than a low-class value. Above the entry ceiling, orders no longer predict entry, and they still predict which bidder wins. In the example class \(H\) outbids every incumbent, and the gap is [[wgap_r1_B]] at \(r_1\) and [[wgap_r2_B]] at \(r_2\). The covariance of the order with entry is [[cov_entry_r1_B]] at \(r_1\) and zero at \(r_2\), while its covariance with a win by the rival stays positive. Among prepared rivals, the share of the high class at each price is the posterior that the price implies.

The informed share, announcements, two clusters and measurement

The gap in win frequencies is \(M(\pi)-m(\pi)\) times the difference between the classes' chances of beating the incumbent. With the prior and the slot fixed and full two-sided trade at each strength compared, when class \(H\) outbids every incumbent the gap does not narrow as the incumbent strengthens, and it widens wherever the stronger incumbent takes wins from the low class.

The sale impact and the mean order \(\lambda(2\pi-1)\) together identify both the presence probability and the prior. In the example at \(r_1\), the informed shares consistent with the order counts and the informed trader's incentives range from about [[theta_min_counts_B]] to [[theta_max_full_eq_r1_B]]. A slot with informed share [[alt_theta_B]] has the example's volume and entry, but its price gap at \(r_1\) is [[alt_ba_r1_B]] against [[ba_r1_B]].

With a uniform incumbent and the prior and the slot fixed, the entry component of the price gap rises with strength while full two-sided trade and recruitment persist, when \(3p^2\lt\min\{\underline\theta,\underline r\}^2\), where \(\underline\theta\) is the lowest value of the rival and \(\underline r\) the lowest strength compared; the example meets this condition. As the incumbent strengthens, entry adds more to the price through competition and less by rescuing a sale that the incumbent alone would not complete.

For a positive spread and \(g_H\gt g_L\), if a public event after the price reveals only whether the rival prepared, the mean price change over preparation reports is positive exactly when preparation was still uncertain after some order, at every prior and in every equilibrium: [[evprep_mean_r1_B]] at \(r_1\) in the example. The mean change over both reports, weighted by their probabilities, is zero. Under full two-sided trade at a prior below the threshold, an order opportunity that passes with no order is no news about the class and, when a purchase recruits, bad news about entry: at \(r_1\) the price after no order minus the expected price before the opportunity is [[pdrop_neutral_r1_B]], and the size of this drop is the weighted difference of the price-impact test.

At one public strength strictly between \(r_F\) and \(r_N\), sale processes can form two clusters. In one the market is uninformative: the mean order is zero and the three prices coincide. In the other the high class buys and the low class abstains; the mean order is \(\lambda/2\), or [[imb_buy_B]] in the example, the rival prepares after every purchase, and a sale executes above the price after no order, by [[U_gap_r160_B]] at [[str_r160]].

Preparation corresponds to the start of substantive diligence or a proposal that requires costly preparation; public offers are a narrower outcome. Incumbent strength is measured from information that is public before that decision, and the price must precede it. The order is the first prespecified public order opportunity after the sale becomes public, for example the opening auction on the first trading day after an approach is disclosed: its time is fixed in advance, and it takes place whether or not an order arrives. The sign of its net imbalance is the order, and an imbalance below a size fixed in advance records the zero order. A sample of first executed trades omits the zero order, so it cannot identify the volume, the effect of an inactive opportunity or the informed share. Target returns before a bid partly reflect anticipation of that bid (Schwert 1996), so each test dates the price before the rival's decision.

A stronger incumbent moves the class value gap from the rival's profit into target proceeds. Because a liquidity order can match the informed trader's direction, the informed trader keeps an advantage after every order. A wider spread can make that advantage worth the trading cost, so the price recruits a rival that a weaker incumbent could not attract.
