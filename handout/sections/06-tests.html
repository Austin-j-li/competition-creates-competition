What the data should show

6 · Empirical implications · 2 minutes

The predictions concern publicly visible sale processes in which one bidder is prepared and a further buyer has not yet committed to diligence. I state them for the benchmark auction at the prior one half, with a slot in the admissible band. Between the sale threshold \(r_S\) and \(h\), a range that contains \(r_1\), the ceiling and \(r_2\), full two-sided trade is the unique outcome.

Entry and incumbent strength

When the price reaches the further buyer before it decides, a stronger prepared bidder can raise the probability that the buyer prepares: by [[entry_jump_B]] from \(r_0\) to \(r_1\) in the benchmark. When the price does not reach the buyer, a stronger prepared bidder can only lower it. Against a strong enough prepared bidder, entry returns to its floor while the price stays informative. The first and third comparisons are Proposition 2; the second is the fixed-information control.

The purchase impact carries the entry response

The purchase impact \(P(1)-P(0)\) exceeds the sale impact \(P(0)-P(-1)\) exactly when a purchase recruits the buyer, and this comparison needs no knowledge of the auction. The price spread between a unit purchase and a unit sale has a class component and an entry component, and the whole entry component sits in the purchase impact. In the benchmark at \(r_1\), the price spread is [[ba_r1_B]] and its entry component is [[ba_share_r1_B]] of it; with a hidden price it would be [[ba_class_r1_B]]. At \(r_2\) the spread is [[ba_r2_B]], the class component alone, and at the ceiling it falls by [[ba_jump_rC_B]].

Prices separate what order counts cannot

Under full two-sided trade, the law of orders depends on the slot only through the volume of nonzero orders, so order counts cannot tell a slot with many informed orders from one with few. Prices can: given the entry floor and the target-payoff spread, the sale impact and the volume identify the informed share.

Orders predict the winner

Above the ceiling, orders no longer predict entry, but they still predict which bidder wins. In the benchmark a prepared challenger wins more often after a purchase than after a sale, by [[wgap_r1_B]] at \(r_1\) and by [[wgap_r2_B]] at \(r_2\). The covariance of the order with entry is [[cov_entry_r1_B]] at \(r_1\) and zero at \(r_2\), while its covariance with a challenger win stays positive. Among prepared challengers, the share of the high class at each price is the posterior that the price implies.

The informed share, announcements, two clusters and measurement

At \(r_1\), the informed shares consistent with both the order counts and the investor's incentives run from about [[theta_min_counts_B]] to [[theta_max_full_eq_r1_B]]. A slot with informed share [[alt_theta_B]] has the benchmark volume and the benchmark entry, but its price spread at \(r_1\) is [[alt_ba_r1_B]] against [[ba_r1_B]].

If a public event after the price reveals only whether the challenger prepared, the mean price change in a sample of preparation reports alone is positive, [[evprep_mean_r1_B]] at \(r_1\) in the benchmark, while the mean over both reports, weighted by their probabilities, is zero. Under full two-sided trade below the ceiling, an order opportunity without an order is bad news about entry and no news about the class: at \(r_1\) the price after no order minus the expected price before the opportunity is [[pdrop_neutral_r1_B]].

At one public strength between \(r_F\) and \(r_N\), sale processes can form two clusters. In one there is no informed trade, the mean order is zero and the three prices coincide. In the other the high class buys and the low class abstains; the mean order is \(\lambda/2\), or [[imb_buy_B]] in the benchmark, every challenger prepares after a purchase, and a sale executes above the price after no order, by [[U_gap_r160_B]] at [[str_r160]].

Preparation corresponds to the start of substantive diligence or a proposal that requires costly preparation; public offers are a narrower outcome. Incumbent strength is measured from information that is public before that decision, and the price must precede it. The order is the first prespecified public order opportunity after the sale becomes public, for example the opening auction on the first trading day after an approach is disclosed: its time is fixed in advance, and it takes place whether or not an order arrives. The sign of its net imbalance is the order, and an imbalance below a size fixed in advance records the zero order. A sample of first executed trades omits the zero order, so it cannot identify the volume, the effect of an inactive opportunity or the informed share. Target returns before a bid partly reflect anticipation of that bid (Schwert 1996), so each test dates the price before the buyer's decision.

A stronger incumbent moves the class value gap from the challenger's profit into target proceeds. Because a liquidity order can match the investor's direction, the investor keeps an advantage after every order, and a wider spread can make that advantage worth the cost of trade. The price then recruits a challenger that a weaker incumbent could not attract.
