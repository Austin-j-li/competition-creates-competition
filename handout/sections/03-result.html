Competition creates competition

3 · The main result · 3 minutes

A stronger incumbent can induce the information that brings a second bidder into the sale. I compare three economies that differ only in incumbent strength: \(F_0\), a stronger \(F_1\), and a stronger still \(F_2\). At \(F_0\), trade cannot cover its cost, so the price carries no class information. At \(F_1\), informed trade pays and a purchase recruits a high-cost challenger. At \(F_2\), informed trade still pays, while acquisition profit falls too far for a purchase to recruit.

Proposition 2 · Competition creates competition

Suppose the classes satisfy the class ranking and the continuous incumbent distributions satisfy the first-order stochastic order described above. Assume

\[ \begin{aligned} &\text{(H1)}\quad B_{F_0}(\pi)\lt c_H\lt B_{F_1}(M(\pi)),\\ &\text{(H2)}\quad \Delta_T(F_0)\lt k\lt\rho\,\kappa(\pi)\,\Delta_T(F_1),\\ &\text{(H3)}\quad B_{F_2}(M(\pi))\lt c_H . \end{aligned} \]

Each economy has a unique equilibrium outcome. At \(F_0\), the investor abstains in both classes and entry is \(\rho\). At \(F_1\), class \(H\) buys, class \(L\) sells, and only a purchase recruits the high-cost challenger. Entry is \(\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\pi\lambda)\gt\rho\). At \(F_2\), both classes trade, the three prices differ, and entry returns to \(\rho\).

What each condition does, and the full statement

(H1) places the preparation cost above profit at the weak incumbent's prior and below profit at the strong incumbent's upper posterior. Thus an uninformative price leaves the expensive challenger out, while a sufficiently favorable price brings it in. Under (H2), every candidate profile gives a correct order net profit at most \(\Delta_T(F_0)-k\lt0\) at \(F_0\). At \(F_1\), every candidate gives net profit at least \(\rho\kappa\Delta_T(F_1)-k\gt0\). These bounds establish the unique order choices. (H3) places \(F_2\) beyond the entry ceiling.

The cost window in (H2) is nonempty exactly when \(\Delta_T(F_0)/\Delta_T(F_1)\lt\rho\kappa(\pi)\). This condition states how much competition must widen the spread, independently of the monetary unit.

Extra preparation favors the high class: a purchase occurs with probability \(b+\lambda\) in \(H\) and \(b\) in \(L\). Entry conditional on \(H\) is therefore \(\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)\) at \(F_1\). Let \(W_H(F)\) denote the probability that a prepared high-class challenger wins. Its ownership probability rises from \(\pi\rho W_H(F_0)\) to \(\pi[\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)]W_H(F_1)\) exactly when \([\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)]W_H(F_1)\gt\rho W_H(F_0)\). This condition accounts for the harder auction against \(F_1\). In the point-mass benchmark, \(W_H=1\), so ownership rises.

The strict comparisons make the outcome independent of the tie rules. With uniform incumbents, the inequalities are continuous in \((r_0,r_1,r_2,c_H,k,\rho,\pi,\lambda,\beta)\). They define an open parameter set relative to \(\beta\le1/3\). Small changes within this set preserve the unique order regimes and the direction of the entry comparison. Prices and entry probabilities change with the parameters.

The benchmark

The benchmark satisfies all three conditions at \(r_0=[[prim_r0]]\), \(r_1=[[prim_r1]]\) and \(r_2=[[prim_r2]]\). From \(r_0\) to \(r_1\), expected acquisition profit at the prior falls from [[Bprior_r0]] to [[Bprior_r1_B]]. The target-payoff spread rises from [[Delta_r0]] to [[Delta_r1]]. The price becomes informative, and entry more than doubles:

Against the strong incumbent \(r_1\): a purchase recruits[[entry_r1_B]]

High-class ownership rises from [[own_r0]] to [[own_r1_B]] between the first two economies.

The preparation threshold \(\tau(r)\) exceeds the prior throughout the benchmark path, so an uninformative price recruits no high-cost challenger. It rises with incumbent strength to the upper posterior at \(r_C\approx[[rC_B]]\), just below \(r_2\). At the prior one half and the benchmark auction and costs, (H1) to (H3) hold exactly when \(\tau(r_1)=[[M_lo_frac]]\lt M\lt\tau(r_2)=[[M_hi_frac]]\). Equivalently, \([[u_lo_frac]]\lt u\lt[[u_hi_frac]]\).

This band is narrow because a high-class challenger always wins on the path. Its expected payment rises slowly with strength, so the preparation threshold also rises slowly. The benchmark upper posterior lies [[margin_return_post_B]] below the band's upper edge. Every strength strictly between \(r_C\) and \(h\) satisfies (H3) and can serve as the third economy. The rise in entry between the first two economies requires (H1) and (H2).

Hold information fixed, and entry cannot rise

To isolate the information effect, I fix the joint law of the class and a signal that the challenger observes. In each economy \(i\), conditional on the class and signal, the two values must have the product law \(G_j\times F_i\). The cost law stays fixed and independent of the class, both values and the signal. A stronger incumbent then weakly reduces profit at every posterior, so entry weakly falls. The decline equals the probability that the cost crosses from affordable to unaffordable under the same tie rule. The controls below show why the equilibrium rise requires a change in information.
