Competition creates competition

3 · The main result · 3 minutes

A stronger incumbent can attract a second bidder by making the price more informative. I compare three economies that differ only in incumbent strength: \(F_0\), a stronger \(F_1\), and a stronger still \(F_2\). At \(F_0\), informed trade cannot cover its cost, so the price carries no class information. At \(F_1\), informed trade pays and a purchase recruits a high-cost challenger. At \(F_2\), informed trade still pays, while acquisition profit falls too far for a purchase to recruit.

Proposition 2 · Competition creates competition

Suppose the classes satisfy the class ranking, \(F_1\) is a continuous first-order stochastic strengthening of the continuous distribution \(F_0\), and \(F_2\) strengthens \(F_1\) in the same sense. Let \(0\lt\pi,\rho,\lambda\lt1\) and \(0\lt\beta\lt\tfrac12\), and assume the high-cost window (H1), the trading-cost window (H2) and the exclusion condition (H3):

\[ \begin{aligned} &\text{(H1)}\quad B_{F_0}(\pi)\lt c_H\lt B_{F_1}(M(\pi)),\\ &\text{(H2)}\quad \Delta_T(F_0)\lt k\lt\rho\,\kappa(\pi)\,\Delta_T(F_1),\\ &\text{(H3)}\quad B_{F_2}(M(\pi))\lt c_H . \end{aligned} \]

Each economy has a unique equilibrium outcome under all four tie rules. At \(F_0\), the investor abstains in both classes and entry is \(\rho\). At \(F_1\), class \(H\) buys, class \(L\) sells, and only a purchase recruits the high-cost challenger. Entry is \(\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\pi\lambda)\gt\rho\), and high-class ownership rises exactly when \(e_HW_H(F_1)\gt\rho W_H(F_0)\), where \(e_H=\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)\) is entry in class \(H\). At \(F_2\), both classes trade, the three prices differ, and entry returns to \(\rho\).

What each condition does, and the full statement

The high-cost window (H1) sets entry through acquisition profit. Gross profit at the prior falls short of the high cost against \(F_0\), so an uninformative price recruits no high-cost challenger; gross profit at the top posterior exceeds the high cost against \(F_1\), so a purchase can recruit one. The trading-cost window (H2) fixes trading before any order profile is known: by the trading bound, every correct order earns at most \(\Delta_T(F_0)-k\lt0\) at \(F_0\) and at least \(\rho\kappa\Delta_T(F_1)-k\gt0\) at \(F_1\). The exclusion condition (H3) stops recruitment at \(F_2\), where gross profit at the top posterior falls short of the high cost.

The trading-cost window is nonempty exactly when \(\Delta_T(F_0)/\Delta_T(F_1)\lt\rho\kappa(\pi)\): competition must widen the spread enough between the two incumbent distributions. The condition involves no unit of money, and a prior away from one half narrows the window, because the price already leans toward the favored class.

Extra preparation favors the high class: a purchase occurs with probability \(b+\lambda\) in \(H\) and \(b\) in \(L\). High-class ownership multiplies entry in class \(H\) by the probability \(W_H(F)\) that a prepared high-class challenger wins, and a stronger incumbent can lower that probability. The ownership condition holds whenever the stronger incumbent leaves the high class a good chance to win, \((1+\rho)W_H(F_1)\ge2\rho W_H(F_0)\gt0\), and the high class places a purchase more often than not, \(b+\lambda\gt\tfrac12\). When class \(H\) outbids \(F_1\), it outbids \(F_0\) too, ownership rises from \(\pi\rho\) to \(\pi e_H\), and class \(H\)'s share of the takeovers that go to a challenger does not fall; it rises strictly when a low-class challenger beats the weak incumbent with positive probability.

Class \(H\) outbids every incumbent against which a price recruits when it has a decisive advantage, a property of values and costs alone. With point-mass classes and uniform incumbents, the advantage holds exactly when \(c_H\ge(h^2-p^2)/(2h)\); in the example this floor is [[cH_DA_floor_B]], below the high cost.

Every comparison is strict, so the outcome in each economy does not depend on how ties are broken. With the example's classes and reserve, the inequalities are strict between continuous functions of \((r_0,r_1,r_2,c_H,k,\rho,\pi,\lambda,\beta)\), so they hold on an open set of these parameters that contains the example. Small changes within this set preserve the unique order regimes and the direction of the entry comparison.

The example

The example satisfies all three conditions at \(r_0=[[prim_r0]]\), \(r_1=[[prim_r1]]\) and \(r_2=[[prim_r2]]\). From \(r_0\) to \(r_1\), expected acquisition profit at the prior falls from [[Bprior_r0]] to [[Bprior_r1_B]], while the target-payoff spread rises from [[Delta_r0]] to [[Delta_r1]]. The price becomes informative, and entry more than doubles:

Entry against the weak incumbent \(r_0\): only zero-cost challengers[[entry_r0]]
Against the strong incumbent \(r_1\): a purchase recruits[[entry_r1_B]]
Against the very strong incumbent \(r_2\): above the entry ceiling[[entry_r2]]

Between the first two economies, high-class ownership rises from [[own_r0]] to [[own_r1_B]], and the high class's share of the takeovers that go to a challenger rises from [[share_H_r0]] to [[share_H_r1_B]].

Corollary 1 gives the slots at which this pattern holds. At the prior one half and the example's auction and costs, (H1) to (H3) hold exactly when the top posterior lies between the preparation thresholds of the strong and the very strong incumbent, \(\tau(r_1)=[[M_lo_frac]]\lt M\lt\tau(r_2)=[[M_hi_frac]]\); equivalently, \([[u_lo_frac]]\lt u\lt[[u_hi_frac]]\). On this set class \(H\) outbids every incumbent on the path, entry rises from \(\rho\) at \(r_0\) to \(\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\pi\lambda)\) at \(r_1\) and returns to \(\rho\) at \(r_2\), and high-class ownership rises from \(\pi\rho\) to \(\pi[\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)]\).

The preparation threshold \(\tau(r)\) exceeds the prior along the whole path, so an uninformative price recruits no high-cost challenger. It rises with strength until it meets the top posterior at the entry ceiling \(r_C\approx[[rC_B]]\), just below \(r_2\). The exclusion condition holds at every strength between \(r_C\) and \(h\), and parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition 2 hold without it. The band for \(M\) is narrow because class \(H\) outbids every incumbent on the path: its expected payment \(r/2+p^2/(2r)\) rises by less than one half per unit of strength, a small change next to the profit gap \(g_H-g_L\), so the threshold rises slowly. The example's top posterior lies [[margin_return_post_B]] below the band's upper edge.

Hold information fixed, and entry cannot rise

To isolate the information effect, I fix the joint law of the class and a signal that the challenger observes, the class value laws and the cost law. Given the class, the signal tells the challenger nothing more about either value; the incumbent's value is independent of the class and the signal, and the two values are independent given both. The cost is independent of the class, both values and the signal. A stronger incumbent then lowers gross profit at every belief, so it can only remove a preparation incentive. Entry falls by exactly the probability that the cost lies between gross profit against the two incumbent distributions. In the order slot, this experiment fixes \(\lambda\), \(\beta\), \(\pi\), the cost law and both order distributions, and the price then reveals the same posterior after each order at every strength.
