What survives

The reversal survives a different noise law, atomless costs, a small value gap, and a buyer who knows more than the investor. Each variant keeps the two things the argument uses: a global cap on what any order can reveal, and an entry floor that keeps the price invertible. What the variants change is the tail, which is how often the price is good enough to bring the expensive challenger in.

Noise and preparation costs

All the trading argument uses of the noise law is a bound on its log-density derivative, which logistic noise also has, so Proposition A.5 keeps the unique trading outcomes and the entry comparisons. What changes is the tail: strong-economy entry is [[logistic_entry_strong]] under logistic noise against [[base_entry_strong]] under Laplace, because the logistic flow threshold sits [[logistic_threshold_noise_sd]] noise standard deviations out. Atomless costs of half-width [[cost_halfwidth]] keep the entry floor and the favorable-price entry event, so Proposition A.6 keeps parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition 2 under either noise law (Table 3).

Acquisition values

With \(h=[[moderate_h]]\) and \(\ell=[[moderate_ell]]\), every strict inequality of Proposition 2 holds and entry rises from [[moderate_entry_weak]] to [[moderate_entry_strong]] between \(r_0=[[moderate_r_weak]]\) and \(r_1=[[moderate_r_strong]]\). What the reversal needs is the placement of incumbent strength relative to the low acquisition value, not a large value gap.

Complementary private information

The price is worth reading even when the buyer knows more than the investor. A buyer knows its own integration plans; a specialist investor may know what the target's customers are doing, how its technology compares with alternatives, or where its demand is heading. These are different facts about the same match, and a more accurate signal about one need not contain the other. With a binary investor signal of accuracy \(a\) and a conditionally independent buyer signal of accuracy \(d\), the entry floor keeps a strictly positive coefficient on public beliefs, so the price still reveals the market's posterior. Proposition A.7 gives sufficient conditions for the same unique outcomes, and nothing requires \(a\gt d\): with \(d=[[signal_buyer_accuracy]]\) and \(a=[[signal_trader_accuracy]]\), entry rises from [[signal_entry_weak]] to [[signal_entry_strong]]. A favorable private signal alone does not justify high-cost preparation against the weak incumbent; combined with a favorable price, it does against the strong one.
