What survives

The reversal survives a different noise law, atomless costs, a small value gap, and a buyer who knows more than the investor. Each replacement keeps the two ingredients of the proof: a global bound on what any order can reveal, and an entry floor that keeps the price invertible.

Noise and preparation costs

Logistic noise has a log-density derivative bounded by \(1/b\), which is all the global trading argument uses, so Proposition A.5 carries the unique trading outcomes and the entry comparisons across unchanged. What changes is the tail. In the strong benchmark economy entry is [[logistic_entry_strong]] under logistic noise against [[base_entry_strong]] under Laplace noise, because the logistic flow threshold sits [[logistic_threshold_noise_sd]] noise standard deviations from the center. The same posterior bounds support different participation rates; the mass of favorable information near the upper bound matters, not only the bound.

Atomless preparation costs of half-width [[cost_halfwidth]] around each atom preserve the entry floor and the favorable-price entry event, so Proposition A.6 keeps the unique trading outcomes and parts (i) and (ii) of Proposition 2 under either noise law. Strong-incumbent entry is [[cost_mix_laplace_entry_strong]] under Laplace noise and [[cost_mix_logistic_entry_strong]] under logistic noise.

Acquisition values

With \(h=[[moderate_h]]\) and \(\ell=[[moderate_ell]]\), every strict inequality of Proposition 2 holds and entry rises from [[moderate_entry_weak]] to [[moderate_entry_strong]] between \(r_0=[[moderate_r_weak]]\) and \(r_1=[[moderate_r_strong]]\). The general construction picks both strengths close to \(\ell\), where \(B_\ell(M)-B_\ell(1/2)=(M-1/2)(h-\ell)\gt0\) for any \(h\gt\ell\); what the reversal needs is the placement of incumbent strength relative to the low acquisition value, not a large gap between the values.

Complementary private information

The price is worth reading even when the buyer already knows more than the investor. Let the investor observe a binary signal with accuracy \(a\) and the buyer a conditionally independent one with accuracy \(d\), seen together with the price before preparing. Entry becomes state-dependent even conditional on the price, since a high-value challenger is more likely to receive good private news, but the entry floor leaves a strictly positive coefficient on public beliefs, so the price still reveals the market's posterior. Proposition A.7 gives sufficient conditions for unique zero orders in the weak economy and unique full signal-contingent orders with entry above \(\rho\) in the strong one. Nothing requires \(a\gt d\): in the declared example \(d=[[signal_buyer_accuracy]]\) and \(a=[[signal_trader_accuracy]]\), and entry rises from [[signal_entry_weak]] to [[signal_entry_strong]]. A favorable private signal alone does not justify high-cost preparation against the weak incumbent; combined with a favorable price, it does against the strong one.
