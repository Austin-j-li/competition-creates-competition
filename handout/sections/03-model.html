The model

Values, preparation, and the sale

A listed target with standalone value zero is sold through a cash second-price auction with reserve \(p\); values and costs are per share. The incumbent has already prepared and knows its value \(R\), uniform on \([0,r]\); a higher \(r\) strengthens it in the first-order stochastic sense. The challenger has value \(\theta\in\{\ell,h\}\) with equal priors, does not know it, and after observing the stock price learns its preparation cost \(C\), equal to \(c_L\) with probability \(\rho\) and \(c_H\) otherwise; paying \(C\) reveals \(\theta\) and permits bidding. The benchmark restricts values and the reserve to

\[ 0\lt p\lt\ell\lt r\lt h. \tag{1} \]

Bids are truthful and weakly dominant; the winner pays the larger of the reserve and the highest competing bid. A challenger indifferent about preparation enters, a tie rule that matters at exactly one posterior plateau in the results.

Trading and pricing

An investor observes \(\theta\) and submits an order \(q\in[-1,1]\) in a small unit with no control rights, paying a linear trading cost \(k|q|\). Its profit and aggregate order flow are

\[ \begin{gathered} q\{V_T-P(X)\}-k|q|,\qquad X=q+Z,\\ f(z)=\frac{1}{2b}e^{-|z|/b},\qquad b\gt 1, \end{gathered} \tag{2} \]

where \(V_T\) is the terminal payoff of a target share and \(Z\) is independent Laplace noise, whose bounded likelihood ratios cap what any order can reveal. Competitive market makers observe aggregate flow and set

\[ P(X)=\mathbb E[V_T\mid X], \tag{3} \]

anticipating preparation and auction outcomes. The challenger observes \(P\), not \(X\).

Timing

Six moves, in order. Select a step to see what each player knows at that point.

  1. The seller commits publicly to a cash second-price auction with reserve \(p\). Everyone knows \(r\), the prior on \(\theta\), and the cost distribution. Only the incumbent knows \(R\).
  2. The investor learns \(\theta\) and submits \(q\in[-1,1]\). Noise demand \(Z\) arrives independently. Aggregate flow is \(X=q+Z\).
  3. Market makers see \(X\) but neither \(q\) nor \(\theta\). They set \(P(X)=\mathbb E[V_T\mid X]\), anticipating the challenger's entry rule and the auction.
  4. The challenger sees the price \(P\) and its private cost \(C\). It still does not know \(\theta\). It pays \(C\) to learn \(\theta\) and become a bidder, or it stays out.
  5. Informed bidders bid truthfully. The incumbent bids \(R\); an entered challenger bids \(\theta\). The highest admissible bid wins and pays the larger of the reserve and the competing bid.
  6. Ownership changes hands and the target's shareholders receive the sale proceeds, which determine \(V_T\). The investor's position pays \(q(V_T-P)\) less its trading cost.

Equilibrium and notation

An equilibrium specifies conditional order distributions \(\sigma_H,\sigma_L\), a measurable price function, beliefs given the price, optimal preparation, and truthful bidding; the investor can mix and can deviate to any order in \([-1,1]\). Write \(t_0\) for expected target proceeds without entry, \(t_H,t_L\) for proceeds given entry and quality, \(g_H,g_L\) for the challenger's gross profits, \(\Delta_T=t_H-t_L\) for the target-payoff spread, and \(B_r(\mu)=g_L+\mu(g_H-g_L)\) for expected gross profit at belief \(\mu=\Pr(\theta=h)\).
