The model

Values, preparation, and the sale

A listed target with standalone value normalized to zero is sold through a cash second-price auction with reserve \(p\); values and costs are per target share. The incumbent has already prepared. Its acquisition value \(R\) is uniform on \([0,r]\), so a higher \(r\) strengthens it in the sense of first-order stochastic dominance, and it knows \(R\) before bidding.

The challenger has acquisition value \(\theta\in\{\ell,h\}\) with equal prior probabilities. The benchmark restricts values and the reserve to

\[ 0\lt p\lt\ell\lt r\lt h. \tag{1} \]

The challenger does not know its value at the start. After observing the stock price it privately learns its preparation cost \(C\), equal to \(c_L\) with probability \(\rho\) and \(c_H\) otherwise, with \(0\lt\rho\lt 1\) and \(0\le c_L\lt c_H\). Paying \(C\) reveals \(\theta\) and permits bidding; declining leaves the challenger outside the sale. Incumbent value, challenger value, preparation cost, and noise demand are independent.

The seller commits to the auction before trading. The highest admissible bidder acquires the target and pays the larger of the reserve and the highest competing bid; bids are truthful and weakly dominant. A challenger indifferent about preparation enters. That tie rule matters in exactly one place, a posterior plateau that appears in the results.

Trading and pricing

An investor observes \(\theta\) and submits an order \(q\in[-1,1]\) in a small reference unit with no control rights. It holds no initial position, cannot acquire the target, and pays a linear trading cost \(k|q|\). Its profit and aggregate order flow are

\[ \begin{gathered} q\{V_T-P(X)\}-k|q|,\qquad X=q+Z,\\ f(z)=\frac{1}{2b}e^{-|z|/b},\qquad b\gt 1, \end{gathered} \tag{2} \]

where \(V_T\) is the terminal payoff of a target share and \(Z\) is independent noise demand with a Laplace density. The Laplace law has bounded likelihood ratios, which caps the information any order can reveal. Competitive market makers observe aggregate flow and set

\[ P(X)=\mathbb E[V_T\mid X], \tag{3} \]

anticipating preparation and auction outcomes. The challenger observes \(P\), not \(X\).

Timing

Six moves, in order. Select a step to see what each player knows at that point.

  1. The seller commits publicly to a cash second-price auction with reserve \(p\). Everyone knows \(r\), the prior on \(\theta\), and the cost distribution. Only the incumbent knows \(R\).
  2. The investor learns \(\theta\) and submits \(q\in[-1,1]\). Noise demand \(Z\) arrives independently. Aggregate flow is \(X=q+Z\).
  3. Market makers see \(X\) but neither \(q\) nor \(\theta\). They set \(P(X)=\mathbb E[V_T\mid X]\), anticipating the challenger's entry rule and the auction.
  4. The challenger sees the price \(P\) and its private cost \(C\). It still does not know \(\theta\). It pays \(C\) to learn \(\theta\) and become a bidder, or it stays out.
  5. Informed bidders bid truthfully. The incumbent bids \(R\); an entered challenger bids \(\theta\). The highest admissible bid wins and pays the larger of the reserve and the competing bid.
  6. Ownership changes hands and the target's shareholders receive the sale proceeds, which determine \(V_T\). The investor's position pays \(q(V_T-P)\) less its trading cost.

Equilibrium

An equilibrium specifies conditional order distributions \(\sigma_H,\sigma_L\), a measurable price function, Bayesian beliefs conditional on the observed price, optimal preparation, and truthful bidding. The investor can mix and can deviate to any order in \([-1,1]\). Two comparisons should be kept apart: a unilateral deviation changes the distribution of order flow with the candidate equilibrium's pricing and preparation schedules held fixed, whereas a comparison across incumbent strengths re-solves those schedules.

Notation. \(t_0\) is expected target proceeds without entry, \(t_H,t_L\) are proceeds conditional on entry and quality, and \(g_H,g_L\) are the challenger's corresponding gross acquisition profits. The target-payoff spread is \(\Delta_T=t_H-t_L\), and at belief \(\mu=\Pr(\theta=h)\) the challenger's expected gross profit is \(B_r(\mu)=g_L+\mu(g_H-g_L)\).
