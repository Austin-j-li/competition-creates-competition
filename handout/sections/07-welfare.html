Welfare and sale terms

Sale terms reach participation through the buyer's expected payment and through the information the market supplies. Here competition is fixed and the second channel varies.

Access to prices

Hold the incumbent at \(r_1\) and compare the feedback equilibrium with one in which the challenger cannot observe the price. The trading bound forces full orders in both, so trading costs coincide and price access changes only which high-cost challengers prepare; each added entrant's gross profit covers its preparation cost, so Proposition A.9 delivers strictly higher target proceeds ([[base_revenue_feedback]] against [[base_revenue_hidden]]) and net acquisition surplus (up by [[base_net_surplus_gain]]). The result ranks access to information under a given sale rule; it ranks neither incumbent strengths nor mechanisms.

Separating information from the price level

A deterministic dividend of [[base_matched_dividend]] added to the traded claim in the price-hidden economy matches mean prices across the two economies while leaving \(V_T-P\), trading incentives, and entry unchanged. Entry still differs, so the gap is information, not the price level. The dividend is a diagnostic payment, outside acquisition surplus and outside the seller's feasible terms.

The payment rule

Whether competition raises the spread depends on how the payment responds to the runner-up's value. In a verifiable-value institution with zero reserve, the highest-value buyer acquires the target, a sale to the next-best buyer at its value is an enforceable fallback, and seller and winner split the surplus above it by Nash bargaining with seller share \(\eta\):

\[ \begin{aligned} T_\eta(R,\theta)&=(1-\eta)\min\{R,\theta\}+\eta\max\{R,\theta\},\\ G_{\theta,\eta}&=(1-\eta)\mathbb E[(\theta-R)_+],\\ \Delta_\eta&=\eta(h-\ell)+(1-2\eta)\mathbb E[(R-\ell)_+]. \end{aligned} \tag{14} \]

Proposition A.8: a stronger incumbent weakly reduces challenger profit at every \(\eta\), and raises the spread for \(\eta\lt1/2\), leaves it at \(\eta=1/2\), and lowers it above. For \(R\gt\ell\) the high-value winner's payment moves with \(R\) at rate \(1-\eta\) and the incumbent's payment after a low-value loss at rate \(\eta\); competition widens the difference exactly when the first rate exceeds the second. The second-price benchmark is \(\eta=0\), so the opposition behind Proposition 2 is a property of that payment rule. An entry reversal under bargaining would require solving its trading and participation game, which the paper does not do.

Reserve comparisons

A reserve that excludes the low-value challenger widens the difference in target proceeds across challenger types and can support informative trading even against a weak incumbent. With quality identifying a value class (values uniform around \(\ell\) and \(h\) at half-width \(\varepsilon_V=[[value_band_halfwidth]]\)), raising the reserve from [[base_p]] to [[value_reserve_high]] lifts weak-incumbent revenue from [[value_revenue_weak_low_p]] to [[value_revenue_weak_high_p]] and strong-incumbent revenue from [[value_revenue_strong_low_p]] to [[value_revenue_strong_high_p]], with entry [[value_entry_weak_high_p]] and [[value_entry_strong_high_p]] and informative trading in both economies. This is a feasible improvement, not an optimal reserve: several continuations coexist at many reserves, so an envelope of found revenues is not the seller's objective.
