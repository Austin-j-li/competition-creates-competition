Welfare and sale terms

Sale terms reach participation through the buyer's expected payment and through the information the stock market supplies. This section holds competition fixed and varies the second channel: by removing price access, by changing the payment rule, and by moving the reserve.

Access to prices

Access to prices raises acquisition outcomes at a fixed level of competition. Hold the incumbent at \(r_1\) and compare the feedback equilibrium with an economy in which the challenger cannot observe the price, reoptimizing trading and pricing in both. The global trading bound forces full orders in both, so the order-flow experiment and the trading costs coincide, and price access changes only which high-cost challengers prepare. Each additional entrant's expected gross profit covers its preparation cost, so Proposition A.9 delivers a strict gain in expected target proceeds and in acquisition surplus net of preparation costs. In the benchmark, expected target proceeds are [[base_revenue_feedback]] with price access and [[base_revenue_hidden]] without it, and net acquisition surplus rises by [[base_net_surplus_gain]].

A deterministic dividend of [[base_matched_dividend]] added to the traded claim in the price-hidden economy matches mean prices across the two economies while leaving \(V_T-P\), trading incentives, and entry unchanged; entry still differs, so the gap is information, not the price level. The result concerns access to information under a given sale rule and incumbent distribution. It ranks neither incumbent strengths nor mechanisms.

The payment rule

Whether competition raises the target-payoff spread depends on how the payment responds to the runner-up's value. In a verifiable-value institution with zero reserve, the highest-value buyer acquires the target after preparation, a sale to the next-best buyer at its value is an enforceable fallback, and the seller and the winner split the surplus above it by Nash bargaining with seller share \(\eta\). For an incumbent distribution on \([0,R_{\max}]\) with \(\ell\lt R_{\max}\lt h\) and \(0\le\eta\lt1\), the transfer, challenger profit, and spread are

\[ \begin{aligned} T_\eta(R,\theta)&=(1-\eta)\min\{R,\theta\}+\eta\max\{R,\theta\},\\ G_{\theta,\eta}&=(1-\eta)\mathbb E[(\theta-R)_+],\\ \Delta_\eta&=\eta(h-\ell)+(1-2\eta)\mathbb E[(R-\ell)_+]. \end{aligned} \tag{14} \]

Proposition A.8 gives the comparative statics. A stronger incumbent weakly reduces challenger profit at every bargaining weight, and its effect on the spread is positive for \(\eta\lt1/2\), zero at \(\eta=1/2\), and negative above. Take \(R\gt\ell\) to see why: a high-value challenger wins and pays \((1-\eta)R+\eta h\), which moves with \(R\) at rate \(1-\eta\), while a low-value challenger loses and the incumbent pays \((1-\eta)\ell+\eta R\), which moves at rate \(\eta\). Competition widens the difference exactly when the first rate exceeds the second. The second-price benchmark is the case \(\eta=0\), so the opposition between acquisition profit and information spread that drives Proposition 2 is a property of that payment rule, not of takeover competition in general.

These are payment-stage results. An entry reversal under bargaining would require solving its trading and participation game, which the paper does not do.

Reserve comparisons

A reserve changes both the payment required to win and the information carried by target shares. One that excludes the low-value challenger widens the difference in target proceeds across challenger types, which can support informative trading even against a weak incumbent. To keep exclusion from resting on a value atom, let quality identify a value class with conditional values uniform around \(\ell\) and \(h\) at half-width \(\varepsilon_V=[[value_band_halfwidth]]\). Raising the reserve from [[base_p]] to [[value_reserve_high]] lifts weak-incumbent revenue from [[value_revenue_weak_low_p]] to [[value_revenue_weak_high_p]] and strong-incumbent revenue from [[value_revenue_strong_low_p]] to [[value_revenue_strong_high_p]]; entry at the higher reserve is [[value_entry_weak_high_p]] and [[value_entry_strong_high_p]], with informative trading in both economies and a unique continuation at each reported reserve.

The higher reserve is a feasible improvement, not an optimal reserve. The sweep over the reserve domain reports outcomes among continuations found and records unresolved reserves; with several continuations coexisting at many reserves, an envelope of found revenues is not the seller's objective.
