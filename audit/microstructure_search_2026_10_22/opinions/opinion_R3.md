**R3 — independent opinion**

**1. Main-text pick.** I choose E, Order slot, with two-sided unit orders and equal probabilities for the three liquidity actions.

**2. Second choice and appendix.** A, Current Laplace, is my second choice. Keep A as a separate continuous-order extension in an appendix. It preserves the partial-sale result and the broader robustness results under its own assumptions. A remark should distinguish E's alternative arrivals from EGJ's additive orders. F can supply an appendix bridge if that link matters. Its equal-weight case gives rising entry at 1.56, 1.58, and 1.60; the original three strengths have no special economic status.

**3. Reasons.**

- E preserves the main economic claims with analytical proofs. All three benchmark outcomes of Proposition 2 remain unique on its menu. Entry follows 0.25, about 0.544, and 0.25 at strengths 1.2, 3, and 3.6. Price access raises target proceeds and net surplus. T2 and X_check support these claims.

- Dow–Gorton (1997) supplies a verified precedent for mutually exclusive informed and same-size liquidity arrivals. E adapts that device; it does not reproduce their complete model. L1 supports this narrower attribution. The selected literature sample does not establish that Laplace noise is unacceptable.

- E gives incomplete revelation a simple economic source: a purchase can reflect information or liquidity needs. Every feasible unit-menu flow has positive mass in both classes. Bayes' rule determines the relevant beliefs. Exogenous liquidity is plausible, but a trader who maximizes trade profits cannot supply the proposed liquidity orders without another motive.

- Coexistence holds on about 1.394–1.748. A strictly rising informative branch exists below about 1.461 and requires investor mixing. Entry is flat at 1.55–1.65, and the partial-sale result disappears. Recast Proposition 3 around coexistence and state the shorter interval with rising entry. Do not claim that its current statement survives.

- At equal liquidity probabilities, the admissible presence interval is about 0.317–0.365. This is an open interval, not a knife-edge. Its width reflects the required posterior thresholds at strengths 3 and 3.6. A faces the same threshold constraint, and its benchmark lies near the upper edge. B–D lose the benchmark ceiling; I keeps a Laplace law; H adds directional restrictions. G's small certified box and F's extra noise source offer less attractive main-text choices.

- The rewrite has a real cost. Reprove the main statements for E and rebuild the exhibits. Check each extension before the paper retains it. The current proofs for general classes, private signals, and continuous orders do not transfer automatically. A remains the safer choice if that full scope is essential.

**4. Main weakness and presentation.** E places substantial weight on one exclusive arrival and a common unit size. State these assumptions in the model, together with the exogenous liquidity motive. Limit uniqueness to the finite menu. Continuous orders create unused flows and require extra beliefs. X_check shows that a fractional purchase can upset the outcome at strength 3.6 under prior beliefs at that flow. Present E as a simple arrival model with these limits, not as a complete account of market activity.

**5. What would change my mind.** I would choose A if continuous-order deviations or the partial-sale mechanism are essential contributions. I would also choose A if the complementary-information result cannot retain comparable force under E. Conversely, an independently checked F with broad parameter slack and a short proof could become my first choice. A move from 1.55 to 1.56 would not count against it.

**6. Confidence.** Medium. E best balances precedent, transparent proofs, and the main economic results. Its wider extensions remain unproved in the permitted record.
