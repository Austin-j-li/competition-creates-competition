# Opinion R4: choice of trading microstructure

## 1. Pick for the main text

Keep candidate A, the current continuous-order model, but present the noise assumption as the score-bounded class of Proposition A.5, with Laplace noise as its tractable extreme member.

## 2. Second choice, appendix, remarks

Second choice: candidate E, the order slot. Put E in an appendix or robustness section as a discrete counterpart at the benchmark: unit orders, Dow–Gorton (1997) arrival, Proposition 2 in full, coexistence on [1.394, 1.748], and the welfare result. Add one short remark with two impossibility facts. With bounded additive noise (candidates C and D) the top flow reveals class H, so Proposition 2(iii) cannot hold at the benchmark. With Gaussian noise (candidate B) the likelihood ratio has no bound, so it cannot hold either. Do not use candidate G anywhere.

## 3. Reasons

- **Survival of results.** A is the only candidate with all of Proposition 2, Proposition 3 with entry that rises at 1.55–1.65, the partial-sale pattern (1, −v), and welfare. It also has the extensions to general classes, priors, atomless costs, and private signals. C and D lose Proposition 2(iii) by a theorem, not by a bad calibration.
- **Analytical strength.** In A, uniqueness covers mixed orders and every deviation in [−1, 1]. E needs the unit menu as a hard primitive. With fractional orders, its part (iii) needs an off-path belief rule. D needs investor mixing, and has no equilibrium at strength 3 under the EGJ pure-strategy tie rule.
- **Conventionality.** The survey shows no dominant law: the largest group has 3 of 15 models. The paper's real assumption is a bounded score and bounded posteriors. Laplace is the extreme member of that class and has three standard constructions. That is a defensible position, not an ad hoc density.
- **Robustness.** A switch does not remove tuning. Any device that fits the benchmark needs a top posterior in (0.705, 0.731). E puts this in a presence band of 0.317–0.365. D uses noise weights (0.1, 0.8, 0.1) with no verified precedent. G works only in a box about 0.001 wide and can give prices that do not rise with flow.
- **Economic plausibility.** E needs an exogenous liquidity motive: a free uninformed trader in the slot would choose zero. F and G stack two noise layers with no clear story for the second layer. H lets noise traders only buy, which fits a target with selling holders poorly.
- **Rewrite cost.** The results for E, F, and G cover point masses at prior 1/2. Sections 5 and 6 of the paper have no counterpart for them. A main-text switch would trade proved generality for a benchmark result. An appendix version of E gives most of the benefit at low cost. E is also the one discrete candidate with an independent check.

## 4. Main weakness of the pick, and its presentation

The size of the effect depends on the noise law: entry at strength 3 is 0.523 under Laplace noise and 0.302 under logistic noise. No model in the survey uses Laplace noise. The benchmark strength 3.6 also sits just above the ceiling 3.593. Present these facts in the open. State the theorem for the noise class first. Say that Laplace gives the most informative flow at a fixed score bound, so its entry number is an upper case. Print the logistic number next to it in the main table. Claim robustness for the sign of the reversal, not for its size.

## 5. What would change my mind

Two things. First, a checked version of E beyond the benchmark: general classes and priors, atomless costs, and private signals, with entry that rises at 1.55–1.65 on an open set. Then E could carry the main text. Second, direct evidence that editors or referees reject the class-based framing of A. In that case switch to E, not to D or G.

## 6. Confidence

Medium.
