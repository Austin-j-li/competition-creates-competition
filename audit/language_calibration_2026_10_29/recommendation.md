# Terminology recommendation for discussion

29 October 2026. No manuscript, deck, or website edits.

## Decision

Prefer **incumbent bidder / potential rival bidder** at first use, then **incumbent / rival** as fixed actor names.

This recommendation combines established vocabulary with the model's economic roles. It does not claim that this exact pair dominates the literature.

Use this definition:

> The incumbent bidder is already prepared and faces no further preparation cost. The potential rival bidder observes the target's stock price before it decides whether to prepare. Preparation reveals the rival's acquisition value and permits a bid.

Incumbent refers to preparation status in this sale process. It does not imply target ownership, a product-market position, or an earlier public offer.

The current model takes incumbent preparation as given. It does not specify a positive historical expenditure. The rival's preparation cost can be zero.

## What the literature supports

- Hirshleifer and Png use first bidder and second bidder. Their first offer precedes the second party's investigation. These are established terms, but the offer sequence differs here. [Publisher abstract, 1989, pp. 587–606](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/2/4/587/1586915).
- Fishman's abstract places costly information acquisition after bids begin. This confirms the timing distinction, but does not verify his internal actor labels. [Author's institution](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1988/a-theory-of-preemptive-takeover-bidding/).
- Bulow and Klemperer use incumbent, potential bidder, potential rival, and potential entrant. Their incumbent holds the current offer in a sequential sale. The terms are established, but incumbent still needs a local definition here. [Author manuscript, pp. 2–3 and 36](https://www.nuff.ox.ac.uk/Economics/Papers/2009/w5/wdaforweb29jun09.pdf).
- Gentry and Stroup distinguish potential bidders from actual entry and due diligence. Their sequential-sale incumbent is also a contest role. See the source versions and access limits in [the model note](model_language.md).

These examples establish usage. They do not measure which pair is most common. The exact incumbent/challenger pair has weaker direct support in this source set.

## Why this pair

**Potential rival bidder** states the unresolved participation decision and locates the rivalry in the takeover. After its definition, rival can remain the actor's name throughout.

**Potential entrant** is equally defensible at the entry stage. It is less convenient as a permanent name after participation becomes known. Use entrant to describe participation status where needed.

**Incumbent/challenger** remains an acceptable alternative if continuity with the manuscript takes priority. It needs the same definition. There is no evidence-based reason to present it as the uniquely standard pair.

**Initial bidder** risks an earlier-offer implication. **Prepared/unprepared bidder** cease to distinguish the actors after rival preparation. **Informed/uninformed bidder** confuse preparation with the separate stock-market information channel.

## Other terms

| Object | Recommended term | Definition or limit |
|---|---|---|
| Financial actor | Informed trader | An outside actor that knows the rival's synergy class and can trade target shares. It cannot acquire the target. |
| Exogenous stock trader | Liquidity trader | Its order is exogenous and independent of the synergy class. Liquidity motives supply the interpretation. |
| Intermediaries | Competitive market makers | They observe the order and set a conditional expected-payoff price. |
| Participation decision | Entry | The decision to incur preparation cost and become eligible to bid. Entry need not produce an admissible bid or acquisition. |
| Participation cost | Preparation cost | Includes diligence and the other requirements for an executable offer. Due diligence alone is narrower. |
| Comparative static | Incumbent strength | A shift of the acquisition-value distribution under first-order stochastic dominance. It is not an observed bid or realized value. |
| Latent acquisition match | High/low synergy class | The class indexes the distribution of acquisition value. The trader knows the class, not the realized value. |

Informed investor is also established and accurate. A switch to informed trader is a consistency choice, not a correction of an error. Choose one permanent label.

Dow and Gorton use informed traders, liquidity traders, and a marketmaker in an alternative-arrival model. This gives a close precedent for the proposed stock-market vocabulary. [Published paper, p. 1095](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/nlangberg/papers_shiva/Dow%20Gorton%201997%20JF.pdf).

Kyle's noise trader is also established. No switch from liquidity trader is necessary merely to claim literature conformity. The model's actual exogenous-order assumption matters more than the label.

Describe sector expertise once. Avoid specialist as the permanent actor name: Glosten and Milgrom use specialist for the intermediary. [Institutional article record](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/bid-ask-and-transaction-prices-specialist-market-heterogeneously-informed-traders).

Keep synergy class unless there is a separate reason to change it. Type can suggest information privately known to the bidder. High/low also avoids the absolute judgment suggested by good/poor.

Keep research intensity, research success, informed presence, and actual trade distinct. An informed trader can abstain. Greater presence does not mean a more precise conditional signal.

## Suggested core sentence

> A stronger incumbent can induce rival entry through a more informative stock price.

The word can preserves the conditional result. The first model slide must define entry as preparation.

## Consistency and one prose issue

Use the same actor names in the paper, deck, and website after the author selects them. Keep simple explanatory sentences around the technical terms.

The current manuscript gives the incumbent its value before trade, but later says that prepared bidders then learn their values. A language revision should clarify that later value discovery applies to the rival. Relevant source: `overleaf/slot/sections/02_model.tex:165–169`. No change was made.
