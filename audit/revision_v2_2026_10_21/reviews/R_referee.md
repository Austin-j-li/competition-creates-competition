## Part 1. Referee report

### Summary

The paper studies a takeover auction in which a prospective challenger observes the target's stock price before it pays a preparation cost. An informed investor knows the challenger's quality class and trades against noise demand. Competitive market makers price order flow and anticipate the preparation that each price induces. A stronger incumbent lowers the challenger's gross acquisition profit at every belief. It also widens the gap in target proceeds between the two challenger classes, because it outbids the low class more often and pays the challenger's bid. The traded claim becomes more sensitive to information about the challenger. On an open set of primitives this switches the unique trading outcome from no trade to full orders, and entry rises from its floor although acquisition profit falls at every belief (Proposition 2). Holding the price experiment fixed restores deterrence (Proposition A.3). Three asymmetric informative equilibria are certified at intermediate strengths (Proposition 3). Access to prices raises expected proceeds and net acquisition surplus in the strong economy (Proposition A.9).

The revision makes four changes. The challenger's value now comes from one of two class distributions with a general prior, and the point-mass equal-prior case is kept as the numerical benchmark. Laplace noise is justified on technical and economic grounds, and the main comparison is extended to every noise density with a bounded score (Proposition A.5). The informed investor and the shareholders' gain are given institutional content. The low preparation cost is set to zero.

The idea is good and the main proposition is clean. Three new pieces deserve credit. The slice decomposition in Section 3.1 is the best explanation of the mechanism in any version of the paper. Proposition 1(c) gives an exact characterization of the class restriction, not only a sufficient condition. The Laplace extremality lemma in Online Appendix A.6 is correct and elegant: the ROC argument through the differential inequality, and the Euler-product decomposition of the logistic law into independent Laplace components, both hold as written. The general-prior version of Proposition 2, with the constant κ(π) and the ownership condition, and the nonemptiness construction for every ranked pair of classes, are also correct as far as I checked them.

### Recommendation

Major revision. The mathematics is in good shape. The exposition and the exhibits do not yet make the case that the general model, the noise class, or the institutional story add economic content. The three conditions for a positive next round are:

1. The class model must earn its place with at least one exhibit the binary model cannot produce, and the maintained restriction should be the natural one (both class supports above the reserve), with the exact characterization kept in the appendix.
2. The economic region of the reversal (the numerical correspondence of Figure 2) should be presented as the result, and the proposition as its certified corner. The size of the effect should be reported as a range across the noise laws, not as the Laplace number alone.
3. The manuscript should shrink by about a third. Appendix A is longer than the text.

### Assessment of the four changes

**1. Quality classes.** The class model is the natural framing in the mathematics. Proposition 1 now reads as a statement about kernels, the split identity shows what competition moves, and Proposition 2 holds at every prior with the nonemptiness result for every ranked pair. The framework is also the selective-entry structure of the auction literature, which the introduction now says.

In the exhibits the generalization looks cosmetic, for four reasons. First, every reported number uses the prior one half, and every table except Table 4 Panel B uses point masses. Second, the one nondegenerate example (uniform classes with half-width 0.05) differs from the benchmark in the sixth decimal: entry 0.522760 against 0.522757. A reader concludes that the class model changes nothing. Third, the objects that the class model creates, the constant κ(π), the prior shift in the threshold flow, the win probability W_H, and the ownership condition, are never shown at work. Fourth, the many-class price-sufficiency result in Appendix A.4 is the most interesting general statement in the revision, and the main text does not mention it.

The restriction itself has a problem. Equal admissibility, G_H(p⁻) = G_L(p⁻), is a knife-edge whenever the reserve cuts into a class. Proposition 1(c) shows why: the spread rises under every strengthening if and only if D ≥ 0 above the reserve and D(p⁻) ≤ 0. The second condition says the high class is weakly more often inadmissible than the low class. The natural ranking says the opposite. The intersection is equality. When both supports lie above the reserve, equality holds trivially, and that is the case in every example, including the low reserve of Section 6.3. At the high reserve of Section 6.3 the low class is excluded, the condition fails, and the paper reports deterrence there. The reader must reconcile the two on their own. The clean solution is to state "both supports above the reserve" as the maintained assumption in the main text, and to keep Proposition 1(c) in the appendix as the characterization of the weakest restriction.

One claim in Section 4.1 deserves a check. The text says that with overlapping supports entry can rise while high-class ownership falls. I searched for this on the sufficient region of Proposition 2 with point-mass classes and uniform incumbents. It is realizable, but only when the low-cost probability is above roughly 0.85, that is, when almost every challenger prepares regardless of the price and entry has little room to rise. The sentence is correct. It should say where the case lives, or go.

**2. Laplace noise and the bounded-score class.** The justification is now convincing on the technical side. The property the proofs use is the bounded score: it gives bounded beliefs under every mixed strategy and the global derivative bound that covers every deviation. Laplace is the extreme member of the class: thinnest tails at a fixed score bound, the most informative flow experiment at every pure profile, and logistic noise equals Laplace noise plus independent noise at a common scale. The class result (Proposition A.5) reads as a strength, because it shows that the mechanism needs only the score bound and not the plateau.

Four points remain. First, the headline magnitude is the most favorable member of the class in every exhibit: strong-economy entry is 0.52 under Laplace noise, 0.30 under logistic, 0.38 under hyperbolic secant, and 0.37 under Student t₃, against a floor of 0.25. The abstract and the introduction should report the range. Second, the hyperbolic-secant and Student numbers hold full orders fixed, and Online Appendix C.9 states that no equilibrium search was run under those laws; Section 5.1 lists them next to equilibrium values with only the phrase "with full orders held fixed". Label them as diagnostics or establish equilibrium status. Third, a referee will still ask why a continuous order interval with Laplace noise rather than a discrete order menu, as in Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang (2015), where bounded beliefs are automatic with full-support noise. The answer exists in the paper but is scattered: the continuous interval makes "trade to the limit" an outcome rather than a menu item, it supports the partial-sale equilibria of Proposition 3, and one derivative bound covers every deviation and every mixture. One paragraph should say this. Fourth, the three microfoundations in Section 2.3 are listed, not argued. One sentence on why exponential-type arrival is more plausible than Gaussian noise for a target in play would help. The sentence "Each member of the class has tails at least as fat as an exponential tail" also needs the median-zero qualifier that the lemma carries.

**3. Institutional motivation.** The paragraph on who trades is concrete and useful: a sector specialist with no net position, research on the target's customers and technology, liquidity sellers among current holders, outside liquidity buyers, market makers who hold inventory to the sale. The shareholder paragraph has the economics right: a holder through the sale receives the proceeds, and entry raises the proceeds in either class.

A skeptical finance referee would still ask the following.

- The specialist knows the quality class of a challenger that has not declared itself. For that to make sense, the identity of the natural further buyer must be common knowledge within the decision interval, and what the market does not know is how well that buyer fits. Section 2.1 should say this.
- "Through entry, not through the price" misleads. Under competitive pricing the expected interim price rises by the same amount as expected proceeds. The point is that the gain is real, not a transfer. Say that.
- The break-even trade scale of 1.83 order units per share of float exceeds the whole float, and part of the investor's profit comes from outside liquidity buyers. The aggregate gain to initial shareholders is therefore unconditional at any admissible order size. The paragraph currently reads as if it could fail.
- The caveat that an informative price lowers proceeds when high-cost preparation already pays at the prior is a trailing sentence in Section 6.1. It is a condition of Proposition A.9 and should appear in its statement or as a remark after it.
- Why does the specialist know only the challenger's class and nothing about the incumbent's value R? An investor informed about R trades the same claim, and the price would then also carry information about the challenger's return to preparation. One sentence on why this channel is set aside would pre-empt the question.
- What is t₀ institutionally? The text explains its role in the model (it cancels from the residuals) but not what it is: the reserve, the walk-away price the board has announced and extracts from a lone prepared bidder when that bidder's value meets it. One sentence in Section 2.2.
- "Trades lawfully on it" is a legal claim without a citation and depends on the jurisdiction. Either cite or neutralize ("research of this kind is not inside information about the issuer").

**4. Zero low preparation cost.** This is a clean simplification. It removes a condition, makes price sufficiency unconditional, and the "ready buyer" story is plausible. Two things should be said. The floor ρ > 0 now carries price sufficiency and the residual lower bound; at ρ = 0 entry can vanish at unfavorable prices, the price need not reveal the posterior, and the residual has no positive lower bound. The floor is therefore an assumption with content, not a normalization. And the tie rule (an indifferent challenger prepares) now matters at zero gross profit; the paper handles this at the reserve endpoint in Appendix A.7 but should flag it where the rule is introduced.

### Major comments

**M1. The sufficient region is a worst-case corner; the economic region is the numerical correspondence.** The trading-cost window in (A2) is nonempty exactly when Δ_T(F₀)/Δ_T(F₁) < (1 − 1/b)ρκ(π). At the benchmark the bound is 0.034 and the actual ratio is 0.025, so the weak economy's spread must be below about three percent of the strong economy's. The cost windows are narrow: k must lie in (0.0167, 0.0224), about ±14 percent around its midpoint, and c_H in (4.80, 6.22), about ±13 percent. At a prior of 0.3 or 0.7 the upper bound on k halves, because κ falls from 0.269 to 0.136, and the window at strengths (1.2, 3) is then empty: it is nonempty only for priors between about 0.40 and 0.60, and at those two priors the benchmark cost c_H = 6 also falls outside the (A1) window. The benchmark survives a change of prior only if the strengths and the cost move with it. None of this is hidden, but it is scattered, and the theorem compares r = 1.2 with r = 3 while the correspondence in Figure 2 shows no trade unique below r = 1.22, certified informative equilibria at r = 1.55 to 1.65 that coexist with no trade, no trade surviving to r = 1.75, and full orders unique above r = 2.84. The paper should say in Section 4.1 that the bound is a worst case over every candidate schedule, give the benchmark ratio and windows, and present Figure 2 as the economic result with the proposition as its certified corner. A referee who sees the windows first will call the result engineered; a referee who sees the correspondence first will not.

**M2. Equal admissibility.** See the assessment of change 1. ### The three most likely reasons for rejection, and how to pre-empt each

**1. "The result is engineered: Laplace plateau, zero-cost floor, cost atoms, and narrow windows."** The sufficient conditions are a worst case, the plateau gives the most favorable magnitude, and the reader sees the Laplace number first. Pre-empt by presenting Figure 2 as the economic region and the proposition as its certified corner (M1); by reporting the entry range across noise laws in the abstract and introduction and establishing equilibrium status for the two additional laws (M4); by adding the "why a continuous order space" paragraph; and by saying that ρ > 0 is an assumption with content (change 4).

**2. "The information structure is contrived."** An outsider knows the class of a buyer that has not declared itself, the buyer knows nothing about its own fit, and the buyer learns only from the price. Pre-empt by stating that the natural further buyer's identity is common knowledge within the interval (M5); by moving the complementary-signal case of Section 5.3, where the buyer's own signal is more accurate than the investor's, forward in the robustness section and citing it in the introduction's first paragraph on the setting; by adding one sentence of evidence that boards watch target prices before launching diligence, with one concrete case from the disclosure records of Online Appendix D; and by saying why information about the incumbent's value is set aside.

**3. "Ninety pages for one mechanism, and the general model adds nothing."** Pre-empt by the cuts in M6 (target: about 45 pages of text and 25 of appendix), by one class exhibit the binary model cannot produce (M3), by the natural maintained restriction (M2), and by removing verification apparatus from the main text.

A fourth, less likely reason is that the welfare and design sections are incomplete (the seller's problem is open, the bargaining game is unsolved). The paper is honest about both; the cure is to shorten them rather than to extend them.
