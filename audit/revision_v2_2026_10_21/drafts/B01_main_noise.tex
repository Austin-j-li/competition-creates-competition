The reversal holds for a class of noise laws.
Let \(\mathcal C_L\) contain the positive, absolutely continuous densities
with \(|(\log f)'|\le L\) almost everywhere, where \(0<L<1\).
Set \(b=1/L>1\).
This score bound gives both the posterior bounds and the global order bound.
\hyperref[result-proposition-a-5]{Proposition A.5} extends the equilibrium
comparison to this class.
Its upper cost restriction uses the supremum of the posterior under full orders.
\par
That supremum need not be attained.

Laplace is the extreme member of this class.
At a given score bound, it has the thinnest tails about the median.
It also gives the most informative price at every pure order profile.
Online Appendix A.6 proves both statements.
\par
Under full orders, its log odds relative to prior odds equal
\((2/b)\max\{-1,\min\{x,1\}\}\).
This expression clips the Gaussian log odds for variance \(b\).
Gaussian noise has neither a finite score bound nor posterior bounds strictly inside \((0,1)\).

Logistic noise belongs to the class.
At a common scale \(b\), it equals Laplace noise plus independent noise.
The Laplace price experiment therefore Blackwell-dominates the logistic experiment at the same orders.
The logistic posterior approaches its bounds only as flow tends to infinity.
\par
In the strong benchmark economy, entry is 0.301509 under logistic noise,
compared with 0.522757 under Laplace noise.
The logistic flow threshold is 5.424598398, or 1.495369 noise standard deviations from the center.
Thus the same posterior bounds can support different participation rates.
The probability of favorable information near the upper bound also matters.

Figure~\ref{fig:3} compares the probability that the posterior exceeds
a preparation threshold near \(M\).
Laplace noise assigns positive probability to the upper posterior bound;
logistic noise does not.
The comparison concerns the location of posterior mass at a common scale.
The Laplace experiment is more informative, but informativeness does not order entry.
