\subsubsection{Atomless preparation
costs}\label{atomless-preparation-costs}

For atomless cost component distributions with
probabilities \(\rho\) and \(1-\rho\), supported within
\([0,\varepsilon_C]\) and
\([c_H-\varepsilon_C,c_H+\varepsilon_C]\), the sufficient support
restrictions are

\[
\begin{gathered}
\varepsilon_C<B_{r_1}(m),\\
B_{r_0}(1/2)<c_H-\varepsilon_C<c_H+\varepsilon_C<B_{r_1}(M),
\end{gathered}
\tag{A.24}\label{eq:paper-a-24}
\]

together with \eqref{eq:paper-a3}.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}

\protect\phantomsection\label{result-proposition-a-6}{\textbf{Proposition
A.6 (atomless preparation costs; analytical).}} \emph{Replace the cost
atoms by atomless distributions satisfying \eqref{eq:paper-a-24} and
retain \eqref{eq:paper-a3}. The unique trading outcomes and the entry
and ownership reversal in parts (i) and (ii) of
\hyperref[result-proposition-2]{Proposition 2} hold under either noise
law.}

Let \(H_C\) be the mixed cost CDF; the entry rule is
\(e(\mu)=H_C(B_r(\mu))\), continuous and nondecreasing. The first line
of \eqref{eq:paper-a-24} makes every realization of the component at
zero prepare at every feasible belief in both economies, since gross
profit at the lowest feasible belief already exceeds the whole support
and gross profit decreases in \(r\). So \(e\ge\rho\) and the
residual bounds in \eqref{eq:paper-11} are unchanged. The price
increment \eqref{eq:paper-a-3} stays strictly positive because \(H_C\)
is nondecreasing, so the inversion and the global trading bounds apply.
At the weak prior, \(e(1/2)=\rho\) by the second line of
\eqref{eq:paper-a-24}. In the strong economy, beliefs close enough to
\(M\) satisfy \(B_r(\mu)>c_H+\varepsilon_C\), an event of positive
probability under either noise law, and there \(e=1\). The proof needs
an atomless distribution, not a density. Online Appendix A.6 has the
details. \(\square\)
