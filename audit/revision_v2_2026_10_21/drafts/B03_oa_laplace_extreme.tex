\subsubsection{Laplace is the extreme member of the class}\label{oa-a-laplace-extreme}

Write \(\mathcal C_L\) for the positive, absolutely continuous densities
with \(|f'|\le Lf\) almost everywhere.
Set \(b=1/L\) and \(\ell_b(z)=e^{-|z|/b}/(2b)\).
The score bound gives \(\int|f'|\le L\), so the convolution argument in A.3 applies.
The three claims below hold for every \(L>0\).
The equilibrium result also requires \(L<1\).
\par
Write \(\beta_f(\alpha)=\sup_{\varphi:\Pr_L(\varphi)\le\alpha}\Pr_H(\varphi)\)
for the ROC curve of the flow experiment, where \(\Pr_j(\varphi)=\mathbb E_j\varphi(X)\).

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}

\protect\phantomsection\label{result-lemma-oa-laplace-extreme}{\textbf{Lemma
(Laplace extremality; analytical).}} \emph{(i) If \(f\in\mathcal C_L\)
has median zero, then \(\Pr_f(|Z|>t)\ge e^{-Lt}\) for every \(t\ge0\).
Equality at a positive \(t\) requires \(f=\ell_b\).
(ii) At every pure order profile, the Laplace flow experiment Blackwell-dominates the experiment from any \(f\in\mathcal C_L\).
Price sufficiency gives the same comparison for the price experiments.
(iii) At a common scale, logistic noise equals Laplace noise plus independent noise:
\(Z_{\mathrm{log}}\overset d=Z_{\mathrm{Lap}}+W\), where
\(W\overset d=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}Y_n\) and the \(Y_n\) are independent Laplace variables with scales \(b/n\).
The logistic comparison holds at mixed order profiles as well.}

\emph{Proof of (i).} Put \(S(t)=\int_t^\infty f(z)\,dz\) for \(t\ge0\).
The log-density bound gives \(f(z)\ge f(t)e^{-L(z-t)}\) for \(z\ge t\), hence \(S(t)\ge f(t)/L\).
Thus \(S'(t)=-f(t)\ge-LS(t)\), and \(S(0)=1/2\) gives \(S(t)\ge e^{-Lt}/2\).
Apply the same argument to \(f(-z)\) and add the two tail bounds.
\par
If their sum equals \(e^{-Lt}\) at a positive \(t\), both tail inequalities are equalities.
Monotonicity of \(e^{Lu}S(u)\) then gives \(S(u)=e^{-Lu}/2\) on \([0,t]\).
Continuity gives \(f(t)=Le^{-Lt}/2\), so equality in \(S(t)\ge f(t)/L\) fixes the complete right tail.
Hence \(f(z)=(L/2)e^{-Lz}\) for every \(z\ge0\); reflection fixes the left tail. \(\square\)

\emph{Proof of (ii).} Equal orders give constant information; otherwise relabel the states so that \(q_L<q_H\).
For a statistical decision rule \(0\le\varphi\le1\), put \(h_\varphi(s)=\int\varphi(x)f(x-s)\,dx\).
Section A.3 gives \(|h_\varphi'|\le Lh_\varphi\); its application to \(1-\varphi\) also gives \(|h_\varphi'|\le L(1-h_\varphi)\).
Let \(v'=\Phi(v)=L\min\{v,1-v\}\), with \(v(q_L)=h_\varphi(q_L)=\alpha\).
Since \(\Phi\) is Lipschitz, \((h_\varphi-v)_+'\le L(h_\varphi-v)_+\) almost everywhere; integration gives \(h_\varphi\le v\).
For Laplace noise, a half-line rule has \(h'=\Phi(h)\) exactly and can realize every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).
\par
These half-lines are optimal by the monotone likelihood ratio, so the ROC curves obey \(\beta_f(\alpha)\le\beta_{\mathrm{Lap}}(\alpha)\); endpoints follow by continuity.
For \(0\le t\le1\), Bayes' rule gives \(\mathbb E(\mu-t)_+=\sup_{0\le\alpha\le1}\{\pi(1-t)\beta(\alpha)-(1-\pi)t\alpha\}\).
The ROC inequality thus orders every posterior call value; both posterior means equal \(\pi\), so it gives convex order.
For two states, this is Blackwell dominance; price sufficiency makes each price experiment equivalent to its flow experiment. \(\square\)

\emph{Proof of (iii).} The density in the paper has characteristic function
\(\chi_{\mathrm{log}}(t)=\pi bt/\sinh(\pi bt)\).
Euler's product gives \(\chi_{\mathrm{log}}(t)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+b^2t^2/n^2)^{-1}\).
Each factor is the characteristic function of a centered Laplace variable with scale \(b/n\).
Take \(Y_n\), \(n\ge1\), independently with these laws; their total variance is \(2b^2\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2}<\infty\).
Their partial sums converge almost surely and in \(L^2\), and their characteristic functions converge to the displayed product.
\par
Separate the first term: \(Z_{\mathrm{Lap}}=Y_1\) is independent of \(W=\sum_{n\ge2}Y_n\).
The product identity gives \(Z_{\mathrm{log}}\overset d=Z_{\mathrm{Lap}}+W\).
The kernel that adds \(W\) is independent of the class and of the investor's order.
It therefore gives Blackwell dominance at every pure or mixed order profile; price sufficiency transfers the comparison to prices. \(\square\)

Part (ii) does not assert dominance at mixed profiles for every member of the class.
Part (iii) supplies that stronger scope for logistic noise.
Blackwell dominance orders the value of information, but it does not order the probability of expensive preparation.
