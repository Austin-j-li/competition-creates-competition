% REPLACES: online_appendix.tex lines 851-866 ("The complete sufficient conditions for Proposition A.7 are ..." to "... conditional on the investor's signal.")
The complete sufficient conditions for Proposition A.7 are

\[
\begin{aligned}
&B_{r_0}(d)<c_H<B_{r_1}(\phi_+(\mu_+)),\\
&(2a-1)\{\Delta_T(r_0)+(1-\rho)(2d-1)w_H(r_0)\}<k\\
&\hspace{25mm}<(1-1/b)m(2a-1)\rho\Delta_T(r_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{OA.29}\label{eq:oa-oa-29}
\]

No low-cost condition joins them. Gross profit at the lowest joint posterior,
\(B_{r_1}(\phi_-(\mu_-))\), is positive on this support, so the zero-cost
realization prepares at every reachable price after either private signal.
That quantity remains a recorded margin in C.3; it is no longer a hypothesis.

I prove unique zero orders and entry \(\rho\) at \(r_0\), unique maximum
correctly signed signal-contingent orders at \(r_1\), and strictly
higher entry at \(r_1\). The result allows \(d>a\) and arbitrary mixed
orders conditional on the investor's signal.

% REPLACES: online_appendix.tex lines 896-902 ("The maps \phi_+,\phi_- are continuous ..." to "... under any candidate equilibrium.")
The maps \(\phi_+,\phi_-\) are continuous and strictly increasing on
\((0,1)\); the favorable private signal gives the larger posterior. The
public posterior based on price inherits \([\mu_-,\mu_+]\) by
conditional expectation. The lowest possible joint posterior is
consequently \(\phi_-(\mu_-)\). Gross profit there is positive, and
gross profits decline in \(r\), so the zero-cost realization prepares
in both economies under any candidate equilibrium.
