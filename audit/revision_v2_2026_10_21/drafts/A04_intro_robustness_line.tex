For every prior and every pair of classes in which the high class has stochastically higher values and both classes are equally likely to fall below the reserve, there are incumbent strengths and costs at which the reversal holds. It survives logistic noise, atomless preparation costs, and private signals that the buyer and the investor hold about different facts, including a buyer signal more accurate than the investor's. At some intermediate incumbent strengths, informative and uninformative equilibria coexist: a computer-assisted proof establishes three informative equilibria, with preparation strictly ordered upward across them. Against a strong enough incumbent, even the most favorable feasible price cannot justify expensive preparation, so participation returns to its floor while trading stays informative; participation is therefore non-monotone in strength. In the strong-incumbent economy, access to prices raises expected target proceeds and acquisition surplus net of preparation costs.
