# Frame 1 options

Question on the slide: Does a stronger bidder in a merger auction scare off its competitor, or draw in more competition?

## Option A

1. A competitor pays to check contracts, debt and technology first.
   - Fact: a competitor checks contracts, liabilities and technology before a firm offer.
2. Preemptive bidding: a first bidder opens high; the competitor stays out.
   - Fact: Fishman (1988) — a first bidder opens high and the competitor does not pay to look.
3. Who else comes decides auction versus talks with one bidder.
   - Fact: a seller runs an auction or negotiates with one bidder.

Speaker script: The competitor checks contracts, debt and technology, and the contest starts in private. In Fishman's preemptive case, a high first bid signals high value, and the competitor saves the cost of a look. The seller thus faces a real choice: broad auction or sole talks.

## Option B

1. Without cash and staff time, a competitor makes no bid.
   - Fact: a competitor lines up cash and staff time; with no preparation, a competitor stays out.
2. A first bidder opens high; the competitor skips a costly look.
   - Fact: Fishman (1988) — a first bidder opens high and the competitor does not pay to look.
3. The seller picks auction or sole talks by who else comes.
   - Fact: a seller runs an auction or negotiates with one bidder.

Speaker script: The Imprivata record shows a sequence: a first approach, calls to others, then early offers subject to more checks. A high first bid gives the competitor a clear reason to save cash and staff time. The seller then weighs real turnout: several bidders suit an auction; one bidder suits sole talks.

## Option C

1. Like a house buyer, the competitor pays to inspect first.
   - Fact: a competitor pays real costs to check the target before a bid. The house phrase is analogy, not a paper fact.
2. A high first bid shows strong value; the competitor stays away.
   - Fact: Fishman (1988) — a high bid tells the competitor the first bidder values the target highly.
3. More bidders mean an auction; one bidder means direct talks.
   - Fact: a seller runs an auction or negotiates with one bidder.

Speaker script: A house sale helps here: each buyer pays for a survey before a firm offer. A merger works the same way, and a high first bid shows the competitor strong value; the competitor stays away. The format follows turnout: a full auction or direct talks with one bidder.

## First choice

Option A is the first choice for the slide. It names real checks, labels the Fishman case once, and ties method to turnout.
