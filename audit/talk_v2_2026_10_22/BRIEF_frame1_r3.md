# Frame 1, round 3: concrete wording for the three points

## Task

Frame 1 of a seminar talk shows one question in large type:

> Does a stronger bidder in a merger auction scare off its competitor, or draw in more competition?

Three short lines go under it. The author likes the three points of the round-2 option A:

1. Who shows up shapes the sale, long before bidding turns public.
2. A high early bid can end the contest: preemptive bidding.
3. The answer bears on how targets are sold: auction or negotiation.

The author asks for more down-to-earth versions. The audience must see a real merger in its mind.
Keep the same three points in the same order:

1. Why it matters who else comes: a further buyer must pay real costs before it can bid.
2. Preemptive bidding: a first buyer can open high so that others do not pay to look.
3. The sale method: the seller invites several buyers or negotiates with one, and that choice
   depends on whether more buyers will come.

## Deliverable

Write `audit/talk_v2_2026_10_22/opening_frame1_v3.md` with these parts:

- Three options. Each option has three lines, one for each point.
- One of the options uses an everyday analogy, such as a house sale, in its first line. The other
  two options stay with mergers.
- For each option, two or three spoken sentences for the speaker script.
- For each line, the fact from the list below that the line uses.
- Your first choice and the reason for it, in two sentences.

## Facts you may use (all from the paper; do not add others)

- Before a buyer can make a binding offer, it must check the target's contracts, liabilities and
  technology. It must also arrange the money and the approvals, and give the work staff time that
  has other uses. These costs are sunk, and they differ across buyers.
- A buyer that does not prepare cannot bid. It stays out.
- One real sale (the Imprivata proxy statement, 2016) shows the stages: an unsolicited approach,
  then calls from the target to other possible buyers, then early offers that depend on more
  diligence.
- Competition for a target often starts in private, before any public bid (Boone and Mulherin
  2007; Gentry and Stroup 2019). Do not state how often this occurs.
- Preemptive bidding (Fishman 1988): a first bidder can open with a high bid. The high bid tells a
  competitor that the first bidder values the target highly, so the competitor does not pay to look.
- A seller can run an auction among several buyers or negotiate with one buyer.

Do not use numbers, dollar amounts, deal names other than Imprivata, or durations such as "months".

## Rules for the slide lines

- Do not mention the stock price, prices of shares, or investors. They come in Frame 3.
- Do not answer the question. Frame 1 only says why the question matters.
- No first person ("I", "we", "our").
- Use "competitor" for the other bidder, as the question does. Use one word for each thing.
- Each line has 12 words at most, and fits one line of a 16:9 Beamer slide in large type.
- Use plain, concrete words: "check the books", not "conduct due diligence". You may use the term
  "preemptive bidding" once, with its meaning beside it.
- Use the simple present tense and the active voice. Do not use -ing forms.
