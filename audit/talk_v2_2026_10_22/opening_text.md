## Frame 2. Why it matters

TITLE: Why entry matters

SUBTITLE: The stakes

BODY:

- Who enters sets the price target shareholders receive.
- One more bidder is worth more than better negotiation.
- Stock prices affect takeovers, and firms learn from prices.

TAKEAWAY: Entry moves the sale price. The stock price can move entry.

## Frame 3. The standard answer, and what it holds fixed

TITLE: The standard answer: strength deters entry

SUBTITLE: What that answer holds fixed

BODY:

- A stronger incumbent raises the price a winning challenger pays.
- So preparation pays less, and fewer challengers enter.
- That logic holds the challenger's information fixed.
- Here the challenger sees the stock price before it decides.

TAKEAWAY: With information fixed, a stronger incumbent only deters entry.

CITATIONS: Fishman 1988; Hirshleifer and Png 1989

## Frame 4. Three literatures, one gap

TITLE: Three literatures, one gap

SUBTITLE: What past work shows, and what it leaves open

BODY (three-row table):

| | It shows | It does not cover |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong incumbents deter entry** | Strength cuts the gain from entry | A stock price before entry |
| **Entry into auctions is a choice** | Entry costs decide who enters | News from a stock price before entry |
| **Firms learn from stock prices** | Prices guide real firm decisions | Strength that changes what prices reveal |

TAKEAWAY: Left open: how a stronger incumbent changes what the stock price reveals before entry.

CITATIONS: Fishman 1988; Hirshleifer and Png 1989 | Samuelson 1985; Levin and Smith 1994; Persico 2000 | Dow, Goldstein and Guembel 2017; Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang 2012, 2015

## Frame 6. This paper

TITLE: This paper: competition creates competition

SUBTITLE: —

BODY:

- An outside investor knows the challenger's quality, and trades the stock.
- A stronger incumbent moves a fixed value gap to target shareholders.
- So the stock price gets more sensitive to the challenger's quality.
- The investor trades on it: the price turns informative.
- Good news makes costly preparation pay: entry rises.

TAKEAWAY: The channel is information. Hold what the challenger learns fixed, and a stronger incumbent cannot raise entry.

## Speaker script

**Frame 1.** The paper asks one plain question. Does a stronger bidder in a merger auction scare off its competitor, or draw in more competition? The stock price can change the answer.

**Frame 2.** Entry matters, because who enters sets the price target shareholders receive. One more bidder is worth more than better negotiation. And stock prices move early: prices affect takeovers, and firms learn from prices.

**Frame 3.** Earlier theory gives a clean answer: strength deters entry. A stronger incumbent makes a winning challenger pay more, so preparation pays less. That logic holds what the challenger knows fixed. The paper drops that hold: the challenger sees the stock price first.

**Frame 4.** Three literatures meet here. One says strong incumbents deter entry. One says entry into auctions is a choice. One says firms learn from stock prices. None asks how strength changes what the price reveals before entry.

**Frame 5.** So picture the setting. The sale is public, the stock still trades, and one bidder is ready: the incumbent. A second bidder, the challenger, must pay to prepare before it can bid. That choice, with the price live, is the paper's stage.

**Frame 6.** Here is the result. An outside investor knows the challenger's quality and trades the stock. A stronger incumbent shifts a fixed gap in value toward target shareholders, so the price reacts more to quality. The investor trades on that, the price turns informative, and good news brings entry in. Hold learning fixed, and the effect dies: the channel is information.
