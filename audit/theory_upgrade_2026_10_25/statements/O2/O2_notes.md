# O2. The liquidity share

Mathematics writer's result: Propositions 1 to 4 hold for every `0 < beta < 1/2`. The bound common to all orders, including zero, uses `beta <= 1/3`; so do its all-profile exclusion consequence and the zero-order part of the research posterior bound. A price reached by a nonzero order has the usual posterior bounds even if it pools zero. This last fact supplies the entry floor needed for the preparation-cost proposition on the larger domain.

## Argument

For any set of orders containing a purchase or sale, its liquidity mass is at least `(1-lambda) beta = b`. Its informed mass in either class is between zero and lambda. Its likelihood ratio therefore belongs to `[1/(1+u), 1+u]`, and its posterior belongs to `[m(pi), M(pi)]`. Apply this to the preimage of any price reached by a nonzero order. No comparison between `b_0` and `b` enters.

In the preparation-cost result the low-cost component prepares at both nonzero-order prices under every candidate. This is enough to force full orders against the stronger incumbent. Against the weaker incumbent, the upper return bound forces abstention even if the entry floor is zero. After the orders are fixed, every price belief is an average of posteriors in `[m, M]`, and the cost windows give the stated outcomes. The assertion that the low component prepares at every price under every profile uses `beta <= 1/3`; the proposition itself has the larger domain just proved.

For the many-class result, the likelihood-ratio bounds at the two nonzero orders put the posterior vectors in the separation set and force the orders. For complementary signals they bound the investor-signal posterior at the same two orders. Each construction computes the zero-order posterior from those orders. Welfare and payment use the forced or stated order profiles or auction-only comparisons; the empirical results use those profiles, Bayes plausibility and full support.

Every other forcing argument uses nonzero orders. Constructed equilibria compute their zero-order belief directly. Positive research forces the full high-class purchase; the two nonpurchase posteriors are then at most one half. Zero research gives prior posteriors. The strong-incumbent research result uses the nonzero-order bounds. The necessity argument for the research parameter region uses the purchase posterior `M_lambda`, after the full-purchase result, rather than a bound on all orders.

## Exclusion above one third

For every `1/3 < beta < 1/2`, fix interior lambda and rho and a ranked auction with positive spread and profit gap. Let class H abstain and class L sell. The zero-order likelihood ratio is `1+lambda/b_0 > 1+u`, so its posterior exceeds M. Choose the prior sufficiently near one that `1-pi < m(pi)`, choose a threshold strictly between M and the zero-order posterior, and choose `rho Delta_T (1-pi) < k < rho Delta_T m(pi)`. Entry is rho after either nonzero order and one after zero. The prices are strictly ordered in the posterior; H strictly abstains, L strictly sells, and high-cost preparation occurs only at zero. This is an equilibrium under all four tie rules despite `B(M) < c_H`.

The print-only instance in `o2_exclusion.py` has `beta=2/5`, `lambda=1/2`, `pi=9/10`, `rho=1/4`, `c_H=33/4`, `k=1/20`, point values 10 and 1, reserve one half and incumbent `U[0,3]`. Its zero posterior is `54/55`, its threshold is `39/40`, and M is `63/65`. H's purchase earns `-1/30`, L's sale earns `7/100`, and `c_H-B(M)=5/104`.
