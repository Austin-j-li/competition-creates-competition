## Shared candidate 1: frequency-weighted impacts and A.14(iv)

**Verdict: proved under full two-sided informed trade; false as an unrestricted recruitment diagnostic.**

Let $w_L=t_L-t_0\ge0$, $A(\mu)=w_L+\Delta\mu$, $\Delta=t_H-t_L>0$, and $\gamma=g_H-g_L>0$. Maintain $0<\pi,\lambda,\rho<1$ and the model's $0<\beta\le1/3$; the full-order price identities themselves only need $\beta<1/2$. Write $f_x=\Pr(X=x)$, $e_x=\Pr(\text{preparation}\mid X=x)$, $I_+=P(1)-P(0)$ and $I_-=P(0)-P(-1)$. Under full two-sided informed trade,

$$
f_+=b+\pi\lambda,\qquad f_-=b+(1-\pi)\lambda,\qquad
f_0=1-2b-\lambda,
$$

$$
M=\frac{\pi(b+\lambda)}{f_+},\qquad
m=\frac{\pi b}{f_-},\qquad \mu_0=\pi.
$$

Subtracting the prior gives, step by step,

$$
f_+(M-\pi)=\pi[(b+\lambda)-f_+]
=\pi(1-\pi)\lambda
=\pi[f_--b]=f_-(\pi-m).
\tag{1}
$$

From A2's competitive price $P(x)=t_0+e_xA(\mu_x)$,

$$
I_+=e_0\Delta(M-\pi)+(e_+-e_0)A(M),
$$

$$
I_-=e_0\Delta(\pi-m)+(e_0-e_-)A(m).
$$

Multiplication by the respective frequencies and cancellation using (1) prove

$$
\boxed{T:=f_+I_+-f_-I_-
=f_+(e_+-e_0)A(M)-f_-(e_0-e_-)A(m).}
\tag{2}
$$

Since all order posteriors are interior, $A(M),A(m)>0$. Under C1 the complete threshold classification is:

| Threshold | Entry after $(-1,0,1)$ | $T$ | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\tau\le m$ | $(1,1,1)$ | 0 | Preparation already certain everywhere. |
| $m<\tau\le\pi$ | $(\rho,1,1)$ | $-(1-\rho)f_-A(m)<0$ | A sale deters preparation. |
| $\pi<\tau\le M$ | $(\rho,\rho,1)$ | $(1-\rho)f_+A(M)>0$ | A purchase recruits relative to a zero order. |
| $M<\tau$ | $(\rho,\rho,\rho)$ | 0 | No order induces high-cost preparation. |

This works on **both sides of one-half**. It does not require $\pi<\tau$ if “recruits” means raises preparation relative to no order. It does require $\pi<\tau$ for the manuscript's indicator $\chi=\mathbf1\{M\ge\tau\}$ to mean recruitment: at high priors $\chi=1$ can coexist with $e_+=e_0=1$.

On the region $B_F(\pi)<c_H$, equivalently $\pi<\tau$, the useful empirical statement is

$$
\boxed{\frac{T}{f_+}=I_+-\frac{f_-}{f_+}I_-
=(e_+-\rho)A(M),}
\tag{3}
$$

with

$$
S=\rho\Delta(M-m)+(e_+-\rho)A(M),\qquad
\rho\Delta(M-m)=\frac{f_++f_-}{f_+}I_-.
\tag{4}
$$

Indeed (1) implies $(\pi-m)/(M-m)=f_+/(f_++f_-)$, proving the second identity. Thus prices and the purchase-to-sale frequency ratio identify the *entry component*, without knowing the prior or the auction objects. Only the sign and $T/f_+$ can be recovered from the relative buy/sell frequencies alone; the level of $T$ requires frequencies per opportunity, including zero opportunities.

### Exact identity with A.14(iv)

For **any** order pattern and prices,

$$
\bar P-P(0)
=f_+[P(1)-P(0)]+f_-[P(-1)-P(0)]
=T.
\tag{5}
$$

Under full trade and $\pi<\tau$, substituting (3) yields

$$
P(0)-\bar P=-f_+(e_+-\rho)A(M).
$$

At prior one-half and C1 this is exactly A.14(iv), while (3) is A.11(ii). They are the same underlying moment, with opposite sign for the zero-order discount. There is no additional independent restriction here. The opening-auction model does not itself supply an observed pre-slot quote; interpreting $\bar P$ as a model pre-opportunity value is different from measuring an actual deal-level price change.

Empirically these identities concern a fixed public state or conditional cell. A pooled $E[P]-E[P\mid X=0]$ across heterogeneous states need not preserve them: the zero-order subsample changes the weights on cells with different baseline prices. One must condition appropriately or have an actual state-specific pre-opportunity value. This qualification applies to the takeover's “in data” formula.

### CM ties

Equation (2) holds under CM. At $\tau=M$, (3) detects any $e_+>\rho$; at $\tau=m$, $T=-f_-(1-e_-)A(m)\le0$, with equality exactly at $e_-=1$. At $\tau=\pi$, however,

$$
T=f_+(1-e_0)A(M)-f_-(e_0-\rho)A(m)
$$

can have either sign or be zero while preparation differs across orders. This is a genuine equilibrium qualification. At benchmark $r=3,\pi=1/2$, set $c_H=B_3(1/2)=103/24$, preserving the two-point cost law. Both correct-order margins are strictly positive. With $e_0=0.9$, $e_- =0.25,e_+=1$, I obtain $T=-0.116335337738$ even though a purchase increases preparation. With $e_0=0.697965866936$, $T=0$. Solver A correctly excludes this tie from the sign classification.

### The purchases-only false positive is an equilibrium

Keep every benchmark primitive except $\pi=0.3,r=2$, and use $\sigma_H=\delta_1,\sigma_L=\delta_0$. Direct calculation gives

$$
\Delta=0.25,\quad \tau=0.664285714286,\quad
(\mu_-,\mu_0,\mu_+)=(0.3,0.161033802846,0.538101526224).
$$

All three entry probabilities equal $\rho=0.25$. Investor optimality holds strictly:

$$
U_H(1)=0.008868654611>0,
\qquad U_L(-1)=-0.00125<0.
$$

Wrong-signed orders are strictly inferior to abstention. The three distinct prices reveal the displayed posteriors, all below the threshold, so the entry and pricing conditions also hold. Thus this is an equilibrium under either investor rule, not merely a proposed profile.

$$
(P(-1),P(0),P(1))
=(0.503125,0.494439612678,0.518006345389),
$$

$$
I_+=0.023566732711,\quad I_-=-0.008685387322,
\quad \boxed{T=0.008685387322>0.}
$$

There is **no recruitment**. Here the sale impact as defined in the paper is negative. The takeover's counterexample is correct. Solver B's $\pi=1/2,r=6$ calculation $T=0.099307824060$ is also algebraically correct, but that purchases-only profile is not an equilibrium: the low-class sale earns $0.240416666667>0$. Use the takeover's example for an equilibrium counterexample.

The precise missing term for arbitrary order patterns is

$$
\boxed{\bar P-P(0)
=e_0\Delta(\pi-\mu_0)
+\sum_x f_x(e_x-e_0)A(\mu_x).}
\tag{6}
$$

To prove (6), subtract $t_0+e_0A(\mu_0)$ from the average price, add and subtract $e_0\sum_xf_xA(\mu_x)$, and use affinity and Bayes plausibility. In the purchases-only example the first term is the whole positive statistic.

The takeover's useful extension is therefore **proved**: if an observed order $x^\circ$ has $\mu(x^\circ)=\pi<\tau$, then

$$
\bar P-P(x^\circ)=\sum_x f_x(e_x-\rho)A(\mu_x)\ge0,
$$

strictly iff some order raises preparation. For purchases-only profiles, the uninformative order is the **sale**, and in the counterexample $\bar P-P(-1)=0$. On the recruiting R example $\pi=0.55,r=1.45$, it equals $0.106865091756$, as claimed. The researcher must know the relevant order pattern; a universally uninformative zero order cannot be assumed.

### Full-order existence, uniqueness and the hidden-price comparison

The all-candidate bound

$$
\rho\Delta\min\{m,1-M\}>k
\tag{7}
$$

forces full orders uniquely, under either investor rule, in both the visible and hidden economies. Conditional on a particular full-order tuple, the actual incentive requirements are $e_-\Delta m\ge k$ and $e_+\Delta(1-M)\ge k$ under IM; I0 requires strict inequalities. Solver F's “only if both margins are strictly positive” is too strong under IM.

The generalized $r_S(\pi)$ calculated from (7) is a **sufficient forcing cutoff**, not generally the exact onset of unique full trade. For example, with benchmark slot and $\pi=0.6,r=2.3$, unique visible full trade already holds below the forcing cutoff $2.394819662574$. The low-class all-candidate margin is $0.012660629311$. For the high class, below the entry threshold the margin is bounded below by $\rho\Delta(1-\tau)-k=0.009744725346$, and above it by $\Delta(1-M)-k=0.052357832500$; both are positive. Thus every candidate forces the full purchase as well.

Yet hiding the price in that example changes the high class's strategy: the full-purchase margin at constant entry $\rho$ is $-0.001910541875$. Under IM the hidden equilibrium has high purchase mass $s=0.272868806049<\lambda$, full low sales, $\mu_0=0.648875449405\ne0.6$, and

$$
\bar P-P(0)=-0.004489103777\ne0.
$$

Therefore “hide the price, hence $T=0$ and $S=\rho\Delta(M-m)$” needs full orders in that economy separately. Solver A supplies (7) for precisely this reason; the corresponding Solver B and takeover statements need it or an equivalent verification.

Solver B's winner-based collinearity extension is algebraically sound after making its hypotheses explicit: $W_H>W_L$, distinct lower-order posteriors, and $e_-=e_0=\rho$. Then $w_x=W_L+(W_H-W_L)\mu_x$, so the line through the two lower points predicts $t_0+\rho A(\mu_+)$ at $w_+$. The purchase residual is $(e_+-\rho)A(\mu_+)$. It is $(1-\rho)A(M)$ only when recruitment is full and the actual purchase posterior equals the full-order bound $M$. Dropping full orders alone does not justify that last substitution.


## Shared candidate 2: what counts and prices identify

**Verdict: the inversions are proved; distinguish the count law from its equilibrium-restricted identified set.** Solver A's local formulation is the most precise. The takeaway is stronger than the original symmetric-prior nonidentification statement, but it is not unconditional identification of every primitive from prices.

### Full two-sided trade

Add and subtract the two nonzero-order probabilities:

$$
v=f_++f_-=2b+\lambda,\qquad
d=f_+-f_-=E[X]=\lambda(2\pi-1).
\tag{8}
$$

If the prior is known and differs from one-half,

$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{d}{2\pi-1},\quad
b=\frac{v-\lambda}{2},\quad
\beta=\frac{v-\lambda}{2(1-\lambda)},\quad
\vartheta=\frac{\lambda}{v}.}
\tag{9}
$$

Thus the known-prior count nonidentification is specific to one-half. If the prior is unknown, the count-equivalent parameters can instead be indexed by $\lambda$:

$$
\pi=\frac12\left(1+\frac d\lambda\right),\quad
b=\frac{v-\lambda}{2},\quad
\beta=\frac{v-\lambda}{2(1-\lambda)},
$$

$$
|d|<\lambda<v<1,\qquad \lambda\ge3v-2.
\tag{10}
$$

The first restrictions ensure interior prior and positive liquidity; the last is exactly $\beta\le1/3$. For $d=0$, positive informed arrival identifies $\pi=1/2$, while leaving $\lambda$ free in the feasible interval. For $d\ne0$, the takeover's prior-interval parameterization is equivalent to (10), with the $|2\pi-1|<1$ endpoint excluded. At the benchmark counts generated by $\pi=0.6$, the slot-feasible interval is

$$
\pi'\in(0.546211715726,0.684625941328],
$$

and at true $\pi=0.7$ it is $(0.592423431452,0.869251882655]$. These numbers are correct as **slot-feasibility** intervals.

They do not certify full-order equilibria at every point. At $r=3$, the same counts as true $\pi=0.6$ are produced algebraically by $\pi'=0.55,\lambda'=0.672243537830$, but the full low sale then earns $-0.012347546117$. That point is not a full-order equilibrium. With the known benchmark auction, low-sale incentives reduce the above interval's lower endpoint to $0.557432711254$, included under IM. One must also check high-order incentives and recruitment status.

Solver B's smaller example family $\pi'\in[0.56,0.65]$ does survive those checks at both $r=3$ and $3.6$. At the endpoints:

| $\pi'$ | $\lambda'$ | $\beta'$ | $I_-$ at $r=3$ | $U_L(-1)$ at $r=3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.56 | 0.560202948192 | 0.190029583414 | 0.069700680236 | 0.003632653098 |
| 0.65 | 0.224081179277 | 0.324306265396 | 0.025741728496 | 0.062591604838 |

For this interval, $m$ increases and $M$ decreases with $\pi'$; its $M$ values run from $0.907440575$ to $0.778316121$, above both preparation thresholds. The low-class bound is positive throughout. For H, split candidate purchase posteriors at $\tau$: the bounds $\rho\Delta(1-\tau)>k$ and $\Delta(1-M)>k$ force a full purchase. Both hold throughout this interval at both strengths. Thus its full-order uniqueness claim is valid even though the coarser bound (7) fails at $\pi'=0.56,r=3$.

**Boundary correction to “never identifies.”** Count-law nonidentification is a valid generic message, but known equilibrium restrictions can collapse the feasible curve. An explicit asymmetric example uses $\pi=0.6,\lambda=0.4,\beta=1/3$, the benchmark costs and values, and a uniform incumbent with

$$
\Delta=0.24,\qquad r=1.973212111193.
$$

The counts are $v=0.8,d=0.08$; full trade is an IM equilibrium with $m=1/3$, $M=9/11$, $\tau=0.663236819828$, $U_L(-1)=0$, and $U_H(1)=0.023636363636>0$. Any alternative count-equivalent slot must have $\lambda'\ge3v-2=0.4$. Its sale posterior is

$$
m(\lambda')=\frac{(\lambda'+d)(v-\lambda')}{2\lambda'(v-d)},
\qquad
m'(\lambda')=\frac{-1-dv/(\lambda')^2}{2(v-d)}<0.
$$

All such priors satisfy $\pi'\le0.6<\tau$, so the low-entry sale constraint applies. It requires $m(\lambda')\ge k/(\rho\Delta)=1/3$, hence $\lambda'\le0.4$. Only $\lambda'=0.4,\pi'=0.6$ remains. Therefore the takeover's universal nonidentification language is false if it includes all known incentive restrictions. The corrected result is local nonidentification at points with slack feasibility, incentive and entry inequalities, as Solver A states. Purely from the unrestricted count mapping, (10) remains correct.

### Sale-price identification with an unknown prior

Assume $e_-=e_0=\rho$, and that the product $\rho\Delta>0$ is known. In the original application, $\rho$ is separately observed from conditional preparation and $\Delta$ is supplied from the auction. Define

$$
Q=\frac{f_-I_-}{\rho\Delta}.
$$

Equation (1) gives $Q=\pi(1-\pi)\lambda$. Substituting (10),

$$
4Q=\lambda-\frac{d^2}{\lambda},\qquad
\lambda^2-4Q\lambda-d^2=0.
$$

Since $Q>0$, exactly one root is positive, giving

$$
\boxed{\lambda=2Q+\sqrt{4Q^2+d^2},\qquad
\pi=\frac12\left(1+\frac d\lambda\right).}
\tag{11}
$$

Then (9) supplies $b,\beta$. At $d=0$, $\lambda=4Q$ and $\pi=1/2$, recovering A.12(d). The Solver F/takeover quadratic in $t=2\pi-1$, $t^2+4(Q/d)t-1=0$, is equivalent for $d\ne0$. Its root in $(-1,1)$ has the sign of $d$; the other root has magnitude greater than one. Formula (11) avoids a separate division by a near-zero imbalance.

At a known asymmetric prior the counts already identify the slot, and prices instead identify

$$
\rho\Delta=\frac{f_-I_-}{\pi(1-\pi)\lambda}.
\tag{12}
$$

This identifies $\Delta$ if $\rho$ is observed, not both unknown factors separately. The alternative known-prior inverse is also correct: with $s=I_-/(\rho\Delta)$,

$$
s=\frac{\pi(1-\pi)u}{1+(1-\pi)u}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
u=\frac{s}{(1-\pi)(\pi-s)},\quad
b=\frac{v}{2+u},\quad \lambda=ub.
$$

On a fully recruiting C1 branch, (3) additionally gives $A(M)=T/[f_+(1-\rho)]$, and then $w_L=A(M)-\Delta M$ when $\Delta,M$ are known. Under partial recruitment replace $1-\rho$ by the observed $e_+-\rho$.

The entry-regime restriction is essential. If $m<\tau\le\pi$, the clean impact is instead $I_+=\Delta(M-\pi)$, so use $Q=f_+I_+/\Delta$. If preparation is constant at a known level $q$, use $q\Delta$. **The sign $T=0$ cannot distinguish $q=\rho$ from $q=1$**; conditional preparation or other regime information is needed. For example, misapplying the floor-normalized sale inverse at the valid sale-deterrence equilibrium $\pi=0.7,r=2.5$ gives the impossible $\lambda=6.332808744858$.

The reported numeric inversions reproduce the benchmark slot at all candidate parameter points in the stated domain, including Solver B's $\pi\in\{0.3,0.45,0.5,0.62\}$, $r\in\{3,3.6,5\}$. At $\pi=0.6,r=3$, $d=0.067224353783$, $f_-=0.330063747746$, $I_-=0.040734163774$; either appropriate inverse returns $\lambda=0.336121768915$, $\beta=0.294655$, and, when estimated, $\pi=0.6$.

The informed share is $\lambda/v$ at every prior; its equality with $M-m$ is specific to one-half. Indeed

$$
\frac{M-m}{\lambda/v}
=\frac{\pi(1-\pi)(2+u)^2}{1+u+u^2\pi(1-\pi)}\le1,
$$

because denominator minus numerator is $(1+u)[1-4\pi(1-\pi)]\ge0$. Equality holds only at one-half. At $\pi=0.6$, $M-m=0.447454669331$, while the informed share is $0.462117157260$.

Finally, for independent opportunities and a known prior,

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\lambda)
=\frac{v-d^2}{n(2\pi-1)^2}.
$$

This follows from $X^2=\mathbf1\{X\ne0\}$. The variance multiplier is 100 at $\pi=0.55$ and 25 at $0.6$. Exact identification away from one-half does not give uniform finite-sample precision near it.

### Purchases only and Corollary A.3

When L abstains and H purchases at effective rate $s_+=\lambda\sigma_H(1)$,

$$
f_-=b,\qquad f_+=b+\pi s_+,\qquad
d=\pi s_+,\qquad v=2b+\pi s_+.
\tag{13}
$$

Counts identify $b=(v-d)/2$; a known prior identifies $s_+=d/\pi$. If H purchases surely when informed, this identifies $\lambda$ at **every** interior prior, including one-half. The realized informed share of nonzero orders is $d/v$, identified even without the prior. This is different from identifying the investor's arrival probability.

On a strictly mixed, fully recruiting R branch, H indifference is

$$
\Delta\frac{(1-\pi)b}{b+\pi s_+}=k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s_+=\frac b\pi\left[\frac{(1-\pi)\Delta}{k}-1\right].
\tag{14}
$$

Thus the conjectured Solver F formula is correct, conditional on the R branch's existence and strict entry regime. For fixed $b,s_+,\pi$, the class-conditional laws are

$$
f_H=(b,1-2b-s_+,b+s_+),\qquad
f_L=(b,1-2b,b).
$$

Vary $\lambda$ locally while setting $\sigma_H(1)=s_+/\lambda$ and $\beta=b/(1-\lambda)$. These laws, posteriors, prices, entry probabilities and deviation payoffs remain unchanged. With an interior mixture and feasible liquidity, this proves local nonidentification of arrival intensity **even after adding prices**.

Solver A's example $\pi=0.6,r=1.4$ checks out: $s_+=0.046575009567$, $\mu_+=0.65>\tau=0.640974440895$, $\mu_0=0.580754953810$, and $U_L(-1)=-0.011428571429$. The slots $\lambda=0.336121768915$ and $\lambda'=0.286121768915,\beta'=0.274017376721$ generate identical observations, with H mixing probabilities $0.138565882588$ and $0.162780377541$. The takeover's $r=1.45$ rates $s_R=0.291736223717$ at prior one-half and $0.203126903009$ at $0.55$, and its alternative $\beta'=0.290282474128$, also reproduce.

## Primitive rescue of the naive impact comparison

**Verdict: the $\pi\ge1/2,\rho\le1/2$ sufficient condition is proved under C1/full recruitment; Solver F's necessity of exact symmetry is false.**

Under full orders and $\pi<\tau$,

$$
I_+-I_-=
\rho\Delta\frac{\pi(1-\pi)\lambda^2(1-2\pi)}{f_+f_-}
+(e_+-\rho)A(M).
\tag{15}
$$

The first term follows by subtracting the two fractions in (1); its sign is exactly that of $1-2\pi$. Therefore:

- At $\pi<1/2$, **every** non-recruiting full-order equilibrium is a false positive for the naive strict-positive comparison.
- At $\pi\ge1/2$, a positive naive difference implies recruitment.
- At $\pi\le1/2$, any positive recruitment increment makes the naive difference positive, whatever $\rho<1$.

For C1 recruitment, $e_+=1$, and (15) rearranges to

$$
I_+-I_-=(1-\rho)w_L+\Delta(M-2\rho\pi+\rho m).
$$

If $\rho\le1/2$, then $M-2\rho\pi\ge M-\pi>0$, proving the converse at $\pi\ge1/2$. The exact full-recruitment condition is

$$
\rho<\frac{w_L+\Delta M}{w_L+\Delta(2\pi-m)}.
\tag{16}
$$

The simpler slot-only sufficient bound $\rho<M/(2\pi-m)>1/2$ follows from $w_L\ge0$. Its minimum over $\pi>1/2$ is $0.911394935321$ at the benchmark ratio, $0.798224193144$ at $u=5$, and $0.661761212758$ at $u=20$, agreeing with the takeover's approximate values. At one-half, (16)'s bound is exactly one.

## Entry-component trend: exact condition and counterexamples

**Verdict: the primitive conditions and Solver A's competitive-component reformulation are proved. FOSD alone is insufficient, including at prior one-half with all benchmark costs unchanged.**

Keep the slot and prior fixed, so $M$ is fixed. Under the standing all-admissible class ranking, A1's cash-flow formula gives

$$
A_F(M)=pF(p)+Q_F(M),\qquad
Q_F(M)=\int_p^\infty[1-G_M(y)][1-F(y)]\,dy,
$$

where $G_M=(1-M)G_L+MG_H$. The first term is the value of rescuing a sale when the incumbent misses the reserve. Let $\widetilde F\le F$ be a strengthening and $\delta(y)=F(y)-\widetilde F(y)\ge0$. Subtract the two expressions:

$$
\boxed{A_{\widetilde F}(M)-A_F(M)
=\int_p^\infty[1-G_M(y)]\delta(y)\,dy-p\delta(p).}
\tag{17}
$$

This proves the common exact pairwise condition. A strict trend requires the integral to **exceed** $p\delta(p)$; equality gives no increase. Because $1-G_M=(1-G_L)+MD\ge MD$,

$$
Q_{\widetilde F}(M)-Q_F(M)
\ge M\int_p^\infty D(y)\delta(y)\,dy
=M[\Delta(\widetilde F)-\Delta(F)]\ge0.
\tag{18}
$$

Thus Solver A's proposed replacement—monotonicity of the entry benefit **after removing reserve rescue**—needs no additional FOSD restriction. It is strictly increasing whenever $\Delta$ increases strictly.

The kernel interpretation is also correct: $A_F(M)=E_F[\alpha_M(R)]$, where $\alpha_M(R)=p$ for $R<p$, and $\alpha_M(R)=\int_p^R[1-G_M(y)]dy$ for $R\ge p$. It drops at the reserve. This is exactly why FOSD does not order its expectation.

The proposed sufficient primitive conditions follow as stated, with strictness specified:

1. **Constant reserve-failure probability:** $\delta(p)=0$. Equation (18) then proves strict total growth whenever $\Delta$ rises strictly. This includes incumbents that always meet the reserve.
2. **Nondecreasing low-class entry benefit:** $w_L(\widetilde F)\ge w_L(F)$. Since $A(M)=w_L+M\Delta$ and $M>0$, strict spread growth gives strict $A(M)$ growth.
3. **Takeover's lower-support condition:** if every challenger value is at least $\ell_0$ and $\int_p^{\ell_0}\delta(y)dy\ge p\delta(p)$, then the low-class entry benefit cannot fall. Strict $\Delta$ growth supplies strictness for $A(M)$ even if this inequality binds.
4. **Uniform expansion with a low reserve:** for $R\sim U[0,r]$, $r>p$, and any admissible class mixture,
   $$
   \frac{dA_r(M)}{dr}
   =\frac{E_{G_M}[\min\{\theta,r\}^2]-3p^2}{2r^2}.
   \tag{19}
   $$
   To obtain (19), integrate $y\,\Pr(\theta>y)$ on $[p,r]$; it equals $\{E[\min(\theta,r)^2]-p^2\}/2$, while differentiating $pF_r(p)$ contributes $-p^2/r^2$. Consequently $\min\{\ell_0,r_{\min}\}^2>3p^2$ is sufficient, not necessary.
5. **Smooth location shift:** differentiating the kernel formula gives
   $$
   \frac{dA_{F_s}(M)}{ds}
   =-p f_s(p)+\int_p^\infty[1-G_M(y)]f_s(y)dy
   =-p f_s(p)+\Pr(p<R<\theta).
   $$
   The probability uses independent $R\sim F_s,\theta\sim G_M$. Nonnegative/positive right side gives the corresponding weak/strict local trend.

All these statements need a fixed posterior mixture. They do not automatically carry to an endogenous research path on which the slot and $M$ change.

While full recruitment persists, its price component is $(1-\rho)A_F(M)$. Strict growth of $\Delta$ and $A_F(M)$ gives all of A.11(iv)'s strict trends. For the **total spread alone**, rising $A_F(M)$ is sufficient but not necessary: a falling entry component can be outweighed by the class component. The exact change is $\rho(M-m)\,\delta\Delta+(1-\rho)\,\delta A(M)$.

### Benchmark margins

At the benchmark,

$$

\qquad
A_r'(M)=\frac1{8r^2}+M\left(\frac12-\frac1{2r^2}\right).
$$

The lower-support reserve margin is $\ell^2-3p^2=1/4$; equivalently $\ell-\sqrt3p=0.133974596216$, or $\ell/\sqrt3-p=0.077350269190$. ### Ceiling jumps

The shared downward-jump statement is correct if the slot/prior stay fixed, full orders persist on both sides, $A(M)$ and $\Delta$ are continuous, the prior stays below the preparation threshold, and there is an actual crossing from purchase recruitment to exclusion. The one-sided spread and purchase-impact limits differ by $(1-\rho)A(M)$; $T$ differs by $f_+(1-\rho)A(M)$. Under C1 the value at a threshold equality is the recruiting value. CM allows intermediate entry at the equality. FOSD and nondecreasing $\tau$ give a down-set of recruiting strengths, but do not alone guarantee a finite ceiling, a strict crossing, or nonempty full-trade intervals on both sides.

## Announcement returns and A.14 positivity

**Verdict: the exact uncertainty condition is proved; $B_F(\pi)<c_H$ is a useful sufficient primitive condition, not a necessary one.**

This argument needs no particular informed order pattern. Let $A$ denote preparation. Cost independence implies that a preparation report leaves the class posterior at $\mu_x$. Consequently,

$$
\Delta_x^1=(1-e_x)A_F(\mu_x),\qquad
\Delta_x^0=-e_xA_F(\mu_x).
$$

Multiplying by report probabilities gives $e_x\Delta_x^1+(1-e_x)\Delta_x^0=0$. The no-preparation report has positive probability only when $e_x<1$. Pooling preparation reports uses the weights $f_xe_x/\Pr(A)$, hence

$$
\boxed{
E[\Delta_X^1\mid A]
=\frac{\sum_x f_xe_x(1-e_x)A_F(\mu_x)}
       {\sum_x f_xe_x}.}
\tag{21}
$$

All $f_x>0$, $e_x\ge\rho>0$, and $A_F(\mu_x)>0$ under the maintained positive spread and liquidity support. Every summand is therefore nonnegative, and the numerator is positive exactly when some $e_x<1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{E[\Delta_X^1\mid A]>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Pr(A)<1.}
\tag{22}
$$

This is valid under C1 and CM, including ties. The mean is zero exactly when preparation was already certain after every order.

If $B_F(\pi)<c_H$, then Bayes plausibility gives

$$
\sum_x f_xB_F(\mu_x)=B_F(\pi)<c_H.
$$

Some on-path order must have $B_F(\mu_x)<c_H$, so its entry is $\rho<1$ and its contribution to (21) is positive. This proves the all-equilibrium sufficient condition at every prior. FOSD lowers $B_F(\pi)$, so once this strict inequality holds it persists along a strengthening with fixed prior and costs.

The condition cannot simply be dropped. Both candidate counterexamples reproduce:

- At $\pi=0.7,r=1.2$, the no-informed-trade equilibrium has entry one everywhere. H and L correct-order deviations earn $-0.015$ and $-0.008333333333$; preparation-report returns are zero.
- At $\pi=0.9,r=3$, full two-sided trade is an equilibrium with $m=0.768030683316>\tau=0.705$ and entry one everywhere. H and L margins are $0.006180200367$ and $0.492020455544$; the mean is again zero.

Conversely, $\pi<\tau$ is not necessary: at $\pi=0.7,r=2$, zero and purchase induce certain entry but a sale leaves entry at $\rho$, giving mean $0.039524804132>0$.

At the benchmark the minimum $c_H-B_r(1/2)$ over $[1.2,3.6]$ is $1.195833333333$. Over the larger open interval $(1,10)$ its infimum is $1.125$, and the infimum of $\tau(r)-1/2$ is $0.125$. The corresponding whole-declared-path sufficient prior bound is $\pi<\tau(1.2)=0.633116883117$. These reproduce the candidates' respective domains and margins.

A positive preparation-report mean does **not** identify price-induced recruitment. If $e_x\equiv\rho$, (21) becomes $(1-\rho)A_F(\pi)>0$. At benchmark $r=3.6$, where the weighted recruitment statistic is zero, the preparation-report mean is $0.701041666667$, exceeding the recruiting-state mean at $r=3$.

## Ranking and recommendation

1. **Adopt the frequency-weighted recruitment result, with its exact domain.** State that, under full two-sided informed trade and $B_F(\pi)<c_H$, the adjusted impact $I_+-(f_-/f_+)I_-$ equals $(e_+-\rho)A_F(M)$ and is positive exactly when a purchase recruits. This needs no symmetric prior or new auction estimation. Add the C1 all-threshold sign classification and identify A.14(iv) as the same moment. Solver A's formulation is the best base; the takeover supplies the useful purchases-only domain counterexample and uninformative-order extension.
2. **Replace blanket count nonidentification by the known/unknown-prior theorem.** At known asymmetric priors counts identify the slot; at unknown priors they generically confound arrival intensity and prior tilt. A clean price moment with known payoff scale identifies both. Keep Solver A's local equilibrium qualification, distinguish realized informed share from arrival intensity on purchases-only branches, and retain the explicit mixed-branch nonidentification.
3. **State the exact announcement strictness condition.** The preparation-report mean is positive exactly when preparation remains uncertain. $B_F(\pi)<c_H$ is a transparent sufficient condition for positivity in every equilibrium. This improves the theorem without adding machinery.
4. **For the entry trend, use Solver A's decomposition or name a primitive condition.** The competitive-bidding contribution always rises under FOSD; the full entry benefit rises only when competitive gains outweigh lost reserve rescue. Constant reserve-failure probability is a simple general sufficient condition; the low-reserve uniform condition transparently validates the benchmark. The mild counterexample should prevent an unrestricted generalization.
5. **Keep the naive-test rescue as an optional corollary.** Priors at least one-half and readiness at most one-half suffice under C1. This is true and inexpensive, but frequency weighting is broader and uses data the empirical design already requires.

The exact-symmetry necessity claim in Solver F C2—and the “there and only there” wording in Solver B C1's introduction—is false for the naive diagnostic. Unrestricted extensions of the zero-order diagnostic, unconditional preservation of full orders after hiding prices, and universal equilibrium nonidentification without boundary qualifications are also false. None of these failures invalidates the corrected weighted result.

## What remains weak after the best workaround

The diagnostic still conditions on a trading pattern, a public-state cell, symmetric liquidity order probabilities, and the paper's information and cost-independence structure. Counts alone need not reveal which equilibrium pattern generated them. A purchases-only equilibrium can produce a positive zero-order discount without any recruitment, and a CM prior-threshold tie can cancel recruitment and deterrence in the weighted moment.

The price inversion still needs the correct entry baseline and a known payoff scale, or a known asymmetric prior. It does not identify all auction primitives automatically, and near one-half count identification is statistically weak. Interior mixed purchases-only equilibria preserve arrival-intensity nonidentification even with prices.

Finally, informative prices and a rising payoff spread do not ensure a rising total entry component. The reserve-rescue tradeoff is economically substantive and survives at the symmetric benchmark prior. Announcement positivity establishes preparation uncertainty, not recruitment caused by prices. These are the remaining boundaries the upgraded theory should state plainly.
