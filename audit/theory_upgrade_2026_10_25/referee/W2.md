## Shared Candidate 1: factorization, entry, and ownership against a given incumbent value

**Verdict: proved, with the branch and conditional-probability scope stated explicitly.**

The relevant sources are [the model's independence and timing](overleaf/slot/sections/02_model.tex:90), [the ownership proof](overleaf/slot/appendix/A3_proposition2.tex:75), and [the intermediate research equilibria](overleaf/slot/appendix/A5_research.tex:122).

Write $A$ for preparation. Conditional on class $j$, the cost and order determine $A$, and are independent of $(\theta,R)$. Thus, for any equilibrium of this information structure,

$$
\begin{aligned}
O_H(F)
 &=\Pr(H)\Pr(A\mid H)\Pr(\theta>R\mid H,A)\\
 &=\pi e_H(F)\int F(y)\,dG_H(y)
 =\pi e_H(F)W_H(F).
\end{aligned}
$$

Every challenger value meets the reserve. Continuity of $F$ eliminates acquisition ties from this unconditional calculation, even if $G_H$ has atoms. The same proof gives $O_L=(1-\pi)e_LW_L$.

In a general equilibrium, $e_H$ is not necessarily a single-valued function of the spread: equilibria can differ at the same auction. The specialized notation $e_H(\Delta)$ is valid on the named branch R, with all non-auction primitives fixed.

For that branch take prior $1/2$, $0<\rho<1$, $0<\beta\le1/3$, $a,k>0$, cap below one, and

$$
\frac12<\tau<1,\qquad
\rho\Delta<2k,\qquad
\lambda_E(\tau)\le\lambda_R(\Delta)<\bar\lambda.
$$

At equality $\lambda_R=\lambda_E$, the purchase must recruit fully, as C1 requires and CM permits. No connectedness of the set of branch economies is assumed. The branch has H buying after research succeeds, L abstaining, and only a purchase recruiting. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_H&=\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1-\beta)\lambda_R],\\
e_L&=\rho+(1-\rho)\beta(1-\lambda_R),\\
\mathsf E_R&=\tfrac12(e_H+e_L)
 =\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)\lambda_R].
\end{aligned}
$$

For $d=2\beta+(1-2\beta)\lambda$,

$$
m_\lambda=\frac{\beta(1-\lambda)}d,
\qquad m_\lambda'=-\frac{\beta}{d^2}<0.
$$

The equation $2a\lambda=\Delta m_\lambda-k$ has one root in $(0,1)$ when $\Delta>2k$: its left side minus right side is negative at zero, positive at one, and has derivative $2a-\Delta m_\lambda'>0$. Implicit differentiation gives

$$
\Lambda'(\Delta)=\frac{m_{\Lambda(\Delta)}}{2a-\Delta m'_{\Lambda(\Delta)}}>0.
$$

Consequently $e_H$ and total entry rise strictly, and $e_L$ falls strictly, with $\Delta$ on R. A first-order stochastic strengthening $F_1\le F_0$ gives

$$
\Delta_1-\Delta_0=\int_p^\infty[F_0(y)-F_1(y)]D(y)\,dy\ge0.
$$

Strict increases require this integral to be positive. FOSD alone does not guarantee strictness.

For a fixed incumbent value $x$, let $q_H(x)$ be the probability that a prepared H challenger wins against that value under the auction's allocation rule. Then the corresponding conditional allocation kernel is

$$
\Pr(H\text{ acquires}\mid R=x)=\pi e_H(F)q_H(x).
$$

The kernel $q_H$ does not depend on $F$; the preceding monotonicity proves the proposed fixed-value comparison. Away from atoms of $G_H$, it is $1$ for $x<p$, and $\Pr_H(\theta>x)$ for $x\ge p$. The candidates' literal “every $x$” strict-survival formula omits a small technical issue: at $x$ equal to a class-value atom, acquisition depends on the auction's value-tie rule. Continuity of $F$ makes those points null **before**, not after, conditioning on that exact value. State the result almost everywhere, or define $q_H$ to include the fixed tie allocation. Outside a law's support this is a counterfactual kernel, rather than an identified conditional probability.

The capture rate

$$
\Pr(A\mid H,\theta>R)=e_H
$$

also follows by independence. On R, $g_H>g_L\ge0$, so $g_H>0$, which implies a positive probability of H winning. These conditional events and the ownership ratios below have positive denominators.

**Corrected statement.** On the interior purchase-recruitment branch, a larger spread raises H participation and its probability of acquisition against each fixed incumbent value at which H can win. No restriction on the path of $W_H$ is needed. At a capped branch, the same formulas yield weak rather than strict growth while the order pattern remains fixed.

Solver B's additional fixed-value challenger-win calculation is also valid: class ranking gives $q_H\ge q_L$, so

$$
\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Pr(\text{challenger acquires}\mid R=x)
=\tfrac12(1-\rho)[(1-\beta)q_H(x)-\beta q_L(x)]
\ge\tfrac12(1-\rho)(1-2\beta)q_H(x).
$$

Call this acquisition by the challenger; when $x<p$, “the incumbent loses” could otherwise include no-sale outcomes. Conditional allocation to the higher-valued prepared bidder is efficient, but this is **not** a welfare theorem about the equilibrium change, which also changes research, trading and preparation costs.

## Shared Candidate 1: the comparison equilibrium, its existence and uniqueness

**Verdict: the zero-research multiplier is proved at every R point. An arbitrary fixed-information comparison is a different claim.**

At zero conjectured research, each order is a liquidity order and has posterior $1/2$. Since $\tau>1/2$, entry is $\rho$ after every order, and all orders have the same competitive price

$$
P=t_0+\rho[t_L-t_0+\Delta/2].
$$

After a hypothetical research success, the correct order earns

$$
U_H(1)=U_L(-1)=\rho\Delta/2-k<0,
$$

abstention earns zero, and a wrong-signed order earns at most $-k$. Abstention is uniquely optimal for both classes. The successful-research value is zero, so a joint deviation to intensity $z$ earns $-az^2/2$, uniquely maximized at zero. This verifies beliefs, prices, preparation, orders and research optimality at Z; the manuscript gives the same construction at [A5:141](overleaf/slot/appendix/A5_research.tex:141).

The inequality needed for this construction is itself the low-class abstention condition on R. Thus **Z exists at every standard R point**, irrespective of whether $W_H$ falls, how the branch is connected, or which class distributions produced the auction objects.

Uniqueness has three distinct meanings:

1. With $\rho\Delta<2k$, there is a unique equilibrium tuple with **zero research**, including its hypothetical-success orders; hence its ownership is uniquely $O_H^Z=\rho W_H/2$.
2. The research game is **not uniquely solved**: R coexists with Z. Under CM an additional threshold equilibrium can coexist. Uniqueness of a best research response at a given tuple does not mean uniqueness of equilibrium research.
3. At the boundary $\rho\Delta=2k$, Z still exists. Under IM, alternative hypothetical-success order mixtures can give the same zero-research outcome; under I0 abstention is selected. The research intensity, on-path prices, entry and ownership at zero research remain unique. A claim of unique complete strategies needs this boundary qualification.

Factorization then gives exactly

$$
\frac{O_H^R(F)}{O_H^Z(F)}
=\frac{e_H(\Delta(F))}{\rho}
=1+\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}[\beta+(1-\beta)\Lambda(\Delta(F))]>1.
$$

Its derivative with respect to $\Delta$ is

$$
\frac{(1-\rho)(1-\beta)}\rho\,
\frac{m_{\Lambda}}{2a-\Delta m'_{\Lambda}}>0.
$$

The additive ownership gain is instead $(e_H-\rho)W_H/2$ and can fall. This is an actual comparison of equilibria at the same incumbent law, but says nothing about which equilibrium is selected.

### Fixed information

Under [Proposition A.3](overleaf/slot/appendix/A6_robustness.tex:8), the joint law of class and signal and the cost law are fixed, and conditional values remain independent of the signal given class. Under C1,

$$
e_H^{\rm fix}(F)=\Pr\{C\le B_F(\mu(S))\mid H\}.
$$

FOSD lowers $B_F(\mu)$ at every fixed belief, so $e_H^{\rm fix}(F_1)\le e_H^{\rm fix}(F_0)$. It also lowers $W_H$. Hence fixed-information H ownership weakly falls. The takeover report's exact decomposition is correct:

$$
O_{H,0}^{\rm fix}-O_{H,1}^{\rm fix}
=\pi(e_{H,0}^{\rm fix}-e_{H,1}^{\rm fix})W_{H,1}
 +\pi e_{H,0}^{\rm fix}(W_{H,0}-W_{H,1}),
$$

with the entry difference equal to the stated conditional cost-crossing probability. A common alternative tie probability preserves the sign, although not this exact crossing formula.

If a **single signal law** is held fixed along an FOSD path, then

$$
\frac{O_H^R(F)}{O_H^{\rm fix}(F)}
=\frac{e_H^R(F)}{e_H^{\rm fix}(F)}
$$

weakly rises where the denominator is positive; it rises strictly when the spread rises strictly on interior R. This ratio need not exceed one and is not generally a function of the spread alone. An arbitrarily more informative control can recruit more H challengers than R. At the benchmark $r=1.6$, full orders frozen at the cap give

$$
e_H^{\rm fix}=0.648802606822,
\quad e_H^R=0.618604318280,
\quad O_H^R/O_H^{\rm fix}=0.953455352639<1.
$$

This frozen profile is not an equilibrium: its low-class sale payoff is $-0.012436022524$. The manuscript explicitly makes this equilibrium/control distinction at [A6:38–59](overleaf/slot/appendix/A6_robustness.tex:38).

If the control freezes an **earlier R equilibrium's** information, its entry at a stronger law is at most that earlier H entry. The later R entry is at least the earlier entry, so the later R ownership is at least the same-law frozen-control ownership. That valid decomposition requires neither a new equilibrium existence claim nor a unique frozen-information equilibrium.

## Shared Candidate 2: the exact level condition and decisive advantage

**Verdict: proved as sufficient restrictions; neither restriction is necessary in the general model.**

For two branch laws with positive ownership,

$$
\frac{O_{H,1}}{O_{H,0}}
=\frac{e_{H,1}}{e_{H,0}}\frac{W_{H,1}}{W_{H,0}}.
$$

Thus the exact finite condition for strict growth is

$$
\frac{W_{H,1}}{W_{H,0}}>\frac{e_{H,0}}{e_{H,1}}.
$$

On a differentiable path, the exact condition for a **positive derivative** is

$$
-\frac{W_H'}{W_H}
<\frac{(1-\rho)(1-\beta)}{e_H}
  \frac{m_{\Lambda}}{2a-\Delta m_{\Lambda}'}\Delta'.
$$

Derivative positivity is a local-slope statement; it should not be called necessary for global strict monotonicity at every point. If $q_s(y)=-\partial_sF_s(y)\ge0$ and differentiation under the integrals is legitimate, then

$$
-W_H' =\int q_s\,dG_H,
\qquad \Delta'=\int_p^\infty q_s(y)D(y)\,dy.
$$

Class ranking does not bound the first integral relative to the second. This validates Solver A's explanation of why a win-probability restriction is substantive.

Constant positive $W_H$ is sufficient whenever the spread rises. Under FOSD, constant $W_H$ is equivalent, for the compared pair, to unchanged $F(y)$ for $G_H$-almost every $y$. Support separation,

$$
\operatorname{ess\,inf}(\theta\mid H)
\ge\sup_s\operatorname{ess\,sup}R_s,
$$

is the simplest sufficient primitive and makes $W_H=1$.

### Decisive advantage

The Solver B condition is

$$
g_H(F)>c_H\quad\Longrightarrow\quad W_H(F)=1.
\tag{DA}
$$

Where $\gamma=g_H-g_L>0$, its antecedent is equivalent to $\tau<1$. Every posterior is strictly below one under the maintained liquidity support and research cap. If an order recruits, then

$$
c_H\le B_F(\mu)=g_L+\mu\gamma<g_H.
$$

DA therefore gives $W_H=1$ at every recruiting economy with this nondegeneracy. In particular it gives the level result on R. If a stronger economy recruits in Proposition 2, DA gives $W_{H,1}=1$, and FOSD forces $W_{H,0}=1$ as well. This proof is independent of slot parameters, trading cost, prior, $\rho$, and the research cost or success technology. It is an allocation restriction conditional on recruitment; it does not independently prove research monotonicity under every possible alternative technology. The statements about all recruiting equilibria should retain $\gamma>0$; a zero profit gap and an exact preparation tie are outside the threshold argument.

For point classes and the **full uniform family**,

$$
W_H(r)=\min\{h/r,1\},\qquad
 g_H(r)=
\begin{cases}
h-p,&r\le p,\\
h-r/2-p^2/(2r),&p\le r\le h,\\
(h^2-p^2)/(2r),&r\ge h.
\end{cases}
$$

For $r>h$, $g_H(r)$ decreases, with supremum $g_H(h)=(h^2-p^2)/(2h)$. Therefore DA on the full uniform family is equivalent to

$$
c_H\ge\frac{h^2-p^2}{2h}.
$$

For a general high class, the corresponding full-family criterion uses $g_H(U[0,\theta_{\min}])$. The takeover report overstates the **necessity** for an arbitrary restricted path that merely “reaches strengths above $\theta_{\min}$.” Necessity needs strengths arbitrarily close above that boundary. For example, on $r\in[20,25]$, $h=10,p=1/2,c_H=3$ satisfies DA because $g_H\le2.49375<3$, although $3<4.9875=g_H(h)$. The condition remains sufficient for every subpath.

### Benchmark verification

With $h=10,p=1/2,c_H=6$,

$$
g_H(h)=4.9875,\qquad c_H-g_H(h)=1.0125.
$$

On the relevant piece, $g_H(r)=6$ gives

$$
4r^2-32r+1=0,\qquad
r_*=4+\frac{\sqrt{63}}2=7.968626966596886.
$$

The small algebraic root lies below $p$, outside this payoff formula's piece. The unique actual crossing is $r_*$. Above it, high-class gross profit is at most the high cost; under the maintained posterior support, high-cost recruitment is impossible. Thus every recruiting uniform-benchmark economy has $r<r_*<h$, with margin $h-r_*=2.031373033403114$. This indeed covers every allowed slot, trading cost, $\rho$, prior, cap and research specification **holding the benchmark auction family and preparation cost fixed**.

It does not cover arbitrary $F$ merely because $h,\ell,p,c_H$ retain their benchmark values. The takeover report's mixture is a valid demonstration:

$$
F_c=0.996\,U[0,1.5]+0.004\,U[c-0.01,c+0.01].
$$

Using all other benchmark parameters and increasing $c:9.99\to10.01$ gives

| Quantity | $c=9.99$ | $c=10.01$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| $\Delta$ | 0.118960000 | 0.119000000 |
| $\tau$ | 0.647559295 | 0.647562211 |
| $\lambda_R$ | 0.295272987 | 0.295370853 |
| Total entry | 0.516465874 | 0.516480946 |
| $W_H$ | 1.000000000 | 0.996000000 |
| $O_H$ | 0.313596622 | 0.312368018 |
| H share of challenger acquisitions | 0.699521212 | 0.698706686 |

Both branch points have positive recruitment, sale and cap margins. Ownership falls about $0.392\%$. Monotonicity of $(\Delta,\tau)$ alone cannot repair the level claim.

### Takeover's point-mass bound: correct inequality, incorrect strict consequence

For point classes,

$$
\Delta=\int_\ell^h(1-F(y))\,dy
\ge(h-\ell)(1-F(h)),
$$

so $W_H\ge1-\Delta/(h-\ell)$. This bound and the branch bound $1-W_H<2k/[\rho(h-\ell)]$ are correct. At the benchmark the latter is $0.017777777778$, and a class-H entry gain of at least $1.809954751\%$ guarantees a strict ownership rise, because the branch inequality is strict.

However, the takeover report also says that

$$
\frac{e_{H,1}}{e_{H,0}}\ge
\frac{h-\ell}{h-\ell-\Delta_1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad O_{H,1}>O_{H,0}.
$$

This only guarantees **weak** growth. Equality is feasible, not just a logical possibility. Keep every benchmark parameter, put $z=0.99$, and let

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_0&=0.99U[0,1]+0.01U[v-0.001,v+0.001],\\
F_1&=0.99U[0,1]+0.01U[10.1,10.2],
\end{aligned}
$$

where $v=9.657180354948485\ldots$ is defined exactly by

$$
e_H(0.01(v-1))=0.99e_H(0.09).
$$

The numerical solution gives

$$
\Delta_0=0.08657180354948485,\quad \Delta_1=0.09,\quad
\frac{e_{H,1}}{e_{H,0}}=\frac1{0.99}
=\frac9{9-\Delta_1}.
$$

Here $W_{H,0}=1,W_{H,1}=0.99$ and

$$
O_{H,0}=O_{H,1}=0.2893720004945357.
$$

Both points are strict interior R equilibria: purchase margins are $0.019845770$ and $0.026744495$, and sale margins are $0.009178525$ and $0.00875$. Replace the sufficient condition's $\ge$ by $>$, or weaken its conclusion to $\ge$. Strictness also follows if another bound in the derivation is strict.

## Solver B Candidate 2: the research-entry elasticity bound

**Verdict: proved, and the proposed bound holds for all $0<\rho<1$. The numerical grid is unnecessary for this theorem.**

Let $\varepsilon_e=\Delta e_H'(\Delta)/e_H$. Define the class-H purchase probability

$$
Q=\beta+(1-\beta)\lambda,
\qquad e_H=\rho+(1-\rho)Q,
\qquad d=2\beta+(1-2\beta)\lambda=2Q-\lambda.
$$

Using $a>0$ and $\lambda=\Lambda(\Delta)>0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_e
 &=\frac{(1-\rho)(1-\beta)\Delta m_\lambda}
 {e_H(2a+\Delta|m_\lambda'|)}\\
 &<\frac{(1-\rho)(1-\beta)}{e_H}\frac{m_\lambda}{|m_\lambda'|}\\
 &=\frac{(1-\rho)(1-\beta)(1-\lambda)d}{e_H}\\
 &=\frac{(1-\rho)(1-Q)(2Q-\lambda)}{\rho+(1-\rho)Q}\\
 &<\frac{2(1-\rho)Q(1-Q)}{\rho+(1-\rho)Q}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Put $s=\sqrt\rho$ and $\overline E=2(1-s)/(1+s)$. Direct completion of the square gives

$$
\overline E[\rho+(1-\rho)Q]-2(1-\rho)Q(1-Q)
=\frac{2(1-s)}{1+s}[(1+s)Q-s]^2\ge0.
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\varepsilon_e<\frac{2(1-\sqrt\rho)}{1+\sqrt\rho}}
\qquad(0<\rho<1).
$$

The maximizer of the final envelope is $Q=s/(1+s)$. In particular $\varepsilon_e<2/3$ at $\rho=1/4$, for every auction producing a branch point and every positive quadratic research-cost coefficient and trading cost. The generality here is not established by a grid. Solver B's more complicated sufficient proof covers $\rho\le0.423911338174\ldots$; its stated $\rho\le0.42$ range is safe. The argument above removes the restriction entirely.

If the quadratic cost is replaced by a twice continuously differentiable convex cost $K(\lambda)$, while success probability is still $\lambda$, the interior condition is $2K'(\lambda)=\Delta m_\lambda-k$. The same proof uses denominator $2K''-\Delta m'$, with the first strict inequality replaced by a weak one when necessary; $\lambda>0$ still supplies strictness in the next line. This gives a precise extension, rather than an unrestricted assertion about every research technology. At a binding cap the smooth entry response is zero while the branch pattern remains unchanged.

### What the bound implies for uniform point masses

For $r>h$,

$$
W_H=\frac hr,\quad
\Delta=(h-\ell)-\frac{h^2-\ell^2}{2r},\quad
\gamma=\frac{h^2-\ell^2}{2r},\quad
\frac{d\log\Delta}{d\log r}=\frac\gamma\Delta.
$$

Hence

$$
\frac{d\log O_H}{d\log r}=\varepsilon_e\frac\gamma\Delta-1,
\qquad
\eta=\frac\Delta\gamma=\frac{2r-h-\ell}{h+\ell}.
$$

For $r<h$ on R, $r>\ell$, $W_H=1$, and $\Delta'(r)>0$, so ownership rises. For $r>h$,

$$
\eta>\frac{h-\ell}{h+\ell}.
$$

If $\rho>1/9$ and

$$
\frac h\ell\ge\frac{3-\sqrt\rho}{3\sqrt\rho-1},
$$

the last lower bound is at least $\overline E$; therefore ownership falls strictly above $h$. At $\rho=1/4$ the class-ratio threshold is five.

**Correct scope of the necessary-and-sufficient result:** for uniform incumbents and point classes with $\rho=1/4,h\ge5\ell$, at an interior R point with $r\ne h$, ownership has a positive derivative if and only if $r<h$. At $r=h$ it generally has a kink, with positive left derivative and negative right derivative if R extends to both sides. For capped research, the below-$h$ slope can be zero. No such equivalence holds for arbitrary incumbent families or for comparisons of endpoints that straddle $h$.

A computed finite-comparison counterexample to the latter interpretation keeps $h=10,\ell=1,p=1/2,\rho=1/4$, and sets $c_H=2.734,k=1.7,\beta=0.2,a=0.001,\bar\lambda=0.9$. Both $r=9.5$ and $r=10.1$ lie on strict R, yet

$$
O_H(9.5)=0.213561332858399
<0.220579423775675=O_H(10.1),
$$

although $W_H(10.1)=0.990099009901<1$. Local ownership above $h$ is falling, exactly as the theorem predicts.

The takeaway is strong but specific: the uniform point-mass branch has an ownership peak at $h$ when it passes through that value under these conditions. It is not a necessity theorem for DA, Proposition 2, or general class distributions.

The takeover's weaker ceiling is also correct. Since $e_H>(1-\rho)Q$,

$$
\varepsilon_e<\psi(\lambda)
=\frac{(1-\beta)(1-\lambda)[2\beta+(1-2\beta)\lambda]}{\beta+(1-\beta)\lambda}
<2(1-\beta).
$$

Writing $N=(1-\lambda)[2\beta+(1-2\beta)\lambda]$, one obtains

$$
N'Q-NQ'=-\beta(1+2\beta)-2\beta(1-2\beta)\lambda
 -(1-2\beta)(1-\beta)\lambda^2<0.
$$

Thus $\psi$ decreases from $2(1-\beta)$, and ownership falls for uniform point masses wherever $r\ge(h+\ell)(3/2-\beta)$ on R. The all-$\rho$ bound above is usually the cleaner formulation.

## Shared Candidate 3: entrant composition, takeover composition, and hazard orders

**Verdict: entrant selection is unconditional on R; takeover selection needs an additional comparison. Solver F's hazard-order conjecture is false.**

For entrant share $S_H^A=e_H/(e_H+e_L)$, differentiation gives

$$
\frac{dS_H^A}{d\lambda}
=\frac{(1-\rho)[\rho+(1-\rho)\beta]}{(e_H+e_L)^2}>0.
$$

Within-class preparation does not select on $\theta$. Therefore

$$
G_{\theta\mid A}=S_H^A G_H+(1-S_H^A)G_L,
\qquad
G_{\theta\mid A,1}-G_{\theta\mid A,0}
=-(S_{H,1}^A-S_{H,0}^A)D\le0.
$$

The entrant value distribution improves in FOSD, with a strictly higher mean for a strict spread increase because the class mean gap is positive. Also $O_L=e_LW_L/2$ weakly falls along FOSD; it falls strictly when the spread increases and the stronger law has $W_L>0$. These are useful additions beyond the original ownership claim.

For $S_H^W=O_H/(O_H+O_L)$, when both class ownership probabilities are positive,

$$
\frac{S_H^W}{1-S_H^W}=\frac{e_H}{e_L}\frac{W_H}{W_L}.
$$

The exact strict finite condition is

$$
\frac{(e_H/e_L)_1}{(e_H/e_L)_0}
>\frac{(W_L/W_H)_1}{(W_L/W_H)_0}.
$$

A nonincreasing $W_L/W_H$ is sufficient, not necessary. If $W_L=0$, the H acquisition share can already be one, so strict growth requires a positive low-class winning probability at the relevant stronger endpoint, or separate treatment of saturation. This distinction is absent from the candidates' unqualified strict composition wording.

The equality construction in Candidate 2 also supplies a strict counterexample to Solver F's claimed necessity: $W_L/W_H$ rises from $0.99$ to $1$, while the H share of challenger acquisitions rises from $0.578476629957$ to $0.579877941276$. Both points are strict R equilibria. Increased entry odds more than offset the worse relative win odds.

### Why the Solver B sufficient orders work

Suppose $G_H$ dominates $G_L$ in likelihood-ratio order, and $\varphi(y)=F_1(y)/F_0(y)$ is nondecreasing where $F_0>0$. FOSD ensures $F_1=0$ where $F_0=0$. Define

$$
d\nu_j(y)=\frac{F_0(y)\,dG_j(y)}{W_j(F_0)}.
$$

Multiplication by the common nonnegative weight $F_0$ preserves likelihood-ratio ordering. For an increasing $\varphi$, this ordering gives $E_{\nu_H}\varphi\ge E_{\nu_L}\varphi$: pairing any $x<y$, the difference has integrand proportional to

$$
[\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)]
[d\nu_H(y)d\nu_L(x)-d\nu_H(x)d\nu_L(y)]\ge0.
$$

Thus

$$
\frac{W_H(F_1)}{W_H(F_0)}
\ge\frac{W_L(F_1)}{W_L(F_0)}.
$$

This proves the required relative-win comparison. For uniform scales $r_1>r_0$, the ratio $F_{r_1}/F_{r_0}$ equals $r_0/r_1$ below $r_0$, $y/r_1$ between the two endpoints, and one above $r_1$; it is nondecreasing. Ordered point classes and uniform classes with ordered lower and upper endpoints satisfy the required class order. These are sufficient conditions, not equivalences.

### The hazard-order conjecture fails

The takeover counterexample is correct and particularly economical. Take $G_L=\delta_1,G_H=\delta_3$, and

$$
F_0=U[0,2],\qquad
F_1=\tfrac12U[0,1]+\tfrac12U[3,4].
$$

The class laws are ordered even in likelihood-ratio and hazard-rate order. The incumbent CDFs coincide on $[0,1]$, $F_1=1/2\le F_0$ on $[1,3]$, and $F_1\le1=F_0$ thereafter. Both incumbent laws are continuous, and $F_1$ is stronger. Nevertheless

$$
(W_{L,0},W_{H,0})=(1/2,1),\qquad
(W_{L,1},W_{H,1})=(1/2,1/2),
$$

so $W_L/W_H$ rises from $1/2$ to one. The failed tilting step in Solver F's notes cannot be supplied under arbitrary FOSD.

Solver F's own different class example also computes correctly: its win ratios are $0.5$ and $0.980392156863$. Its classes are **not** hazard-rate ordered: the survival ratio rises at $2.5$. That example refutes a FOSD-class lemma, while the point-class example refutes the stronger hazard conjecture.

There is also a fully specified equilibrium failure, not merely an auction-ratio failure. Solver A takes $h=10,\ell=1,p=1/2,\rho=0.05,k=0.2,\beta=0.2,a=5,\bar\lambda=0.9,c_H=2.7$, and

$$
F_z=\tfrac12U[0,1]+\tfrac12U[z-0.1,z+0.1],\qquad z:9.8\to10.2.
$$

Both are strict R points. The purchase margins are $0.096170246$ and $0.086243788$, while the sale margins are $0.09$ and $0.0875$. Research rises $0.137359005\to0.139940295$, but $W_H:1\to1/2$ with $W_L=1/2$. Therefore

$$
S_H^W:0.763038931\to0.618748984,
\qquad O_H:0.172196422\to0.086588656.
$$

H's entrant share rises $0.616865761\to0.618748984$. Since $F_z(p)=1/4$, the sale-completion probability is $1-\tfrac14(1-\mathsf E)$, which rises. Consequently H's share of **all** completed sales falls too. Neither interpretation of conditional ownership removes the difficulty under arbitrary FOSD.

## Do the same workarounds cover Proposition 2 and Propositions A.4, A.5 and A.7?

**The factorization, exact win/entry trade-off, and DA corollary do. The R formula and coexistence with Z do not carry over wholesale.**

### Proposition 2

[Section 4:35](overleaf/slot/sections/04_equilibrium_results.tex:35) already states the correct necessary and sufficient inequality. Here $e_{H,0}=\rho$ and $e_{H,1}=\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\lambda)$; factorization proves it immediately. The stated sufficient condition $(1+\rho)W_{H,1}\ge2\rho W_{H,0}>0$, together with $b+\lambda>1/2$, is correct because $e_{H,1}>(1+\rho)/2$.

DA at the recruiting law $F_1$ forces $W_{H,1}=W_{H,0}=1$, so the ownership comparison is automatic. Normalizing each economy by its same-incumbent ready-buyer floor gives $1$ at $F_0$ and $e_{H,1}/\rho>1$ at $F_1$.

But the floor at $F_1$ is **not a coexisting no-trade equilibrium in Proposition 2's game**. Hypothesis H2 gives $k<\rho\kappa\Delta_1$, which makes correct orders strictly profitable even at entry $\rho$; the proof forces full orders in every candidate. A same-incumbent hidden-price or exogenously suppressed-information economy can implement the floor, but this is a different information experiment. Do not import the R/Z coexistence theorem into this result.

### Proposition A.4

[A6:84](overleaf/slot/appendix/A6_robustness.tex:84) already states the identical ownership trade-off. Under its support windows the whole low component enters and the high component enters only after a purchase, so the same $e_{H,1}$ formula applies. DA with $c_-$ in place of $c_H$ is sufficient: recruitment of the high component implies $g_H>c_->0$. For this particular full-component result, an implication using $c_+$ would also suffice, since $B_1(M)>c_+$.

The ready-component floor is well defined under the stated support inequalities, and a hidden-price control leaves exactly that component entering. The R elasticity equation and research-zero existence result are not statements about A.4, which has exogenous investor presence and a different cost law. No change to the paper's baseline two-point cost is needed.

### Proposition A.5

[A6:180](overleaf/slot/appendix/A6_robustness.tex:180) explicitly **constructs** the ownership inequality. At the zero-spread endpoint $v_*$, $W_H>0$ and $e_H>\rho$. Continuity permits a nearby $r_1$ with $e_HW_H(r_1)>\rho W_H(v_*)$. Choosing $r_0\in(v_*,r_1)$ gives $W_H(r_0)\le W_H(v_*)$, so the desired comparison follows. The other strict inequalities yield an open set.

DA would be an additional sufficient restriction, but imposing it would discard part of A.5's verified generality. The manuscript's wide-class example has $G_H=U[1,19]$, $G_L=U[0.6,1.4]$, prior $0.7$, benchmark slot and $\rho$, and strengths $1\to4.5$. Direct evaluation gives

$$
W_{H,1}=0.924382716049,
\quad O_{H,0}=0.175,
\quad O_{H,1}=0.419819341112.
$$

Keep A.5's existing construction and trade-off. There is no ownership error to fix by narrowing it to DA.

### Proposition A.7

The stated proposition assumes $r_1<h$, and [the subsequent ownership line](overleaf/slot/appendix/A6_robustness.tex:481) correctly uses $W_H=1$. With general ranked classes, extend the conditional independence to $(T,Y,\theta,R)$ given class; then entry remains independent of the values given class and

$$
O_{H,1}=\tfrac12 e_{H,1}W_H(F_1),\qquad
O_{H,0}=\tfrac12\rho W_H(F_0).
$$

In the additional one-pair-recruitment case,

$$
e_{H,1}=\rho+(1-\rho)d(b+a_T\lambda)>\rho.
$$

Without that extra case restriction, average the signal-specific entry probabilities over the order and private-signal law; the positive-probability event $(X,Y)=(1,+)$ still gives $e_{H,1}>\rho$. The exact level condition and DA implication then apply. The same-incumbent normalization by $\rho W_H/2$ is valid. With the price hidden, $B_1(d)\le B_0(d)<c_H$ leaves only zero-cost entry, so the floor has an appropriate control interpretation. The research-branch function $\Lambda(\Delta)$, its elasticity bound, and R/Z coexistence are not A.7 conclusions.

Thus the candidates correctly identify a common **ownership decomposition**. They should not call all four propositions instances of the same research equilibrium comparison. The current benchmark Proposition 4(a) is also correct: [its statement](overleaf/slot/sections/05_research.tex:52) restricts the auction to the benchmark, and [its proof](overleaf/slot/appendix/A5_research.tex:266) explicitly invokes $h>r$. The unqualified prose immediately before the proposition, and any planned generalization, need the qualification.

## Ranking and recommendation

1. **Best unrestricted ownership workaround: the common factorization plus the R/Z ownership multiplier.** It retains an actual same-incumbent acquisition comparison, uses an existing equilibrium, and needs no new restriction on auction overlap. Pair it with the fixed-value ownership/capture-rate statement from the two Solver B files. Solver A's C1 is the cleanest starting point, with the uniqueness and fixed-information distinctions made above.
2. **Best way to retain the original level claim: no displacement on the branch, with decisive advantage as a primitive sufficient condition.** State the plain support condition in the theorem or corollary. Use DA and the verified $1.0125$ cost margin/$7.968626967$ strength threshold to show the benchmark satisfies it across all its recruiting uniform economies. Keep the exact finite inequality as the general statement.
3. **Best additional economic implication: selection into entry.** H's entry rises, L's falls, and the distribution of prepared challengers improves. This is unconditional within R and distinct from final ownership. Conditional takeover composition is secondary because it requires a relative-win condition; the likelihood-ratio/reverse-hazard conditions are valid optional sufficient primitives.
4. **Useful optional lemma: the all-$\rho$ elasticity ceiling.** The short proof above strengthens Solver B's contribution and sharply explains the local uniform point-mass failure. It is unnecessary machinery for the main repair, and its local necessity result should not be substituted for Proposition 2's finite trade-off.

Suggested statement at the model's actual generality:

> On the interior research branch, research, total entry and class-H entry rise strictly with the target-payoff spread. Holding the incumbent's realized value fixed, a class-H acquisition becomes more likely wherever the high class can win. At every branch economy a zero-research equilibrium coexists, and the ratio of high-class ownership on the research branch to ownership in that equilibrium rises strictly with the spread. Actual high-class ownership across incumbent laws rises when the entry gain outweighs the loss in win probability; in particular it rises if class H outbids every incumbent on the branch.

Add that FOSD gives weak growth, with strict growth when the spread increases. If the statement includes a binding research cap, replace strict growth by weak growth on the capped portion. Keep the within-equilibrium branch qualification and make no selection claim.

## What remains weak after the best workaround

The original claim about the **unconditional number or probability of high-class acquisitions across stronger incumbents** remains false without a restriction. A normalized ownership ratio cannot be presented as that level prediction. Fixed-realization statements may involve latent values and counterfactual conditioning; they do not automatically provide an observable empirical ownership trend.

The equilibrium comparison also leaves selection unresolved: Z and R coexist. It does not establish that realized research or ownership rises as the incumbent strengthens, that the R set is an interval, or that a cap is reached before another order pattern starts. Those are separate questions.

DA is sufficient and especially transparent for the uniform point-mass benchmark, but is not necessary, does not hold merely from the benchmark class values and costs under arbitrary incumbent laws, and should not narrow A.5's existing existence result. The hazard-order conjecture, the acquisition-share “iff,” and the takeover report's strict consequence of a weak finite bound must be dropped or corrected. None of these repairs establishes a welfare ranking.
