A short primitive theorem then recovers the entire strength interval for uniform incumbents, including dispersed, separated classes. Both proposed replacements for the benchmark's increasing-\(J\) Sturm certificate are valid. The two disconnected examples are real; the Solver B example needs numerical corrections.

## Shared Candidate 1: reduced-coordinate coexistence region

**Verdict: proved.** This treats Solver A 1, Solver C 1 and the common content of Solver B 1 once.

Keep the classes, reserve, prior, two-point preparation cost and slot fixed. Assume the standing class ranking, finite class means, \(\Delta>0\), \(\gamma>0\), \(0<\rho<1\), \(k>0\), an interior prior and slot, and rules IM/C1. Define

\[
d=\mathbb E_H\theta-\mathbb E_L\theta=\Delta+\gamma,\qquad
q_\pi=\max\{\pi,1-\pi\},\qquad
b=(1-\lambda)\beta,\qquad M=M(\pi).
\]

The equality \(d=\Delta+\gamma\) follows by adding the two class-gap integrals in Appendix A1. In particular, \(d\) stays fixed when only the incumbent law changes.

The statement to retain is

\[
\boxed{
N\text{ and an informative equilibrium coexist}
\iff
\pi<\tau\le M,\qquad
\frac{k}{1-\tau}\le\Delta\le\frac{k}{\rho q_\pi}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

The inequalities must be intersected with the auction's attainable coordinates, including \(0<\Delta<d\).

**Proof, including boundaries and uniqueness.**

1. The trading payoffs in A2 imply that a high-class sale and a low-class purchase have strictly negative returns. Thus only a high-class purchase, a low-class sale, and abstention need consideration.
2. If \(\pi\ge\tau\), C1 makes entry equal to one under no trade. No trade requires \(\Delta q_\pi\le k\). Any positive high-class purchase raises its posterior above \(\pi\), giving return strictly below \(\Delta(1-\pi)\le k\). Any positive low-class sale lowers its posterior below \(\pi\), giving return at most \(\Delta\mu_-<\Delta\pi\le k\). Neither class can trade in another equilibrium. Hence coexistence requires \(\pi<\tau\).
3. If \(\pi<\tau\), no trade gives entry \(\rho\) after every order. Its two potentially profitable deviations earn \(\rho\Delta(1-\pi)\) and \(\rho\Delta\pi\). Hence no trade is an equilibrium exactly when \(\rho\Delta q_\pi\le k\). Under this inequality, a positive low-class sale strictly lowers its return below its zero-rate return, so its unique equilibrium sale rate is zero.
4. Let \(s=\lambda\sigma_H(1)>0\). With the low class abstaining,

   \[
   \mu_+(s)=\frac{\pi(b+s)}{b+\pi s},\qquad
   1-\mu_+(s)=\frac{(1-\pi)b}{b+\pi s}.
   \]

   A purchase without recruitment earns strictly less than \(\rho\Delta(1-\pi)\le k\), so it cannot be used. Recruitment first occurs at

   \[
   s^*=\frac{b(\tau-\pi)}{\pi(1-\tau)}.
   \]

   A feasible recruiting rate exists exactly when \(\tau\le M\). C1 permits recruitment at equality. Among recruiting rates, the gross purchase return decreases strictly from \(J=\Delta(1-\tau)\) at \(s^*\) to \(\Delta(1-M)\) at \(\lambda\).
5. If the latter return is at least \(k\), the unique informative rate is \(\lambda\). Otherwise a recruiting equilibrium exists exactly when \(J\ge k\), and there is a unique rate in \([s^*,\lambda)\) where the return equals \(k\). At \(J=k\) this is the threshold pair with entry one; at \(\tau=M\) it is a full purchase. The low class abstains and the zero-order posterior is below \(\pi\), so entry after a sale and after zero is \(\rho\). The price construction in A4's product lemma completes the equilibrium tuple.

Thus

\[
s_I=\min\left\{\lambda,\frac b\pi
\left[\frac{(1-\pi)\Delta}{k}-1\right]\right\},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf E_I=\rho+(1-\rho)
\min\left\{\frac{(1-\pi)b\Delta}{k},\,b+\pi\lambda\right\}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

This is nondecreasing in \(\Delta\), and strictly increasing before full purchases. Consequently, any two coexisting strengths can be compared even if coexistence fails between them. This is a comparison of their informative equilibria, not a prediction about equilibrium selection.

**Convexity and the prior ceiling.** The unrestricted region in (1) is convex because

\[
\tau\le1-\frac{k}{\Delta},
\qquad
\frac{d^2}{d\Delta^2}\left(1-\frac{k}{\Delta}\right)
=-\frac{2k}{\Delta^3}<0,
\]

defines a hypograph of a concave function, and the remaining restrictions are half-spaces. Intersecting with \(0<\Delta<d\) preserves convexity. This does not imply that an arbitrary attainable path or its intersection with the region is connected.

The two spread bounds give \(1-\tau\ge\rho q_\pi\). Therefore

\[
\tau\le1-\rho q_\pi,\qquad
\pi<1-\rho q_\pi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\pi<\frac1{1+\rho}.
\]

For \(\pi\ge1/2\), the last implication is direct; for \(\pi<1/2\), the last bound follows from \(\rho<1\). Solver A correctly describes this as necessary, not sufficient.

### Solver B 1: the full-purchase interval and the location of gaps

**Verdict: the substantive claim is proved.** Its scope is full-purchase equilibria **coexisting with no trade**, with the slot and prior fixed.

Let

\[
\Delta_F=\frac{k}{1-M},\qquad
\Delta_N=\frac{k}{\rho q_\pi},\qquad
S=\{s:B_s(\pi)<c_H,\ B_s(M)\ge c_H,\ \Delta(s)\le\Delta_N\}.
\]

FOSD strengthening lowers \(B_s(\mu)\) at every fixed posterior and raises \(\Delta(s)\). The first restriction defining \(S\) is an up-set; the other two are down-sets. Hence \(S\) is an interval, with no need to assume that \(\tau\) is globally nonnegative.

On \(\Delta\ge\Delta_F\) and \(\tau\le M\),

\[
J=\Delta(1-\tau)\ge\Delta(1-M)\ge k.
\]

It follows that

\[
\boxed{C_F=S\cap\{\Delta\ge\Delta_F\}}
\tag{3}
\]

is an interval, possibly empty or a singleton. Entry there is the constant \(\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\pi\lambda)\). Every mixed-purchase coexistence strength has \(\Delta<\Delta_F\), so it precedes every full-purchase coexistence strength.

If \(s_1<s<s_2\), with \(s_1,s_2\in C\) but \(s\notin C\), then \(s\in S\), since \(S\) is an interval. Formula (3) forces \(\Delta(s)<\Delta_F\). This proves the claim about where gaps can occur.

**Corrections to the wording.**

- A hole does not “lie in \(C_R\)” literally: at a hole there is no informative equilibrium. Say that every internal gap lies in the **spread range below the full-purchase cutoff**.
- Global “\(\Delta\) and \(\tau\) both rise under FOSD” needs the positive-threshold qualification. For example, retain \(h=10,\ell=1,p=1/2\), set \(c_H=0.1\), and strengthen

  \[
  0.9U[0.59,0.61]+0.1U[1.56,1.58]
  \quad\text{to}\quad
  0.9U[0.59,0.61]+0.1U[1.79,1.81].
  \]

  The spread rises from \(0.057\) to \(0.080\), but \(\tau\) falls from \(-0.029073018\) to \(-0.029147982\). On and above a coexistence point, \(\tau>\pi>0\), so this qualification does not weaken (3).
- Continuity alone does not give unique or attained cutoff roots. Use the set definitions above, or explicitly assume the needed crossings. A left endpoint created by \(\tau=\pi\) is excluded under C1.

## Solver A 2 and Solver C 3: uniform incumbents and preparation affordability

**Verdict: proved.** Solver A's dispersed-class version contains Solver C's point-mass result.

Assume

\[
F_r=U[0,r],\quad r\in[r_0,r_2],\quad p<r_0<r_2,
\]

\[
\operatorname{supp}(G_L)\subseteq[p,r_0],\qquad
\operatorname{supp}(G_H)\subseteq[r_2,\infty),\qquad
v_H-r_2\ge c_H,
\tag{4}
\]

where \(v_H=\mathbb E_H\theta\), \(v_L=\mathbb E_L\theta\) and \(m_{2,L}=\mathbb E_L\theta^2\). Retain positive \(\Delta,\gamma\).

A high-class value always wins, paying \(\max\{p,R\}\), whose expectation is \(r/2+p^2/(2r)\). For each low-class value \(\theta\le r\), gross profit is \((\theta^2-p^2)/(2r)\). Averaging and using the class-gap identity gives

\[
g_H=v_H-\frac r2-\frac{p^2}{2r},\quad
g_L=\frac{m_{2,L}-p^2}{2r},\quad
\Delta=\frac r2-v_L+\frac{m_{2,L}}{2r}.
\tag{5}
\]

Set \(X=g_H-c_H\), \(d=v_H-v_L\), and

\[
A=-g_H'=\frac{r^2-p^2}{2r^2}>0.
\]

Since \(v_H-c_H\ge r_2\ge r\),

\[
X\ge rA.
\tag{6}
\]

Because every low-class value lies in \([p,r]\),

\[
r\Delta'-\Delta
=v_L-\frac{m_{2,L}}r
=\mathbb E_L\!\left[\theta\left(1-\frac{\theta}{r}\right)\right]\ge0.
\tag{7}
\]

The fixed-gap identity gives \(\gamma'=-\Delta'\). Differentiation therefore yields

\[
J'=\frac{dX\Delta'-\Delta\gamma A}{\gamma^2}
\ge\frac{A[d\,r\Delta'-\Delta\gamma]}{\gamma^2}
\ge\frac{A\Delta(d-\gamma)}{\gamma^2}
=\boxed{\frac{A\Delta^2}{\gamma^2}>0}.
\tag{8}
\]

Every inequality has the required sign: \(\Delta'\ge0\), \(A>0\), \(\Delta>0\), \(\gamma>0\). This proves the result, including equality in the cost-cover restriction. It uses no polynomial root count.

For point masses, (4) becomes \(r_2+c_H\le h\), together with \(p<\ell\le r_0<r_2\le h\); Solver C's stricter support inequalities are sufficient. The benchmark margin is

\[
10-3.6-6=0.4.
\]

For dispersed classes \(G_L=U[0.75,1.1]\), \(G_H=U[9,11]\), the same margin is \(0.4\). Independent integration gives

| Classes | \(J(1.2)\) | \(J(3.6)\) | \(J'(1.2)\) | \(J'(3.6)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Benchmark point masses | 0.006114718615 | 0.252192932077 | 0.055388984598 | 0.081264223486 |
| Stated dispersed classes | 0.013040019719 | 0.266716664200 | 0.071343516144 | 0.080043450802 |

These values agree with Solver A and Solver C. The sign on the entire interval follows from (8), not the endpoint samples.

**Recovering the endpoint formula.** Increasing \(J\) makes \(\{J\ge k\}\) an up-set. Intersecting with \(S\) gives an interval at every fixed prior. At prior \(1/2\), Solver A correctly also requires \(\tau(r_0)>1/2\), attained \(r_A,r_N\), \(r_A<r_N\), and \(\tau(r_N)\le M\) to obtain exactly \([r_A,r_N]\). These endpoint assumptions must remain.

**How far from necessary.** Solver A's weaker point-mass sufficient bound is valid:

\[
c_H\le h-r_2+\frac{\ell}{2}-\frac{p^2}{2r_2}.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed,

\[
\frac{\Delta\gamma A}{d\Delta'}
<\Delta\frac{A}{\Delta'}
=\frac{(r-\ell)(r^2-p^2)}{2r(r+\ell)}
<\frac{r-\ell}{2}.
\]

Thus \(X\ge(r-\ell)/2\) suffices for \(J'>0\), and the resulting cost bound decreases with \(r>\ell>p\). The benchmark endpoint bound in (9) is \(6.865277778\). It covers \(c_H=6.6\), which violates the simpler \(c_H\le6.4\) condition. Solver C's exact *local* bound at \(r_2\) is also correct:

\[
\bar c(3.6)=7.271599980617.
\]

This is a local derivative bound, not by itself a verified uniform bound over the whole path.

**Correct scope.** Certain victory of class H and affordability against the largest incumbent are insufficient for arbitrary FOSD families. Solver A's disconnected example below satisfies both. The uniform-family restriction in (4) does real work.

## Solver B 2: the positive product \(\Delta(g_H-c_H)\) does not fall

**Verdict: the main sufficient condition is proved.**

Suppose \(X=g_H-c_H>0\) initially and \(\Delta X\) is nondecreasing. It remains positive, so \(X>0\) throughout. FOSD makes \(\gamma=d-\Delta>0\) nonincreasing. For any \(s<t\),

\[
J(t)=\frac{\Delta(t)X(t)}{\gamma(t)}
\ge\frac{\Delta(s)X(s)}{\gamma(t)}
\ge\frac{\Delta(s)X(s)}{\gamma(s)}=J(s).
\tag{10}
\]

This proves the monotonicity and hence interval coexistence. Strict product growth, or strict decline of \(\gamma\), gives strict growth of \(J\). Existence of a root \(J=k\), and whether the interval ends at \(s_N\) or \(s_C\), still require the relevant endpoint conditions. If checking only \(S\), positivity of \(X=\gamma(1-\tau)\) follows automatically from \(\tau\le M<1\).

For a differentiable path, the division-free condition is

\[
(\Delta X)'=X\Delta'+\Delta g_H'\ge0.
\]

Where \(\Delta'>0\), this is \(X\ge\eta\Delta\), with \(\eta=-g_H'/\Delta'\). The undivided version handles flat spreads correctly.

**Analytic benchmark verification.** At the benchmark,

\[
X(r)=4-\frac r2-\frac1{8r},\qquad
Y(r):=\eta(r)\Delta(r)
=\frac{(r-1)(r^2-1/4)}{2r(r+1)}.
\]

The first decreases for \(r>1/2\). The second is positive and increases for \(r>1\), since

\[
\frac{Y'}Y
=\left(\frac1{r-1}-\frac1r\right)
+\left(\frac{2r}{r^2-1/4}-\frac1{r+1}\right)>0.
\]

Consequently \(X-Y\) is minimized at \(r_2=3.6\), where

\[
X-Y=\frac{9667}{8280}=1.167512077295>0.
\tag{11}
\]

This rigorously replaces the Sturm count for A4-path(b). The reported elasticity ratios also reproduce: \(X/Y\) falls from \(73.117647059\) to \(2.170126490\), and the exact \(J\)-growth comparison at \(r_2\) is \(2.422884159\). The maximum spread share is \(0.104320988\); dropping the factor \(1-\Delta/d\) strengthens the required ratio by at most \(11.6472\%\), consistent with “at most 12%.”

### The additional squared-product condition replaces the second certificate too

Solver B's separate claim in “Other findings” is correct at the benchmark. The stronger inequality \(X\ge2\eta\Delta\) gives

\[
(\Delta X^2)'=\Delta'X(X-2\eta\Delta)\ge0.
\]

Since \(X\) decreases and \(Y=\eta\Delta\) increases,

\[
\min_{[1.2,3.6]}(X-2Y)
=\frac{937}{5520}
=0.169746376812>0.
\tag{12}
\]

Thus \(\Delta X^2\) increases strictly, and division by the positive decreasing \(\gamma^2\) proves

\[
[\Delta(1-\tau)^2]'>0
\quad\text{on the entire }[1.2,3.6].
\]

This is stronger than A4-path(c)'s stated interval \([1.2,2]\). The alternative comparison in Solver B also checks out:

\[
d(1-\tau_2)=2.417470710,\quad
\Delta_2(1+\tau_2)=1.625584846,\quad
\max 2\Delta(\eta-1)=0.128679656.
\]

Hence its uniform upper bound \(1.754264502\) is below \(2.417470710\). The endpoint derivative-sign margin is \(0.674132241\).

On the CM threshold branch,

\[
\mathsf E_T
=\rho+(1-\pi)b
\frac{k-\rho J}{\Delta(1-\tau)^2}.
\]

Increasing \(J\) and this denominator make entry fall where the branch exists. At the benchmark the changes are strict. With only weak monotonicities in a general theorem, claim weak decline unless an additional strict change is supplied. Neither the simple product condition nor \(r+c_H\le h\), by itself, proves the second certificate; the verified squared-product inequality is the extra argument.

### False necessity in the \(\eta=1\) special case

Solver B 2 says \(J\) rises throughout the recruitment-feasible path **if and only if**

\[
c_H-g_L\ge M^2d.
\tag{13}
\]

The sufficient direction is correct when \(a:=c_H-g_L>0\) is constant. The necessary direction needs the path's feasible thresholds to approach \(M\).

To see the exact statement, use \(g_L\) constant and \(\gamma=d-\Delta\):

\[
J=\Delta\left(1-\frac a\gamma\right),\qquad
\frac{dJ}{d\Delta}=1-\frac{ad}{\gamma^2}.
\]

Thus \(J\) is nondecreasing precisely where

\[
\tau^2\le\frac a d.
\]

For a truncated path the relevant bound is \(a\ge d(\sup\tau)^2\) over its feasible thresholds, not necessarily \(a\ge dM^2\).

**Explicit counterexample to necessity.** Take \(h=10,\ell=1,p=1/2,c_H=2\), \(R_z=U[z-0.1,z+0.1]\), \(z\in[5,5.5]\), \(\pi=0.3,\rho=1/4,k=2\), and a slot with \(\beta=0.2,\lambda=8/17\), giving \(M=0.7\). Then

\[
\tau:0.4\longrightarrow0.444444444,\qquad
J:2.4\longrightarrow2.5,\qquad
J':0.28\longrightarrow0.111111111>0.
\]

No-trade returns range from \(0.7\) to \(0.7875<2\), so coexistence holds throughout. Yet

\[
c_H-g_L=2<9(0.7)^2=4.41.
\]

This disproves the unqualified “if and only if,” while leaving the main product condition intact.

## Solver A 3: small trading costs and a finite bound on profit erosion

**Verdict: proved, including the non-differentiable formulation.**

On \(I_N=\{s:\Delta(s)\le k/(\rho q_\pi)\}\), suppose for all \(s<t\)

\[
g_H(s)-g_H(t)\le L[\Delta(t)-\Delta(s)].
\tag{14}
\]

The stated primitive integral inequality is exactly (14): integrate the CDF difference against \(1-G_H\) for the left side and against \(D=G_L-G_H\) for the spread change. The alternative expression with \(g_L\) follows from \(g_H=g_L+d-\Delta\).

Define

\[
H_k(s)=g_H(s)-c_H+k-\frac{kd}{\Delta(s)}.
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
J-k=\frac{\Delta}{\gamma}H_k.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(s<t\) in \(I_N\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_k(t)-H_k(s)
&=g_H(t)-g_H(s)
+kd\frac{\Delta(t)-\Delta(s)}{\Delta(s)\Delta(t)}\\
&\ge
\left[\frac{kd}{\Delta(s)\Delta(t)}-L\right]
[\Delta(t)-\Delta(s)]\\
&\ge
\left[\frac{d\rho^2q_\pi^2}{k}-L\right]
[\Delta(t)-\Delta(s)].
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore \(kL\le d\rho^2q_\pi^2\) makes \(H_k\) nondecreasing. Its nonnegative set is an up-set within the no-trade spread range; intersecting with the prior and recruitment restrictions gives an interval. Since \(q_\pi\ge1/2\), \(kL\le d\rho^2/4\), with (14) on \(\{\Delta\le2k/\rho\}\), works for every prior. Strictness and root-attainment qualifications in Solver A are sufficient.

All reported benchmark checks reproduce:

\[
L=\frac{119}{44}=2.704545455,\qquad
\frac{d\rho^2}{4L}=\frac{99}{1904}=0.051995798319,
\]

\[
\frac{d\rho^2}{4}-kL=0.086534090909,\qquad
\frac{0.051995798319}{0.02}=2.599789916.
\]

The slot-specific prior-start margins are \(0.208304780194\) and \(0.199076155364\); the simpler margin \(1-\rho-\tau_A\) is \(0.109260924292\). Extending the uniform path down to \(r_0=1.01\) gives \(L=38.313432836\) and a conservative cost ceiling \(0.003670383716\), although (4) still proves increasing \(J\). This correctly demonstrates non-necessity.

## Solver C 2 and Solver B 3: crossing conditions

### Solver C's undivided boundary condition

**Verdict: proved as a sufficient condition.**

For a \(C^1\) path,

\[
J'=\frac{d\Delta'X+\Delta\gamma g_H'}{\gamma^2}.
\]

At \(J=k\), substitute \(X=k\gamma/\Delta\):

\[
\boxed{
J'=\frac{\mathcal B_k}{\Delta\gamma},
\qquad
\mathcal B_k=dk\Delta'+\Delta^2g_H'.
}
\tag{16}
\]

If \(J(s)\ge k>J(t)\) for \(s<t\), continuity gives a last \(u\in[s,t]\) with \(J(u)=k\); its right-hand values are below \(k\), so \(J'(u)\le0\). This contradicts \(\mathcal B_k(u)>0\). The equality set is closed in a compact interval, so the last point exists; no informal crossing-count argument is needed.

For coexistence, it is enough to impose this condition at equalities in the relevant strip with \(\Delta<\Delta_F\). Beyond that cutoff \(J\ge k\) is automatic in \(S\).

At the benchmark crossing, independently calculated values are

\[
\Delta_A=0.055669845084,\quad
\Delta'_A=0.242681698116,\quad
g'_{H,A}=-0.435670424529,
\]

\[
dk\Delta'_A=0.043682705661,\quad
-\Delta_A^2g'_{H,A}=0.001350200002,\quad
\mathcal B_k(r_A)=0.042332505658>0.
\]

Solver C's shifted-uniform illustration also works. With \(c_H=2,k=1,z\in[1.1,7.5]\), \(x=z-1\), the roots are \(x=4\pm\sqrt7\). Only \(x=1.354248689\) is attained; the upper root \(6.645751311\) exceeds the path's upper \(x=6.5\). At the lower root,

\[
\mathcal B_1=8\sqrt7-14=7.166010489>0,
\]

although \(J\) later declines.

**Logical qualification.** The strict boundary condition is not literally weaker than monotonicity: a monotone function can cross with zero derivative. For an economic example, reparameterize the benchmark uniform path as

\[
r(s)=r_A+0.1s^3,\qquad -1\le s\le1.
\]

Then \(J(s)\) is strictly increasing, from \(0.012126406\) to \(0.029020029\), but \(\mathcal B_k(0)=0\). Thus strict boundary transversality and unrestricted monotonicity are not ordered assumptions. The **up-set property itself** is the weaker condition that the interval proof needs.

### Solver B's quotient formulation and small-cost versions

**Verdict: needs a regularity qualification; the underlying idea is valid.**

At a crossing with \(\Delta'>0\),

\[
\gamma J'
=\frac{\Delta'}{1-\tau}
\left[d(1-\tau)^2-k\eta\right],
\qquad \eta=-g_H'/\Delta'.
\tag{17}
\]

The sign statement and strict-crossing argument are then valid. If \(\Delta'=g_H'=0\), however, \(\eta\) is \(0/0\), and Solver B provides no definition. Merely assuming \(C^1\) does not remove this case; the preceding cubic reparameterization exhibits it. If \(\Delta'=0< -g_H'\), the undivided formula directly gives \(J'<0\). Solver B also incorrectly writes

**A rigorous version preserving the weak small-cost bound.** Let

\[
a_\pi=\max\{1-M,\rho q_\pi\}.
\]

Replace a possibly undefined ratio bound by

\[
-g_H'\le L\Delta'
\tag{19}
\]

on the relevant mixed-purchase spread band. This handles stationary points and excludes profit erosion at a flat spread. On the part also satisfying the no-trade restriction,

\[
\Delta\le\frac{k}{a_\pi},
\qquad
H_k'=g_H'+\frac{kd\Delta'}{\Delta^2}
\ge\left[\frac{da_\pi^2}{k}-L\right]\Delta'\ge0
\]

whenever \(kL\le da_\pi^2\). A crossing with \(\pi<\tau\) also has \(\Delta>k/(1-\pi)\), so bounds are needed only above that lower spread level. ### The dispersion claim is false

Solver B 3 says spreading the classes raises \(\eta\). The pointwise inequality \(D\le1-G_H\) proves \(\eta\ge1\) when the denominator is positive; it does not order \(\eta\) across different class distributions.

**Counterexample computed from primitives.** Keep \(p=0.5\), \(G_H=\delta_{10}\), and use the location path

\[
R_s\sim0.9U[0.79+s,0.81+s]+0.1U[1.99+s,2.01+s]
\]

for small \(s\) around zero. First take \(G_L=\delta_1\); then replace it by the mean-preserving spread \(G_L=U[0.5,1.5]\). Both versions satisfy the class ranking and reserve condition.

At \(s=0\), \(-g_H'=1\) in both. With the point mass, \(\Delta'=0.1\), so \(\eta=10\). With the dispersed low class, its CDF averages \(0.3\) on the lower incumbent component and \(1\) on the upper:

\[
\Delta'=0.9(0.3)+0.1(1)=0.37,\qquad
\eta=\frac1{0.37}=\frac{100}{37}=2.702702703.
\]

Dispersion therefore **reduces** \(\eta\). Remove the comparative claim; retain only the within-economy bound and integral interpretation.

## Ranking and recommendation

1. **Lead with the common reduced-coordinate theorem, augmented by Solver B's full-purchase interval.** It is general, economically informative, exact at the tie boundaries, and needs no new auction restriction. Its entry comparison remains meaningful across disconnected coexistence components.
2. **Use Solver A 2 as the principal primitive interval corollary; present Solver C 3 as its simple point-mass specialization.** Uniform incumbent growth, separation of class supports and \(v_H-r_2\ge c_H\) have clear economic content. They cover the benchmark and genuine class dispersion, with a short proof and a \(0.4\) benchmark cost margin.
3. **Keep Solver B 2 as the general path-level sufficient condition.** It is a concise statement of the economic balance that matters. Its benchmark verification is elementary. If replacing both Sturm certificates, use the separately verified squared-product argument (12), not just the basic condition.
4. **Use Solver A 3 when the goal is a theorem for general incumbent families with quantitative restrictions.** The finite-difference form avoids derivative and stationary-point problems and proves the all-prior interval result with substantial benchmark slack. It is less immediately intuitive than the affordability condition.
5. **Keep Solver C's strict boundary condition, or a corrected Solver B 3, as a technical alternative.** It is useful when global monotonicity fails but crossing behavior can be proved. Checking a known numerical root alone cannot establish that no other crossing exists.

Recommended manuscript package: a general theorem comprising (1)–(3), the primitive corollary (4)–(8), and the analytic benchmark verification. Solver A's exact two-component construction is the cleanest counterexample to an unrestricted strength-interval claim. Solver B's full-support perturbation is a useful robustness supplement after correcting its numbers.

## What remains weak after the best workaround

- Arbitrary FOSD paths can still leave and re-enter the mixed-purchase range of coexistence. Neither convexity of the reduced region, certain high-class victory, nor preparation affordability alone prevents this.
- The full four-case prior classification requires additional level-crossing, attainment and start-feasibility conditions. Proving an interval does not establish its claimed endpoints, a singleton at the lowest prior, or the maximum-prior formula.
- The economic conclusion is a comparison along the informative branch wherever it exists. It does not determine selection between no trade and informative trade, nor the response of ownership or endogenous research intensity when the slot changes.
- The primitive uniform-family corollary is a genuine restriction. The general product and erosion conditions broaden the result but still constrain how incumbent improvements affect challenger profits relative to the target's proceeds spread.
- The scoped benchmark result survives this review. Its two Sturm-dependent signs can receive analytical proofs, but the remaining threshold ordering and boundary claims still need their own existing arguments.
