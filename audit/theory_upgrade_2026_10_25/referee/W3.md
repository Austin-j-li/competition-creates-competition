## Shared Candidate 1: the continuation proposed by all three solvers

**Verdict: proved as a pointwise, full-recruitment theorem under IM, with C1 or the full-preparation selection under CM.** The same idea is treated once here.

Fix the prior at $1/2$, $0<\rho<1$, $k,a>0$, $0<\bar\lambda<1$, $0<\beta<1/2$, and an auction with $\Delta,\gamma>0$, $\tau>1/2$. “Full recruitment” below means $e(1)=1$, not merely $e(1)>\rho$. Define

\[
m_\lambda=\frac{\beta(1-\lambda)}{2\beta+(1-2\beta)\lambda},\qquad
M_\lambda=1-m_\lambda,\qquad
m'_\lambda=-\frac{\beta}{[2\beta+(1-2\beta)\lambda]^2}<0.
\]

### 1. Positive research forces a strict full H purchase

Wrong-signed orders yield at most $-k$, while abstention yields zero. Positive research requires a strictly positive success value. If the H purchase is not strictly profitable, a strictly profitable L sale must supply that value. A strictly profitable sale forces L to sell with probability one. Its posterior is then $m_\lambda<1/2<\tau$, so its return is $\rho\Delta m_\lambda-k>0$. But the H purchase earns at least that much, because its entry is at least $\rho$ and its posterior is at most $M_\lambda$. This is a contradiction. Hence H buys strictly and with probability one.

This proof uses the nonzero-order posterior bounds only. The extension from $\beta\le1/3$ to $\beta<1/2$ is valid; an all-profile bound for the zero order is unnecessary.

### 2. Bayes' rule and the low class's indifference

Let $z=\sigma_L(-1)$, $b=(1-\lambda)\beta$, and $b_0=(1-\lambda)(1-2\beta)$. In order $(-1,0,1)$,

\[
f_H=(b,b_0,b+\lambda),\qquad
f_L=(b+\lambda z,b_0+\lambda(1-z),b).
\]

Thus E1 gives

\[
\mu_+=M_\lambda,\qquad
\mu_-=\frac{b}{2b+\lambda z},\qquad
\mu_0=\frac{b_0}{2b_0+\lambda(1-z)}.
\tag{1}
\]

Both nonpurchase posteriors are at most $1/2$, so both have entry $\rho$. In particular, **one must not keep the sale posterior at $1/2$ after L starts selling**. Its sale payoff is

\[
U_L(-1)=\rho\Delta\frac{b}{2b+\lambda z}-k,
\]

which strictly decreases in $z$. Its unique consistent IM choice is

\[
z(\Delta,\lambda)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\rho\Delta\le2k,\\
\displaystyle\frac{b}{\lambda}\left(\frac{\rho\Delta}{k}-2\right),
&\rho\Delta m_\lambda<k<\rho\Delta/2,\\
1,&\rho\Delta m_\lambda\ge k.
\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]

In the middle case, $\mu_-=k/(\rho\Delta)$ and the net sale payoff is exactly zero. At the full-sale boundary only $z=1$ is consistent: any smaller sale probability would make selling strictly profitable and require a full sale. At the first-selling boundary only $z=0$ is consistent at positive research: any positive sale probability would make selling strictly unprofitable.

At these consistent profiles,

\[
\max_qU_L(q)=(\rho\Delta m_\lambda-k)^+.
\tag{3}
\]

This identity is valid even during mixing, although the actual sale posterior there is generally **not** $m_\lambda$.

### 3. The research condition and the apparent disagreement

With full recruitment,

\[
U_H(1)=\Delta m_\lambda-k,
\qquad
V=\frac12\left[\Delta m_\lambda-k+(\rho\Delta m_\lambda-k)^+\right].
\]

A joint deviation to research $y$, with optimal orders at the candidate prices and entry, yields

\[
yV-\frac a2y^2.
\]

The prices and entry are held fixed in this deviation. One must not differentiate the equilibrium price schedule with respect to research. Strict concavity gives the unique best response

\[
\lambda=\min\{\bar\lambda,V/a\}.
\]

Consequently the correct interior equation is

\[
\boxed{2a\lambda=\Delta m_\lambda-k+(\rho\Delta m_\lambda-k)^+.}
\tag{4}
\]

**Solver A and Solver B are right. Solver D is also right:** its two equations are exactly the two pieces of (4). The tentative equation in the weakness brief with another factor $1/2$ on the right of $2a\lambda$ is wrong.

Let $G(\lambda;\Delta)$ be the right side of (4) minus $2a\lambda$. It is continuous and strictly decreasing. For $\Delta>2k$,

\[
G(0;\Delta)=\Delta/2-k+(\rho\Delta/2-k)^+>0,
\qquad G(1;\Delta)=-k-2a<0.
\]

There is exactly one root $L(\Delta)\in(0,1)$. At that root the H purchase is strictly profitable: if the positive part vanishes, its payoff is $2a\lambda>0$; otherwise $\rho\Delta m_\lambda>k$ implies $\Delta m_\lambda>k$. The same argument applies when the root is clipped at the cap. If $\Delta\le2k$, a full H purchase cannot have a strictly positive return, so there is no such positive-research equilibrium.

Define $c(\Delta)=\min\{L(\Delta),\bar\lambda\}$. The exact existence condition is

\[
\boxed{\Delta>2k,\qquad \tfrac12<\tau\le M_{c(\Delta)}.}
\tag{5}
\]

This condition is necessary as well as sufficient: positive research forces the H purchase; full preparation then forces (1)–(4), and recruitment requires (5).

### 4. Prices, price beliefs, preparation, and bids

Set $e(-1)=e(0)=\rho$, $e(1)=1$, and

\[
P(x)=t_0+e(x)[t_L-t_0+\Delta\mu(x)].
\tag{6}
\]

This is the conditional expected terminal payoff, so E4 holds. Second-price entry cannot lower proceeds, hence $t_L-t_0\ge0$. For $\mu(x)>\mu(y)$, threshold-consistent entry satisfies $e(x)\ge e(y)$, and

\[
P(x)-P(y)=e(x)\Delta[\mu(x)-\mu(y)]
+[e(x)-e(y)][t_L-t_0+\Delta\mu(y)]>0.
\]

If the sale and zero order have the same posterior, they also have the same entry and price. Pooling these orders therefore preserves that posterior. E2 holds in both the distinct-price and pooled-price cases. For example, in the benchmark at $r=1.911774629371$, $z=0.417745925753=\beta/(1-\beta)$, the two posteriors equal $0.367943952646$ and their common price is $0.497885379020$.

E3 follows from $B(\mu)-c_H=\gamma(\mu-\tau)$, including full preparation at equality under C1 or as a permitted CM selection. Equation (2), the strict H purchase, and negative wrong-signed payoffs establish E5. Truthful bids establish E6. The concave joint-deviation calculation establishes E7. This checks the entire equilibrium, not just an intensity equation. All orders have positive liquidity probability, so no unspecified off-path belief is needed.

### 5. The selling switch, monotonicity, and the cap

During abstention or mixed selling,

\[
2a\lambda=\Delta m_\lambda-k,\qquad
\frac{d\lambda}{d\Delta}=\frac{m_\lambda}{2a-\Delta m'_\lambda}>0.
\tag{7}
\]

At the transition to a full sale, $\rho\Delta m_\lambda=k$. Combining this with (7) gives exactly Solver D's switch:

\[
\boxed{\lambda_S=\frac{(1-\rho)k}{2a\rho}.}
\]

Beyond that switch, if research remains interior,

\[
2a\lambda=(1+\rho)\Delta m_\lambda-2k,\qquad
\frac{d\lambda}{d\Delta}=
\frac{(1+\rho)m_\lambda}{2a-(1+\rho)\Delta m'_\lambda}>0.
\tag{8}
\]

The roots coincide at the switch. Comparing slopes **at that common point** gives a strict upward jump, since the difference after cross-multiplication has numerator $2a\rho m_\lambda>0$. This is an upward kink, not a claim that the slope thereafter exceeds every earlier slope, nor a claim that the whole schedule is convex. At the research cap the slope becomes zero.

The interior switch exists only if $\lambda_S<1$, and is encountered before the cap only if $\lambda_S<\bar\lambda$. Solver D's $\Delta_S=\Delta^*(\lambda_S)$ must not be evaluated outside $0<\lambda<1$. In the benchmark $\lambda_S=1.2$, and in the referee's economy it is 25: neither is an attainable interior intensity. Selling can nevertheless become certain **after** research has reached the cap, at $\Delta=k/(\rho m_{\bar\lambda})$.

The spread at which the research schedule reaches the cap is

\[
\Delta_{\rm cap}^{*}=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{2a\bar\lambda+k}{m_{\bar\lambda}},&\bar\lambda\le\lambda_S,\\[6pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2a\bar\lambda+2k}{(1+\rho)m_{\bar\lambda}},&\bar\lambda>\lambda_S.
\end{cases}
\tag{9}
\]

This is the same formula in all three files. At a capped intensity the low-class rule must always be evaluated using (2), not an intensity-only comparison with $\lambda_S$.

Since the purchase probability is $b+\lambda/2$, entry is

\[
\boxed{\mathsf E=\rho+(1-\rho)[\beta+(1/2-\beta)c(\Delta)].}
\tag{10}
\]

It is independent of $z$ and strictly increasing in the spread before the cap, constant thereafter while full recruitment survives. The schedules join continuously at the first sale, full sale, and cap. This proves the common comparative-statics claim on the equilibrium's existence set.

### 6. What actually ends the branch

Before the cap, the relevant frontier is the **moving** posterior $M_{L(\Delta)}$. Recruitment can fail there while $\tau<M_{\bar\lambda}$. After the cap, the frontier is the fixed $M_{\bar\lambda}$. Thus:

- The *interior* recruiting branch ends at the cap or at a downward exit from (5), whichever occurs first.
- Reaching the cap does not itself end the *full* recruiting branch. Its capped part survives for as long as $\tau\le M_{\bar\lambda}$.
- FOSD alone does not make the existence set connected. The path can leave and re-enter (5).
- Under merely nondecreasing $\tau$, the first equality $\tau=M_{\bar\lambda}$ need not be the endpoint: equality may persist on a plateau. Use the last point of the set $\{\tau\le M_{\bar\lambda}\}$, or impose a strict crossing.

For $1/2<\tau<1$, the exact uncapped recruitment margin is

\[
\phi_a^*=J-k+(\rho J-k)^+-2a\lambda_E(\tau),
\qquad J=\Delta(1-\tau).
\]

Because $G$ decreases, $\phi_a^*\ge0\iff L(\Delta)\ge\lambda_E(\tau)$. The complete recruiting condition is the intersection of this condition with $\lambda_E\le\bar\lambda$. The old $\phi_a\ge0$ is sufficient, but may be unnecessarily strong once full selling contributes profits.

One upward sign crossing before the cap, no subsequent downward exit before it, and attainment of the cap spread give a connected branch to the cap. Afterwards monotonicity of $\tau$ gives a connected capped segment. These are the conditions under which the cap-before-selling inequality disappears. It disappears as a restriction on the **broader IM branch**, not as a replacement for recruitment feasibility or path conditions.

Solver B's assertion that $s_N$ lies inside the branch is not implied by its general hypotheses. With the benchmark and $a=0.2$, the branch starts at $r_R=2.203523115209>r_N=1.747877538268$, already with full selling. Solver A's wording avoids this overstatement.

## Shared Candidate 2: cap-before-selling and single crossing

**Verdict: the ordering condition is exact; single crossing needs a separate argument.** Solver A states the needed endpoint signs explicitly. Solver B and Solver D should retain those signs when replacing a crossing assumption by a derivative bound.

For the abstention-only branch the two relevant spreads are

\[
\Delta_{\rm cap}^{R}=\frac{2a\bar\lambda+k}{m_{\bar\lambda}},
\qquad \Delta_N=\frac{2k}{\rho}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\Delta_{\rm cap}^{R}\le\Delta_N
\iff \rho(2a\bar\lambda+k)\le2k m_{\bar\lambda}.}
\tag{11}
\]

This is independent of the auction, but does not ensure that an auction path reaches either spread, or remains recruiting until then. With a continuous, strictly increasing spread, an attained start before both endpoints, and a positive recruitment margin thereafter, (11) gives the original interval ending at the cap. If (11) fails, that original branch ends **including** its point at $\Delta_N$, since low-class abstention is still optimal at equality. An interior cap endpoint is excluded by definition.

For a differentiable path,

\[
\lambda_E(\tau)=\frac{\beta(2\tau-1)}{\beta+(1-2\beta)(1-\tau)},\qquad
\lambda_E'(\tau)=\frac{\beta}{[\beta+(1-2\beta)(1-\tau)]^2},
\]

\[
\boxed{\phi_a'=J'-2a\lambda_E'(\tau)\tau'
=\Delta'(1-\tau)-[\Delta+2a\lambda_E'(\tau)]\tau'.}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus a positive lower bound on $J'/(2\lambda_E'\tau')$, together with $a$ below it, makes $\phi_a$ strictly increasing. Where $\tau'=0$, read the condition directly as $\Delta'(1-\tau)>0$. A negative initial value and a positive value before or at the intended endpoint are still needed for a nondegenerate branch. Strict increase without these signs can produce no crossing at all. Continuity on a compact declared interval supplies the bounded derivatives needed when interpreting the infimum bound.

Solver A's uniform sufficient bound is correct. If $\Delta'\ge d>0$, $\Delta\le D$, $\tau\le T<1$, $0\le\tau'\le t$, and $d(1-T)>Dt$, then

\[
a<\frac{[d(1-T)-Dt][\beta+(1-2\beta)(1-T)]^2}{2\beta t}
\]

implies $\phi_a'>0$. Its primitive derivative

\[
\tau'=\frac{-g_L'+\tau\Delta'}{\gamma}
\]

also follows correctly from $\gamma'=-\Delta'$.

Solver B's elasticity comparison is correct. Solver D's version with denominator $k+2a\lambda_E$ is the comparison **at a zero of $\phi_a$**, where $J=k+2a\lambda_E$; away from a zero that denominator must be $J$. Upward transversality at every zero, together with endpoint signs and the usual continuity/differentiability conditions, is sufficient for one upward crossing. Neither a globally increasing margin nor a derivative bound is necessary for a single-crossing sign pattern.

### Increasing J does not imply single crossing: Solver A's exact counterexample

Solver A's translated-uniform construction is valid and is the cleanest evidence. With its parameters,

\[
R_\Delta\sim U[\Delta+.9,\Delta+1.1],\quad
\tau=\frac4{9-\Delta},\quad
J=\frac{\Delta(5-\Delta)}{9-\Delta},\quad
\lambda_E=\frac{\Delta-1}{4(6-\Delta)}.
\]

Every incumbent lies between the two class values; the class-H challenger always wins. On $1.1\le\Delta\le2.99$,

\[
J'=1-\frac{36}{(9-\Delta)^2}>0,
\quad
\phi_a=\frac{20\Delta^3-214\Delta^2+595\Delta-441}
{20(9-\Delta)(6-\Delta)}.
\]

The denominator is positive. The numerator is strictly concave on this interval, since its second derivative is $120\Delta-428<0$. Its signs at $1.1,2,2.9$ are negative, positive, negative, giving exactly two crossings. Independently recomputed values are:

| Spread | J | \(\phi_a\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.1 | 0.543037974684 | -0.024308964092 |
| 2.0 | 0.857142857143 | 0.094642857143 |
| 2.9 | 0.998360655738 | -0.072607086198 |

The roots are $1.203586565480$ and $2.692672076379$. Low-class selling is absent throughout, since $\rho\Delta<0.3<2k=1.1$. Research is below $0.44<\bar\lambda=0.8$. Therefore neither selling nor the cap causes the downward exit. This disproves automatic continuation from an existing start to the cap even while J increases and H always wins.

### Solver B's second counterexample

The uniform-family example with $c_H=4.75,k=.02,\rho=.25,\beta=.3,a=2,\bar\lambda=.9$ also works. J increases throughout $[1.2,5]$. After clearing the positive squared denominator, the numerator of (J') is

\[
32r^6-1280r^5+7384r^4-96r^3-6352r^2+320r-8.
\]

Its Bernstein coefficients on $[1.2,5]$ are all positive; the smallest is $3285.196288$. Thus the increase is not inferred merely from sampled values. Recalculation gives:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\phi_a=0 &: 1.464098811833, 2.535976070112, 3.062024486482,\\
\phi_a^*=0 &: 1.464098811833, 1.896793044813, 4.911424487440.
\end{aligned}
\]

The recruiting set within the stated domain $\tau>1/2$ is

\[
(1.366025403784, 1.464098811833]
\ \cup\ [1.896793044813, 4.911424487440].
\]

**Solver B's first left bracket must be open.** At that endpoint $\tau=1/2$, outside the standing hypotheses. In fact, at this exact economy under C1 the construction fails: at $z=0$ a sale gets full entry and earns $\Delta/2-k=0.004519052838>0$, whereas any positive $z$ lowers its posterior below the threshold and gives a sale payoff at most $\rho\Delta/2-k=-0.013870236790<0$. Zero research also fails because both correct orders at the prior earn a positive payoff. This endpoint cannot be restored by treating it as an ordinary C1 recruitment equality.

### Solver D's local perturbation

The first-order numbers are correct. Moving mass from below the reserve to a narrow bump centered at $0.75$ changes each class's gross profit by $-.25\epsilon$, leaves the spread and profit gap unchanged, and raises the threshold by $0.028129395218\epsilon$ at $r=1.6$. ## Solver B/Solver D Candidate 3: complete equilibrium classification and all tie rules

**Verdict: the positive-research C1 classification is correct. The following completes it, and identifies the false auxiliary claims.** Let $L(\Delta)$ and $c(\Delta)$ be as above. For $\rho\Delta>2k$, define $Q(\Delta)\in(0,1)$ by

\[
aQ=\rho\Delta m_Q-k,
\qquad q(\Delta)=\min\{Q(\Delta),\bar\lambda\}.
\tag{13}
\]

The left side rises and the right side falls, so the root is unique. It increases with the spread:

\[
Q'(\Delta)=\frac{\rho m_Q}{a-\rho\Delta m'_Q}>0.
\]

### IM with C1

There are exactly these **outcome types**:

1. **Zero research:** $\lambda=0$, entry $\rho$, iff $\rho\Delta\le2k$. With strict inequality both hypothetical informed classes must abstain. At equality, under IM, H can choose any mixture of buying and abstention and L any mixture of selling and abstention; none is realized because research is zero. All have $V=0$, prior posteriors and the same prices. Thus the literal equilibrium tuples are not exhausted by a single abstention-strategy Z tuple.
2. **Full recruitment:** $\lambda=c(\Delta)$, H buys, L follows (2), iff (5) holds.
3. **Floor entry with positive research:** $\lambda=q(\Delta)$, both classes trade fully, iff $\rho\Delta>2k$ and $\tau>M_{q(\Delta)}$. Entry is $\rho$. Its cap condition is $\rho\Delta m_{\bar\lambda}-k\ge a\bar\lambda$, including equality.

To prove exhaustion, take positive research. H must buy fully by the earlier argument, and the sale and zero order cannot recruit. Under C1 either the purchase fully recruits, already classified, or it has entry $\rho$. In the latter case H's strict payoff is $\rho\Delta m_\lambda-k>0$. L's return at any partial sale is at least this, forcing a full sale. Both class payoffs then equal $\rho\Delta m_\lambda-k$, giving exactly (13) and its cap. There is no fourth positive-research case.

For the uncapped roots, $Q<L$. Evaluating the recruiting fixed-point residual at Q gives

\[
G(Q;\Delta)=(1-\rho)\Delta m_Q>0.
\]

Clipping gives $q\le c$, with strict inequality when $Q<\bar\lambda$. The two positive-research outcomes coexist precisely on

\[
\boxed{\rho\Delta>2k,\qquad M_{q(\Delta)}<\tau\le M_{c(\Delta)}.}
\tag{14}
\]

**Solver B's “band is never empty” is false:** if both roots exceed the cap, $q=c=\bar\lambda$ and (14) is empty. In the benchmark at $r=3$, $Q=0.484522378048$ and $L=0.740604715974$, both above $\bar\lambda=0.336121768915$. There is no threshold band between their clipped posteriors. Solver D correctly uses the clipped roots in its band formula.

The classification also proves that IM/C1 has at least one outcome at every point in the stated domain: below the no-trade bound Z exists; above it, $q\le c$ ensures that either full recruitment or floor entry exists. This does not establish existence under I0.

### CM: the interior threshold tuple and the missing cap boundary

At a reachable challenger tie, $\lambda=\lambda_E(\tau)$. Set $J=\Delta(1-\tau)$ and $W=(\rho J-k)^+$. For $0<\lambda_E<\bar\lambda$, the necessary and sufficient purchase entry is

\[
e_T=\frac{2a\lambda_E+k-W}{J},\qquad \rho\le e_T\le1.
\tag{15}
\]

L still follows (2). At an interior threshold equilibrium E7 requires equality, so (15) is necessary. Conversely it makes E7 hold; H's payoff is strict because either $W=0$ and it equals $2a\lambda_E>0$, or $W>0$ and $e_T\ge\rho$ makes it at least W. The previous price and Bayes construction applies. The endpoints $e_T=1$ and $e_T=\rho$ coincide with the relevant recruiting/floor boundary outcomes when those are admitted under CM.

**At $\lambda_E=\bar\lambda$, (15) must be replaced by an inequality.** The complete additional family is

\[
\boxed{e(1)\in
\left[\max\left\{\rho,\frac{2a\bar\lambda+k-W}{J}\right\},1\right],}
\tag{16}
\]

provided the lower endpoint is at most one. Each permitted value has its own competitive purchase price and keeps the unique best research choice at the cap. Solver B's explicit CM addition requires $\lambda_E<\bar\lambda$, so it misses this family; Solver D's “add threshold tuples” needs (16) to be complete.

At the benchmark ceiling the whole interval $e(1)\in[.25,1]$ is permitted, as the manuscript already states. At the referee's ceiling the lower endpoint is instead $0.771162031308$: its overall entry range on these cap-threshold tuples is $[0.586771850019,0.66]$. It is false to transfer the benchmark's whole interval $[\rho,1]$ to every economy.

### I0: include the full-sale equality in the excluded region

The strict H purchase and the strict trades in (13) survive I0. The zero-research equality selects abstention. For **any positive-research candidate**, including a CM threshold tuple, the low-class strategy exists under I0 only when

\[
\rho\Delta\le2k
\quad\text{or}\quad
\rho\Delta m_\lambda>k.
\tag{17}
\]

Thus delete a full-recruitment tuple exactly when

\[
\rho\Delta m_{c(\Delta)}\le k<\rho\Delta/2.
\]

The endpoint with full selling at **zero** sale profit is excluded too. Solver A and Solver B state this correctly. Solver D's open-ended gap descriptions omit that endpoint. For the benchmark under I0/C1, the recruiting gap is ((1.747877538268,2.124148132815]).

Under I0/CM, apply (17) to the threshold tuple's intensity, not to the full-recruitment intensity. Partial preparation can survive even when the full-recruitment tuple is absent. At the benchmark $r=2$, for example,

\[
\lambda=\lambda_E=0.223834939087,\quad
e(1)=0.359944219030,\quad
U_H(1)=0.010209604097,\quad U_L(-1)=0.000982142857.
\]

Both trades are strict, $V/a=\lambda$, and overall entry is $0.287449022389$. This is an I0/CM equilibrium inside the I0 gap for **full** recruitment. Statements that “only the nonrecruiting equilibrium remains” there require C1. In the referee's relevant gap, by contrast, the threshold tuple would still require low-class mixing and is also removed by I0.

### Remaining numerical and wording corrections

- At the referee's start, the research value is $0.052409610221$, not Solver B's $0.05243$. At its exact first-sale threshold it is $0.281146161064$, not Solver D's $0.281144$. These are small numerical inaccuracies, not failures of the construction.
- Solver B maps the referee's capped full-sale spread $5.333333333333$ to $r\approx12.59$ using a formula valid only below $h=10$. For $r\ge10$, the actual spread is $9-49.5/r$; the correct strength is **13.5**. Recruitment has already ended at 8.914448, so this correction does not change the verified recruiting interval.
- In the referee's example the uncapped schedule still exceeds $\lambda_E$ at $r=9$, but the capped schedule does not: $L=0.498599115092$, $\lambda_E=0.413223140496>0.4$. Solver D's positive uncapped margin at 9 therefore does not extend the capped recruiting branch.
- Solver B's two values $J(7.28)=0.793416858115$ and $J(7.30)=0.793227311224$ are correct; the actual peak is $r=7.167192999831$, not 7.28. The capped recruiting result does not require increasing J there.
- Solver D's phrase that nothing economic happens at the first sale is too strong: the low class's orders, sale beliefs and prices change, zero research ceases to be an equilibrium, and the role of investor indifference changes. The precise result is continuity of research and entry along the IM recruiting selection.
- In the full-sale regime H's payoff need not be at least $2a\lambda$, because L supplies some of the research return. For example, at the benchmark with $a=.2,r=2.25$, $U_H=0.091333991433<2a\lambda=0.099167489291$. Solver B's mixed-sale E5 inequality must not be carried over unchanged to its full-sale check; strict positivity, which does hold, is enough.

## Ranking and recommendation

1. **Adopt Solver A Candidate 1, with the common proof above.** It is true, economically substantive, uses the existing model, and states its path limitation accurately. Present (4), (5), and (10) as the general result. Low-class mixing extends the research mechanism through the former endpoint; full profitable selling adds another return to research.
2. **Add the corrected Solver B/Solver D classification.** It identifies the low-research floor-entry outcome and fills the benchmark's gray band. Include the zero-research equality convention, clipped coexistence band, interior CM formula (15), cap family (16), and the I0 filter (17). These are short extensions of existing lemmas, not a different model.
3. **Retain Solver A Candidate 2 as an optional corollary for the original abstention branch.** The other two versions of that ordering argument are equivalent after adding endpoint signs and crossing/attainment qualifications. It is a narrower result, not the best main repair.

Recommended substantive statement:

> With prior one half and the investor allowed to mix at indifference, the equilibrium with full preparation after a researched purchase has research and entry that rise with the target-payoff spread until research reaches its cap. Low-class selling does not interrupt these schedules. The equilibrium exists exactly while the purchase posterior reaches the preparation threshold; along an incumbent-strength path, persistence to the cap requires that this recruitment condition continue to hold.

This is a statement about a specified equilibrium selection, with full recruitment. It does not claim uniqueness, or monotone entry for every CM threshold outcome or for switches between coexisting outcomes.

## What remains weak after the best workaround

- FOSD and even increasing J do not guarantee a connected recruiting branch or survival to the cap. Solver A's exact counterexample proves the limitation without selling, a binding cap, or any loss of class-H auction dominance.
- The continuation through mixed low-class selling depends on IM. Under I0 full recruitment can disappear, and existence of some other outcome requires its own condition.
- Research and entry can rise on the recruiting selection while ownership falls through the separate $W_H(F)$ factor. Nothing here removes that issue.
- Post-cap recruitment ends when the threshold exceeds the attainable posterior, even if the uncapped research margin remains positive. This is an economic endpoint, not a defect in the research first-order condition.
- The analytical construction is established here; the manuscript and its figure/registry pipeline have not been revised or released by this referee task. Only this report was created under the repository.
