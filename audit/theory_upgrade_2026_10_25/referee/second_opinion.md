# Second opinion on WORKAROUNDS.md (the second reader)

25 October 2026. Read-only review of `WORKAROUNDS.md` against the five referee reports (`W1.md` to `W5.md`), the solver files where a memo claim had no the referee source, and the manuscript at `overleaf/slot/` (commit 69c25f4). Line numbers refer to `WORKAROUNDS.md`.

Headline. No recommendation in the memo contradicts an the referee verdict, and every number I traced reproduces the referee's. The fidelity problems are omitted qualifiers, almost all in the sentences the author is meant to paste into the paper: nine that matter and seven small ones (section A). The chosen workarounds match the referee's rankings; I disagree on one (section B, W2). The author's general-then-condition-then-benchmark structure works result by result but not as a single-condition design, and the research results are proved only at the even prior (section C). The prose is close to what the author wants; the remaining engineering words are listed with replacements (section D).

## A. Fidelity

Items are ordered by how much they matter. Each gives the memo line, what the referee says, and the fix.

A1. Line 68, the W3 main-text sentence, omits the prior and the tie rule. the referee's recommended statement (W3, "Ranking and recommendation") opens "With prior one half and the investor allowed to mix at indifference". the referee's I0 section shows the continuation disappears exactly where the low class would mix, and on the benchmark path that gap is (1.748, 2.124]. So the sentence as written is false under one of the paper's own investor rules, on the paper's own path. The paragraph above it mentions the rule; the sentence the author will paste does not. Fix: begin the sentence "At the even prior, when an indifferent investor may mix, ...".

A2. Line 46, "The counterexample's costs are moderate in scale-free terms." This is Solver G's phrase (W2_G.md, line 273). the referee declined it: "Neither an empirical calibration nor a claim that such costs are typical has been established", and later "Terms such as 'almost necessary,' 'extreme,' 'common,' and 'generic' need either their precise mathematical qualifications or external calibration evidence." The memo adopts a solver judgement the referee set aside. Fix: give the two ratios the referee reports (preparation cost one quarter of the high value, trading cost two percent of it) and stop.

A3. Lines 44 and 142, "against an incumbent of any given value it raises the chance that class H acquires the target." the referee (W2, shared candidate 1) proves the rise "at each fixed incumbent value at which H can win", almost everywhere: the kernel q_H(x) is zero above every high-class value, and at a class-value atom it depends on the auction's tie rule. Line 38 has the qualifier; the main-text sentence and decision 3 drop it. Fix: add "wherever class H can win at that value" in both places.

A4. Line 70, "Under I0 ... only floor-entry research remains there." the referee (W3, I0 section): that holds with rule C1; under I0 with CM a threshold tuple survives inside the gap (benchmark r = 2: research 0.224, entry after a purchase 0.36, entry 0.287). Fix: "only floor-entry research remains when an indifferent challenger prepares". The same omission sits at line 15, "Inside the region the informative equilibrium is unique": the referee proves uniqueness under rules IM and C1, and the manuscript (A4_coexistence.tex:357) adds the threshold pair T on (r_A, r_J] when an indifferent challenger may mix. Fix: "unique when an indifferent challenger prepares".

A5. Line 110, "With an unknown prior, counts confound presence with the prior's tilt along a curve." the referee (W5, "Boundary correction") shows the investor's own trading incentives can collapse the curve to a point (π = 0.6, λ = 0.4, β = 1/3, Δ = 0.24 leaves only λ′ = 0.4), so the count-law statement is right and the universal one is not; the referee calls the correct result "local nonidentification at points with slack feasibility, incentive and entry inequalities". Fix: add "before the investor's trading incentives are imposed" and say the identified set is generically, not always, a curve.

A6. Line 7, the opening, three points. (i) "Orders predict the winner whenever the incumbent can tell the two classes apart" misdescribes the referee's condition Pr_F(D(R) > 0) > 0, which is about where the incumbent's value lands relative to the two classes' values, not about anything the incumbent knows. (ii) "The empirical tests work at every prior once each price impact is weighted" omits full two-sided trade and a fixed public state; the referee's purchases-only equilibrium at π = 0.3, r = 2 has no recruitment and a positive weighted statistic (line 122 has this; the opening does not). (iii) "Three trend claims also keep a named condition": four. The research branch's persistence to the cap along a path keeps one (Solver A's two-crossing example, W3), as the memo's own line 136 says.

A7. Line 108, "Off prior 1/2 a regime appears ... a sale deters preparation." the referee's sign table (W5, shared candidate 1): T < 0 needs m < τ ≤ π, so with τ > 1/2 the regime lives at priors above one half (the referee's examples are 0.7 and 0.75). Fix: "at priors above one half".

A8. Line 21, "a uniform incumbent whose upper bound stays between the two classes' values." the referee's condition (W1, equation 4): every low-class value lies in [p, r_0] and every high-class value is at least r_2. For dispersed classes "between the values" could mean between the means, which is not enough. Fix: "whose upper bound lies above every low-class value and below every high-class value".

A9. Lines 62 and 68, "research rises faster with the spread: the schedule kinks upward" and "once a sale pays outright, its profit adds a second return to research." the referee (W3, section 5): an upward kink at the switch only, "not a claim that the slope thereafter exceeds every earlier slope"; and the switch intensity λ_S = (1 − ρ)k/(2aρ) is 1.2 in the benchmark and 25 in the referee's economy, so neither economy ever reaches it: full selling arrives only after research is capped. The appendix (item W3.4) records this; the main text does not. Fix: after the sentence add "in the benchmark the cap comes first, so this second return never operates there".

Smaller points.

A10. Line 23, "coexistence there needs a trading cost about thirty times the benchmark's." Source is Solver B (W1_B.md, line 261: k ≥ ρΔ/2 ≥ 0.625). Consistent with the referee's numbers (no-trade deviation returns 0.75 and 0.8125 at z = 7, 7.5 against k = 0.02) but not a refereed statement; the referee's own coexistence sets in that family use k = 2 and 1.5. Label it as Solver B's figure.

A11. Line 94, "the counterexample is the benchmark with the incumbent allowed above h." With the benchmark c_H = 6, recruitment ends at r_C = 3.59, so above h the gap falls only after recruitment has ceased. The fall during recruitment needs c_H = 3 (window (8.06, 12.19]). Say which case is meant.

A12. Line 86, "the covariance between orders and acquisitions." the referee's object is the challenger-win indicator; "acquisitions" can be read as high-class ownership, the very thing W2 says is not general. Write "between the order and a challenger win".

A13. Line 130, "The proofs of A.14(iii) and (iv) use M + m = 1." Part (iv) does; part (iii) uses Bayes plausibility at the even prior (the order-weighted posteriors average to 1/2). Both are even-prior facts, so write "use the even prior where the prior π would serve".

A14. Line 145, "the full-sale point 2.124" is the existing registry key rS_B, not a new one.

A15. Line 79, the symbol collision r̄_R versus the support bound r̄, comes from the Solver D solver file (W3_D.md, line 123), not from the referee. It is true (02_model.tex:53 and 03_competition_information.tex:42 use r̄ for the support bound). Label the source.

A16. Line 7, "entry rises with the spread wherever the informative equilibrium exists." the referee: nondecreasing, strict only while the high class mixes. Line 21 has it right; the opening should say "never falls".

Nothing in the memo recommends a statement the referee found false, and the provenance lines (25, 50, 72, 96, 124) match the verdict tables.

## B. Judgement

W1 (lines 15 to 23). The package, region theorem plus uniform affordability corollary plus the balance condition, is the referee's ranking and I agree. Two changes to the main-text sentence (line 21). First, carry the unconditional full-purchase fact: "the strengths at which the investor buys for sure and coexistence holds always form one interval; any gap lies where the high class still mixes." It is general, bold, and it is what the benchmark's constant-entry segment [r_F, r_N] becomes. Second, "A stronger incumbent moves the economy through this fixed region" reads as a monotone passage, which is exactly what fails. Say "raises both the spread and the threshold, so the economy can enter the region, leave it and return".

W2 (lines 38 to 48 and decision 3 at line 142). The package matches the referee. One disagreement. The same-incumbent ratio O_H on the research branch over O_H at zero research equals e_H/ρ, class-H entry over its floor; W_H cancels, so it says nothing beyond "class-H entry rises", which the proposition already states. the referee ranked it first among unrestricted statements but also wrote that "a normalized ownership ratio cannot be presented as that level prediction" and that selection between the two equilibria is unresolved. I would state class-H entry, the improving pool and the fixed-value statement in the proposition, and keep the ratio as a one-line remark. The rest of decision 3 is right: level rise as a corollary under outbidding, decisive advantage for the whole benchmark family with the 1.0125 margin, the exact trade-off in the appendix, and the same assumption reused for A.13.

W3 (lines 62 to 70 and line 147). The IM continuation and the three-outcome map are the right choice and match the referee. Three changes: name the tie rule in the sentence (A1); say the benchmark never reaches the second return (A9); and carry the path condition in words, as the referee's statement does ("along a path, the branch runs to the cap as long as a researched purchase keeps covering the trading and research costs"), because the paper's headline is a path claim and the memo drops the clause to the "what remains weak" paragraph. Filling the gray band is right.

W4 (lines 86 to 92). Agree entirely; the sentence tracks the referee's suggested theorem. Add "with the prior and slot fixed" to the trend clause: with endogenous research M − m moves too (the referee, "What remains weak"). Leading with outbidding rather than the weaker "D nondecreasing" is the right trade: it is shared with W2 and has an economic name; the uniform exact condition belongs in a remark, as the memo has it.

W5 (lines 108 to 118 and line 147). Agree with replacing the unweighted test, with the identification theorem and with the exact announcement condition. Two cautions. The identification sentence (line 118) should carry the entry regime (sale and no order at the floor) and the known payoff scale, as the referee's inversion does; and the noise near one half (variance factor 100 at π = 0.55) belongs beside the identification claim, not two sentences later. For the entry component, the bold general fact is the decomposition itself (the competitive part always rises; reserve rescue can fall), which deserves the main text more than the two sufficient conditions.

## C. Structure

The proposal (general theorem, one named condition, benchmark as a checked special case) works for W4 and W5 cleanly, for W2 and W1 with qualifications, and for W3 only pointwise. It fails as a single-condition design, and it fails on "any prior" for the research results. The memo should say both.

C1. The prior. the referee proves the research branch and its continuation at the even prior only (W2: "For that branch take prior 1/2"; W3: "Fix the prior at 1/2"). The memo's appendix records this (items W2.2 and W3.3), but the structure as the author states it would present Propositions 4 and A.2 at any prior, which this study does not support. W4's second difference and the unweighted-test corollary also need the even prior or π ≥ 1/2. General at every prior: the W1 region, the W4 sign and post-ceiling covariance, the W5 weighted test, identification and announcement results.

C2. One shared condition is not enough. Outbidding, or decisive advantage, serves W2 and W4 only. Solver A's W1 two-piece path has class H winning for sure and covering c_H against the strongest incumbent (margin 2.19), yet coexistence splits. Solver A's W3 counterexample has H always winning and J rising, yet the recruitment margin crosses zero twice. The W5 entry trend fails under stochastic dominance at the benchmark prior and costs while H always wins. So the paper needs four named conditions: outbidding (W2, W4); a uniform incumbent with separated classes and affordability, or the balance condition on Δ(g_H − c_H) (W1); cheap research with the right end signs (W3 path); a constant chance of missing the reserve, or the low-reserve uniform condition (W5). Moreover, for point masses the W1 affordability condition c_H ≤ h − r_2 = 6.4 and the W2 decisive-advantage condition c_H ≥ (h² − p²)/(2h) = 4.9875 bound the preparation cost from opposite sides; the benchmark c_H = 6 sits in the window. The memo says "one assumption serves two results" (true, line 142) but never says that the conditions differ across the five results or that two of them bracket c_H. The benchmark section of the rewrite should list all four with their margins (0.4, 1.0125, a_mono − a = 0.137, ℓ² − 3p² = 1/4).

C3. Where the benchmark does not exercise the general theory. (i) The research kink: λ_S = 1.2, so the "second return to research" never operates in the benchmark; the theorem's second piece has no benchmark figure. (ii) The W4 peak at r = h sits at a jump of D (point classes): the referee gives Ω′ of 0.01 from the left and −0.09 from the right at r = 10, and the smooth characterization D(r) = Γ(r)/r fails there; the benchmark checks the uniform condition only in its one-sided form. (iii) The benchmark's cap-before-selling margin is 0.0016, and under the general theorem its recruiting equilibrium runs to r_C = 3.593 rather than ending at 1.678, so Section 5's narrative and figure change along with the theorem. (iv) The W1 endpoints r_A, r_F, r_N and the four prior cases of Corollary A.2 need crossing and attainment conditions the general theorem does not supply (memo line 23 says this; keep it). (v) In its favor: the benchmark cannot show the W2 failure at all (ownership falls only above h, and recruitment stops at 7.97 < h), and the general theorem explains why. That is the structure working as intended.

C4. The point-mass incumbent. The model requires a continuous F. Two general statements invite a reading as an economy with a known incumbent, which the model does not allow. The W2 fixed-value statement conditions on R = x and must be stated as a conditional kernel holding almost everywhere (the referee: "a counterfactual kernel, rather than an identified conditional probability"; at class-value atoms it depends on the auction's tie rule). The W4 reading of E_F[D(R)] as the incumbent "falling between a low-class and a high-class challenger of the same rank" uses two counterfactual draws, not two actual bidders (the referee, W4). Both belong in the paper as interpretations, not as degenerate-incumbent economies.

C5. What "one named condition" means per result. For W4 and W2 it is a single inequality with an economic name. For W1 it is a family restriction (uniform incumbent, separated classes) plus affordability; the general-family version is a balance condition on derived objects with no clean name. For W3 the path condition is quantitative (a below a bound built from the slopes of the spread and the threshold); "cheap research" is the honest label. For W5 it is a condition on the reserve. The rewrite should not promise one condition in Section 2; it should promise one per result and say they differ.

## D. Readability

Passages a finance economist would stumble on, with a plain replacement.

- Line 7, "whenever the incumbent can tell the two classes apart": "whenever the incumbent's value sometimes lands between what a low-class and a high-class challenger would pay".
- Line 9, "registry certificates": "not the exact interval checks the paper's own numbers go through".
- Lines 17, 30, 134, "Sturm count", "certificate", "hand proofs": "the polynomial root count behind the benchmark proof"; "both root counts become short direct proofs".
- Line 17, "governs entry on the threshold branch under CM": "sets entry on the threshold branch when an indifferent challenger may prepare with any probability".
- Line 23, "log-concave in that family": "single-peaked there, so its upper level sets are intervals".
- Line 25, "the erosion ratio": undefined symbol; "the rate at which class H's profit erodes per unit of spread".
- Lines 38, 46, "fixed-value statement", "latent incumbent value": "the statement that holds the incumbent's realized value fixed"; "a value nobody observes".
- Line 40, "A primitive version certifies the benchmark without restricting the path": "A condition on values and costs alone covers every economy in the benchmark family, whatever the strength".
- Line 46, "scale-free terms", "spread elasticity", "interior branch": "relative to the high value"; "the proportional response of class-H entry to the spread"; "while research is below its cap".
- Lines 62, 70, 151, 174 to 178, the codes IM, I0, C1, CM: "an indifferent investor may mix" or "abstains"; "an indifferent challenger prepares" or "may prepare with any probability". The author has asked for no tie-rule labels in plain text; in the appendix define the four phrases once and use the phrases.
- Line 70, "Existence is pointwise": "The equilibrium exists strength by strength". "a below a_mono": "research cheap enough that the recruitment margin keeps rising".
- Line 86, "Matched draws": "Pair a low-class and a high-class challenger at the same rank within their classes". "the covariance between orders and acquisitions": "between the order and a challenger win".
- Line 88, "the second difference of acquisition frequencies across sale, no order and purchase": "how much a purchase raises the challenger's win rate relative to no order, minus how much a sale lowers it".
- Line 94, "The recruitment content sits in the second channel of the covariance and in the second difference": "Only the part of the covariance that runs through entry, and the purchase-minus-sale comparison, speak to recruitment".
- Line 110, "the prior's tilt along a curve": "a more lopsided prior with a rarer informed investor produces the same counts".
- Line 122, "a fixed public state", "the right entry baseline and a known payoff scale": "one target at one strength, not a pool of deals"; "that entry after a sale and after no order sits at its floor, and that ρΔ is known".
- Line 141, "where the reduction lives", "present the plane in words": "where the two-number summary of the auction is stated".
- Line 145, "checker rows", "registry keys": "new benchmark margins for the parameter table in A9".
- Line 114 is the model for the rest: two forces named in words, the sign of each, the condition that decides.
- Appendix (line 151 on): "FOSD", "up-set", "quantile coupling" are fine in a formal appendix; define "up-set" once ("a set that, once it contains an incumbent, contains every stronger one").

General remark. The main-text sentences are in the right register. The discussion paragraphs splice formula fragments into prose (line 15: "Δ ≤ k/(ρ·max{π, 1 − π})"); a reader of the paper would take the words and look up the formula in the appendix, so the memo can do the same.
