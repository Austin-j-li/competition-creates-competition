# S10 check: do the abstract, introduction and conclusion match the propositions

Report of the production assistant, as checker, to the coordinator. The manuscript is unchanged; this report is the only file written. The manuscript was built into a private folder outside the repository (no missing number in the three texts). Line numbers refer to the abstract in `main.tex`, the introduction and the conclusion.

## Verdict

66 result-stating claims were read against Propositions 1 to 4, Corollaries 1 to 3 and the Section 4 to 8 text: 39 match, 23 miss a qualifier, 4 overclaim, 0 are wrong. No claim contradicts a proposition. The gaps are of four kinds, all repairable by words inside the sentence:

1. The abstract and the conclusion state the coexistence result "exactly" without the default tie rules, which Proposition 3(a) needs (Table A.4 shows the result changes under the other tie rules).
2. Weak results are stated as strict ones, or without the class ranking that Proposition 1(b) needs.
3. Three sentences promote a possibility to a fact ("moves the economy across that region" twice, "so the price recruits", "the bidder pool depends").
4. The introduction cites only Corollary 1 by its printed number; Propositions 1 to 4 and Corollaries 2 and 3 are cited by section only, or by the roadmap.

The earlier report of the prose writer describes the abstract and the conclusion as carrying the tie rules for Proposition 3(a) and the ownership clause "on the open set". The text does not: the abstract has no tie rule in the coexistence sentence, and the open-set sentence does not state the condition under which ownership rises.

## Empirical sentences in the abstract

None. The last sentence, "access to prices raises expected target proceeds and acquisition surplus net of preparation costs", is a welfare comparison of two model economies (Proposition "Access to prices", Section 7.1): the challenger sees the price or does not, and expected proceeds and net surplus are compared. It names no observable, test or sample, so it is the allowed kind. It needs the hypotheses of Proposition 2 (see A-13).

## Example numbers

Every example number in the introduction sits after "As an illustration, in the example" (paragraphs 7, 8, 9) or in a sentence that names the example and the strong incumbent (paragraph 12, the entry component). The abstract and the conclusion hold no number. The only typed numeral is the prior \(1/2\), which the main text types in the same way; a number for it exists (low; L-3).
