# W2: ownership by the high class

Weakness key: `W2-ownership`

Where: high-class ownership O_H = π · Pr(enter | H) · W_H(F). Proposition 4(a) (`prop:research`, overleaf/slot/sections/05_research.tex line 52 onward; proof in overleaf/slot/appendix/A5_research.tex around lines 233 to 283) claims that ownership rises with strength on the research branch. The proof uses W_H = 1 (h exceeds every incumbent strength in the benchmark). Proposition 2's ownership part (`prop:ccc`, sections/04_equilibrium_results.tex lines 11 to 70; proof overleaf/slot/appendix/A3_proposition2.tex) already carries a W_H condition. Propositions A.4, A.5 and A.7 (appendix/A6_robustness.tex) also make ownership claims.

The weakness. W_H(F) falls with strength, so ownership can fall while research and entry rise. On the research branch ownership is ½·W_H(F)·[ρ + (1 − ρ)(β + (1 − β)λ_R)] (prior 1/2). Verified counterexample: h = 10, ℓ = 1, p = 1/2, R ~ U[0, r], c_H = 2.5, ρ = 0.05, k = 0.2, β = 0.2, a = 5, cap λ̄ = 0.9. From r = 11 to r = 12 the research branch holds, entry rises from 0.279883 to 0.282574, and high-class ownership falls from 0.157434 to 0.147305.

Floated but unchecked direction: compare ownership with the same-incumbent counterfactual (no research, or fixed information), so that the direct effect of W_H cancels and only the information channel remains. Alternatives: the share of acquisitions won by class H (a conditional probability), or the condition "class H outbids every incumbent on the path" (W_H = 1), or an elasticity trade-off W_H′/W_H against the entry elasticity.
