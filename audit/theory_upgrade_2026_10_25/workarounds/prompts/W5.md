# W5: tests that rely on the symmetric prior

Weakness key: `W5-symmetry-tests`

Where: overleaf/slot/appendix/A8_empirical.tex: Proposition A.11 (`prop:spread`, line 45, the price gap and its entry component), Proposition A.12 (`prop:informedshare`, line 85, identification of the informed share), Corollary A.3 (`cor:purchasesonly`, line 144), Proposition A.14 (`prop:announcement`, line 198); summary in overleaf/slot/sections/08_empirical.tex.

The weakness, in three parts.
(a) A.11: the test "the purchase price impact exceeds the sale impact if and only if a purchase recruits" uses prior 1/2, through M − 1/2 = 1/2 − m. At prior π, without recruitment, I₊ − I₋ = ρΔ{[M − π] − [π − m]}, which is not zero, plus a recruitment term. A.11's strict trend in the entry component also needs A_F(M) = t_L − t_0 + ΔM to rise with strength, which first-order dominance does not give (pF(p) can fall).
(b) A.12 and Corollary A.3: count-based nonidentification of the informed share uses prior 1/2. At prior π, counts give E[X] = λ(2π − 1), so λ is identified directly and the nonidentification result does not carry over.
(c) A.14: the positivity conditions for announcement returns.

Floated but unchecked direction: measure each test against a prior-adjusted no-recruitment benchmark (for example compare I₊ − I₋ with ρΔ{[M − π] − [π − m]}, or use a ratio of impacts scaled by posterior moves), so that the test is free of the prior. Also: which of these tests survives at every prior with a simple correction, which needs prior 1/2, and does a nonsymmetric prior give a better identification result that the paper should state instead?
