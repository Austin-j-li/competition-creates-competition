Discrete trading: what it keeps from the paper, and what to put in it

The team replaced the paper's continuous trading stage with discrete unit orders. It then studied ten changes to the setup. This report gives the answer, the evidence and a recommendation. The Laplace model stays the benchmark; this study is exploratory.

The short answer. Discreteness alone does not kill the paper's results; noise with bounded support does. With three-point noise the top flow reveals the class, so feedback and coexistence die. With noise equal to the difference of two geometric counts, every flow is on path and beliefs stay inside (0, 1). Then Proposition 2 and a Proposition 3 analog hold with short closed-form proofs. Two-sided unit orders keep the paper's cH = 6. They reproduce its bounds m, M, its ceiling rC and its no-trade boundary rN exactly. Keep the Laplace model, and add one remark, one online-appendix section and one footnote.

The work: 25 memos from four workstreams (A to D) and their independent checks, then one red-team pass over the headline claims. Five claims were refuted: one number in the brief and four claims in the memos. Two further general statements fell, and six claims were overclaimed. No shared number was wrong. This report uses the corrected versions. The memos are in audit/discrete_exploration_2026_10_21/memos/.

1 · Does discreteness kill the results?
No. Bounded noise support does the damage.

With three-point noise, the top flow reveals the class and pays the investor nothing. Coexistence and feedback die. Entry returns to ρ only above r = 7.969.

Geometric-count noise has full support on the integers. Every flow is on path, and the flow that recruits still pays the investor.

2 · Which discrete model works?
Two-sided unit orders with geometric-count noise.

At the paper's cH = 6 and strengths (1.2, 3, 3.6), entry moves 0.25 → 0.569293 → 0.25. The bounds m, M, the ceiling 3.592659 and the boundary 1.747878 are the paper's, exactly.

The buy-only version also works, but only for cH in (4.804167, 5.312161].

3 · Which modifications help?
Two trading rounds and costly research. Unequal noise helps on a narrow window.

Two rounds and costly information acquisition create coexistence at the paper's own strengths, with one caveat each. Unequal three-point noise gives an upward entry branch only 0.0144 wide.

A reserve set after the price, costless disclosure and a second potential challenger each remove a part of the mechanism.

4 · What goes into the paper?
One remark, one appendix section, one footnote.

State the two-sided discrete companion after Proposition 2 or in Section 5.1. Put its proofs in the online appendix. Add a footnote at Proposition 3.

Keep out the reserve, disclosure, stake, incumbent and sequential-entry results, and the three-point model.

1Two discrete noise laws, and what each flow tells the market

Both discrete bases keep the paper's auction and Reading N with point masses at ℓ and h. Nature draws the challenger's class, the investor sees the class, and preparation reveals the value. The prior is π = 1/2, the low cost is cL = 0, and the investor is always present (λ = 1). Only the noise law and the order menu change.

PropertyThree-point noiseDiscrete-Laplace noise
LawNoise Z ∈ {−1, 0, 1}, each value with probability 1/3Z = G1 − G2, two independent geometric counts of unit noise orders. Pr(Z = z) = (1 − a)/(1 + a) · a|z|, with a = e−1/b = 0.606531 at b = 2

Extreme flowsConclusive: posterior 0 or 1Never conclusive; a one-step likelihood ratio is at most e1/b

The order flow is X = q + Z. Under full orders the market's posterior that the challenger is class H takes few values:

  • Three-point, buy-only. Flows −1, 0, 1, 2 give posteriors 0, 1/2, 1/2, 1. The middle flows say nothing. The top flow reveals class H. There the investor's residual is zero, so the flow that recruits pays the investor nothing.
  • Discrete-Laplace, buy-only. Bad news (x ≤ 0) gives m1 = 1/(1 + e1/b) = 0.377541. Good news (x ≥ 1) gives M1 = 0.622459. Every flow maps to one of these two values.
  • Discrete-Laplace, two-sided. Class H buys one unit and class L sells one. The posteriors are m = 0.268941 (x ≤ −1), 1/2 (x = 0) and M = 0.731059 (x ≥ 1). These are the paper's bounds 0.269 and 0.731.

Why the second law works.

  • Every integer flow has positive probability under every strategy, so Bayes' rule fixes every belief. No refinement is used.
  • Beliefs stay in [m1, M1] (buy-only) or [m, M] (two-sided). So the flow that recruits still leaves the investor a positive residual.
  • Each investor class compares one unit order with no order, so its choice is one comparison of a gain with the cost k. In the buy-only base the purchase payoff K obeys ρΛΔT ≤ K ≤ (1 − π)ΔT for every strategy, with Λ = 0.470007 at b = 2. No derivative bound is needed. analytical

The law has a simple story for Section 2.3. Noise is the count of unit noise purchases minus the count of unit noise sales, and each count is geometric.

In words. Bounded noise support is the loss, not discreteness. With three-point noise the top flow reveals the class, so the flow that recruits pays the investor nothing. The results: no feedback, no coexistence, a ceiling at gH(r) = cH, and an unused flow that needs a belief rule. Geometric-count noise has full support and a bounded one-step likelihood ratio. Every flow is on path, posteriors stay inside (0, 1), and the flow that recruits keeps a positive residual. No discrete version reproduces the paper's order pattern (1, −vj) or its upward entry order at 1.55, 1.60 and 1.65.

3The Proposition 2 analog

In each discrete base, each investor class compares one unit order with no order. Its choice is one comparison of a gain with the trading cost k. So Proposition 2 needs only bounds on that gain, not the paper's derivative bound.

Two-sided unit orders: the recommended companion

Proposition C1.1 needs three conditions. With cL = 0 the first holds at every strength.

  • (D1) 0 ≤ cL ≤ Br₁(m);
  • (D2) Br₀(1/2) < cH ≤ Br₁(M);
  • (D3) ΔT(r0) < k < ρmΔT(r1).

Then three parts hold, under both investor tie rules. analytical

  1. At r0, no trade is the unique outcome, and entry is ρ.
  2. At r1, full orders (1, −1) are the unique outcome. The price takes three values, and entry and ownership rise.
  3. At an r2 with Br₂(M) < cH, full orders stay unique and entry returns to ρ.

At the paper's benchmark (cH = 6, b = 2, k = 0.02), entry is 0.25 → 0.569293 → 0.25 at strengths (1.2, 3, 3.6). High-class ownership rises from 0.125 to 0.358422. Condition (D2) gives the window cH ∈ (4.804167, 6.217155].

Why three thresholds match the paper exactly. analytical

  1. The discrete law is the Laplace density at the integers. For integer orders, Bayes' rule uses only density ratios, and these equal the paper's. So the top posterior is the paper's M = 1/(1 + e−2/b) = 0.731059. The ceiling solves the paper's own Br(M) = cH, so rC = 3.592658519.
  2. Under no trade every flow has posterior 1/2, whatever the noise law. A unit deviation earns ρΔT/2 − k, the paper's (A.12). So rN = 𝔯(2k/ρ) = 1.747877538. The bound 𝔯(k) = 1.220997512 matches for the same reason. Here 𝔯(d) is the root above ℓ of ΔT(r) = d.

What does not transfer.

  • The uniqueness bound rU: 2.124148 against the paper's 2.837417. Unit orders need no factor 1 − 1/b.
  • Entry and ownership at r1: 0.569293 and 0.358422 against 0.522757 and 0.324192. The mass with μ ≥ τ is one atom, Pr(X ≥ 1) = 0.425725.
  • The fixed-experiment control: flat at 0.569293, against 0.562178 → 0.522757.
  • The partial-sale pattern (1, −vj) at 1.55 to 1.65.

Buy-only orders

The buy-only discrete-Laplace base gives the same three parts, with no belief rule. analytical

  • No trade is unique at r0 = 1.2, because K ≤ (1 − π)ΔT(r0) = 0.008333 < k for every purchase rate.
  • Full purchases are unique at r1 = 3, because K ≥ ρΛΔT(r1) = 0.078335 > k.
  • Entry is 0.25 → 0.625 → 0.25 at (1.2, 3, 4.2). High-class ownership is 0.125 → 0.358422 → 0.125.

The cost is the parameter range. The cH window is (4.804167, 5.312161], and the ceiling is rC = 3.986097. The paper's cH = 6 recruits nobody at any r > 1, because Br(M1) < 6 there. The window (5.121880, 5.312161] keeps the paper's strengths (1.2, 3, 3.6).

Feedback is present. At r1 a purchase nets +0.175836 when the high-cost type enters after good news, and +0.058335 when entry stays at ρ. The brief's +0.060 for the second number was wrong. confirmed

Three-point noise

With equal three-point noise, one exact cutoff decides trade: a purchase pays if and only if k < K = (2/3)ρ(1 − π)ΔT, which is ρΔT/3 at π = 1/2. At k = 0.02 trade starts at r = 1.973212. Entry rises from 0.25 to 0.375, against the paper's 0.523. It returns to ρ only above rC(1) = 7.968627, where gH(r) = cH. So part (iii) fails at r2 = 3.6. Existence needs the directional completion: posterior one at the unused top flow. analytical

4The Proposition 3 analog: coexistence

The paper's Proposition 3 rests on computer-assisted certificates at r = 1.55, 1.60 and 1.65. There no trade coexists with informative equilibria, and entry rises with strength across them (0.545053, 0.548756, 0.551361). The discrete-Laplace bases give the coexistence part in closed form. No discrete base gives the upward order at those three strengths.

Buy-only discrete-Laplace, cH = 5

  • No trade and full purchases are both equilibria exactly on [rF, rN] = [1.443322, 1.747878]. This holds under every pair of tie rules; if the investor chooses zero at indifference, the left end is open. analytical
  • The feedback condition is (1 + ρ)a/(1 + a)2 > ρ/2, that is 0.2938 against 0.125. The margin is large.
  • Under challenger mixtures (CM) there is exactly one more outcome, a partial purchase rate s*. Entry is strictly ordered within each strength: no trade < s* < full purchases. analytical
  • The paper's three strengths lie inside the interval: two outcomes under the paper's tie rule (an indifferent challenger prepares), three under challenger mixtures (CM).
  • Entry rises across strengths only on the branch ŝ, on (1.405430, 1.443322), just below rF. That branch coexists with no trade, not with full purchases. analytical (local); its left end is a numerical diagnostic
  • At 1.55, 1.60 and 1.65, entry is flat at 0.625 on the full branch. On the s* branch it falls: 0.366, 0.329, 0.299.

Two-sided discrete-Laplace, cH = 6

  • No trade and full orders both exist exactly when ΔT(r) ∈ [k/cT, 2k/ρ], with cT = 0.227825 > ρ/2 = 0.125. At k = 0.02 this is r ∈ [1.515898, 1.747878], which contains 1.55, 1.60 and 1.65. analytical
  • Feedback creates the overlap. Without recruitment the full-order coefficient would be 0.102271 < 0.125, and the two ranges would not meet.
  • Full-order entry is flat at 0.569293, so the upward order is not reproduced. The mixed branches are known only on a grid. Full buys with partial sells exist on (1.515898, 1.533807), and there entry rises. A second such branch, with entry ρ, lies on (1.819330, 1.840115). numerical diagnostic

Unequal three-point noise with mixtures at indifference (C9)

  • Noise probabilities (2/5, 2/5, 1/5), cH = 6, k = 0.02. On [63/40, 317/200] = [1.575, 1.585] there are exactly three outcomes: no trade and two informative outcomes. analytical
  • On one branch entry rises: 0.526900 < 0.535188 < 0.543596 at r = 1.575, 1.58 and 1.585. analytical
  • The scope is narrow. The branch lives on (1.574345, 1.588760], width 0.0144. The investor mixes at zero profit. The top flow still reveals class H. The result needs the directional completion, and under the paper's tie rule (an indifferent challenger prepares) only two outcomes remain. overclaimed as "ordered equilibria recovered".

Suggested footnote at Proposition 3.

The coexistence of no trade with full orders has a closed-form proof in the discrete companion, which supports the computer-assisted certificates. Feedback raises the full-order payoff coefficient cT = 0.227825 above the no-trade coefficient ρ/2 = 0.125. So both outcomes exist exactly for ΔT(r) ∈ [k/cT, 2k/ρ]. In the buy-only variant the condition reads (1 + ρ)a/(1 + a)2 > ρ/2.

5The modifications, ranked

Each modification changes the discrete-Laplace base, unless the card names the three-point base. The rank uses four criteria:

  • what the modification recovers from the paper, or what new result it creates;
  • its status after the checks;
  • its robustness to tie rules, unused-flow beliefs, cL and parameters;
  • its simplicity.
  1. 1

    Two-sided unit orders with geometric-count noise (C1)

    Mechanism. The sale after bad news restores the paper's two-unit likelihood span, so the posterior bounds are the paper's m, M.

    Key numbers. Entry 0.569293; rC = 3.592659; rN = 1.747878.

    Recovers. Prop. 2(i) to (iii) at the paper's own cH = 6 and strengths; coexistence on [1.516, 1.748]; the direction "the buyer is the strong side".

    Status. analytical for the corners; numerical diagnostic for the branches.

    Robustness. The corner results hold under both tie rules. No flow is unused. The floor holds at cL = 1 (B3(m) = 2.37).

    Caveat. No (1, −v) at 1.55 to 1.65; full-order entry is flat; the full mixed correspondence is open.

  2. 2

    Two trading rounds before entry, with fresh investors (C2)

    Mechanism. A second binary signal widens the belief range to [m2, M2] = [0.269, 0.731].

    Key numbers. Coexistence at (r, k) = (1.7, 0.02) with cH = 6; entry 0.25 → 0.448747 → 0.25 at (1.2, 3, 3.6).

    Recovers. Prop. 2 at cH = 6 and the paper's strengths; coexistence at the original costs; a price path that falls without news.

    Status. analytical; computer-assisted at the benchmark.

    Robustness. No belief rule; it holds in both designs of the exercise; at cL = 1 the equilibrium path is the same.

    Caveat. Two units of capacity do part of the work: M2 equals the paper's M. The ceiling margin at 3.6 is 0.0027.

  3. 3

    Costly information acquisition by the investor (C6)

    Mechanism. Recruited entry raises the private value of information, so research and entry are complements.

    Key numbers. At r = 3, no research and full research coexist for research costs κ ∈ [0.031667, 0.087918]. At κ = 0.02, entry is 0.25 → 0.625 → 0.25 at (1.2, 3, 4.2).

    Creates. Coexistence at the strong benchmark; "competition creates research", in local form.

    Status. analytical

    Robustness. Challenger mixtures add a third equilibrium. The barrier at cH = 6 needs b > 1.9576. No belief rule.

    Caveat. The feedback is the base model's feedback in another coordinate. Research can lower welfare.

  4. 4

    Unequal three-point noise with mixtures at indifference (C9, three-point base)

    Mechanism. A non-uniform law makes a camouflage flow informative, so the purchase payoff moves with the purchase rate.

    Key numbers. Exactly three outcomes on [1.575, 1.585]; entry 0.5269 < 0.5352 < 0.5436.

    Creates. Coexistence and an upward entry branch in the three-point base; existence for every parameter vector.

    Status. analytical

    Robustness. Needs the directional completion; one informative branch needs challenger mixtures; the window is 0.0144 wide.

    Caveat. A mixture at zero profit, not an optimal order size. The top flow still reveals class H.

  5. 5

    An option to delay preparation (C3)

    Mechanism. A second price screens entry. With discrete-Laplace noise the challenger needs two good prices.

    Key numbers. Discrete Laplace at discount factor δ = 0.95: entry 0.625 → 0.448747 at r = 3, ceiling 3.986 → 5.606. Three-point: entry 3/8 → 11/24.

    Creates. Coexistence in a cost window; a delay result that is not monotone; a ceiling further out.

    Status. analytical after the checks.

    Robustness. Needs private preparation decisions and δ > 0.899. The closed plateau endpoints were wrong.

    Caveat. The sign of the entry effect flips across the two bases. The coexistence example uses a trading cost other than the benchmark k.

  6. 6

    An informed investor with a stake (C5, both bases)

    Mechanism. A holder gains from entry in both states, so class L may buy to recruit.

    Key numbers. Prop. 2 holds if and only if the stake ω < 0.1805 (three-point) or 0.1878 (discrete Laplace). In the three-point base, class L buys from ω = 2.027, and no equilibrium exists above 2.599 under the paper's rules.

    Creates. Exact robustness bounds for Prop. 2; new mixed branches; coexistence in the three-point base.

    Status. analytical; computer-assisted

    Robustness. The weak-economy bound depends on the directional belief. Challenger mixtures repair the nonexistence.

    Caveat. Manipulation destroys 96.7% of the information surplus. The claim of a "rent transfer" is wrong.

  7. 7

    The incumbent bids after the price (C7)

    Mechanism. After good news, strong incumbent types bid just enough to deter the high-cost challenger.

    Key numbers. Entry 0.625 → 0.366988; a transfer of 0.171896 to the incumbent.

    Creates. A weaker form of the mechanism; a pure-transfer result; new mixed branches.

    Status. analytical under rule IC-P and a selection; numerical diagnostic for the branches.

    Robustness. Two selections. The boundary tie differs from the paper's. At cH = 5 a second weak-economy equilibrium reverses the rise.

    Caveat. The belief completion is an extra assumption. The claim of silence by every type was wrong.

  8. 8

    A public toehold for the challenger (C10)

    Mechanism. A stake s adds s(wL + ΔTμ) to the entry gain.

    Key numbers. The cH window widens from 0.508 to 0.602 at s = 0.2; rC(0.1) = 4.395.

    Recovers. A wider Prop. 2 window; a ceiling further out.

    Status. numerical diagnostic with fixed bids.

    Robustness. Bids are held truthful. Under optimal bids ΔT falls by 17% at s = 0.1.

    Caveat. A partial-equilibrium result only.

7Recommendation for the paper

The Laplace case stays the benchmark. Nothing here changes the paper's main text. Three additions are worth the author's time, and the rest should stay out.

Put in the paper

  1. One remark after Proposition 2 or in Section 5.1, five to eight lines. State the discrete companion. Orders are units in {−1, 0, 1}. Noise is the difference of two independent geometric counts of unit orders, with scale a = e−1/b. Give its exact matches and what does not transfer. Put the geometric-count story for the noise beside the noise justification of Section 2.3.
  2. One online-appendix section, two to three pages. Give Proposition C1.1 with conditions (D1) to (D3) and its proof. Give the coexistence result on [1.515898, 1.747878] with its closed-form endpoints. Give the exact-match statement with its two reasons, both tie rules, and the fact that no flow is unused.
    • Add the buy-only variant as one paragraph: its window (4.804167, 5.312161], its ceiling 3.986097, and its analytical Proposition 3 analog. Mention the third outcome under challenger mixtures.
    • State that the two-sided mixed branches are numerical diagnostics, and that the upward entry order at 1.55 to 1.65 is not reproduced.
  3. One footnote at Proposition 3. The coexistence of no trade with full orders has a closed-form proof in the discrete companion (text in Section 4 above).

A possible text for the remark. This draft uses only the results above; the author should adapt it.

Remark (a discrete companion). Let orders take values in {−1, 0, 1}. Let noise be the difference of two independent geometric counts of unit orders, so that Pr(Z = z) ∝ a|z| with a = e−1/b. At the benchmark the posterior bounds are m and M, the ceiling is rC = 3.592659, and the no-trade boundary is rN = 1.747878. All four equal the Laplace values. Entry rises from 0.25 to 0.569293 and returns to 0.25. The proof needs no derivative bound, because the investor's choice is one comparison. The uniqueness bound rU, the entry levels and the order pattern (1, −v) do not transfer.

Keep out

  • The reserve set after the price. It is a hold-up result that rests on cL ≤ 0.1 and a tie rule, and it is not the paper's question.
  • Target disclosure. It answers a different question. At most, one sentence in the conclusion: the mechanism matters when the target cannot certify fit.
  • The stake, the incumbent's response and sequential entry. Each rests on a belief completion or a selection, and each changes the question.
  • The three-point model. It is dominated as a companion.

Two extensions that merit further work

  • Costly information acquisition (C6). The result is analytical and clean. No research and full research coexist on a cost interval at the strong benchmark. A stronger incumbent raises the private return to research within an entry regime. It connects the paper to the verified record of Dow, Goldstein and Guembel (2017). Open: the welfare sign and the tie rule.
  • Two trading rounds (C2). It recovers Prop. 2 and coexistence at the paper's own costs, and it adds a testable price-path implication. Before any use, repeat the exercise with a fixed cumulative purchase limit, to remove the capacity confound.

A caution on parameters. Any buy-only discrete statement needs cH ∈ (4.804167, 5.312161]. In the buy-only base the paper's cH = 6 recruits nobody when b > 1.9576, which includes b = 2. Any two-sided discrete statement keeps cH = 6.

9How the work was done and checked, and the memos

  • Phase 1, four workstreams. A: the three-point model, by a full characterization (A1) and a brute-force enumeration of all pure and mixed equilibria (A2). B: the discrete-Laplace model, by a numerical verification of the brief's claims (B1) and the theory (B2). C: ten modifications, C1 to C10. D: the literature, from verified records only.
  • Phase 2, independent checks. A1 and A2 checked each other, and so did B1 and B2. Each modification got an independent check by another member (X_C1 to X_C10). The checker reproduced or refuted each headline claim and did not explore further.
  • Phase 3, one red-team pass. It attacked the headline claims of all 25 memos in 76 verdict rows. It re-derived the key steps and recomputed the key numbers with five print-only scripts. These use exact fractions where the inputs are rational, and 30 to 40 digits otherwise. The synthesis then fixed the verdicts, the survival table and the rank.
