## 3. Patterns

1. **A hand-built filing dataset is no longer rare in sale-process work.** In six papers above, five use the "Background of the Merger" text or similar filing text (Gentry-Stroup, Liu-Officer-Tu, Boone et al., Eckbo et al. on initiation, Gorbenko-Green-Mirani). The extraction is a cost to pay, not a moat. By 2029 the field will have several such datasets. The moat must be the question and the model.
2. **A close competitor is inside the supervision team.** "Deal Terms in Takeovers" has hand-built offer-level data and a structural model. Any solo paper on the same offer-level data needs a clearly different object.
3. **Theory wins at the JF on process design.** Chen-Wang (2023), Gorbenko-Malenko (2024) and Daley-Geelen-Green (2024) are theory papers. Zhe Wang's job-market paper was theory. These papers have a sharp mechanism and little or no data. The JF accepts a solo theory paper on process design. This supports CCC as a candidate. It does not show that such a paper places well. I found no placement evidence beyond Zhe Wang at Penn State.
4. **Structural papers need a mechanism that data can identify.** Gentry-Stroup and Dimopoulos-Sacchetto estimate entry. They answer one counterfactual: auction against negotiation. This question is now crowded.
5. **Trading before deals wins with an institutional hook.** The strong papers add a source of information or a legal event: advisor banks (RFS 2019), SEC cases (RFS 2019, JF 2024), trading networks (JFE 2017). Papers that only show abnormal volume before announcements are old and crowded.
6. **A policy hook is present in most winners.** Examples: deal protections, insider-trading enforcement, advisor conflicts, disclosure.
7. **Saturated questions:** who starts a deal (Eckbo group, three papers); auction against negotiation as a revenue comparison; payment method and opportunism; abnormal option volume at the announcement date; first-bid strength.
8. **Open questions** (my judgment, not verified by a full literature search): what happens inside the private phase at the date level (who is contacted, who signs a confidentiality agreement, when information leaks); how post-signing prices reveal the chance of a rival bid; legal rules that change entry (standstills, deadlines); sellers' outreach choices.
9. **Job-market evidence** is thin (section 1). Of about 20 candidates on the lists I could open, none wrote on sale processes.

## 5. CCC in this picture

CCC fits the pattern in point 3: solo theory on process design that the JF has accepted. It is closest to Betton-Eckbo-Thompson-Thorburn (2014, unverified), Edmans-Goldstein-Jiang (2012) and Dessaint et al. (2019), and it adds the entry channel. The scan gives no evidence that a theory-only paper places better than a paper with a new empirical fact. I see CCC as a second paper, or as the model inside a paper that also tests it (G7 or G2). It is a job-market paper only if an empirical test of the price-to-entry channel exists. A reduced-form test of this channel is the missing piece in everything I saw.
