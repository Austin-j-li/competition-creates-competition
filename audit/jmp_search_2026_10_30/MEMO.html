Finding the job-market paper: results of the search

30 October 2026. Market: fall 2029, business schools worldwide, a solo paper aimed at JF, RFS or JFE.

1. The answer

Job-market paper: the price of one more party under confidentiality (process leaks). A board that brings one more party under a confidentiality agreement puts part of the sale into the stock and option markets. The paper measures that step, party by party, on the private dates the market cannot see. It then asks whether boards respond by narrowing the sale, or widen it because the run-up recruits bidders.

The reasons:

  • It is solo on its face. It is far from Alex's projects. Neither the joint informal-bids paper nor "Deal Terms in Takeovers" uses returns or options.
  • It uses tools that no other student with the census has. These are options and microstructure data, plus the price-formation model that the current paper already built.
  • The first kill test is cheap. It takes two days on CRSP and Alex's 404 hand-coded deals.

Second paper: "Competition Creates Competition" (CCC), finished as theory and submitted in 2027. CCC and the leak paper form one research statement: what the market learns from a private sale, and what the sale learns from the market. CCC shows that a price before a deal can recruit a rival. The leak paper measures what the sale puts into the price, and whether the sale then contracts or expands. The sign of that response is the empirical face of CCC's recruitment channel.

Reserves, in order:

  1. Exit cascades: how many bidders are really in a takeover auction? This is the only shortlisted idea with no verified predecessor. It needs no market data.
  2. Seller commitment: what a soft final round costs before the first bid. This is a strong question, but only with Alex's written agreement on scope. His voice notes already assign deadlines and "limited commitment power" to the joint data, and "Deal Terms in Takeovers" already varies the commitment assumption. Without his consent, it becomes the commitment chapter of the joint paper.

Decision gate: 31 March 2027. If the leak paper fails its first two kill tests, promote exit cascades or an upgraded CCC.

2. What the search learned

  • Agreement among the agents was partly an echo. Six of seven sources picked seller commitment. All of them read one paragraph of the dossier in which Alex names deadline revisions as evidence of limited commitment. The novelty check then found that "Deal Terms in Takeovers" (March 2026 draft, page 13 and Appendix B.2) already separates final rounds from revision rounds and varies the commitment assumption. A 2026 structural study of judicial sales already estimates limited commitment (read from a conference abstract only).
  • Lining up NDA dates with the market is not new by itself. Fidrmuc and Xia (2022) already line up first-NDA dates with insiders' trades and returns. The open object is narrower: the marginal party, the option market and the board's response.
  • The close competitor sits in the supervision team. "Deal Terms in Takeovers" covers 673 mergers (820 in the 30 October seminar version), every formal offer, and the terms of exclusivity, diligence, financing and regulation. Any idea about offer terms or the choice among submitted offers overlaps it.
  • Filing data are not a moat. At least five recent papers build data from background sections: Gentry and Stroup; Liu, Officer and Tu; Boone et al.; Eckbo, Norli and Thorburn; Gorbenko, Green and Mirani. The question and the model must carry the paper.
  • Counts from Alex's hand-coded workbook differ slightly between reads (390 to 404 deals; 81 or 82 final-round markers), because the agents keyed deals in different ways. This memo uses 404 deals and 81 markers.
  • The census frame is about 5,400 completed sales of public targets with a background section, 2001 to 2025, or about 220 a year. The count comes from EDGAR form indexes with a deregistration filter.
  • Every source calls CCC a second paper. A referee will ask one thing first. The paper's own reserve comparison shows that a seller can raise the reserve and erase the reversal, so the seller's optimal terms are the open theory step.

4. The pitches

4.1 Job-market paper: the price of one more party under confidentiality

Question. When a board brings one more party under a confidentiality agreement, how much of the sale reaches the stock and option markets? Does the board invite fewer parties where that information reaches the market faster?

Why business schools care.

  • A large part of the takeover premium appears before the announcement.
  • Insider-trading enforcement centres on takeovers.
  • Boards and bankers weigh "leak risk" against competition with no evidence.
  • The United Kingdom forces an announcement after a leak (Takeover Code, Rule 2), and the United States does not. No one can say which rule gives target shareholders more.

The new fact. Abnormal turnover, drift and call-option demand step up in the days after each wave of confidentiality agreements, weeks before any public news. The step grows with the number of new parties and with their type: a financial sponsor brings lenders and co-investors. A design result follows, with its sign left open by the theory:

  • boards sell liquid, option-listed targets through shorter lists and earlier exclusivity; or
  • they sell them through longer lists, because the run-up recruits.

Either sign makes a paper.

Model. The board chooses how many parties to invite, of which type, and when. Each party adds competition and a hazard that the sale reaches the market. A tip pays only above a trading cost or a detection risk, so leaks reach the price faster in liquid, option-listed targets. A leak works through two channels:

  • It repels. The run-up raises the price that a later bid must beat, so a bidder that faces it asks for secrecy, a short list and exclusivity.
  • It recruits. The price tells outsiders about their own fit with the target, as in CCC.

The model states which condition gives which sign. Key primitives:

  • the leak hazard per party type;
  • the pass-through of a run-up caused by the process into later bids;
  • the entry response of outsiders;
  • the value distributions and entry costs.

Data.

  • The census of sale processes: exact dates of NDA cohorts, counts and types, contacts, bids and exclusivity.
  • One new field: press reports and statements about "unusual trading" in the background text.
  • Prices: CRSP daily data, with TAQ if access exists.
  • Options: single-stock options from OptionMetrics on WRDS, or ThetaData. The ThetaData root list holds 19 of 20 checked targets; the history and the entitlement are not confirmed.
  • Validation: SEC insider-trading litigation releases.
  • Pilot: Alex's 404 hand-coded deals. 69% of their NDA and bid rows carry an exact date, and the median confidential window is 223 days.

Identification.

  • The dose–response. It uses the private calendar. The board and its bank set the dates, and the market cannot see them until the proxy is filed. Compare the days after each cohort with shifted placebo dates in the same deal and with matched firms on the same dates.
  • The breadth response. It uses variation in the cost of trading on a tip within size and industry cells: whether options are listed, option spreads, and the intensity of enforcement.
  • Incidence. It splits each bidder's revision into the part that tracks public news and the part that tracks the run-up caused by the process.

Structural flag: yes, as a second stage. Estimate the leak hazard per party type, the pass-through and the entry response. Use a Bayesian likelihood with the HMC tools. The counterfactuals:

  1. a forced announcement after an unexplained price move, as in the UK;
  2. perfect confidentiality;
  3. the seller's optimal number of parties with and without leaks.

Each one reports the change in target shareholder value and the transfer from early sellers to informed traders.

The question no one else with the same data would ask. On the day the fifth confidentiality agreement was signed, a day the market could not see, did the price move? And did the winner later pay for that move?

Closest papers.

  • Fidrmuc and Xia (2022, European Financial Management): first-NDA dates against insiders' own trades. This paper studies the marginal party, the market and the board's response.
  • Acharya and Johnson (2010, JFE): more finance participants, more suspicious trading in buyouts. They count syndicates; this paper follows the time path of the rival parties.
  • Augustin, Brenner and Subrahmanyam (2019, Management Science): informed options trading before announcements, timed from the announcement.
  • Szydlowski, "Leaks and Takeovers" (working paper): targets leak to recruit bidders. It gives the theory channel; this paper measures it.
  • Liu and Officer (working paper): private bid revisions track the target's public price.

Main risk. Leaks may cluster at the end of the process, when lawyers, boards and lenders join. The stage profile would then show one spike, and day-level dates may be too coarse. Single-stock options may not be available. Bids may track the "unaffected" price that fairness opinions use. In that case the repelling channel is weak, and the paper becomes a paper about recruitment.

Kill tests, cheapest first.

  1. One to two days, after a WRDS login. On the 404 deals, compare abnormal turnover and returns in the five days after each exact-dated NDA wave with placebo dates. Test whether the step grows with the size of the wave. No step: stop.
  2. One week. Confirm single-stock option history, from OptionMetrics or the ThetaData entitlement. No options: the paper runs on stock data only.
  3. One month. Test whether the number of parties falls, or rises, with option listing and liquidity. No relation in either direction: the paper is a measurement paper, more suited to JFQA than to JF.
  4. One month. Check whether the hand-coded bids track the current price or the unaffected price.

Timeline. Kill tests from November 2026 to March 2027, with the go/no-go on 31 March 2027. A short model of the breadth choice in spring 2027. The census, with the leak fields, in 2027. The main facts and a first draft by June 2028. The structural step and the counterfactuals by December 2028. Seminars in spring 2029, and the market version in summer 2029.

4.2 Second paper: CCC, with the seller's terms and one test

Question. When a board chooses its sale terms, and bidders and traders respond, does stronger competition still create competition?

What to add. The paper's own reserve comparison shows a reserve that erases the reversal. The strongest version gives the seller's choice of terms with the full pricing and trading continuation, which the conclusion names as the next step. Add one test, in words: in sales that are public before rivals prepare, later entry rises with incumbent strength; in confidential sales, it falls.

Kill tests.

  1. Two to four weeks of theory. Does the reversal survive a seller who sets terms with the continuation in view? If not, CCC stays a theory paper with a stated limit.
  2. One month. Count the sales in the census frame that were public before rivals prepared, with later rival entry and a public measure of incumbent strength. If there are fewer than a few hundred, the test is a section, not a result.

Target. Submit to JF or RFS during 2027, so that a revise-and-resubmit can exist by fall 2029.

Full plan: ccc_seller_terms.md. In the example, a seller who commits to its best reserve posts a price near the high class's value. Proceeds are then about 1.25 at every strength, and no high-cost rival prepares. So the headline becomes: competition creates competition when the seller cannot commit to walk away.

4.3 Reserve 1: exit cascades. How many bidders are really in a takeover auction?

Question. How much of the competition that a board reports to a court is still alive on the day of the final bid?

The new fact. Exits cluster at shared diligence steps far more than independent exit choices would produce. The stated reasons within a cluster name one shared cause. The premium tracks the number of bidders still alive after the first shared step, not the number who signed. The design corollary: a seller that releases the worst fact in stages keeps more bidders to the bid stage.

Model. Each bidder has a private synergy. All bidders face one shared fact about the target, which the data room reveals in stages, and staying at each stage costs money. A bad shared fact sends out every bidder below one synergy line on the same date. The survivor then bids knowing two things: the shared fact is bad, and no rival could carry it.

What the paper must not claim.

  • That survivors respond to news of a rival's exit. The date on which they learned of it does not exist in the filings.
  • That the seller's choice of information access is new theory. That theory is Cusumano and Pieroth (2024).

The contribution is the measured cascade and its effect on price.

Data. Exit dates, exit reasons and entry dates from the census. One cheap new field: the dates when data-room access opened and when management meetings took place.

Kill tests.

  1. One week. On the hand-coded set, compare the time clustering of exits with a bound from independent exits, then repeat on the exact-dated rows. Clustering that vanishes with exact dates: stop.
  2. Two weeks. Do the reasons within a cluster name shared causes, while lone exits name causes specific to the bidder?
  3. One month. Add access and meeting dates for 100 deals. Do the clusters follow them?

Closest papers. Milgrom and Weber (1982); Gorbenko and Malenko (2014); Liu, Officer and Tu (2023); Daley, Geelen and Green (2024, JF); Cusumano and Pieroth (2024).

4.4 Reserve 2: seller commitment. What does a soft final round cost before the first bid?

Precondition. Agree a written boundary with Alex before any work:

  • the solo paper owns the anticipated continuation rule and the preparation that bidders skip;
  • the joint paper owns informal-bid selection;
  • "Deal Terms in Takeovers" owns the valuation of offer conditions.

Question. How much competition never forms because bidders expect the seller to reopen a round that it called final?

The result to aim at. The count of reopened rounds is not the result: "Deal Terms" and the pilot already show that rounds reopen. The result is a holdback fact. Where the adviser has a record of reopened rounds, final-round bids sit lower relative to earlier informal bids, and later raises are larger. This holds within size, industry and year, and within the same adviser over time. The structural step then prices commitment: proceeds under the observed discretion against proceeds under a deadline that binds, with bidder preparation free to adjust.

Main theoretical risk. With symmetric private values, a soft final round costs the seller nothing. The loss must come from asymmetric bidder types, a lost reserve, or preparation that bidders skip.

Pilot. On the 81 hand-coded deals with a final-round marker, two or more bidders raised after the final round in 15 deals, about 19%.

Kill tests.

  1. Alex's consent.
  2. One week. Confirm that those 15 deals are reopened contests, not negotiations with the winner only.
  3. Two weeks. Audit 50 filings for evidence of readiness before a deadline.
  4. After 200 census deals. Find the share of final rounds that reopen, and the share of that variation that the adviser explains.
