## 2. The four stress tests

**A. Seller commitment.** (1) Overlap: the worst on the list. Alex's voice notes name deadline revisions as evidence of limited commitment and assign the deadline fields to the joint data. Deal Terms separates a deadline-based final round from a revision round and toggles the commitment assumption in Appendix B.2; its sample now reaches 820 deals through 2022. The joint informal-bids model also assumes a first-price formal stage, and A tests that assumption. (2) Key variable: the bidders' expectation that the seller will reopen is not in any filing. Observed reopening is. The pilot fact, that 56 of 64 winners signed above their first final-round bid, is also what a sealed round followed by a bilateral negotiation with the winner produces, so it is not evidence of noncommitment. The stronger pilot fact is that two or more bidders raised after the final round in 15 of 81 marked deals, about 19%, which is a reopened contest and clears the 10% threshold that Opus B and Opus C set. The belief would be identified only through the model's functional form or through an adviser record that is itself a choice. (3) Standard theory: with symmetric private values a soft final round is an open auction, and revenue equivalence makes it free. The sealed-against-open ranking with asymmetric bidders is not sign-determined. The first-order loss must come from preparation that bidders skip because they expect another round, and that lost preparation is the unobservable in (2). Liu, Luo and Xiao already estimate a limited-commitment auction (conference abstract only), so "first structural estimate" is gone. (4) Appeal: six of seven sources picked A, and the dossier names it twice. The vote count is a dossier effect.

**B. Process leaks.** (1) Overlap: low. Alex's projects use bids and selection, not market data; Deal Terms has no return or option variable. (2) Key variable: the event dates exist (69% of NDA and bid rows carry an exact date in the reference set). The stock data exist. Single-stock option history is unverified; the ThetaData root list covers 19 of 20 checked targets, and the entitlement and the start of the history are unknown. The leak date itself does not exist, but the design does not need it: it needs the event date and the market response. (3) Standard theory: in a one-period Kyle market more noise trade does not change price informativeness, so "liquid targets leak more" needs a fixed cost or a detection risk per tipped trader. The floor channel, that a run-up raises the price a later bidder must beat, needs bids that track the current price. Liu and Officer show private bid revisions track the target's market value. Against that, fairness opinions and boards benchmark to an "unaffected" price that nets out rumour run-up (institutional practice, unverified here). If bids track the unaffected price, the floor channel is weak, and a leak narrows a sale only through rival recruitment, which is Szydlowski's channel (author's summary only) and CCC's, and runs the other way. The sign of the breadth response is therefore an empirical question, and the paper should present it as one. (4) Appeal: leakage is one of Austin's three stated preferences, and the fit criterion rewards the options skills. Both pull votes toward B apart from the question. The question stands anyway: no verified paper links the full sequence of NDA dates to option activity, and none shows a board breadth response.

**C. Standstills.** (1) Overlap: none; Deal Terms has no standstill field. (2) Key variable: standstill and "don't ask, don't waive" terms sit in confidentiality agreements that are not filed. Proxies mention waiver requests in some deals. A causal design needs the clause before the bid, and that clause is mostly unobserved. (3) Standard theory: a standstill is a commitment device, so C is A with a contract instead of a deadline. The first-order condition is that early effort responds to the right to return, and returns after signing are about 5% of deals, so the sample of returns is thin. (4) Appeal: the core brief names standstills as a preferred topic. Moderate dossier effect.

**D. Informative exits.** Two versions exist. The Astra version, in which a survivor responds to news of a rival's exit, needs the date the survivor learned of the exit, which is the brief's own example of a variable that does not exist. The Muse version, in which exits cluster at shared diligence steps, needs exit dates, exit reasons and access dates, which the filings give. I rank the Muse version. (1) Overlap: Alex's notes call the first approacher's exit informative and use exit reasons to bound values, so the object sits near the joint data, but the joint question is informal-bid selection. (2) Key variable: exact exit dates exist for most rows; the date access opened is a new but cheap field. (3) Standard theory: common-value dropout is Milgrom and Weber, and the seller's choice of information access is Cusumano and Pieroth. The effect is first-order only if the common component of target value is large relative to private synergies; Gorbenko and Malenko find dispersed strategic synergies, so this is unknown. (4) Appeal: Astra and Muse picked it, and the dossier names informative exits. Moderate dossier effect.

**E. The price after exclusivity.** (1) Overlap: Deal Terms records exclusivity requests and revised offers; the joint model has a diligence update to the formal bid. Moderate. (2) Key variable: the exclusivity grant date and the winner's last bid before it exist in many filings. The variable passes. (3) Standard theory: a buyer with a decayed outside option on the other side can cut the price; a rational seller prices that in before the grant, so the ex ante loss needs private information about diligence findings and no commitment. Three sources point against the headline fact: the pilot (winners mostly signed above their first final-round bid), Liu, Officer and Tu (17% of revisions cut the price) and Higbee et al. (headline prices are sticky). (4) Appeal: the corridor story carries the votes. The story meets contrary evidence.

**F. Antitrust selects the buyer.** (1) Overlap: Deal Terms values regulatory conditions in the target's choice among offers. The solo version must sit before the offer. (2) Key variable: the buyer who declined or was excluded for antitrust reasons before any offer. Filings record this in a minority of deals. Losing bidders are anonymous, so a product-overlap exposure measure cannot be built for them. This is Astra's own kill condition. (3) Standard theory: merger choice under policy is Nocke and Whinston. The effect is first-order where review is long and costly, which is the largest deals; most public targets clear early. (4) Appeal: policy salience in 2021 to 2025. Salience may fade by 2029.

**H. The proxy reveals the auction.** (1) Overlap: Deal Terms records post-announcement bids; Alex wants announcement returns related to process features. Moderate. (2) Key variable: the first filing date with a background section, the runner-up gap and the arbitrage spread all exist. (3) Standard theory: the market updates on disclosed competition. The effect is small by construction: a two-point change in topping probability times a 10% bump moves the price by about 0.2%, and topping bids occur in about 5% of deals. It is also not CCC's empirical home: CCC's rival learns its own class from the price before it pays to prepare, while a disclosed runner-up already did diligence and knows its value. (4) Appeal: practicality, not the question.

**I. The private-equity floor.** (1) Overlap: Gorbenko and Malenko (2014) relate credit spreads to financial-bidder valuations with losing bids; Gorbenko (2019) estimates participation by type. The core brief says a repeat of these on new data is weak. (2) Key variable: bidder type, dated bids by round and credit spreads all exist. The variable passes. (3) Standard theory: the winner pays the runner-up's value, and the runner-up's value moves with credit. This is auction logic plus the 2014 estimate. First-order only when a fund is the runner-up in a strategic sale: 65 of 224 pilot deals had a financial bidder with any bid, 16 with a formal bid. (4) Appeal: the "credit reaches corporate control" frame. It is a frame.

**J. Competition for the record.** (1) Overlap: none. (2) Key variable: a bidder's genuine chance to bid, built from access dates, time before deadlines and refused requests. The filing is the record, and the claim is that the record is dressed. Court files and bidder-side disclosures exist for a few deals only. (3) Standard theory: a board produces evidence for a reviewer that cannot see quality. First-order if boards hold private benefits from a preferred buyer; plausible, hard to show. (4) Appeal: the governance hook.

**R. CCC.** (1) Overlap: none; it is solo and Alex reacted to it as a reader. (2) Key variable: a public sale state with trade, an incumbent whose strength is public, and a rival's preparation date. Public sale announcements number about 1,000 in 1990 to 2014 (Zha Giedt, unverified); preparation dates are in the background sections. The informed trader's knowledge of the rival's class is unobservable, but the test uses entry against incumbent strength in public against confidential processes, which is observable. (3) Standard theory: the transfer of the class value gap from rival profit to target proceeds is new. The setting is a minority case, and the paper's own reserve comparison erases the reversal at one point. (4) Appeal: it is the candidate's own paper. To a committee it is a theory talk without a fact.

## 3. Picks for the final pitches

I pick four: B, A, D and CCC. I drop F because its key variable fails for the parties that matter most.

### B. Process leaks: the price of one more party under confidentiality

**Sharpest question.** When a board brings one more party under a confidentiality agreement, how much of the sale reaches the stock and option markets, and does the board invite fewer parties where that reaches them faster?

**The result that makes an editor read past the abstract.** A dose-response: abnormal turnover, drift and call-option demand step up in the days after each wave of confidentiality agreements, weeks before any public news, and the step grows with the number and type of new parties. Then the design result, with its sign left open: boards sell liquid, option-listed targets through shorter lists and earlier exclusivity, or they sell them through longer lists because the run-up recruits. Either sign is a paper. The model fixes which condition gives which sign: a leak repels when bids track the current price, and recruits when the price tells outsiders about their own fit, as in CCC.

**What the paper must not claim.** The stage profile from initiation to first agreement is Fidrmuc and Xia. The pass-through of the market price into private bids is Liu and Officer. The paper's objects are the marginal party, the option market and the board's response.

**Kill tests, cheapest first.**
1. One to two days, after Austin obtains a WRDS login: on the 404 hand-coded deals, compare abnormal turnover and returns in the five days after each exact-dated agreement wave with placebo dates in the same deal. Test whether the step grows with the wave size. No step means stop.
2. One week: confirm single-stock option history for those targets, through OptionMetrics on WRDS or the ThetaData entitlement. No options means the paper runs on stock data alone and loses its second measure.
3. One month: within size and industry cells, test whether the number of parties under agreement falls with option listing and liquidity. No relation in either direction means the paper is a measurement paper.
4. One month: check whether bids in the hand-coded set track the current or the unaffected price. Unaffected-price bids weaken the floor channel and point the model toward recruitment.

### A. Seller commitment: what a soft final round costs before the first bid

**Precondition.** A written boundary with Alex, before any work: the solo paper owns the anticipated continuation rule and lost preparation; the joint paper owns informal-bid selection; Deal Terms owns the valuation of offer conditions. Without this, A is not a candidate.

**Sharpest question.** How much competition never forms because bidders expect the seller to reopen a round it called final?

**The result that makes an editor read past the abstract.** Not the count of reopened rounds, which Deal Terms and the pilot already show. The result is a holdback fact: in processes run by advisers with a record of reopened rounds, final-round bids sit lower relative to earlier informal bids and later raises are larger, within target size, industry and year, and within the same adviser over time. The structural step then prices the commitment: proceeds under the observed discretion against proceeds under a deadline that binds, with preparation free to adjust.

**Kill tests, cheapest first.**
1. One conversation: Alex's consent and the written boundary.
2. One week: on the 81 pilot deals with a final-round marker, confirm that the 15 deals with raises by two or more bidders are reopened contests and not winner-only negotiations, and check how those 81 deals were chosen. Fewer than 8 confirmed contests mean the pilot fact is not noncommitment.
3. Two weeks: audit 50 filings for readiness evidence before a deadline (data-room access, bidder requests for time, refused late offers). If filings record calendars only, the belief cannot be separated from slow diligence; stop.
4. After 200 census deals: the share of final rounds that two or more bidders reopen, and the share of that variation the adviser explains. Below 10%, or near zero, stop.

### D. Exit cascades: how many bidders are really in a takeover auction?

**Sharpest question.** How much of the competition a board reports to a court was alive on the day of the final bid?

**The result that makes an editor read past the abstract.** Exits cluster at shared diligence steps far beyond what independent exit choices produce, the stated reasons in a cluster name one shared cause, and the premium tracks the count of bidders alive after the first shared step, not the count of signers. The design corollary: staged release of the worst fact keeps more bidders to the bid stage.

**What the paper must not claim.** That survivors respond to news of a rival's exit; that date does not exist. That the seller's information design is new theory; it is Cusumano and Pieroth. The contribution is the measured cascade and its price effect.

**Kill tests, cheapest first.**
1. One week: on the hand-coded set, compare the time clustering of exits with a bound from independent exits given each deal's entry dates. Repeat on exact-dated rows only. Clustering that vanishes with exact dates means stop.
2. Two weeks: test whether exit reasons in a cluster name shared causes (price, a business fact) and lone exits name own causes (financing, fit).
3. One month: add the date access opened and management-meeting dates for 100 deals and test whether clusters follow them.

### R. CCC: the seller's sale terms with the full continuation, plus one test

**Sharpest question.** When a board chooses its sale terms, and bidders and traders respond, does stronger competition still create competition?

**The result that makes an editor read past the abstract.** The paper's own Table 5 shows a reserve that erases the reversal at one point. The strongest version closes that gap: the seller's choice of terms with the full pricing and trade continuation, which the conclusion names as the next step. The known-extension list covers fixed comparisons of a reserve or a payment rule; the open item is the seller's optimal choice with bidders and the trader free to respond. Then one test in words: in sales that are public before rivals prepare, later entry rises with incumbent strength; in confidential sales it falls.

**Kill tests, cheapest first.**
1. Two to four weeks of theory: whether the reversal survives a seller who sets terms with the continuation in view. If it does not, CCC stays a theory paper with a stated limit.
2. One month: count public sale states in the census frame with later rival entry and public incumbent strength. Below a few hundred, the test is a section, not a result.

## 4. Portfolio view

**The job-market paper is B, process leaks.** It is far from Alex's projects, so it is solo on its face. It uses the tools no other student with the census has: options and microstructure data, and a price-formation model in the Dow and Gorton style that CCC already built. Its first kill test costs two days.

**The second paper is CCC.** It stays a theory paper for the Journal of Finance or a theory outlet. If it holds an R&R by 2029, it becomes the strongest single item on the CV, and that is a real possibility that no unbuilt idea offers.

**B and CCC form one research statement:** what the market learns from a private sale, and what the sale learns from the market. CCC shows that prices before a deal can recruit a rival. B measures what the sale puts into the price and whether boards narrow or widen the sale in response. The sign question in B is the empirical face of CCC's recruitment channel against the floor channel.

**The third paper is the joint informal-bids paper with Alex.** A is the natural solo spin-off if Alex cedes it; if not, A folds into the joint project as its commitment chapter, which is where Alex's notes put it. D is the alternative third solo paper from the same census, and it needs no market data.

**The methods paper with Sam Livingstone stays separate.** It supplies the estimator for the joint paper and for the structural step in B.

## 5. Disagreement with the synthesis

The synthesis ranks A first at 7.83 on practicality 6.5. Every stress test flags A: ownership, an unobserved belief, revenue equivalence in the static case, and a dossier-named appeal. I move A's practicality to 4.5, keep its overlap at −2, and apply the overlap column in both rankings, which Ranking 1 ignores; so A sits below B in both seats and leaves the lists without Alex's consent. The synthesis gives CCC practicality 5; the paper exists, so I move it to 8 and its placement to 6, which puts it third for the editor and fourth for the committee. Note 4 says H is CCC's cleanest empirical home; it is not, because CCC's rival learns its own class before it prepares and a disclosed runner-up already knows its value. I move H's originality from 7 to 5. The synthesis gives I originality 7; the credit-to-financial-bidder link is Gorbenko and Malenko (2014), so I move it to 5. For E, the novelty check adds Higbee et al. to the pilot and to Liu, Officer and Tu; I move E's importance from 7.5 to 6 because the probable fact is stickiness, which is explained. For B, the synthesis frames the moat as "no one has aligned NDA dates with trading"; Fidrmuc and Xia did, so the moat is the marginal party, the option market and the breadth response, and I keep B's originality at 8 on that narrower claim. The synthesis is right to drop L.
