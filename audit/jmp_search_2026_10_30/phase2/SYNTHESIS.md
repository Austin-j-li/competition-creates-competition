# Phase 2 synthesis: merged ideas, one scale, shortlist

30 October 2026. Inputs: the seven phase 1 files in `../phase1/` and the dossier. These scores are the synthesis scores. They are provisional until the novelty check and the adversarial ranking are complete.

## 1. How to read the scores

- **Scale.** Each cluster gets O (originality), I (importance), P (practicality) and F (placement power and fit), from 0 to 10. Weighted = 0.30 O + 0.25 I + 0.25 P + 0.20 F.
- **Where three or more sources scored an idea**, the score is the median of their scores. Otherwise it is the synthesis judgment, informed by the sources.
- **Overlap** is a separate column: 0 (none), −1 (occupied literature or a partial overlap with the supervision team), −2 (the question may belong to a supervisor's project). Adjusted = Weighted − 0.5 × |overlap|.
- **Votes are not evidence of novelty.** All phase 1 agents read the same dossier. An idea that the dossier names (for example, Alex's note that deadline revisions "can show the target's limited commitment power", voice notes ¶62 and ¶169) attracts many votes for that reason alone. Other teams can reach the same idea from the same filings.

## 5. Notes that change the reading

1. **Seller commitment (A) is the consensus pick, with one discriminating risk.** Alex's voice notes already name deadline revisions as evidence of "limited commitment power", and two sources report that he wants the deadline fields for the joint paper. The first kill test for A is a conversation with Alex, before any work. Its theoretical risk: with symmetric private values, a soft final round costs the seller nothing (revenue equivalence). The cost must come from asymmetric bidder types, a lost reserve, or costly entry. The pilot facts are encouraging. On 81 hand-coded deals with a final-round marker, a later bid beat the "final" round in 14 to 15 deals, and the winner signed above its first final-round bid in 56 of 64 deals, by a median of 4.2%.
2. **Process leaks (B) has equal standing for reasons the rubric rewards.** Four sources reached it independently. It is the only cluster that uses Austin's options and microstructure skills. It sits far from Alex's projects. CCC becomes its theory companion: prices before a deal can recruit or repel bidders. Its moat holds only if no one has aligned confidentiality-agreement or contact dates from filings with trading on those dates. The closest work is Acharya and Johnson (2010), Augustin, Brenner and Subrahmanyam (2019), Fidrmuc and Xia (2022), Szydlowski (leaks) and Liu and Officer. A kill test takes two days on CRSP and the 404 hand-coded deals, but needs a WRDS login by Austin.
3. **The premise of the price-after-exclusivity idea (E) meets a contrary pilot.** In the hand-coded set, winners mostly signed above their first final-round bid. That is not the same object as the last bid before exclusivity, but the novelty check and a direct count must test the premise before E stays on the list.
4. **The proxy idea (H) is the cleanest empirical home for CCC.** After signing, the signed bidder is the incumbent, a topping bidder is the rival, and the merger-arbitrage price is what the rival watches.
5. **CCC is a second paper in every source.** GPT Pro adds one substantive point: the paper's own reserve comparison shows that a seller can raise the reserve and erase the weak-against-strong reversal. A referee will ask for the seller's optimal terms.
6. **The supervision team's "Deal Terms in Takeovers" draft** (March 2026, 673 completed mergers, 2015 to 2021, every formal offer and its non-price terms) touches A, E, F, H and I. The novelty check reads it directly.
