## Your task

Rank the nine shortlisted ideas (A, B, C, D, E, F, H, I, J in the synthesis) and CCC as a comparator. Rank them twice, from two seats:
- **a hiring committee** at a strong business school in fall 2029, which sees the job-market talk and the paper;
- **the editor of a top-3 finance journal**, who decides whether the paper goes to referees.

The synthesis scores are provisional. Disagree with them, and with the synthesis ranking, wherever your reading of the evidence differs. Say why.

## Four stress tests for each idea

1. **Supervision overlap.** Does the question already belong to Alex Gorbenko's projects: the joint informal-bids paper, "Deal Terms in Takeovers", or his stated agenda in the dossier? A solo job-market paper cannot sit inside them.
2. **The key variable.** Does the variable the idea needs exist in merger filings or market data at all? Examples: the bidders' expectation that a seller will reopen a round; the date a survivor learns of a rival's exit; the leak date. If it does not exist, the idea fails regardless of its theory.
3. **Standard theory.** Is the mechanism already standard? For example, with symmetric private values a soft final round costs the seller nothing, by revenue equivalence. Name the condition that makes the effect first-order, and say whether takeover data are likely to meet it.
4. **The source of the appeal.** Does the idea's appeal come from the question, or from the dossier that all agents read? An idea that the dossier names attracts votes for that reason alone.
