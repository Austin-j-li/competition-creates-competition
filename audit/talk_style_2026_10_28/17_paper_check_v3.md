Items checked and correct:

- **Frames 1–2:** No contradiction with the paper's motivation. Fixed-information deterrence, costly preparation, and the distinction between public strength and a chosen preemptive bid agree with the introduction. The Bulow–Klemperer reference concerns the value of an additional bidder, while this paper keeps the sale rule fixed. See `sections/01_introduction.tex:3–15,139–153` and `sections/07_welfare.tex:59–60`. This is a comparison with the paper, not a separate review of the cited publications.

- **Frame 3, table:** The three rows give defensible summaries of the introduction. Dow–Goldstein–Guembel concerns feedback between real decisions and information production. Edmans–Goldstein–Jiang (2015) concerns corrective decisions and trade incentives. Roberts–Sweeting concerns selective entry. Edmans–Goldstein–Jiang (2012) concerns target prices and takeovers. Kyle–Vila concerns concealed acquirer purchases amid liquidity trade. The new investor has no net position before trade and remains outside the auction. “With no stake” should retain that initial-position meaning. See `sections/01_introduction.tex:127–160`.

- **Frame 4, mechanism:** The two-payoff mechanism and the fixed-information control match the paper. The sentence about an information payment that beats a tax is intuition, not a separate sufficient condition. H1–H3 supply the exact conditions.

- **Frame 10:** The weak signs are correct. The split identity is correct under the retained model's class restrictions. Both synergy distributions meet the reserve, as S3 states. First-order dominance alone would not suffice if reserve admissibility differed. The buyer-profit gap is the good-class gross profit minus the poor-class gross profit. See `slides.tex:178,249–251`; `sections/03_competition_information.tex:25–65`.

- **Frame 11:** The belief band holds for every order because S4 retains the restriction that the liquidity chance is at most one third. No extra restriction is missing here. The broader domain in B1 applies to Proposition 2, whose proof uses nonzero-order bounds before it derives full orders. B2 correctly separates the zero-order restriction. See `slides.tex:212`; `appendix/A2_beliefs_trading.tex:35–44,66–87`; `appendix/A3_proposition2.tex:46–55`.

- **Frame 11, price and return claims:** “Each part larger under good synergy” must mean positive shareholder spread and larger good-class buyer profit. With positive entry floor, these conditions support price revelation and the threshold rule. A correct order's gross return equals entry times the spread times residual doubt. The default preparation rule includes equality at the threshold. See `appendix/A2_beliefs_trading.tex:106–119,190–208`.

- **Frames 12 and B1:** H1–H3, the three order profiles, the beliefs, the entry probabilities, and the price distinctions match Proposition 2. The result holds at arbitrary interior priors. It does not require prior 1/2. The open-set claim concerns the example's continuous parameter family. See `sections/04_equilibrium_results.tex:11–49,100–108`.

- **B1, ownership:** The general ownership inequality and the special case where the good class always wins are correct. The strict takeover-share condition in that special case is positive poor-class win probability against the weak buyer. See `sections/04_equilibrium_results.tex:35–42`; `appendix/A3_proposition2.tex:74–103`.
