# Words for a wider audience (28 October 2026)

Source: the author's discussion with the faculty reader on 28 October (empirical anchors thread) and the
30-minute outline in `06_outline_30min.md`. Names: first buyer, second buyer, informed investor.

## The three tests a wider audience will run

1. Who is the informed investor, and why does it know something the second buyer does not?
2. Second buyers already look at the stock price. So what is new?
3. So what? Why should anyone outside takeover theory care?

The faculty reader ran all three. The words below answer each one before it is asked.

## One sentence to repeat at the open and the close

> A stronger rival does not change whether the second buyer reads the stock price. It changes what the price says.

Short form for the end: "Stock markets help decide who contests a sale."

Avoid: "when the first buyer bids high" (points to jump-bid signalling) and "second buyers do not look at the
price" (the model assumes they look). Say "a stronger buyer already at the table" and "at fixed information".

## The "so what", in three plain lines

1. **For evidence.** Deterrence should hold where no stock price reaches the second buyer before diligence, and it
   can fail where one does. (§8, "Entry and incumbent strength".)
2. **For sellers.** Sale terms set two things at once: what a buyer pays to win, and what the stock price can reveal
   before anyone prepares. (§1, §9.) Against the strong first buyer, letting the second buyer see the price raises
   shareholder proceeds, under the main result's conditions (§7.1).
3. **For markets.** Trade by an outside specialist during a sale is more than a transfer between traders: it can
   bring in a better-fitting buyer. (Proposition 2, good-fit ownership.)

Guardrails: the reversal is conditional, not a general pattern; the welfare result is the price-access comparison
only; the paper does not show that a public sale process or more disclosure is optimal.

## Prepared answers (speech or questions, not slides)

- **Is the informed investor legal?** Trading on one's own research is legal. The rules forbid trading on
  information obtained through a breach of duty (Rule 10b-5) or, once a tender offer is under way, information from
  the bidder, the target or their agents (Rule 14e-3). The model's investor uses neither source.
- **Don't buyers already look at prices?** "Yes, and the model assumes they do. A stronger buyer at the table
  changes how much the price tells them about their own fit."
- **Do such investors exist after a sale is public?** Merger-arbitrage evidence: target prices and arbitrage
  positions forecast revised and competing bids (Jindra and Walkling 2004; Hsieh and Walkling 2005). Check these
  figures in the papers before citing them.
- **Where would deterrence show up?** Unlisted targets and confidential sales, where no price reaches the second
  buyer before diligence.
