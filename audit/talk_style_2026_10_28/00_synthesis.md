## 2. The reconstructed template

The exemplar decks share ingredients but not one fixed order (report 02). The common sequence is:
question → answer in words → players and values → timeline and information → actions, payoffs, equilibrium →
leading example → main result and intuition → extensions → conclusion. The closest exemplar for this paper is
Goldstein's feedback keynote: three setup bullets and a dated timeline (trade and price → real decision →
payoffs), with equations only on later slides. Quint's own lectures do not use a separate timeline slide. The
template needs confirmation by the author against the faculty reader's advice.

## 4. Style rules for the rebuild (from report 04)

- About 50 words on the face, 120–170 words spoken. One claim per slide; title or takeaway, never both.
- One-line titles that state a claim. One metaphor through the talk.
- Theorems in one coloured block. Conditions in words on the face; exact forms on backups.
- One point per click (Beamer overlays). No footlines, credit lines or subtitles on faces; backups carry them.
- One name set on every face. Each symbol defined in words at first use, and again after a gap.
- Example numbers rounded to two or three decimals on faces; full precision on backups.
- Full speaker notes, not cues: the voice carries more than the slide.

## 6. A correction independent of the restyle

S8 prints t(θ) = max{p, min(R,θ)} and g(θ) = (θ − max{p,R})₊. The paper defines t(θ) and g(θ) as expectations over
R (§2.4; Appendix A1: t(θ) = p + ∫_p^θ [1 − F(y)] dy). The slide's own t₀ = p[1 − F(p)] is an expectation. The fix
is E_R[·] around both expressions.
