# Language on the slide face and spoken delivery

Scope: slide text and speech only. S1–S26 and B1–B12 follow the
verbatim pack. PDF pages follow the compiled Madrid deck.

## 1. Diagnosis: the language

The pack inverts the guides. The guides put the talk in the voice
and notes on the slide. The pack puts the paper on the slide and a
caption in the voice.

| Measure, S1–S26 | Pack | Guide |
|---|---:|---|
| Face words | Median 148; range 79–229 | Short bullets |
| "Say" words | Median 30; range 16–42 | Main channel |
| Share on the face | 84% of all words | Under half |

Li, rule 4, p. 2: slides are audience notes, visual material, and
exact statements. "Less than half of what you plan to say should be
on the slides." "Slides are not a teleprompter." Quint (2016),
slide 36: slides form "an outline of the major points… not every
detail." Quint (2009), p. 3: dense slides make the room "give up
on reading them or give up on listening."

Five patterns cause the inversion.

1. **The four-layer slide.** Title, subtitle, body, takeaway, and
   footline stack on one frame. S6 (PDF p. 7) adds three "Line:"
   items, a small line, and a credit line to a table. S9 (PDF p. 10)
   adds a small line, an outcome line, and a method line to five
   numbered lines. S14 (PDF p. 15) adds three conditions, scope, and
   two result lines to a table. Each layer is small. The stack is a page.
2. **Paper sentences as bullets.** S7 flow boxes run 24–26 words.
   The S21 set-up line runs 37 words. S1 and S12 lines run 21–28
   words. Labels such as "Line:", "Small line:", and "Readout line:"
   mark manuscript parts. Li, rule 4(d), p. 2, asks for short,
   simple sentences in large type.
3. **"Say" lines too short for the slot.** Thirty words take about
   15 seconds. Slots run 1.5–2 minutes. S9 pairs 216 face words
   with 25 spoken words. S14 pairs 229 with 38. The speaker must
   read the slide or improvise. Quint (2009), p. 4, rejects slide
   reading: the speaker can "just send them by email." The timing
   lock states the fault as a rule: "Trim Say lines first; never
   trim printed text." Cut print first.
4. **All points at once.** Each compiled frame arrives complete
   (PDF pp. 2, 8, 15). The room reads the takeaway during line one.
   Quint (2016), slide 31, builds points "one by one." Slides 32–35
   warn that visible points make the room "read ahead." Build one
   reasoning step per click: one line, column, or term.
5. **Flat theorems, crowded notation.** Propositions on S11, S14,
   S19, and S21 look like set-up on S6–S9. Quint (2009), p. 3, sets
   theorems in blue so theorems "stood out." Li, rule 11, p. 3,
   reserves "Proposition" for precise proved claims. S6–S9 also
   introduce over 30 symbols before Proposition 1. Li, rule 8,
   p. 3, allows "at most seven." Rules 8–9 add two duties: restate
   symbols in words, and give $B/b$, $P/p$, and $m/M$ distinct
   spoken names.

## 2. Titles, takeaways, and names

**Titles.** The best titles state one repeatable claim: S11
"Strength takes from the buyer and gives to the signal," S12 "One
order can point. It cannot prove," and S18 "Freeze the news, and
strength only scares buyers off." Three faults remain. Topic titles
name no claim: S6 "Who draws what, and who sees it" and S9
"Equilibrium: orders, prices, beliefs, preparation, bids." Long
titles wrap to two lines: S1, S4, and S21 (PDF pp. 2, 5, 22).
Metaphors compete: a tax and a payment race (S4), slices move
between pockets (S10–S11), a signal clears a bar (S16), markets
hold moods (S19), and research turns a dial (S22). Keep one metaphor.

**Takeaways.** A takeaway earns space only by an added condition or
next step. S2 passes: deterrence holds "when what the buyer knows
stays fixed." S11, S12, and S17 restate the title in new words. S14
splits one claim: the title states the rise, and the takeaway adds
the return. Fix the title and cut the takeaway. Rule: title claim
or takeaway claim, never both. Drop subtitle stacks with the spare
layer. "Proposition 2 · Paper §4.1 · General result" (S14) helps a
reader, not a listener. Keep "Proposition 2." Put sections on backups.

**Names.** The pack mixes paper names with slide names. Face counts
show the drift: first buyer 18, incumbent 3; second buyer 26,
challenger 1; fund 17, specialist investor 2. The same drift hits
diligence bill and preparation cost, buyer profit and gross profit,
target proceeds and sale proceeds, and live, flat, and informative
prices. "Signal," "belief," and "news" also swap for the order $X$,
the belief $\mu$, and the price $P$. Pick the paper set and hold the
set on each face: incumbent, challenger, specialist investor,
preparation cost, gross profit, target proceeds, target spread,
informative, uninformative. "Bidder" may name both bidders as a
class. Section 3 uses this set. Each rewrite also keeps the standing
rules: no first person on the face, no status labels, and no example
numbers before the results.

## 3. Five model rewrites

Scripts run 60–90 seconds at a slow pace, with display pauses. First person
appears in speech only.

### S1 — Key question

*Current:* 13-word title, three column paragraphs, one footline;
126 face words, 29 spoken words (PDF p. 2).

*Face:*

> **Does a stronger incumbent always deter entry?**
>
> - One bidder is ready. One more must be found.
> - Preparation costs money before any bid exists.
> - The stock price moves before preparation.

*Say:* "Thank you. I ask one question. Does a stronger incumbent
always keep the next bidder out? Three groups care. The seller
cares first. One bidder leaves money on the table, so the seller
must find one more bidder. The challenger cares next. Preparation
means diligence, financing, and approvals. The challenger pays
before any bid exists, and the cost stays sunk in a loss. The
market cares last. The stock still trades at the decision point.
So the price may do more than forecast a second bid. The price may
cause a second bid. I hold the sale rule fixed. Entry moves."

*Backup:* Bulow and Klemperer (1996) to B1. Column text to speech.

### S7 — The order slot

*Current:* five dense flow boxes, one $b$ and $u$ line, one credit
line; 181 face words (PDF p. 8).

*Face:*

> **One order trades before preparation**
>
> - The specialist investor sees the class. Liquidity trade can copy each order.
> - Market makers see the order only. The challenger sees the price only.
> - One public order: sell, no order, or buy.
>
> *Figure note:* Keep flow F4 with names only in boxes. Build in
> three clicks: slot, price, decision.

*Say:* "Now the market. One slot opens before the decision. With
chance lambda, the specialist investor fills the slot. The
specialist sees the fit class and picks sell, no order, or buy. A
nonzero order costs $k$. Else a liquidity trader fills the slot for
reasons outside the sale. That trader can make the same three
choices. No observer sees who filled the slot. Camouflage comes
built in. Market makers see the order alone and set one price. The
challenger then sees the price and the preparation cost. The
challenger sees no order and no class. How much can one price say?"

*Backup:* $\beta$ chances and $0<\beta\le1/3$, $b=(1-\lambda)\beta$,
$u=\lambda/b$, and credits to B2 and B1. Speech restates $1+u$ on
S12. Order sizes stay on B11.

### S11 — Proposition 1

*Current:* two proposition lines, one identity, one gloss, one
takeaway, one footline, all at one weight (PDF p. 12).

*Face:* Set the proposition in blue as one block.

> **Strength moves the fit gap to shareholders**
>
> **Proposition 1.** (1) At each fixed belief, gross profit
> $B(\mu)$ weakly falls. (2) Under the class ranking, the target
> spread $\Delta_T$ weakly rises.
>
> $$\Delta_T(F)+g_H(F)-g_L(F)=\mathbb{E}_H[\max\{\theta,p\}]-\mathbb{E}_L[\max\{\theta,p\}]$$

*Say:* "Proposition 1 needs no market. Part one first. Hold the
belief fixed. A stronger incumbent weakly lowers gross profit at
that belief. That part restates the received answer in the model.
Part two next. Under the class ranking, the same strength weakly
raises the target spread. The spread states the gain to
shareholders from a good fit over a poor fit. The display ties both
parts in one line. No incumbent distribution sits on the right
side. Strength leaves the fit gap at one size. Strength moves the
gap between two pockets. Which pocket speaks louder in the price?"

*Backup:* Integral forms, strict signs, overlap conditions, and
equal admissibility stay on B5.

### S12 — The bounded signal

*Current:* band display plus price inversion, threshold, exclusion
bound, and two-numbers rule in two minutes (PDF p. 13).

*Face:*

> **One order can point. It cannot prove.**
>
> $$m(\pi)=\frac{\pi}{\pi+(1-\pi)(1+u)}\ \le\ \mu\ \le\ \frac{\pi(1+u)}{\pi(1+u)+1-\pi}=M(\pi)$$
>
> - Preparation at cost $c_H$ pays exactly when $\mu\ge\tau$.
>
> *Figure note:* Keep band F5 in symbols. Add the $\tau$ mark on a click.

*Say:* "Camouflage caps the news. Take a moment with the display.
After each order, under each strategy, the belief stays in one
band. Little $m$ marks the floor. Big $M$ marks the cap. One plus
$u$ caps the move in odds from one order. A liquidity order can
copy the specialist order at each point. A purchase can say good
fit, likely. A purchase cannot say good fit, certain. At a positive
spread, the price reveals the belief. The line below adds the cost
test. Preparation pays at beliefs at or past tau. Gross profit
short at the cap means no order recruits. Recruitment lives at the cap."

*Backup:* Two-numbers rule, price-inversion conditions, and the
$t_0$, $t_L$, $W_H$ scope note to a beliefs backup. Name the rule
in speech at first real use, before Proposition 3.

### S14 — Proposition 2

*Current:* table, three conditions, scope, two result lines, and
takeaway; 229 face words, the pack peak (PDF p. 15).

*Face:* The table is the display. Build one column per click.

> **Entry rises, then returns to its floor**
>
> |  | Weak $F_0$ | Strong $F_1$ | Very strong $F_2$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Specialist orders | None | Buys on good fit; sells on poor fit | Buys on good fit; sells on poor fit |
> | Price | Uninformative | Informative | Informative |
> | Entry | $\rho$ | $\rho+(1-\rho)(b+\pi\lambda)>\rho$ | $\rho$ |
>
> *Table note:* Proposition 2, under (H1)–(H3). Full statement: B6.

*Say:* "Read one column at a time. Weak incumbent first. The spread
cannot pay the trading cost. The specialist stays out, the price
stays uninformative, and entry sits at rho. Strong incumbent next.
The spread pays for trade. Good fit buys and poor fit sells. A
purchase alone reaches the cap, and profit at the cap covers the
preparation cost. Entry rises past the floor. Very strong incumbent
last. Trade stays full. Profit at the cap now falls short. Entry
returns to rho. Three conditions drive the path: short profit at
the prior, full profit at the cap under strength, and trade paid by
a wider spread. Price news and entry need not travel as one."

*Backup:* (H1)–(H3) in symbols, class ranking, the strength chain,
uniqueness under four tie-rule pairs, open-set scope, beliefs
$(m(\pi),\pi,M(\pi))$, and $e_HW_H(F_1)>\rho W_H(F_0)$ to B6.

## 4. Style sheet for the 30-minute version

1. Put under half of the speech on the face: 40–60 face words,
   120–170 spoken words per slide.
2. Give each slide one job and one claim. State no claim twice.
3. Keep titles on one line, near ten words. Cut words before type.
4. Print three lines at most, or one display, or one exhibit with
   one reading line. A table counts as the display.
5. Write notes, not paper sentences. One line, one fact.
6. Cut footlines, small lines, credit lines, and subtitles from
   faces. Backups carry citations and scope.
7. Set each proposition in one blue numbered block. Backups carry
   symbol conditions and proofs.
8. Build one reasoning step per click. Show no line early.
9. Hold one name set: incumbent, challenger, specialist investor,
   preparation cost, gross profit, target proceeds, target spread,
   informative, uninformative.
10. Keep seven live symbols at most. Define each in words at first
    use. Restate the words after a gap.
11. Show an equation only for a spoken walk. Pause first. Cut each
    equation speech asks the room to skip.
12. Leave white space at the foot. Space marks the claim.

## 5. Delivery in a theory seminar

**First 60 seconds.** Learn the opening word for word (Quint 2009,
p. 3). Use four moves: thanks, question, answer, fixed object.
"Thank you. I ask whether a stronger incumbent always deters entry.
The answer is no. Strength cuts profit at each belief and widens
the spread that pays for trade. The sale rule stays fixed, and
entry moves." Then reach the model fast (Quint 2009, p. 2; Li,
rule 3, p. 2).

**Transitions.** Close each part on the next object. Open with that
object. Model to mechanism: "The auction splits one value into two
claims. What does strength do to the split?" Mechanism to result:
"One force cuts profit. One force widens the spread. Which force
wins in one price?" Result to control: "Entry rose. Freeze the
information and watch the rise die."

**Equations aloud.** Go slow, and slower at displays (Quint 2016,
slides 29–30). Pause for one breath (Li, rule 10, p. 3). Name
objects in words first: "gross profit $B$, at belief mu." State
each rise, fall, and fixed term. End with one plain reading. On
S11: "No incumbent sits on the right side, so strength moves the
gap at a fixed size." Face the room and read from the laptop
(Quint 2009, pp. 3–4).

**Questions and close.** Let each question end, then breathe once
(Quint 2009, p. 4; Quint 2016, slides 41–45). Answer in one
sentence when one sentence does the job. Defer with a short answer
and a named backup. Close in the opening words, as on S26:
"Strength can bring the challenger in. A wider spread pays for
trade, and good news pays the preparation cost. Sale terms set the
price to win and the price that speaks at once. Thank you." Add no
caveat past the answer (Li, rule 13, p. 4).
