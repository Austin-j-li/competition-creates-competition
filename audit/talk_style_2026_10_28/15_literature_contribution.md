## What is new, strand by strand

| Strand | What it gives this paper | What this paper adds |
|---|---|---|
| Prices guide real decisions: Dow, Goldstein and Guembel (2017) (the paper's closest antecedent); Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang (2015) (the trading set-up: an informed trader, unit orders, a trading cost, a price that guides a real decision) | The price as an information source; real decisions feed back into the incentive to produce information | The decision maker is an outside bidder, and the sale splits one value between the traded claim and that bidder. The same force that cuts the bidder's profit raises what the price reveals |
| Costly entry into auctions: Fishman (1988); Hirshleifer and Png (1989); Roberts and Sweeting (2013) | Costly preparation; deterrence; pre-entry information about one's own value | The pre-entry information is a market price whose content moves with competition. The deterrence result becomes a fixed-information result |
| Prices and trading around takeovers: Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang (2012); Kyle and Vila (1991) | Target prices affect takeover activity; informed orders with liquidity cover | An outside investor with no stake, whose trade tells a rival bidder about its own synergy before it commits |

Claims to avoid: "first to show bidders learn from prices" (Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang 2012; Luo 2005 already show
price effects on takeovers); "Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang is the anchor" (the paper calls Dow, Goldstein and Guembel
the closest antecedent; Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang 2015 supplies the trading set-up).

## Draft: literature slide

Title: Where the paper sits: a price that guides entry into a sale

| Strand | Builds on | Adds |
|---|---|---|
| Prices guide real decisions — Dow, Goldstein and Guembel (2017); Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang (2015) | The price as an information source for a real decision | The decision is a rival's entry, and the sale splits one value between shareholders and that rival |
| Costly entry into auctions — Fishman (1988); Roberts and Sweeting (2013) | Costly preparation and deterrence | Pre-entry information that moves with competition |
| Takeover prices and trading — Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang (2012); Kyle and Vila (1991) | Target prices shape takeovers; informed orders hide in liquidity trade | An outside investor with no stake informs a rival bidder |

Bold line: **Existing work holds the entrant's information fixed, or lets prices guide the firm's own decisions. Here
competition decides how much the price tells a rival.**

Speech (one minute): "Two papers matter most. Fishman shows that a stronger rival deters entry when the entrant's
information is fixed. Dow, Goldstein and Guembel show that a real decision feeds back into the incentive to produce
information; I take the trading set-up from Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang. The new piece is the sale: one value is
split between shareholders, whose claim trades, and the rival, who decides whether to enter."

## Draft: contribution slide (website version, sharpened)

Title: This paper: strength can pay for the signal that brings the second buyer in

Large line (website text, kept): **A stronger first buyer taxes the second buyer's profit and pays for information about
that buyer. Entry rises when the information payment beats the tax.**

Three results:
1. One shift, two effects: strength cuts the second buyer's profit and widens what shareholders gain from good
   synergy (Proposition 1).
2. Entry rises, then falls back, and each outcome is unique (Proposition 2).
3. Freeze the information, and strength cannot raise entry (control).

Then: costly research amplifies the rise; recruitment shows up in the purchase impact.

Roadmap: Model → Mechanism → Main result → Research → Tests.
