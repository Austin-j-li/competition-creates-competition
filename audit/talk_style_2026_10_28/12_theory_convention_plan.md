# Theory-convention plan — synthesis of reports 10 and 11 (28 October 2026)

Direction from the author: financial-economics theory convention, as easy to understand as possible; define
terminology once; named symbols (a letter with a short word gloss) at definition and wherever a later deduction uses
them. Overleaf deck = handout without click builds; website = animated; no industry story.

## Where the two reports agree

- One definitions slide opens the model block. Not a glossary before the model.
- Slide 5 becomes a compact "observes / chooses" table with glossed symbols.
- Payoffs slide in symbols: t(θ) = E_R[max{p, min(R, θ)}], g(θ) = E_R[(θ − max{p, R})_+], t + g = θ; t_j, g_j, t_0,
  Δ_T = t_H − t_L, B(μ) = g_L + μ(g_H − g_L). "Two pockets" leaves the slide faces (speech only).
- Equilibrium slide states the paper's conditions. Keep the order belief μ(x) apart from the belief at a price.
- Result slides carry the paper's label and the formal statement; one plain intuition line below each.
  (H1)–(H3) go on the Proposition 2 face. Slide 11 shows K_H, K_L and their bounds. Slide 13 is Proposition A.2 with
  its fixed objects. Slide 14 states Proposition 4 with its scope. Slide 15 uses frequency-weighted impacts.
- Notation: drop the seven-symbol cap. About 10–15 recurring glossed symbols, plus symbols local to one slide.
- Backup B15 has three errors: μ glossed as Pr(H | P) (the paper's μ(x) is the belief after the order); R ~ U[0, r]
  given as the general law (it is the example); entry written e_r(μ) (the paper writes e(x)).
