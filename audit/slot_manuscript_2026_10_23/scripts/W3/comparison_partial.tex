\noindent
This note compares two trading stages for the model of \emph{Competition Creates Competition}. In the first,
the Laplace set-up, an investor chooses a continuous order and Laplace noise hides it. In the second, the
order-slot set-up, the investor shares one unit order slot with a liquidity trader. The auction, the
preparation costs and the challenger's decision are the same in both. Section~\ref{sec:setups} states the two
set-ups. Section~\ref{sec:results} puts the main results side by side. Section~\ref{sec:tradeoffs} states the
trade-offs as results with their conditions. Section~\ref{sec:method} sets out the proof method and works one
proof in each set-up. Section~\ref{sec:standing} records where each manuscript stands against that method.
Section~\ref{sec:decisions} lists decision points.

The Laplace manuscript is the version of 22 October 2026 (main text, Appendix A and the Online Appendix). The
slot manuscript is in preparation; its Sections 1 to 4 and Appendix A.1 to A.3 are drafted, and this note cites
its results by section. Every number for the slot comes from the project registry. Every number for the Laplace
manuscript comes from an independent outward recomputation of its benchmark or from its printed tables. Each
result carries one status label: analytical, computer-assisted, numerical diagnostic or open.

\section{The two set-ups}\label{sec:setups}

\paragraph{Shared primitives.} Both set-ups keep Sections 2.1, 2.2 and 3.1 of the Laplace manuscript. A
challenger of class \(j\in\{H,L\}\), with prior \(\pi=\Pr(j=H)\), has a value \(\theta\sim G_j\); \(G_H\)
dominates \(G_L\), and both supports lie above the reserve \(p\). The incumbent's value is \(R\sim F\), uniform
on \([0,r]\) in the benchmark. The challenger's preparation cost is \(0\) with probability \(\rho\) and \(c_H\)
otherwise. Prepared bidders bid their values in a second-price auction with reserve \(p\). The auction objects
are \(t_0\), \(t_j\), \(g_j\), \(\DT=t_H-t_L\), the gross profit \(B(\mu)=g_L+\mu(g_H-g_L)\) at a belief
\(\mu\), and the threshold \(\tau=(c_H-g_L)/(g_H-g_L)\). A stronger incumbent lowers \(g_H\) and \(g_L\) and
raises \(\DT\) (Proposition 1 of either manuscript; analytical). The benchmark auction is
\((h,\ell,p,\rho,c_H,k)=(\reg{prim_h},\reg{prim_ell},\reg{prim_p},\reg{prim_rho},\reg{prim_cH},\reg{prim_k})\),
prior \(\reg{prim_pi}\), and strengths \(r_0=\reg{prim_r0}\), \(r_1=\reg{prim_r1}\), \(r_2=\reg{prim_r2}\).
Entry \(\Ent\) is the probability that the challenger prepares; high-class ownership \(\Own_H\) is the
probability that a class-\(H\) challenger acquires the target.

\subsection{The Laplace set-up}\label{sec:laplace}

\paragraph{Primitives.} The investor observes the class and submits an order \(q\in[-1,1]\) at the linear cost
\(k|q|\). Noise demand \(Z\) has the Laplace density \(f(z)=\eu^{-|z|/b}/(2b)\) with scale \(b>1\). Market makers
see the flow \(X=q+Z\) and set \(P(X)=\E[V_T\mid X]\), where \(V_T\) is the terminal payoff of a target share
(Laplace manuscript, Section 2.3).

\paragraph{Timing.} The seller announces the sale rule. The investor learns the class and trades. Market makers
price the flow. The challenger sees the price and its cost and decides whether to prepare. Prepared bidders learn
their values and bid. Payoffs are realized (Section 2.3).

\paragraph{What the challenger learns from the price.} For any two orders, the likelihood ratio of a flow lies in
\([\eu^{-2/b},\eu^{2/b}]\). Under every mixed order profile the posterior after every flow therefore lies in
\([m(\pi),M(\pi)]\), with \(m(\pi)=\pi/[\pi+(1-\pi)\eu^{2/b}]\) and \(M(\pi)=\pi/[\pi+(1-\pi)\eu^{-2/b}]\)
(Proposition A.1; analytical). Every flow has a positive density in both classes, so every price is on path and
no off-path belief is needed. The zero-cost challenger prepares after every price, so entry is at least
\(\rho\). Competitive pricing then gives \(P=t_0+e(P)[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu_X]\), and the challenger inverts this
identity to recover \(\mu_X\) whenever \(\DT>0\) (Proposition A.2; analytical). The investor's residual
advantage at a flow is \(A_H=e\DT(1-\mu_X)\) in class \(H\) and \(A_L=e\DT\mu_X\) in class \(L\); both lie in
\([\rho\kappa(\pi)\DT,\DT]\) with \(\kappa(\pi)=\min\{m(\pi),1-M(\pi)\}\) (equation (11)). The score bound
\(|f'|\le f/b\) turns these bounds into a lower bound \((1-s/b)\bar A-k\) on the marginal profit of a correctly
signed order of size \(s\) (Appendix A.2).

\paragraph{Calibration.} The benchmark uses \(b=\reg{lap_b}\), so \(M(1/2)=\eu/(1+\eu)=\reg{lap_M_half}\) and
\(m(1/2)=\reg{lap_m_half}\). Entry is \(\reg{entry_r0}\) at \(r_0\), \(\reg{lap_entry_r1}\) at \(r_1\) and
\(\reg{entry_r2}\) at \(r_2\); high-class ownership is \(\reg{own_r0}\), \(\reg{lap_own_r1}\) and \(\reg{own_r2_B}\)
(Proposition 2 and Table 2; analytical). The threshold flow at \(r_1\) is \(x^*=\reg{lap_xstar_r1}\).

\subsection{The order-slot set-up}\label{sec:slot}

\paragraph{Primitives.} One public order opportunity comes before the challenger's decision. With probability
\(\lambda\in(0,1)\) the informed investor fills it: it sees the class and chooses \(q\in\{-1,0,1\}\), and a
nonzero order costs \(k\). With probability \(1-\lambda\) a liquidity trader fills it and sells, abstains or buys
one unit with probabilities \(\beta\), \(1-2\beta\), \(\beta\), where \(0<\beta\le1/3\); its motive is exogenous.
No one observes who filled the slot. Market makers see the order \(X\in\{-1,0,1\}\) and set \(P=\E[V_T\mid X]\)
(slot manuscript, Section 2.3). The device is the alternative-arrival structure of \citet{DowGorton1997}; the
unit menu, the presence probability and the unit cost follow \citet{EdmansGoldsteinJiang2015}.

\paragraph{Timing.} As in the Laplace set-up, with one change: at the trading date the slot draw selects the
investor with probability \(\lambda\) and a liquidity trader otherwise, and the selected trader places its order.
Section 5 of the slot manuscript adds a research stage before this date.

\paragraph{What the challenger learns from the price.} Two numbers summarize the slot: \(b=(1-\lambda)\beta\),
the probability of a liquidity order in one direction, and \(u=\lambda/b\), the informed presence mass relative
to the liquidity mass in one direction. The largest likelihood ratio that one order can carry is \(1+u\). Under
every investor strategy the posterior after a nonzero order lies in \([m(\pi),M(\pi)]\), with
\(M(\pi)=\pi(1+u)/[\pi(1+u)+1-\pi]\) and \(m(\pi)=\pi/[\pi+(1-\pi)(1+u)]\); the bound at the zero order needs
\(\beta\le1/3\), and no other result needs it (Section 3.2; analytical). Every order has positive probability in
both classes, so every price is on path. Equal prices give equal entry and hence equal posteriors, so the price
reveals the posterior (Section 3.2). Each class compares one gross return with \(k\): a purchase earns
\(K_H=e(1)\DT[1-\mu(1)]-k\) in class \(H\), a sale earns \(K_L=e(-1)\DT\mu(-1)-k\) in class \(L\), and under every
candidate \(\rho\kappa(\pi)\DT\le K_j+k\le\DT\) (Section 3.3; analytical).

\paragraph{Calibration B.} The slot has two primitives: \(1+u=\eu\) and \(\beta=\reg{beta_B}\), an exact
decimal. Then \(\lambda=u\beta/(1+u\beta)=\reg{lambda_B}\) and \(b=\reg{b_B}\). With \(1+u=\eu^{2/b}\) at the
Laplace scale \(b=\reg{lap_b}\), the posterior bounds of the two set-ups coincide: \(M(1/2)=\reg{M_half_B}\) and
\(m(1/2)=\reg{m_half_B}\). Entry is \(\reg{entry_r0}\), \(\reg{entry_r1_B}\) and \(\reg{entry_r2}\) at the three
strengths, and high-class ownership is \(\reg{own_r0}\), \(\reg{own_r1_B}\) and \(\reg{own_r2_B}\)
(Section 4.2; computer-assisted). The three prices at \(r_1\) are \(\reg{price_r1_m1_B}\), \(\reg{price_r1_0_B}\)
and \(\reg{price_r1_p1_B}\) after a sale, no order and a purchase.

Three facts organize the table. First, the reversal and its welfare consequence are the same results in both
set-ups, with the same margins where the margins concern the auction: the cost window
\((\reg{lap_cH_lo},\reg{lap_cH_hi})\) and the three-part prior window \((\reg{prior_lo_B},\reg{prior_hi_B})\)
are identical, because calibration B gives the slot the Laplace top posterior. Second, the slot replaces three
certified points with a complete equilibrium set, and adds research, coexistence at every prior and derived
observables. Third, two comparative statements lose their strict form in the slot: the fixed-information control
and reserve deterrence at the exclusion reserve. Section~\ref{sec:tradeoffs} states each change as a result.

Uniqueness has a different scope in the two set-ups. In the Laplace set-up it covers arbitrary mixed orders on
a continuum and every unilateral deviation in \([-1,1]\); it is proved at the three benchmark strengths and
on the two outer regions, \(r<\fr(k)=\reg{lap_r_unique_zero}\) and \(r>r_U\). In the slot it covers arbitrary
mixtures on three orders, and the whole set is known at every strength on the path. The second is a weaker
uniqueness claim per economy and a stronger completeness claim along the path.

\section{Trade-offs}\label{sec:tradeoffs}

Each trade-off below is a result with its condition. Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} concerns the dynamics each
set-up generates; Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concerns the conclusions each supports.

\subsection{Dynamics}\label{sec:dynamics}

\paragraph{The partial sale.}
\lap{At \(1.55\), \(1.60\) and \(1.65\) the certified equilibria have pure orders \((1,-v)\) with
\(v=\reg{lap_P3_v_r155}\), \reg{lap_P3_v_r160} and \reg{lap_P3_v_r165} (Proposition 3; computer-assisted). The
interior sale exists because the low class's marginal profit is strictly decreasing in its order (Online
Appendix B.2), so a root of its first-order condition is a global optimum. The sale magnitude rises across the
three nodes; the branch between them is a numerical diagnostic.}
\slo{On the unit menu a sale is all or nothing. The only continuous margin is a mixing probability. The low class
abstains up to \(r_N=\reg{rN}\), mixes on \((r_N,r_S)\) and sells with probability one from
\(r_S=\reg{rS_B}\) (Section 4.3; computer-assisted). The mixture needs the investor rule IM; under I0 the
equilibrium set is empty on \((r_N,r_S]\).}
The partial sale is therefore a property of the continuous order menu. Its condition is that the investor can
choose a size, with a density that moves smoothly with it.

\paragraph{Strictly rising entry at intermediate strengths.}
\lap{Entry rises strictly across the three nodes, \(\reg{lap_P3_entry_r155}<\reg{lap_P3_entry_r160}<\reg{lap_P3_entry_r165}\),
because the entry enclosures are disjoint (Proposition 3; computer-assisted). The rise is carried by the sale
magnitude: a larger \(v\) makes a sale more informative and moves the threshold flow. Its conditions are the
continuous menu, the fixed presence of the investor and an interval certificate at each node.}
\slo{With a fixed presence probability, entry rises strictly on \([r_A,r_F]=[\reg{rA},\reg{rF_B}]\), where class
\(H\) mixes: entry is \reg{entry_rA_B} at \(r_A\), \reg{entry_mb_r140_B} at \reg{str_r140}, \reg{entry_mb_r145_B}
at \reg{str_r145} and \reg{entry_r1_B} from \(r_F\) (Section 4.3; computer-assisted). The purchase rate
\(s_+=b[\DT(r)/k-2]\) adjusts so that a purchase that recruits earns exactly \(k\). The rise needs rule IM, and
entry is flat on \([r_F,r_C]\); at \(1.55\) to \(1.65\) it equals \reg{entry_r1_B}. With research, entry rises
strictly on \([\reg{res_rR},\reg{res_rcap})\), which contains the three strengths:
\(\reg{res_entry_r155}<\reg{res_entry_r160}<\reg{res_entry_r165}\) (Section 5; slope analytical, interval
computer-assisted). The rise is carried by the research intensity, which solves
\(2a\lambda=\DT(r)m_\lambda-k\); class \(H\) buys with probability one after a success. Its conditions are a
quadratic research cost with \(a\) in \((\reg{res_a_lo},\reg{res_a_up})\) and a cap in
\((\reg{res_cap_lo},\reg{res_cap_hi})\); zero research is an equilibrium at every strength on the branch.}
Each set-up therefore has a strict rise at intermediate strengths, carried by a different object: a sale size,
a mixing probability or a research intensity. The slot's fixed-presence rise sits at lower strengths than the
Laplace nodes, and its research rise sits at the Laplace nodes with lower entry levels.

\paragraph{Coexistence.}
\lap{No trade is an equilibrium exactly on \(r\le r_N=\reg{lap_rN}\) and is unique below
\(\fr(k)=\reg{lap_r_unique_zero}\); full orders are unique above \(r_U=\reg{lap_rU}\) (Proposition A.4;
analytical). Between the two bounds the manuscript certifies three informative equilibria, each beside no trade,
and proves no uniqueness; the equilibrium set there is open.}
\slo{No trade and an informative equilibrium coexist exactly on \([r_A,r_N]\). The high class uses one of five
forms (no trade, a mixed purchase that recruits, a full purchase, mixed orders at the entry floor, the threshold
pair), and the set at each strength is listed (Section 4.3; computer-assisted). Under rule CM the threshold pair
is also an equilibrium on \((r_A,r_J]\), \(r_J=\reg{rJ}\), with entry that falls strictly from \reg{entry_rA_B}
to \(\rho\); at \(r_C\) entry after a purchase can take any value in \([\rho,1]\).}
The slot's completeness follows from a finite support enumeration: each class has three orders, and each gross
return falls strictly with the class's own rate. The Laplace set-up has no such enumeration on a continuum.

\paragraph{Research.}
Only the slot models research. The investor chooses an intensity \(z\in[0,\bar\lambda]\) at cost \((a/2)z^2\)
before it learns the class, and the intensity is the presence probability. Zero research is an equilibrium
exactly when \(\rho\DT\le2k\) at an uninformative price, a condition that does not involve \(a\). The rising
marginal cost meets the falling research value once, so the research response is unique and moves smoothly with
strength. The region in which the claims hold is a strip of caps and a window of costs, and the benchmark cap
sits \reg{margin_lambda_hi_B} below the top of the strip, the same boundary as the return margin at \(r_2\).
The equilibrium set with research on \((r_N,r_U^R)=(\reg{res_rN},\reg{res_rUR})\) is open.

\paragraph{Timing.}
Both set-ups price one trade before the challenger's decision, and neither models the choice of a trading
date. The Laplace flow is the aggregate net demand of the decision interval. The slot is one public order
opportunity at a known date, which is a stronger institutional assumption with a sharper empirical counterpart:
the first prespecified opportunity, with a recorded zero when no order arrives. An exploratory extension with
several slots before the decision, outside both manuscripts, keeps the posterior bound and the return to the
floor at \(r_2\) for every finite number of slots; its complete equilibrium list is open. In both set-ups the
ceiling \(r_C\) is a tie event with positive probability (the flow tail \(x\ge1\) in the Laplace set-up, the
purchase order in the slot), so entry is discontinuous in strength there and the challenger's tie rule
decides entry at \(r_C\) itself.

\subsection{Conclusions each set-up supports}\label{sec:conclusions}

\paragraph{The fixed-information control: falls, or does not rise.}
\lap{With full orders held fixed and the challenger free to decide again, entry falls strictly from
\reg{lap_frozen_r0_entry} at \(r_0\) to \reg{lap_frozen_r1_entry} at \(r_1\) (Section 4.2; closed forms). The
threshold flow \(x^*\) moves continuously with strength, so the crossing probability has a density and the
fall is strict.}
\slo{With the same control, entry is unchanged at \reg{p4_frozen_r0_entry} on \((1,r_C]\) and equals \(\rho\)
above \(r_C=\reg{rC_B}\) (Section 4.2; analytical, values computer-assisted). The frozen posteriors are three
atoms \(m<1/2<M\), the threshold \(\tau(r)\) exceeds \(5/8\) for \(r>1\), and only the top atom can recruit, so
entry is a step function of strength. The crossing probability of the fixed-experiment proposition is
\reg{p4_control_crossing_r0_r1} from \(r_0\) to \(r_1\) and \reg{p4_control_crossing_r1_r2} from \(r_1\) to \(r_2\).}
The condition is the number of prices: with three prices entry moves in steps, so a strict fall at fixed
orders needs the threshold to cross an atom. The weak statement ``does not rise'' holds in both set-ups.

\paragraph{Reserve deterrence: strict, or flat.}
\lap{At the reserve \reg{lap_reserve_excl_p}, which excludes the low class, preparation falls from
\reg{lap_reserve_excl_r0_entry} at \(r_0\) to \reg{lap_reserve_excl_r1_entry} at \(r_1\) with unique
continuations at both nodes (Table 5; analytical).}
\slo{At the reserve \reg{rsv_points_excl}, entry is \reg{p2_reserve_points_exclude_r0_entry} at both strengths:
full orders are unique at both, and only a purchase recruits at both. Strict deterrence returns at the reserve
\reg{rsv_deter}, where entry falls from \reg{p2_reserve_points_deter_r0_entry} to
\reg{p2_reserve_points_deter_r1_entry}; this reserve exceeds \(r_0\) and excludes the weak incumbent
(Section 7.3; analytical, values computer-assisted). Strict deterrence after the low class is excluded holds
for reserves between \(p_H\approx\reg{p2_reserve_ph}\) and \(h-c_H/M\approx\reg{p2_reserve_weak_top}\).}
The condition is the same step structure: strict deterrence in the slot needs the stronger incumbent to push the
top-posterior profit below the high cost.

\paragraph{Uniqueness and completeness.}
The Laplace set-up proves uniqueness of the trading outcome against a continuum of deviations at each benchmark
strength, and leaves the set between \(r_N\) and \(r_U\) open. The slot proves uniqueness against three orders
and lists the whole set along the path, with the dependence on the two tie rules in one table (Section 4.3).
Three statements depend on the rules: the strict rise on \([r_A,r_F]\) needs IM; the threshold pair and the
entry interval at \(r_C\) exist only under CM; under I0 the set is empty on \((r_N,r_S]\). Proposition 2 and
the research results at the six benchmark strengths hold under all four pairs.

\paragraph{The admissible region.}
Both manuscripts have the same thin boundary at \(r_2\). The Laplace manuscript asserts that its region is open
and prints prior windows; it does not print the part (iii) margin, \(c_H-B_{r_2}(M)=\reg{lap_return_cost}\). The
slot manuscript states the region as an exact band in \(u\) with a table of margins. In the slot the thin
margin appears in five coordinates that measure one distance: \reg{margin_return_post_B} in the top posterior,
\reg{margin_return_B} in cost units, \reg{margin_u_hi_B} in \(u\), \reg{margin_lambda_hi_B} in \(\lambda\) at
the benchmark \(\beta\), and \reg{gap_r2_rC_B} in strength. The two set-ups differ in the trade bound: the
Laplace window for \(k\) ends \reg{lap_margin_A2_upper} above \(k\), the slot's ends \reg{margin_kstrong_B}
above \(k\), and the prior window for parts (i) and (ii) is \((\reg{prior_lo_B},\reg{prior_ii_hi})\) in the
slot against \((\reg{lap_prior_lo},\reg{lap_prior_ii_hi})\) in the Laplace set-up.

\paragraph{The empirical mapping.}
\lap{Section 7 states three predictions: a stronger prepared bidder raises the probability of further
preparation when the price precedes the decision and weakly lowers it when it does not; a buyer that prepares
after a favorable return wins more often; against a strong prepared bidder preparation returns to its floor
while the price stays informative. The flow is aggregate net demand, so the model's largest price gap is not a
unit spread, and no observable is derived from the flow.}
\slo{The same three predictions hold (Section 8, in preparation). The slot adds derived observables. The
conditional execution spread \(S=\rho\DT\vartheta+(1-\rho)J\,A(M)\), with \(\vartheta=u/(2+u)\) the informed
share of nonzero orders, is \reg{ba_r1_B} at \(r_1\), and \reg{ba_share_r1_B} of it is the entry component; the
entry component is zero above \(r_C\). Sell impact and volume identify \((\vartheta,\lambda,\beta)\) under full
orders; counts alone leave the informed share in \([\reg{theta_min_counts_B},\reg{theta_max_full_eq_r1_B})\).
Orders predict the winner among prepared challengers above \(r_C\). The formulas need an observation protocol:
the first prespecified public opportunity, with a recorded zero; a sample of first executed trades cannot
identify the neutral price effect.}
The slot's observables are results with the status analytical (formulas) and computer-assisted (values); the
data counterpart of each protocol is open.

\paragraph{Assumptions each set-up adds.}
The Laplace set-up assumes a continuous order, a Laplace law with \(b>1\) and the score bound; its extension to
every bounded-score density is proved (Proposition A.5). The slot assumes a unit menu, a presence probability and
an exogenous liquidity motive; an uninformed trader with the model's own trade payoff would not fill the slot.
With research it adds a quadratic cost and a cap inside the admissible strip. The slot needs no off-path belief
on its menu; the Laplace set-up needs none because every flow has a density. A continuous menu inside the slot
would need a convention for unused sizes (slot manuscript, Section 6.4).
