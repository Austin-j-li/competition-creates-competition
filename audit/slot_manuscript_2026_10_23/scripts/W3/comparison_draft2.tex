\noindent
This note compares two trading stages for the model of \emph{Competition Creates Competition}. In the first,
the Laplace set-up, an investor chooses a continuous order and Laplace noise hides it. In the second, the
order-slot set-up, the investor shares one unit order slot with a liquidity trader. The auction, the
preparation costs and the challenger's decision are the same in both. Section~\ref{sec:setups} states the two
set-ups, and Section~\ref{sec:results} puts the main results side by side. Section~\ref{sec:tradeoffs} states
the trade-offs as results with their conditions.

Section~\ref{sec:method} sets out the proof method and works one proof in each set-up.
Section~\ref{sec:standing} records where each manuscript stands against that method, and
Section~\ref{sec:decisions} lists decision points. The Laplace manuscript is the version of 22 October 2026
(main text, Appendix A and the Online Appendix). The slot manuscript is in preparation; its Sections 1 to 4
and Appendix A.1 to A.3 are drafted, and this note cites its results by section. Every slot number comes from
the project registry. Every Laplace number comes from an independent outward recomputation of its benchmark or
from its printed tables. Each result carries one status label: analytical, computer-assisted, numerical
diagnostic or open.

\section{The two set-ups}\label{sec:setups}

\paragraph{Shared primitives.} Both set-ups keep Sections 2.1, 2.2 and 3.1 of the Laplace manuscript. A
challenger of class \(j\in\{H,L\}\), with prior \(\pi=\Pr(j=H)\), has a value \(\theta\sim G_j\). The class
\(G_H\) dominates \(G_L\), and both supports lie above the reserve \(p\). The incumbent's value is \(R\sim F\),
uniform on \([0,r]\) in the benchmark. The challenger's preparation cost is \(0\) with probability \(\rho\) and
\(c_H\) otherwise. Prepared bidders bid their values in a second-price auction with reserve \(p\).

The auction objects are \(t_0\), \(t_j\), \(g_j\) and \(\DT=t_H-t_L\). At a belief \(\mu\) the challenger's
gross profit is \(B(\mu)=g_L+\mu(g_H-g_L)\), and the threshold belief is \(\tau=(c_H-g_L)/(g_H-g_L)\). A
stronger incumbent lowers \(g_H\) and \(g_L\) and raises \(\DT\) (Proposition 1 of either manuscript;
analytical). The benchmark auction is
\((h,\ell,p,\rho,c_H,k)=(\reg{prim_h},\reg{prim_ell},\reg{prim_p},\reg{prim_rho},\reg{prim_cH},\reg{prim_k})\),
with prior \(\reg{prim_pi}\) and strengths \(r_0=\reg{prim_r0}\), \(r_1=\reg{prim_r1}\), \(r_2=\reg{prim_r2}\).
Entry \(\Ent\) is the probability that the challenger prepares. High-class ownership \(\Own_H\) is the
probability that a class-\(H\) challenger acquires the target.

\subsection{The Laplace set-up}\label{sec:laplace}

\paragraph{Primitives.} The investor observes the class and submits an order \(q\in[-1,1]\) at the linear cost
\(k|q|\). Noise demand \(Z\) has the Laplace density \(f(z)=\eu^{-|z|/b}/(2b)\) with scale \(b>1\). Market makers
see the flow \(X=q+Z\) and set \(P(X)=\E[V_T\mid X]\), where \(V_T\) is the terminal payoff of a target share
(Laplace manuscript, Section 2.3).

\paragraph{Timing.} The seller announces the sale rule. The investor learns the class and trades. Market makers
price the flow. The challenger sees the price and its cost and decides whether to prepare. Prepared bidders learn
their values and bid, and payoffs are realized (Section 2.3).

\paragraph{What the challenger learns from the price.} For any two orders, the likelihood ratio of a flow lies in
\([\eu^{-2/b},\eu^{2/b}]\). Under every mixed order profile the posterior after every flow therefore lies in
\([m(\pi),M(\pi)]\), with \(m(\pi)=\pi/[\pi+(1-\pi)\eu^{2/b}]\) and \(M(\pi)=\pi/[\pi+(1-\pi)\eu^{-2/b}]\)
(Proposition A.1; analytical). Every flow has a positive density in both classes, so every price is on path and
no off-path belief is needed. The zero-cost challenger prepares after every price, so entry is at least
\(\rho\). Competitive pricing then gives \(P=t_0+e(P)[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu_X]\), and the challenger inverts this
identity to recover \(\mu_X\) whenever \(\DT>0\) (Proposition A.2; analytical).

The investor's residual advantage at a flow is \(A_H=e\DT(1-\mu_X)\) in class \(H\) and \(A_L=e\DT\mu_X\) in
class \(L\). Both lie in \([\rho\kappa(\pi)\DT,\DT]\), with \(\kappa(\pi)=\min\{m(\pi),1-M(\pi)\}\)
(equation (11)). The score bound \(|f'|\le f/b\) turns these bounds into a bound on marginal profit. A
correctly signed order of size \(s\) has marginal profit at least \((1-s/b)\bar A-k\) (Appendix A.2).

\paragraph{Calibration.} The benchmark uses \(b=\reg{lap_b}\), so \(M(1/2)=\eu/(1+\eu)=\reg{lap_M_half}\) and
\(m(1/2)=\reg{lap_m_half}\). Entry is \(\reg{entry_r0}\) at \(r_0\), \(\reg{lap_entry_r1}\) at \(r_1\) and
\(\reg{entry_r2}\) at \(r_2\). High-class ownership is \(\reg{own_r0}\), \(\reg{lap_own_r1}\) and
\(\reg{own_r2_B}\) (Proposition 2 and Table 2; analytical). The threshold flow at \(r_1\) is
\(x^*=\reg{lap_xstar_r1}\).

\subsection{The order-slot set-up}\label{sec:slot}

\paragraph{Primitives.} One public order opportunity comes before the challenger's decision. With probability
\(\lambda\in(0,1)\) the informed investor fills it. It sees the class and chooses \(q\in\{-1,0,1\}\), and a
nonzero order costs \(k\). With probability \(1-\lambda\) a liquidity trader fills the slot. It sells, abstains
or buys one unit with probabilities \(\beta\), \(1-2\beta\), \(\beta\), where \(0<\beta\le1/3\), and its motive
is exogenous. No one observes who filled the slot.

Market makers see the order \(X\in\{-1,0,1\}\) and set \(P=\E[V_T\mid X]\) (slot manuscript, Section 2.3). The
device is the alternative-arrival structure of \citet{DowGorton1997}. The unit menu, the presence probability and
the unit cost follow \citet{EdmansGoldsteinJiang2015}.

\paragraph{Timing.} As in the Laplace set-up, with one change at the trading date. The slot draw selects the
investor with probability \(\lambda\) and a liquidity trader otherwise, and the selected trader places its
order. Section 5 of the slot manuscript adds a research stage before this date.

\paragraph{What the challenger learns from the price.} Two numbers summarize the slot. The first is
\(b=(1-\lambda)\beta\), the probability of a liquidity order in one direction. The second is \(u=\lambda/b\),
the informed presence mass relative to the liquidity mass in one direction. The largest likelihood ratio that
one order can carry is \(1+u\). Under every investor strategy the posterior after a nonzero order lies in
\([m(\pi),M(\pi)]\), with \(M(\pi)=\pi(1+u)/[\pi(1+u)+1-\pi]\) and \(m(\pi)=\pi/[\pi+(1-\pi)(1+u)]\)
(Section 3.2; analytical). The bound at the zero order needs \(\beta\le1/3\), and no other result needs it.

Every order has positive probability in both classes, so every price is on path. Equal prices give equal entry
and hence equal posteriors, so the price reveals the posterior (Section 3.2). Each class compares one gross
return with \(k\). A purchase earns \(K_H-k\) with \(K_H=e(1)\DT[1-\mu(1)]\) in class \(H\), and a sale earns
\(K_L-k\) with \(K_L=e(-1)\DT\mu(-1)\) in class \(L\). Under every candidate,
\(\rho\kappa(\pi)\DT\le K_j\le\DT\) (Section 3.3; analytical).

\paragraph{Calibration B.} The slot has two primitives: \(1+u=\eu\) and \(\beta=\reg{beta_B}\), an exact
decimal. Then \(\lambda=u\beta/(1+u\beta)=\reg{lambda_B}\) and \(b=\reg{b_B}\). With \(1+u=\eu^{2/b}\) at the
Laplace scale \(b=\reg{lap_b}\), the posterior bounds of the two set-ups coincide: \(M(1/2)=\reg{M_half_B}\) and
\(m(1/2)=\reg{m_half_B}\). Entry is \(\reg{entry_r0}\), \(\reg{entry_r1_B}\) and \(\reg{entry_r2}\) at the three
strengths. High-class ownership is \(\reg{own_r0}\), \(\reg{own_r1_B}\) and \(\reg{own_r2_B}\)
(Section 4.2; computer-assisted). The three prices at \(r_1\) are \(\reg{price_r1_m1_B}\), \(\reg{price_r1_0_B}\)
and \(\reg{price_r1_p1_B}\) after a sale, no order and a purchase.

Three facts organize the table. First, the reversal and its welfare consequence are the same results in both
set-ups. Where a margin concerns the auction alone, it is the same number: the cost window
\((\reg{lap_cH_lo},\reg{lap_cH_hi})\) and the three-part prior window \((\reg{prior_lo_B},\reg{prior_hi_B})\)
are identical, because calibration B gives the slot the Laplace top posterior. Second, the slot replaces three
certified points with a complete equilibrium set. It adds research, coexistence at every prior and derived
observables. Third, two comparative statements lose their strict form in the slot: the fixed-information
control and reserve deterrence at the exclusion reserve.

Uniqueness has a different scope in the two set-ups. In the Laplace set-up it covers arbitrary mixed orders on
a continuum and every unilateral deviation in \([-1,1]\). It is proved at the three benchmark strengths and on
the two outer regions, \(r<\fr(k)=\reg{lap_r_unique_zero}\) and \(r>r_U\). In the slot it covers arbitrary
mixtures on three orders, and the whole set is known at every strength on the path. The second is a weaker
uniqueness claim per economy and a stronger completeness claim along the path.

\section{Trade-offs}\label{sec:tradeoffs}

Each trade-off below is a result with its condition. Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} concerns the dynamics each
set-up generates. Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concerns the conclusions each supports.

\subsection{Dynamics}\label{sec:dynamics}

\paragraph{The partial sale.}
\lap{At \(1.55\), \(1.60\) and \(1.65\) the certified equilibria have pure orders \((1,-v)\), with
\(v=\reg{lap_P3_v_r155}\), \reg{lap_P3_v_r160} and \reg{lap_P3_v_r165} (Proposition 3; computer-assisted). The
interior sale exists because the low class's marginal profit falls strictly with its order (Online Appendix
B.2). A root of its first-order condition is therefore a global optimum. The sale magnitude rises across the
three nodes; the branch between them is a numerical diagnostic.}
\slo{On the unit menu a sale is all or nothing, and the only continuous margin is a mixture probability. The low
class abstains up to \(r_N=\reg{rN}\), mixes on \((r_N,r_S)\) and sells with probability one from
\(r_S=\reg{rS_B}\) (Section 4.3; computer-assisted). The mixture needs the investor rule IM. Under I0 the
equilibrium set is empty on \((r_N,r_S]\).}

The partial sale is therefore a property of the continuous order menu. Its condition is that the investor can
choose a size, with a density that moves smoothly with it.

\paragraph{Strictly rising entry at intermediate strengths.}
\lap{Entry rises strictly across the three nodes,
\(\reg{lap_P3_entry_r155}<\reg{lap_P3_entry_r160}<\reg{lap_P3_entry_r165}\), because the entry enclosures are
disjoint (Proposition 3; computer-assisted). The sale magnitude carries the rise: a larger \(v\) makes a sale
more informative and moves the threshold flow. The conditions are the continuous menu, a fixed presence of the
investor and an interval certificate at each node.}

\slo{With a fixed presence probability, entry rises strictly on \([r_A,r_F]=[\reg{rA},\reg{rF_B}]\), where class
\(H\) mixes (Section 4.3; computer-assisted). Entry is \reg{entry_rA_B} at \(r_A\), \reg{entry_mb_r140_B} at
\reg{str_r140}, \reg{entry_mb_r145_B} at \reg{str_r145} and \reg{entry_r1_B} from \(r_F\). The purchase rate
\(s_+=b[\DT(r)/k-2]\) adjusts so that a purchase that recruits earns exactly \(k\). The rise needs rule IM, and
entry is flat on \([r_F,r_C]\); at \(1.55\) to \(1.65\) it equals \reg{entry_r1_B}.}

\slo{With research, entry rises strictly on \([\reg{res_rR},\reg{res_rcap})\), which contains the three strengths:
\(\reg{res_entry_r155}<\reg{res_entry_r160}<\reg{res_entry_r165}\) (Section 5; slope analytical, interval
computer-assisted). The research intensity carries the rise. It solves \(2a\lambda=\DT(r)m_\lambda-k\), and class
\(H\) buys with probability one after a success. The conditions are a quadratic research cost with \(a\) in
\((\reg{res_a_lo},\reg{res_a_up})\) and a cap in \((\reg{res_cap_lo},\reg{res_cap_hi})\). Zero research is an
equilibrium at every strength on the branch.}

Each set-up therefore has a strict rise at intermediate strengths, and a different object carries it: a sale
size, a mixture probability or a research intensity. The slot's fixed-presence rise sits at lower strengths than
the Laplace nodes. Its research rise sits at the Laplace nodes with lower entry levels.

\paragraph{Coexistence.}
\lap{No trade is an equilibrium exactly on \(r\le r_N=\reg{lap_rN}\) and is unique below
\(\fr(k)=\reg{lap_r_unique_zero}\). Full orders are unique above \(r_U=\reg{lap_rU}\) (Proposition A.4;
analytical). Between the two bounds the manuscript certifies three informative equilibria, each beside no trade,
and proves no uniqueness. The equilibrium set there is open.}
\slo{No trade and an informative equilibrium coexist exactly on \([r_A,r_N]\). The high class uses one of five
forms, and the set at each strength is listed (Section 4.3; computer-assisted). The forms are no trade, a mixed
purchase that recruits, a full purchase, mixed orders at the entry floor, and the threshold pair. Under rule CM
the threshold pair is also an equilibrium on \((r_A,r_J]\), \(r_J=\reg{rJ}\); its entry falls strictly from
\reg{entry_rA_B} to \(\rho\). At \(r_C\) under CM, entry after a purchase can take any value in \([\rho,1]\).}

The slot's completeness follows from a finite support enumeration. Each class has three orders, and each gross
return falls strictly with the class's own rate. The Laplace set-up has no such enumeration on a continuum.

\paragraph{Research.}
Only the slot models research. The investor chooses an intensity \(z\in[0,\bar\lambda]\) at cost \((a/2)z^2\)
before it learns the class, and the intensity is the presence probability. Zero research is an equilibrium
exactly when \(\rho\DT\le2k\) at an uninformative price; this condition does not involve \(a\). The marginal
cost rises and the research value falls, so they meet once. The research response is therefore unique and
moves smoothly with strength.

The region in which the research claims hold is a strip of caps and a window of costs. The benchmark cap sits
\reg{margin_lambda_hi_B} below the top of the strip. This is the same boundary as the return margin at \(r_2\),
measured in another coordinate. The equilibrium set with research on \((r_N,r_U^R)=(\reg{res_rN},\reg{res_rUR})\)
is open.

\paragraph{Timing.}
Both set-ups price one trade before the challenger's decision, and neither models the choice of a trading
date. The Laplace flow is the aggregate net demand of the decision interval. The slot is one public order
opportunity at a known date. This is a stronger institutional assumption with a sharper empirical counterpart:
the first prespecified opportunity, with a recorded zero when no order arrives. An exploratory extension with
several slots before the decision lies outside both manuscripts. It keeps the posterior bound and the return
to the floor at \(r_2\) for every finite number of slots; its complete equilibrium list is open.

In both set-ups the ceiling \(r_C\) is a tie event with positive probability. The event is the flow tail
\(x\ge1\) in the Laplace set-up and the purchase order in the slot. Entry is therefore discontinuous in
strength at \(r_C\), and the challenger's tie rule decides entry at \(r_C\) itself.

\subsection{Conclusions each set-up supports}\label{sec:conclusions}

\paragraph{The fixed-information control: falls, or does not rise.}
\lap{Hold full orders fixed and let the challenger decide again. Entry falls strictly from
\reg{lap_frozen_r0_entry} at \(r_0\) to \reg{lap_frozen_r1_entry} at \(r_1\) (Section 4.2; closed forms). The
threshold flow \(x^*\) moves continuously with strength, so the crossing probability has a density and the fall
is strict.}
\slo{With the same control, entry is unchanged at \reg{p4_frozen_r0_entry} on \((1,r_C]\) and equals \(\rho\)
above \(r_C=\reg{rC_B}\) (Section 4.2; analytical, values computer-assisted). The frozen posteriors are three
atoms \(m<1/2<M\), and the threshold \(\tau(r)\) exceeds \(5/8\) for \(r>1\). Only the top atom can recruit, so
entry is a step function of strength. The crossing probability is \reg{p4_control_crossing_r0_r1} from \(r_0\)
to \(r_1\) and \reg{p4_control_crossing_r1_r2} from \(r_1\) to \(r_2\).}

The condition is the number of prices. With three prices entry moves in steps, so a strict fall at fixed orders
needs the threshold to cross an atom. The weak statement ``does not rise'' holds in both set-ups.

\paragraph{Reserve deterrence: strict, or flat.}
\lap{The reserve \reg{lap_reserve_excl_p} excludes the low class. There, preparation falls from
\reg{lap_reserve_excl_r0_entry} at \(r_0\) to \reg{lap_reserve_excl_r1_entry} at \(r_1\), with unique
continuations at both nodes (Table 5; analytical).}
\slo{At the reserve \reg{rsv_points_excl}, entry is \reg{p2_reserve_points_exclude_r0_entry} at both strengths.
Full orders are unique at both, and only a purchase recruits at both. Strict deterrence returns at the reserve
\reg{rsv_deter}, where entry falls from \reg{p2_reserve_points_deter_r0_entry} to
\reg{p2_reserve_points_deter_r1_entry} (Section 7.3; analytical, values computer-assisted). This reserve
exceeds \(r_0\) and excludes the weak incumbent. Strict deterrence after the low class is excluded holds for
reserves between \(p_H\approx\reg{p2_reserve_ph}\) and \(h-c_H/M\approx\reg{p2_reserve_weak_top}\).}

The condition is the same step structure. Strict deterrence in the slot needs the stronger incumbent to push the
top-posterior profit below the high cost.

\paragraph{Uniqueness and completeness.}
The Laplace set-up proves uniqueness of the trading outcome against a continuum of deviations at each benchmark
strength. It leaves the set between \(r_N\) and \(r_U\) open. The slot proves uniqueness against three orders
and lists the whole set along the path. One table states the dependence on the two tie rules (Section 4.3).
Three statements depend on the rules. The strict rise on \([r_A,r_F]\) needs IM. The threshold pair and the
entry interval at \(r_C\) exist only under CM. Under I0 the set is empty on \((r_N,r_S]\). Proposition 2 and the
research results at the six benchmark strengths hold under all four pairs.

\paragraph{The admissible region.}
Both manuscripts have the same thin boundary at \(r_2\). The Laplace manuscript asserts that its region is open
and prints prior windows. It does not print the part (iii) margin, \(c_H-B_{r_2}(M)=\reg{lap_return_cost}\). The
slot manuscript states the region as an exact band in \(u\) with a table of margins. In the slot the thin margin
appears in five coordinates that measure one distance: \reg{margin_return_post_B} in the top posterior,
\reg{margin_return_B} in cost units, \reg{margin_u_hi_B} in \(u\), \reg{margin_lambda_hi_B} in \(\lambda\) at
the benchmark \(\beta\), and \reg{gap_r2_rC_B} in strength.

The two set-ups differ in the trade bound. The Laplace window for \(k\) ends \reg{lap_margin_A2_upper} above
\(k\), and the slot's ends \reg{margin_kstrong_B} above \(k\). The prior window for parts (i) and (ii) is
\((\reg{prior_lo_B},\reg{prior_ii_hi})\) in the slot and \((\reg{lap_prior_lo},\reg{lap_prior_ii_hi})\) in the
Laplace set-up.

\paragraph{The empirical mapping.}
\lap{Section 7 states three predictions. A stronger prepared bidder raises the probability of further
preparation when the price precedes the decision, and weakly lowers it when it does not. A buyer that prepares
after a favorable return wins more often. Against a strong prepared bidder, preparation returns to its floor
while the price stays informative. The flow is aggregate net demand, so the model's largest price gap is not a
unit spread, and no observable is derived from the flow.}
\slo{The same three predictions hold (Section 8, in preparation), and the slot adds derived observables. The
conditional execution spread is \(S=\rho\DT\vartheta+(1-\rho)J\,A(M)\), with \(\vartheta=u/(2+u)\) the informed
share of nonzero orders. It is \reg{ba_r1_B} at \(r_1\), and \reg{ba_share_r1_B} of it is the entry component;
the entry component is zero above \(r_C\). Sell impact and volume identify \((\vartheta,\lambda,\beta)\) under
full orders. Counts alone leave the informed share in \([\reg{theta_min_counts_B},\reg{theta_max_full_eq_r1_B})\).
Orders predict the winner among prepared challengers above \(r_C\).}

The slot's formulas need an observation protocol: the first prespecified public opportunity, with a recorded
zero. A sample of first executed trades cannot identify the neutral price effect. The observables are results
with the status analytical (formulas) and computer-assisted (values). The data counterpart of each protocol is
open.

\paragraph{Assumptions each set-up adds.}
The Laplace set-up assumes a continuous order, a Laplace law with \(b>1\) and the score bound. Its extension to
every bounded-score density is proved (Proposition A.5). The slot assumes a unit menu, a presence probability
and an exogenous liquidity motive. An uninformed trader with the model's own trade payoff would not fill the
slot. With research it adds a quadratic cost and a cap inside the admissible strip. The slot needs no off-path
belief on its menu, and the Laplace set-up needs none because every flow has a density. A continuous menu
inside the slot would need a convention for unused sizes (slot manuscript, Section 6.4).

\section{The proof method}\label{sec:method}

An equilibrium proof has three failure modes. A step can be circular. A step can use a property of the
equilibrium before the proof establishes it. A conclusion can hold at one parameter point only. The method
below removes all three. It has five parts, and Section~\ref{sec:worked} applies it to one result in each
set-up.

\subsection{The equilibrium as an explicit object}\label{sec:object}

The proof starts from the equilibrium written out as one tuple:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
\item the class-contingent order distributions;
\item the posterior after every order or flow;
\item the price function on every order or flow;
\item the challenger's belief at every price on the image;
\item the entry rule at every price and cost;
\item the bids.
\end{itemize}
The tuple is a proof artifact. Each later step may use only what the tuple states.

In the slot manuscript, Definition 1 fixes the tuple \((\sigma_H,\sigma_L,\mu,P,\bar\mu,\zeta)\) with truthful
bids. Appendix A.3 displays one tuple for each of the three economies of Proposition 2. In the Laplace
manuscript the components of Proposition 2 sit in four places. The orders are in the statement, the posterior
in (A.8), the entry rule and the price in equation (9), and the price belief in Proposition A.2. The components
are complete, and the proof is sound. The five-part form displays them in one place, with the price function on
all of \(\mathbb R\), its two atoms and their probabilities.

\subsection{Verification against the fixed object}\label{sec:verification}

The conditions are listed: Bayes consistency, challenger beliefs at each price, entry optimality, competitive
prices, investor optimality and truthful bids (and research optimality with research). Each condition is checked
by direct computation against the stated tuple. A unilateral deviation holds the candidate's price function and
entry rule fixed and changes only the law of the flow that reaches them. A comparison across economies solves
the equilibrium again in each economy. The two comparisons never mix.

Two shortcuts break this part, and this project found one instance of each. The first uses price sufficiency
before it is established. A constructed tuple must show by direct algebra that equal prices give equal entry and
hence equal posteriors. A lemma whose premises include the conditions under proof cannot supply that step. The
second shortcut is the word ``symmetrically''. The wrong-sign order of class \(L\) in the Laplace proof of
Section~\ref{sec:worked} needs its own line. A purchase of size \(s>0\) earns
\(-s\int f(x-s)A_L(x)\,dx-ks<0=\Pi_L(0)\), because the residual \(A_L\) is nonnegative.

\subsection{Uniqueness through bounds on every profile}\label{sec:bounds}

A uniqueness claim rests on bounds that hold for every candidate profile, not only for the proposed one. The
device that makes this possible is the posterior bound. Under every strategy the posterior after every flow or
order lies in \([m(\pi),M(\pi)]\): from the score bound in the Laplace set-up, from the liquidity floor in the
slot. The bound lets the proof bound beliefs before it knows the equilibrium.

Three further bounds follow in order. The zero-cost challenger gives the entry floor \(e\ge\rho\) at every
price. The floor and the posterior bound give the residual bounds \(\rho\kappa\DT\le A_j\le\DT\) under every
candidate. The residual bound and the trade bound force the orders: zero orders when \(\DT<k\), full orders when
the lower residual bound exceeds \(k\). Only then does the proof read the posteriors, the entry and the prices
of the forced profile.

The proofs of part (iii) show two routes with different hypotheses. The entry-first route bounds every reachable
belief below \(M(\pi)\) and concludes that entry is \(\rho\) in every candidate, before any price inversion.
It then forces full orders. It needs the posterior bound at every flow or order, which in the slot is the
zero-order bound and \(\beta\le1/3\). The orders-first route forces full orders from the bounds at the nonzero
orders alone, then reads the three posteriors and excludes high-cost entry at each. It needs no bound at the
zero order and holds for every \(\beta<1/2\). The Laplace worked proof uses the first route, the slot appendix
the second, and both are non-circular.

\subsection{Strict margins and the open parameter set}\label{sec:margins}

Every hypothesis is a strict inequality, and the proof records its margin at the benchmark in a table.
Structural assumptions are listed apart from the strict economic hypotheses: weak dominance between the
classes, supports above the reserve, continuity of \(F\), the bound \(\beta\le1/3\). The open set is stated in
named coordinates. A printed interval in one coordinate is a slice through the benchmark with the other
coordinates fixed, and the text says so. The admissible region is then a result: a table with each boundary,
the benchmark point and its distance to the boundary (slot manuscript, Section 4.2). A result that holds on an
open set survives a perturbation of every primitive at once, with a radius that the margins bound.

\subsection{Certificates for finite steps}\label{sec:certificates}

A finite step is certified by exact rational arithmetic when its inputs are rational. It is certified by
outward interval arithmetic when a transcendental constant enters. Exact decimal inputs are parsed as decimals.
A root is certified by a bracket with opposite endpoint signs, and a count of roots by a Sturm sequence. The
named script and run log are part of the proof, and the status label is computer-assisted.

This project's own audit supplies the lesson. The first margins file for the Laplace manuscript computed every
sign in interval arithmetic. It then wrote both endpoints with a nearest-rounded decimal format. Most of its
interval rows printed equal endpoints, so the true value lay outside the printed pair. The first registries of
two slot proof packages had the same defect in dozens of rows. In every case the sign and the displayed digits
were correct, and only the export failed.

The repaired files round the lower endpoint down and the upper endpoint up from exact endpoints. A pair of
binary floats on either side of a computed threshold, as in the Laplace numerical layer, is not a certificate
either. The certificate is the outward endpoint, not the agreement of a value with a true root.

\subsection{One worked proof in each set-up}\label{sec:worked}

The result is part (iii) of Proposition 2 in each manuscript. At the strongest incumbent \(F_2\), the unique
trading outcome is full orders, and entry returns to \(\rho\). The Laplace version follows the manuscript's
Appendix A.2, which proves part (iii) in three sentences by reference to Steps 4 and 5 of parts (i) and (ii).
The slot version follows Step 5 of Appendix A.3 of the slot manuscript. Each part gives the Laplace proof and
then the slot proof.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.}
\lap{Structural: \(G_H\le G_L\) with both supports above \(p\); \(F_2\) continuous. Strict: (L1)
\(\DT(F_2)>0\); (L2) \(0<k<(1-1/b)\rho\kappa(\pi)\DT(F_2)\); (L3) \(B_{F_2}(M(\pi))<c_H\); and \(b>1\),
\(0<\rho<1\), \(0<\pi<1\), \(c_H>0\). In the manuscript, (L1) and (L2) follow from (A2) at \(F_1\) and
Proposition 1(b). (L3) is the one new hypothesis of part (iii).}
\slo{Structural: the same classes; \(F_2\) continuous; \(0<\beta<1/2\) (the model's \(\beta\le1/3\) is not used).
Strict: (S1) \(0<k<\rho\kappa(\pi)\DT(F_2)\); (S2) \(B_{F_2}(M(\pi))<c_H\); and \(0<\lambda<1\), \(0<\rho<1\),
\(0<\pi<1\), \(c_H>0\). (S1) implies \(\DT(F_2)>0\).}

\paragraph{Part 1: the object.}
\lap{\(\sigma_H^*=\delta_{+1}\), \(\sigma_L^*=\delta_{-1}\). The posterior on \(\mathbb R\) is
\(\mu^*(x)=[1+\tfrac{1-\pi}{\pi}\exp\{-(|x+1|-|x-1|)/b\}]^{-1}\). It equals \(m(\pi)\) on \(x\le-1\), rises
strictly on \((-1,1)\) and equals \(M(\pi)\) on \(x\ge1\). The price is
\(P^*(x)=t_0+\rho[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu^*(x)]\), with image \([P_-,P_+]\) and atoms at both ends. The atom \(P_+\) has
probability \(1/2\) in class \(H\) and \(\eu^{-2/b}/2\) in class \(L\). The belief at a price is the inverse,
\(\beta^*(P)=(P-t_0-\rho[t_L-t_0])/(\rho\DT)\). The entry rule is \(e^*(P,0)=1\) and \(e^*(P,c_H)=0\) at every
price, and bids are truthful.}

\slo{\(\sigma_H=\delta_1\), \(\sigma_L=\delta_{-1}\). The posteriors after \((-1,0,1)\) are \((m,\pi,M)\). The
prices are \(P(x)=t_0+\rho[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu(x)]\), three distinct numbers. At the benchmark they are
\(\reg{price_r2_m1_B}\), \(\reg{price_r2_0_B}\) and \(\reg{price_r2_p1_B}\). The belief at each price is the
posterior of its one order. The high-cost preparation probability is \(\zeta\equiv0\), so \(e\equiv\rho\), and
bids are truthful.}

\paragraph{Part 2: the conditions.}
In both set-ups the conditions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
\item[(C1)] Bayes consistency of the posterior and of the belief at each price;
\item[(C2)] competitive prices;
\item[(C3)] entry optimality at every reached price and cost, with the tie rule;
\item[(C4)] investor optimality against the fixed \(P\) and \(e\), over pure and mixed orders;
\item[(C5)] truthful bids.
\end{itemize}
The Laplace (C4) ranges over \(q\in[-1,1]\); the slot (C4) over \(q\in\{-1,0,1\}\).

\paragraph{Part 3: verification.}
\lap{(C1): under \(\sigma^*\) the flow densities are \(f(x-1)\) and \(f(x+1)\), and Bayes' rule gives
\(\mu^*\). \(P^*\) is a strictly increasing affine image of \(\mu^*\), so \(\mu^*(X)=\beta^*(P^*(X))\). (C2):
the cost is independent of the class, so \(\E[V_T\mid j,x]=t_0+\rho(t_j-t_0)\); average over the class. (C3): at
every reached price the belief lies in \([m,M]\), so \(B_{F_2}\le B_{F_2}(M)<c_H\) by (L3). The high-cost
challenger stays out, and the zero-cost challenger prepares. (C4): the residuals are \(A_H=\rho\DT(1-\mu^*)\)
and \(A_L=\rho\DT\mu^*\), both in \([\rho\kappa\DT,\DT]\).}
\lap{For a correctly signed order of size \(s\), \(U_j(s)=s\bar A_j(s)-ks\) with
\(\bar A_j(s)=\int f(x\mp s)A_j(x)\,dx\). The translation lemma (Laplace manuscript, Online Appendix A.3) gives
\(|\bar A_j'|\le\bar A_j/b\). Hence \(U_j'(s)\ge(1-1/b)\rho\kappa\DT-k>0\) almost everywhere by (L2), and the
unit order strictly dominates every smaller size. A wrong-signed order of size \(s\) earns
\(-s\int f(x\pm s)A_j(x)\,dx-ks<0\) in each class (Section~\ref{sec:verification}). (C5): weak dominance in a
second-price auction.}

\slo{(C1): the order probabilities are \(f_H=(b,b_0,b+\lambda)\) and \(f_L=(b+\lambda,b_0,b)\), with
\(b_0=(1-\lambda)(1-2\beta)\), and Bayes' rule gives \((m,\pi,M)\). The three prices differ because \(\DT>0\)
and \(m<\pi<M\), so each price has one order in its preimage. (C2): the price formula with \(e\equiv\rho\). (C3):
\(B_{F_2}(\mu)\le B_{F_2}(M)<c_H\) at each of the three posteriors by (S2), with no tie. (C4):
\(K_H=\rho\DT(1-M)>k\) and \(K_L=\rho\DT m>k\) by (S1), so \(U_H(1)>0=U_H(0)>U_H(-1)\) and
\(U_L(-1)>0=U_L(0)>U_L(1)\). A mixture over other orders averages strictly smaller payoffs. (C5): as in the
Laplace set-up.}

\paragraph{Part 4: uniqueness through bounds on every profile.}
\lap{Take any equilibrium with arbitrary mixed orders. Step 1: the triangle inequality gives
\(\eu^{-2/b}\le a_H(x)/a_L(x)\le\eu^{2/b}\) for the mixed flow densities. Hence \(\mu(x)\in[m,M]\) at every
flow and \(\beta(P)\in[m,M]\) at every price. Step 2: by (L3), the high-cost challenger stays out at every
reached price, so \(e\equiv\rho\) in every equilibrium; this step uses no price inversion. Step 3: with
\(e\equiv\rho\), \(P(x)=t_0+\rho[t_L-t_0+\DT\mu(x)]\) for almost every flow, and the residuals under a deviation
lie in \([\rho\kappa\DT,\DT]\).}
\lap{Step 4: the derivative bound of Part 3 holds against this profile's own \(P\) and \(e\), so both classes
use the unit order. Step 5: the orders pin \(\mu=\mu^*\), \(P=P^*\) up to a flow-null set, \(\beta=\beta^*\)
on the image and \(e\equiv\rho\). The null set is never reached, because every order gives the flow a positive
density.}

\slo{Take any equilibrium. Step 1: under every strategy \(b\le f_j(\pm1)\le b+\lambda\), so \(\mu(1)\le M\) and
\(\mu(-1)\ge m\). Step 2: the floor \(e\ge\rho\) and Step 1 give \(K_H\ge\rho\DT(1-M)\ge\rho\kappa\DT>k\) and
\(K_L\ge\rho\DT m>k\) by (S1). Every equilibrium therefore has \(\sigma_H=\delta_1\) and
\(\sigma_L=\delta_{-1}\). Step 3: the forced orders give the posteriors \((m,\pi,M)\). Step 4: by (S2),
\(B_{F_2}\) lies below \(c_H\) at each posterior and at every average of them, so \(\zeta\equiv0\) and
\(e\equiv\rho\) with no tie. Step 5: the price formula fixes the three prices. Every comparison in Steps 2 and 4
is strict, so the outcome is the same under all four tie-rule pairs.}

Table~\ref{tab:margins3} gives the margins at \(r_2=\reg{prim_r2}\) in both set-ups. The auction objects are

\subsection{A checklist}\label{sec:checklist}

A reader can apply these questions to any equilibrium claim.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
\item Is the equilibrium displayed as one tuple, with every component on every reachable history?
\item Are the conditions listed, and is each one checked by a computation against that tuple?
\item Does every deviation hold the candidate's price function and entry rule fixed?
\item Does any step use a property of the equilibrium before the proof establishes it for every candidate?
  Price sufficiency, a posterior and an entry rate are the usual cases.
\item Does the uniqueness argument start from bounds that hold for every profile? In which order does it force
  beliefs, residuals, orders, entry and prices?
\item Is every hypothesis a strict inequality with a recorded margin, apart from the structural assumptions?
\item Is the open set stated in named coordinates, with each printed interval identified as a slice?
\item Is every finite step certified by exact or outward arithmetic, with a named script and run log? Are the
  exported endpoints outward?
\item Which conclusions depend on a tie rule, and is each rule stated?
\item Does every number in the text match its certificate at the printed precision?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Where each set-up stands against the standard}\label{sec:standing}

\paragraph{The Laplace manuscript.} Proposition 2 meets the core of the standard. Each condition is checked
against a fixed candidate, and no step assumes its conclusion. Uniqueness rests on the posterior bounds, the
entry floor, the residual bounds and the derivative bound, all of which hold for every profile. Eleven
compressed steps across the appendices hide no gap; each has a one-line expansion. Four points fall short in
form:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
\item no result displays its equilibrium as one tuple;
\item the part (iii) margin at the benchmark, \(c_H-B_{r_2}(M)=\reg{lap_return_cost}\), is not printed (Table 2
  prints the four margins of parts (i) and (ii));
\item the thresholds \(\fr(k)\), \(r_N\), \(r_U\) and \(r_C\) are closed forms evaluated in binary floats, with
  two float neighbours recorded as bounds, so they are analytical formulas without a certificate;
\item the open set is asserted and proved nonempty, and the prior windows are printed, but the region is not
  tabulated.
\end{itemize}

Proposition 3 certifies seven things at each of three strengths:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
\item an exact root in a stated bracket;
\item the low class's unique best response against the schedule at that root;
\item the high class's unique best response against every schedule in the bracket;
\item the exclusion of wrong-signed orders;
\item Bayes consistency and competitive prices for the fixed candidate;
\item coexistence with no trade;
\item the strict order of the three entry enclosures.
\end{itemize}
It does not certify uniqueness of the equilibrium, a continuous branch between the nodes, or a parameter box
around them. Two sentences of Section 4.3 rest on the numerical grid: the sale magnitude rises toward one, and
entry rises along the continuation. The manuscript labels both as diagnostics. The printed benchmark numbers
follow from the pinned primitives and reproduce by independent arithmetic. The numerical layer that produced
the tables is a separate revision of the repository, not its main line, as of this note.

\paragraph{The slot manuscript.} Its proof packages were written to the five-part standard. An independent
check of each package confirmed every core theorem: the posterior bounds; the three outcomes of Proposition 2
under all four tie-rule pairs; the complete equilibrium set on the path; the research results at six strengths;
the information controls; the cost and class extensions; the observables. The checks also found corrections,
and every package applied them:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=0pt]
\item the interval export defect of Section~\ref{sec:certificates};
\item one summary row that merged three strength intervals, and the entry interval at \(r_C\) under rule CM;
\item an omitted coexistence point at the prior \(\pi_{\max}=\reg{pimax}\) under rule CM;
\item the premises of the lemma on informed trade, and the zero-order mass in the no-trade economy;
\item a product construction that invoked price sufficiency in the wrong direction;
\item the interpretation of \(u\);
\item a signal pair at the benchmark that did not show complementarity, replaced by
  \((\reg{sig_comp_aT},\reg{sig_comp_d})\);
\item the claim that the entry component of the spread rises above \(r_C\) (it is zero there);
\item the endpoint \(r_S\), which is an exact root and not its rounded decimal.
\end{itemize}
Sections 5 to 9 and Appendix A.4 to A.9 of the manuscript are in preparation from the checked packages.

\section{Decision points}\label{sec:decisions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Which set-up carries the main text.} The Laplace set-up keeps the partial sale and three certified
  nodes with a strict rise on a continuum. The slot keeps a complete equilibrium set, research, coexistence at
  every prior and derived observables. Its price is an exogenous liquidity motive and a unit menu. The two can
  also be nested: one as the model, the other as a remark or an appendix.
\item \textbf{What the strict rise at intermediate strengths must carry.} A sale size (Laplace, three nodes); a
  mixture probability (slot, \([r_A,r_F]\), rule IM); or a research intensity (slot,
  \([\reg{res_rR},\reg{res_rcap})\), a quadratic cost and a cap). The choice fixes which assumptions the text
  defends.
\item \textbf{The fixed-information control and reserve deterrence.} ``Falls strictly'' and strict deterrence at
  the exclusion reserve hold in the Laplace set-up. ``Does not rise'' and flat entry at that reserve hold in the
  slot, with strict deterrence at \reg{rsv_deter}. Each statement is exact in its set-up.
\item \textbf{The thin boundary at \(r_2\).} Both set-ups sit \reg{lap_return_post} below the ceiling in
  posterior units. A larger \(r_2\) or a smaller \(c_H\) widens this margin in both. The cost window is
  \((\reg{lap_cH_lo},\reg{lap_cH_hi})\) at \(r_2=\reg{prim_r2}\).
\item \textbf{Tie rules in the statements.} The slot results name IM and C1 and list where I0 and CM change a
  conclusion. The Laplace manuscript uses the challenger's rule at \(r_C\) and at two reserve events, and needs
  no investor rule. A common convention across the two documents is a choice.
\item \textbf{The five-part form in the Laplace manuscript.} An explicit tuple for Proposition 2, the part (iii)
  margin, outward certificates for the four thresholds and a tabulated region would bring it to the standard.
  No result changes.
\item \textbf{Provenance.} The numerical revision that produced the Laplace tables is not on the repository's
  main line. A merge, or a note that names the revision, settles the record.
\item \textbf{The empirical section.} The slot's observables need an observation protocol, the first
  prespecified public opportunity. The Laplace predictions need none and derive no observable. The data
  counterpart is open in both.
\item \textbf{Open items that a reader will ask about.} Laplace: the first informative equilibrium, the set
  between \(r_N\) and \(r_U\), a certified branch. Slot: the set with research on \((r_N,r_U^R)\); the selection
  between the two outcomes on \([r_A,r_N]\); the seller's reserve with the full continuation; the trading game
  under the bargaining rule.
\end{enumerate}
