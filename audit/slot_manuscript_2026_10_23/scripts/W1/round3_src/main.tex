\begin{abstract}
A stronger incumbent bidder can attract a challenger when it makes the target's stock price more informative. I
study a takeover auction in which a prospective buyer sees the price before it pays to prepare a bid. A stronger
incumbent lowers the buyer's acquisition profit at every belief about its quality. It also makes target proceeds more
sensitive to that quality, so an investor who knows the buyer's quality class gains more from trade. The investor
shares one order opportunity with liquidity traders, so no order is conclusive.

On an open set of parameters, a stronger incumbent turns an uninformative price into an informative one, with unique
trading and preparation outcomes. Challenger entry rises, and so does the probability that a challenger from the
high-value class acquires the target. With the information in the price held fixed, entry does not rise. Between
the two economies, informative and uninformative equilibria coexist, and entry rises weakly with incumbent strength
along the informative branch. When the investor pays for research, research and entry rise together over a range of
strengths. In the strong-incumbent economy, access to prices raises expected target proceeds and acquisition surplus
net of preparation costs.
\end{abstract}
