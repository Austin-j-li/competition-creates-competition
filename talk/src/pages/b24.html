Why each outcome is unique

Trading bounds and the four-step proof

Lemma A.3 (informed trading): the gain before order cost \(k\) from a correct purchase under high synergy is \(K_{H}=e(1)\,\Delta_{T}\,[1-\mu (1)]\); from a correct sale under low synergy, \(K_{L}=e(-1)\,\Delta_{T}\,\mu (-1)\). Under every order strategy, \(\rho \,\Delta_{T}[1-M(\pi )]\le K_{H}\le \Delta_{T}\) and \(\rho \,\Delta_{T}\,m(\pi )\le K_{L}\le \Delta_{T}\). With \(\kappa (\pi )=\min\{m(\pi ),1-M(\pi )\}\), each gain lies between \(\rho \,\kappa (\pi )\,\Delta_{T}\) and \(\Delta_{T}\); the purchase bound uses \(1-M(\pi )\) and the sale bound uses \(m(\pi )\). A wrong-signed order earns at most \(-k\). High synergy buys when \(K_{H}>k\) and stays out when \(K_{H}<k\); low synergy compares \(K_{L}\) with \(k\) in the same way.

Four-step proof: 1. Weak: each gain is at most \(\Delta_{T}\), below \(k\), so no informed order; the belief stays at the prior; entry stays at \(\rho\). 2. Strong: each gain is at least \(\rho \,\kappa (\pi )\,\Delta_{T}\), above \(k\), so full orders hold under every candidate; beliefs are \((m(\pi ),\pi ,M(\pi ))\). 3. A purchase reaches \(M(\pi )\), rival gross profit there strictly exceeds the high cost, so entry after a purchase is \(1\); after other orders entry stays at \(\rho\). 4. Very strong: full orders stay, but rival gross profit at \(M(\pi )\) falls short of the high cost, so entry returns to \(\rho\) after each order.
