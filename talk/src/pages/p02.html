Received answer: strength cannot bring the rival in

  • The rival pays for diligence before it learns its value.
  • Against a stronger first bidder, the rival wins less often and pays more when it wins.
  • A strong incumbent therefore deters its rivals from entering
  • Fishman (1988) formalises the result. In equilibrium, a strong first bidder bids preemptively and the rival does not pay for diligence.

Plot twistThe received answer assumes what the rival knows about its value stays fixed, but the rival sees the stock price before diligence.

Fishman (1988); Hirshleifer and Png (1989). Auction entry: Samuelson (1985); McAfee and McMillan (1987); Levin and Smith (1994); Gentry and Stroup (2019).
