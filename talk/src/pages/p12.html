Effects of the incumbent strength

First building block Two incumbents, \(\tilde{F}\) and \(F\), with \(\tilde{F}\) FOSD \(F\); everything else fixed.

\({\boldsymbol{t}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}\): expected payoff to shareholders \({\boldsymbol{g}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}\): expected payoff to the rival

The bar length \(\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid j]\) is the same against both incumbents.

Rival gross profit

\(g_{H}\) and \(g_{L}\) both fall, so \(B(\mu )=g_{L}+\mu (g_{H}-g_{L})\) falls at every belief.

Shareholder spread

\(\Delta_{T}\) widens against \(\tilde{F}\), raising what an informed trader gains from \(j\).

A stronger incumbent lowers rival gross profit, yet widens the shareholder spread so informed orders gain more.
