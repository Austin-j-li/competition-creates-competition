When informed orders pay

Second building block. Informed orders pay when even the smallest gain is above the order cost.

\(K_{H}\) and \(K_{L}\) are the gains before \(k\) from a purchase in class H and a sale in class L.

\(K_{H}=e(1)\,\Delta_{T}\,[1-\mu (1)]\)

purchase in class H

\(K_{L}=e(-1)\,\Delta_{T}\,\mu (-1)\)

sale in class L

Entry is at least \(\rho\) and at most 1. The belief band puts the last factor between \(\kappa (\pi )\) and 1.

\[\rho\,\kappa (\pi )\,\Delta_{T}\le K_{H},\,K_{L}\le\Delta_{T},\quad\text{with }\kappa (\pi )=\mathrm{min}\{m(\pi ),1-M(\pi )\}\]

Whether informed orders are sent turns on the shareholder spread against the order cost. A stronger incumbent widens the spread, so it can make informed orders pay.
