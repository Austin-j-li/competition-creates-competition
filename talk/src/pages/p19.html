Main resultEntry can rise from the weak incumbent \(F_{0}\) to the strong incumbent \(F_{1}\) and fall back to \(\rho\) at the very strong incumbent \(F_{2}\), with a unique equilibrium outcome at each of the three.

With news fixedWhen what the rival can learn stays fixed, a stronger incumbent can only lower entry. The rise comes from the news that trading puts in the price.

Between \({\boldsymbol{F}}_{\mathbf{0}}\) and \({\boldsymbol{F}}_{\mathbf{1}}\)Entry is at least \(\rho\) in every equilibrium at every strength, and the outcome at \(F_{0}\), \(F_{1}\) and \(F_{2}\) is unique with no selection used. In the example the equilibrium played matters only at strengths to , where it can set entry at \(\rho\) or above \(\rho\); \(r_{0}\), \(r_{1}\) and \(r_{2}\) lie outside those strengths.

ExtensionA wider shareholder spread raises costly research by the informed trader, up to a cap. Where research rises, entry rises and the rivals who prepare are more often high-synergy rivals.
