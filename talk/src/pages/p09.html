Perfect Bayesian equilibrium with competitive prices.
Informed trader

In class \(j\) it sends only orders that maximise

\[U_{j}(q)=q\cdot e(q)\cdot \Delta_{T}\cdot [\mathbf{1}\{j=H\}-\mu (q)]-k|q|\]

Entry × shareholder spread × how far the market’s belief is from the truth, minus the order cost. It may mix when indifferent.

Market makersBelief \(\mu (x)=\mathrm{Pr}(j=H\mid X=x)\) by Bayes’ rule. Price \(P(x)=\mathbb{E}[V_{T}\mid X=x]\)

RivalReads the price by Bayes’ rule and prepares if and only if \(B(\mu )\ge C\). Once prepared, it learns its value \(\theta\) and bids \(\theta\).

IncumbentAlready prepared; bids its value \(R\).

No belief assumedThe liquidity trader can send each order in each class. So every order has positive probability, and Bayes’ rule gives every belief.

AssumptionsValues and costs are per share; the target’s standalone value is 0; all agents are risk neutral. Every rival value meets the reserve \(p\), and means are finite. \((j,\theta )\), \(R\), \(C\), who trades and the liquidity order are independent. Neither bidder trades target shares. Parameters and distributions are common knowledge.
