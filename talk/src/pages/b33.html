The order slot · Paper §2.3

Informed trader: fills the slot with probability \(\lambda\); sees synergy \(j\); picks \(q\in \{-1,0,1\}\); a nonzero order costs \(k>0\)
Liquidity trader: fills the slot with probability \(1-\lambda\); sells, does nothing, or buys with chances \(\beta ,\;1-2\beta ,\;\beta\), where \(0<\beta \le 1/3\)
Order slot: one public order \(X\in \{-1,0,1\}\); no observer sees who filled it
Market makers: see \(X\) only; set \(P=\mathbb{E}[V_{T}\mid X]\), where \(V_{T}\) is the target shareholders' payoff per share
Rival: sees the price \(P\) and cost \(C\); sees neither \(X\) nor \(j\)

A liquidity order in one direction arrives with probability \(b=(1-\lambda )\beta\). Set \(u=\lambda /b\). The number \(1+u\) caps the likelihood ratio of one order.

Alternative arrival: Dow and Gorton (1997). Unit orders and unit cost: Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang (2015). Zero-profit pricing: Glosten and Milgrom (1985); Kyle (1985).
