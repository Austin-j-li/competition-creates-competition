Literature review and contribution

StrandWhat it showsHere
Price feedback: Dow, Goldstein and Guembel (2017, closest antecedent); Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang (2015) Corporate governance decisions based on stock market signals Extend the framework to model third party decisions (rival) in a merger auction setting
Costly entry: Fishman (1988); Roberts and Sweeting (2013) Holding the bidder’s information fixed, a stronger opponent can only lower entry The stock market as an information channel that informs the auction entry decision
Takeover trade: Edmans, Goldstein and Jiang (2012); Kyle and Vila (1991) The trader is the bidder itself, or price matters as the cost of the deal The trader is an outsider specialist with no stake. Its order reaches the rival before the rival prepares, serving as an information channel
Price formation: Glosten and Milgrom (1985); Dow and Gorton (1997) Market makers price each order without knowing whether an informed trader placed it The trading stage of this paper builds on this strand of literature

Contribution: I extend models of costly auction entry by treating the target’s share price as a signal about the rival’s own synergies and showing how incumbent strength shapes what the rival can learn from it.
