Payoffs and Proposition 1 in full

Payoff objects · Paper §2.4 and §3.1

Take a prepared rival with value \(\theta \ge p\). It pays \(\max\{p,R\}\) when it wins. The incumbent pays \(\theta\) when it wins.

\[t(\theta )={\mathbb{E}}_{R}\left[ \max\{p,\min(R,\theta )\} \right],  g(\theta )={\mathbb{E}}_{R}\left[ (\theta -\max\{p,R\})_{+} \right],  t(\theta )+g(\theta )=\theta\]

\({\mathbb{E}}_{R}\) averages over the incumbent's value \(R\sim F\).

Synergy means: \(t_{j}={\mathbb{E}}_{j}[t(\theta )]\) and \(g_{j}={\mathbb{E}}_{j}[g(\theta )]\) for \(j\in \{H,L\}\). Without entry, \(t_{0}=p[1-F(p)]\).

Shareholder spread: \(\Delta_{T}=t_{H}-t_{L}\). Read it as: how much more shareholders get with high synergy than with low synergy.

At belief \(\mu =\mathrm{Pr}(H)\), expected rival gross profit is \(B(\mu )=g_{L}+\mu (g_{H}-g_{L})\).

Win chance: \(W_{j}(F)={\mathbb{E}}_{j}[F(\theta )\mathbf{1}\{\theta \ge p\}]\). A high-synergy rival outbids \(F\) when \(W_{H}(F)=1\).

The traded claim and the rival's claim sum to the same value. Strength moves the split.
