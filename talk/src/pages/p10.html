Effects of the incumbent strength

First building block Two incumbents, \(\tilde{F}\) and \(F\), with \(\tilde{F}\) FOSD \(F\); everything else fixed.

\(\theta =t(\theta )+g(\theta )\),   so in each class  \(\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid j]=t_{j}+g_{j}\)

\({\boldsymbol{t}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}\): expected payoff to shareholders \({\boldsymbol{g}}_{\boldsymbol{j}}\): expected payoff to the rival

The bar length \(\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid j]\) is the same against both incumbents.

Rival gross profit
\[B(\mu ;\tilde{F})\le B(\mu ;F)\text{ for every belief }\mu\]

\(g_{H}\) and \(g_{L}\) both fall, so \(B(\mu )=g_{L}+\mu (g_{H}-g_{L})\) falls at every belief.

Shareholder spread
\[\Delta_{T}(\tilde{F})\ge \Delta_{T}(F)\]

\(\Delta_{T}\) widens against \(\tilde{F}\). An informed trader who knows the class can trade on it.

A stronger incumbent lowers rival gross profit, but it raises the shareholder spread so the informed orders gain more.
