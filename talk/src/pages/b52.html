Frequency-weighted price impacts can reveal recruitment

Weighted price impact (Proposition A.10)

Within one public state (fixed target, prior, slot and incumbent distribution), assume the class ranking, \(\Delta_{T}>0\), \(g_{H}>g_{L}\), full informed orders (class \(H\) buys; class \(L\) sells) and \(\pi <\tau\).

At the prior \(\pi\), rival gross profit falls short of \(c_{H}\).

Define order frequencies \(\bar{f}(x)=\mathrm{Pr}(X=x)\) and impacts \(I_{+}=P(1)-P(0)\) and \(I_{-}=P(0)-P(-1)\).

Then \(\bar{f}(1)=b+\pi \lambda\), \(\bar{f}(-1)=b+(1-\pi )\lambda\), and

\[\bar{f}(1)I_{+}-\bar{f}(-1)I_{-}>0 \Leftrightarrow e(1)>\rho .\]

At \(\pi =1/2\), \(\bar{f}(1)=\bar{f}(-1)\), so

\[I_{+}>I_{-} \Leftrightarrow e(1)>\rho .\]

Equivalent form: \(\bar{f}(1)I_{+}-\bar{f}(-1)I_{-}=\mathbb{E}[P(X)]-P(0)\). The no-order price is below the mean price exactly when a purchase recruits.

A purchase is good news about the rival's synergy; a sale order is bad news. If a purchase also brings in a rival with costly diligence, it adds the entry part. Weighted by frequency, it then moves the price more than a sale order, and only then.

Without recruitment, orders predict the winner when a prepared class-\(H\) rival wins more often than a class-\(L\) one: B13. Empirical slot: fixed in advance, such as the opening auction on the first trading day after the sale is public; record no order as \(x=0\).
