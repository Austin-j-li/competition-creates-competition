Informative and uninformative markets at one strength

Coexistence in full: interval parts and marked strengths

StrengthValue in the exampleRole
\(r_{A}\)first strength with an informative market
\(r_{F}\)first strength with a certain purchase in it
\(r_{N}\)last strength with an uninformative market
\(r_{J}\)the full purchase is alone from here
\(r_{S}\)the informed trader sells for certain on low synergy from here
\(r_{C}\)entry ceiling: the top belief stops short of \(c_{H}\) above \(r_{C}\)
  • Before \(r_{A}\), a purchase cannot pay at any mix rate, so no informative market exists
  • On \([r_{A},r_{F})\), the mix rate adjusts with strength and entry rises with it
  • From \(r_{F}\) to \(r_{N}\), a full purchase coexists with an uninformative market and entry in it is constant
  • Past \(r_{N}\), an uninformative market fails: a unit order against the uninformative price pays. Mixed orders at the entry floor join the full purchase on \((r_{N},r_{J})\). From \(r_{J}\), the full purchase is alone

In general, strengths with coexistence need not form one interval. Sure-purchase strengths form one interval. Under the nonfalling product \(\Delta_{T}(g_{H}-c_{H})\), the whole set forms one interval at each prior. After no order: \(\mu (0)=\pi\) at \(r_{0}\), \(r_{1}\) and \(r_{2}\); in the informative market below \(r_{S}\), \(m(\pi )\le \mu (0)<\pi\). Entry after no order is \(\rho\) in each case.
