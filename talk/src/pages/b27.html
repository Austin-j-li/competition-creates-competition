At the strong incumbent \(F_{1}\), in expectation

Price access (Proposition A.7)

Under Proposition 2's hypotheses, hold the incumbent at \(F_{1}\) (strong) and all other primitives fixed. Compare two economies. With access, the rival sees the price before it decides whether to prepare. With no access, it sees neither the price nor the order. Market makers set a competitive price in both.

Let \(\mathcal{R}\) denote the expected payoff of the target shareholders and \(\mathcal{W}\) expected acquisition surplus net of preparation costs (net surplus); superscript \(a\) is access, \(n\) is no access. Both economies keep unique full informed orders: class \(H\) buys; class \(L\) sells. Then

\[{\mathcal{R}}^{a}>{\mathcal{R}}^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathcal{W}}^{a}>{\mathcal{W}}^{n}.\]

Access adds preparation by the costly rival only after a purchase, where rival gross profit at the top belief, \(B_{1}(M(\pi ))\), exceeds \(c_{H}\).
