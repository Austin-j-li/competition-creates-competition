Market makers

See the order \(x\), not who sent it. They are competitive, so \(P(x)=\mathbb{E}[V_{T}\mid X=x]\).

The price
\[\begin{aligned}P(x)&=\htmlClass{pf-t0}{[1-e(x)]\,t_{0}}+\htmlClass{pf-tE}{e(x)}[\htmlClass{pf-tH}{\mu (x)\,t_{H}}+\htmlClass{pf-tL}{(1-\mu (x))\,t_{L}}]\\&=t_{0}+e(x)[t_{L}-t_{0}+\Delta_{T}\,\mu (x)]\end{aligned}\]

\(\mu (x)=\mathrm{Pr}(j=H\mid X=x)\), updated by Bayes’ rule.
\(e(x)\): entry after order \(x\), decided after the price is set.
\(t_{0}\): expected shareholder payoff when the rival does not prepare.

What the rival learns from the price

With a positive shareholder spread \(\Delta_{T}\), the price rises with the belief, so the rival reads \(\mu (x)\) off the price: \(\mu (x)=\mu (P)\).

A high price attracts the rival, and the rival’s entry is part of what makes the price high.
