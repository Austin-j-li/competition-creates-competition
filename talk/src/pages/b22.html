Why each outcome is unique

Posterior bounds, entry and prices

Lemma A.1 (order probabilities and beliefs), for any order strategy: each order has positive probability in each synergy class. For a sale or a purchase, the chance of that order in class \(j\) lies between \(b\) and \(b+\lambda\), where \(b=(1-\lambda )\beta\) is the probability of a liquidity order in one direction and \(\lambda\) is presence. With \(u=\lambda /b\), each nonzero order has likelihood ratio between \(1/(1+u)\) and \(1+u\), so the belief \(\mu\) after that order lies in \([m(\pi ),M(\pi )]\), where \(M(\pi )=\pi (1+u)/[\pi (1+u)+1-\pi ]\) and \(m(\pi )=\pi /[\pi +(1-\pi )(1+u)]\). The no-order case lies in the same band when \(\beta \le 1/3\). Under full orders, high synergy buys and low synergy sells, the beliefs after a sale, no order, a purchase are \((m(\pi ),\pi ,M(\pi ))\). A belief at a price lies between the smallest and largest beliefs of the orders with that price.

Lemma A.2 (entry and prices): entry \(e(x)\ge \rho\) after each order \(x\), with entry floor \(\rho \in (0,1)\). The price after order \(x\) is \(P(x)=t_{0}+e(x)[t_{L}-t_{0}+\Delta_{T}\,\mu (x)]\), with payoff \(t_{0}\) without entry and \(t_{L}\) under low synergy. When \(\Delta_{T}>0\) and rival gross profit rises with the belief \(\mu\), equal prices give equal beliefs, so the price reveals \(\mu (x)\); entry is \(\rho\) when \(\mu (x)<\tau\) and \(1\) when \(\mu (x)\ge \tau\), with threshold \(\tau\) (at \(\mu (x)=\tau\), any entry in \([\rho ,1]\) can hold when an indifferent costly rival may prepare with any probability); prices rise strictly with \(\mu\).
