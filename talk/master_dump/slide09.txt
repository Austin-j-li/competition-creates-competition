SPEAKER NOTES:
Now the first stage: how the order is priced. Market makers see the order but not who sent it, and competition sets the price at the expected payoff of shareholders. What can shareholders expect? Read the tree from the left. After the order, either the rival prepares or it does not. If it does not, shareholders are paid t zero. If it does, it has high or low synergy, and shareholders are paid t H or t L. The price is the average of those three payoffs. The first line of the formula is the tree read branch by branch; the second line is the same thing rearranged, and it is the one I use from here on. Only two probabilities depend on the order: the belief that the rival has high synergy, and the chance that it prepares. That is all the price reflects. And the rival, who sees only the price, can recover that belief from it: with a positive shareholder spread, a higher belief means a higher price. With no spread, the price would say nothing about the class. So a high price attracts the rival, and the rival’s entry is part of what makes the price high.
