SPEAKER NOTES:
A high price attracts the rival, and the rival’s entry is part of what makes the price high. The trading strategy, price and preparation decision therefore have to be found together. Equilibrium, player by player. The informed trader sends only orders that maximise its payoff. Substituting the price, that payoff is one product: entry, times the shareholder spread, times how far the market’s belief is from the truth, minus the order cost. Market makers use Bayes’ rule and break even on every order. The rival reads the price by Bayes’ rule and prepares when rival gross profit covers its cost. Once prepared, it learns its value and bids that value. The incumbent is already prepared and bids its own value. Truthful bidding is weakly dominant in the auction; trading, prices and the rival’s preparation must be mutually consistent. Two points theorists ask about. First, I assume no belief anywhere: the liquidity trader can send each order in each class, so every order is on the path. Second, I select no equilibrium: at the three incumbents I compare, the outcome is unique. The draws are independent, neither bidder trades target shares, and everything but the private draws is common knowledge.
